## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Connectivity Realities in Indian Transit:** High-speed 4G/5G coverage is inconsistent across urban corridors, flyovers, subways, and rural stretches [KNOWN]. The edge device must operate seamlessly under intermittent connectivity, buffering telemetry packets in onboard SPI Flash memory and re-transmitting via lightweight MQTT payloads upon reconnection [RESEARCHED].
2. **Harsh Operating Environment:** Vehicle cabins experience high thermal stress (up to 45°C–50°C in Indian summers), high mechanical vibration, and wide power fluctuation (9V to 32V vehicle battery transients) [RESEARCHED]. Device enclosure must be IP65-rated, utilize automotive-grade buck converters, and include passive heatsinking [ASSUMPTION].
3. **Non-Lane Chaotic Traffic Conditions:** Standard Western Intelligent Transport System (ITS) assumptions regarding strict lane discipline and uniform vehicle speeds do not hold in Indian traffic [RESEARCHED]. Computer vision models for collision avoidance must handle heterogeneous traffic (two-wheelers, auto-rickshaws, pedestrians) [KNOWN].
4. **Digital Literacy & Multilingual Diversity:** Commuters and bus drivers speak regional languages and vary widely in tech familiarity [KNOWN]. Interface designs must prioritize high-contrast visual cues, localized audio alerts, regional language support (Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, etc.), and single-tap operations [INFERENCE].
5. **Hardware Budget Constraints:** SRTUs and private fleet owners operate on thin margins and cannot afford multi-thousand-dollar telematics hardware per bus [RESEARCHED]. The edge hardware design must target an affordable Bill of Materials (BOM) under ₹3,500–₹5,000 ($40–$60 USD) per unit [ASSUMPTION].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Cloud-Centric Mobile App Telematics (Software-Only)
* **Mechanism:** Driver mounts a low-cost smartphone running a specialized background app that streams phone GPS, accelerometer, and camera data to a central cloud server.
* **Problem Fit:** Low. Solves software tracking but lacks dedicated hardware reliability, hardwired ignition detection, tamper-resistance, and AIS-140 compliance [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Cost:** Very low development complexity; minimal hardware cost.
* **Explainability & Scalability:** Highly explainable, easy cloud scaling.
* **Hackathon vs. Production Feasibility:** Highly feasible for hackathon; unacceptable for real-world municipal fleet production due to device theft, driver app manipulation, and lack of vehicle power integration [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Standalone Microcontroller Hardware Telematics (Hardware-Heavy)
* **Mechanism:** Dedicated microcontroller (ESP32/STM32) connected to GPS, GSM, and basic IMU sensors. Logs telemetry directly to an onboard SD card and sends raw HTTP POST requests to a database.
* **Problem Fit:** Medium. Delivers low-cost hardware tracking but lacks computer vision capabilities for driver drowsiness monitoring or advanced ML travel time forecasting [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Cost:** Medium hardware assembly complexity; low hardware cost (approx. ₹2,500).
* **Explainability & Scalability:** Simple rule-based execution; poor scalability for multi-modal routing or GTFS-RT open integration.
* **Hackathon vs. Production Feasibility:** Moderate build effort, but lacks the software sophistication expected in an SIH software track submission [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Hybrid Intelligent Edge-Telemetry & Cloud GTFS-RT Ecosystem (Recommended)
* **Mechanism:** Dual-layer architecture consisting of:
  1. **Edge Intelligence Unit:** An ESP32 telemetry module (GPS/NavIC + IMU + OBD-II) paired with a Raspberry Pi/Smartphone vision edge worker running MediaPipe/MobileNet for real-time driver alertness and passenger density detection.
  2. **Cloud Analytics Engine:** A scalable Python/FastAPI + PostgreSQL/PostGIS backend running LightGBM dynamic ETA prediction, generating protobuf GTFS-RT feeds, and managing V2I emergency hazard broadcasts.
  3. **Commuter PWA & Kiosk:** A React PWA providing live bus tracking, crowding indicators, and NCMC digital ticketing simulation.
* **Problem Fit:** Exceptional. Fulfills the "intelligent devices" mandate while delivering a sophisticated software platform aligned with SIH Software category expectations [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Cost:** High technical depth; manageable hardware cost (approx. ₹4,500 for prototype); balances hardware integration with deep software engineering.
* **Explainability & Scalability:** Clear separation of edge and cloud responsibilities; highly scalable standard interfaces (MQTT, REST, GTFS-RT).
* **Hackathon vs. Production Feasibility:** Highly feasible for a 4–6 student team using benchtop test rigs and simulated multi-bus telemetry [ASSUMPTION].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Edge Telemetry Acquisition:** Onboard the vehicle, the ESP32-S3 samples the GNSS receiver every 1 second. It reads latitude, longitude, speed, heading, and NavIC satellite lock. Simultaneously, the MPU6050 accelerometer reads 3-axis forces to detect harsh braking or sudden impact [RESEARCHED].
2. **Local Edge Inference:** The cabin vision camera passes 15 FPS video to the local OpenCV/MediaPipe pipeline on the Raspberry Pi edge device. If the computed Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) falls below 0.20 for longer than 1.5 seconds, an instant local buzzer alerts the driver, and a high-priority "fatigue_flag" is raised [RESEARCHED].
3. **Transmission & Ingestion:** Telemetry and alert payloads are formatted into compressed JSON/AIS-140 compliant byte packets and published via MQTT over TLS 1.3 to the cloud EMQX message broker [RESEARCHED].
4. **Stream Processing & Validation:** The cloud ingestion worker validates packet signatures, parses raw GPS coordinates, and performs map-matching using PostGIS to snap noisy points onto known bus route polylines [INFERENCE].
5. **Dynamic ETA & GTFS-RT Broadcast:** The ETA microservice feeds current segment speed, time-of-day, historical route delay, and weather factors into the pre-trained LightGBM model. Updated travel time predictions are converted into GTFS-Realtime `TripUpdates` and `VehiclePositions` Protobuf format and cached in Redis [RESEARCHED].
6. **Commuter & Operator Delivery:** The commuter React PWA polls/subscribes to Redis WebSockets, instantly reflecting updated bus markers, occupancy icons, and arrival countdowns on interactive Leaflet/Mapbox maps [INFERENCE].

### Detailed AI Component Analysis

#### 1. Driver Drowsiness & Distraction Detection
* **Exact Purpose:** Continuously monitor driver face stream to detect micro-sleeps, prolonged eye closure, yawning, and mobile phone usage [RESEARCHED].
* **Input:** RGB video frame stream (640x480 @ 15 FPS) from cabin-facing USB camera.
* **Output:** Drowsiness Confidence Score (0.0 to 1.0), Alert Status (NORMAL, WARNING, CRITICAL), Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) value.
* **Why Simpler Rule System Fails:** Basic fixed eye-closure thresholds fail across different human facial structures, lighting conditions, and wearing glasses. ML-based facial landmark tracking adapts dynamically [RESEARCHED].
* **Required Data:** NTHU-Driver-Drowsiness Dataset / YawDD dataset + custom annotated team facial videos [RESEARCHED].
* **Evaluation Metric:** Precision, Recall, and Frame-level F1-score (targeting >92% sensitivity on micro-sleep detection).
* **Failure Mode & Fallback:** If camera is obstructed or ambient light drops below threshold (night without IR), system falls back to IMU-based erratic steering detection (sudden lateral acceleration spikes) [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight:** High severity alerts generate a short 3-second anonymized clip forwarded to the depot manager dashboard for human review [INFERENCE].

#### 2. Dynamic Vehicle ETA Predictor
* **Exact Purpose:** Predict segment travel times for public transit buses operating under non-lane, congested urban conditions [RESEARCHED].
* **Input:** Current bus speed, route ID, stop segment ID, time of day, day of week, recent 15-minute moving average speed along segment, weather condition.
* **Output:** Estimated travel time (seconds) to downstream stops with lower/upper confidence bounds.
* **Why Simpler Rule System Fails:** Static speed rules (Distance / Average Speed) produce errors exceeding 300% during urban peak traffic shifts and junction choke points [RESEARCHED].
* **Required Data:** Historical GTFS-RT vehicle position logs, OpenStreetMap road network segments, local rainfall logs [RESEARCHED].
* **Evaluation Metric:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) in minutes across test trips.
* **Failure Mode & Fallback:** If model inference fails or required features are missing, system falls back to a rolling 5-minute historical average speed heuristic for that specific route segment [INFERENCE].

#### 3. Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **Panic Button & Emergency SOS:** Handled purely by hardware interrupts and direct, un-ML-filtered MQTT/SMS alerts to guarantee zero false-negative latency during actual safety crises [INFERENCE].
* **Geofence Breach Detection:** Handled via deterministic spatial PostGIS polygon intersection queries (`ST_Contains`), which are 100% accurate and mathematically reliable [INFERENCE].

### Offline & Resilience Strategy

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        OFFLINE DATA SYNCHRONIZATION FLOW                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

[ NORMAL NETWORK CONDITION ]
Edge Device ---> MQTT Stream (QoS 1) ---> Cloud Ingestion ---> DB Store & Redis Cache

[ NETWORK LOSS DETECTED ]
Edge Device ---> Local SPI Flash / SQLite Buffer (Circular Buffer, 64MB memory)
             ---> Retains last 20,000 telemetry points (~6 hours tracking data)

[ NETWORK RECOVERED ]
Edge Device ---> Initiates Chunked Sync (Batch size: 50 records)
             ---> Transmits with 'offline_buffered=true' flag
             ---> Cloud Ingestion validates timestamps & backfills PostGIS history
```

* **Bandwidth Reduction:** Telemetry payloads use compact JSON key-value pairs (e.g., `{"v":"B12","t":1725523200,"l":[28.61,77.20],"s":34.2}`) or Protocol Buffers, reducing transmission size from 1.2KB to under 120 bytes per tick [INFERENCE].
* **Conflict Handling:** Records are strictly ordered by hardware GPS atomic clock timestamps (`timestamp`), eliminating out-of-order synchronization corruption [INFERENCE].
* **Offline Commuter PWA:** The React PWA leverages Service Workers and IndexedDB to cache static route maps, stop schedules, and recent offline tickets, ensuring access even without active internet access [INFERENCE].

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

* **Data Privacy Compliance (DPDP Act 2023):** Video frames captured by the cabin-facing camera are processed entirely in ephemeral RAM on the edge unit. No raw facial video or identifiable passenger images are stored or streamed to the cloud; only anonymized scalar metrics (EAR values, numerical crowd counts) are transmitted [RESEARCHED].
* **Location Privacy:** Commuter location tracking on the mobile app is strictly opt-in and utilized solely for local stop proximity calculations. Location coordinates are never persisted on the backend [INFERENCE].
* **Device Authentication & Transport Encryption:** All edge hardware units authenticate via unique TLS X.509 client certificates and token authentication. Telemetry streams are encrypted in transit via TLS 1.3 over MQTT [RESEARCHED].
* **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC):**
  * *Commuter:* Read-only access to GTFS-RT public positions, routes, and ETAs.
  * *Fleet Operator:* Full access to vehicle diagnostic logs, driver scorecards, and historical telemetry.
  * *System Admin:* Device provision, certificate revocation, and system-wide configurations.
* **Liability & AI Risk Management:** Audio-visual driver alerts serve strictly as driver assistance (ADAS Level 0/1 equivalent). The software platform disclaims direct legal control over vehicle operation; ultimate operational control rests with the certified driver [INFERENCE].
