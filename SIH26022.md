## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Agarbatti (incense stick) manufacturing is a vital home-based micro-enterprise for rural women artisans in India, supported by government initiatives under the Ministry of MSME, KVIC, and DAY-NRLM [KNOWN]. However, traditional open-air sun drying leaves artisans vulnerable to rainfall, high humidity, airborne dust, fungal contamination, uneven moisture evaporation, stick bending, and severe fragrance loss due to uncontrolled thermal degradation [RESEARCHED]. This results in product rejection rates as high as 25–40% during monsoons and reduced market income [INFERENCE]. This proposal presents **AgroScent SmartDry & Pack**, an affordable, solar-powered hybrid forced-convection drying chamber combined with a low-power, eco-friendly aroma-preserving sealing device [ASSUMPTION]. Powered by a low-cost solar-thermal air collector and a 12V PV-battery system, the system features an ESP32 microcontroller with ambient/chamber humidity and temperature sensors to auto-regulate DC exhaust fans and auxiliary heating elements [INFERENCE]. A compact, impulse-heat sealing unit utilizes aqueous-coated Kraft paper / biodegradable barrier pouches to lock in volatile essential oils [RESEARCHED]. An optional lightweight ML curve-fitting model running locally on the microcontroller estimates remaining drying time and sends visual/buzzer alerts to artisans [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Geography & Weather:** Deployed in rural interiors across Indian states (e.g., Karnataka, Assam, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar) where ambient relative humidity can exceed 85–90% during monsoons and sunshine can drop to 2–3 hours daily [RESEARCHED].
* **Connectivity Realities:** Cellular coverage in rural homes is frequently spotty (2G/3G or weak 4G); cloud-only IoT architectures will fail in the field [INFERENCE]. Edge processing and local visual/audible signaling are strictly necessary [INFERENCE].
* **Power Supply:** Rural grid power suffers from frequent outages (6–12 hours/day) and low voltages [KNOWN]. The drying and packaging system MUST be completely grid-independent via solar energy [INFERENCE].
* **Digital Literacy & User Experience:** Rural women artisans may have limited formal education or smartphone access [KNOWN]. Interfaces must rely on physical push-buttons, simple LED lights (Red/Yellow/Green), and localized voice/audio alerts [INFERENCE].
* **Hardware & Maintenance:** Spare parts must be locally accessible (e.g., standard 12V automotive fans, standard PVC/aluminum fittings, off-the-shelf heating elements) to allow local mechanics or SHG technicians to perform repairs [INFERENCE].
* **Economic Constraints:** Individual artisan household budget for capital equipment is low (₹5,000 to ₹12,000 max, or up to ₹25,000 per SHG cluster under government subsidy) [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 2: Smart Hybrid Solar-PV Forced Convection Chamber with ESP32 Control and Integrated Low-Power Packaging System** [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 (Passive Solar Box) Rejected:** Passive solar dryers lack airflow control. Trapped moisture leads to high humidity inside the box during morning hours, causing fungal mold growth and severe stick warping. Furthermore, temperature spikes above 60°C under bright sun evaporate the essential volatile fragrances, directly violating explicit requirement #5 [PS].
* **Approach 3 (Automated Conveyor System) Rejected:** Industrial conveyor systems require continuous AC motor power (> 500W), making off-grid solar operation prohibitively expensive due to large battery bank requirements. The mechanical complexity, weight, and high cost render it completely unviable for individual home-based rural women artisans [INFERENCE].

### Architectural Rationale for Selected Approach
Approach 2 achieves the optimal balance between physical thermal physics and low-cost embedded electronics [INFERENCE]:
1. **Thermal Efficiency:** Direct solar-thermal collector pre-heats incoming air by 10–15°C above ambient, drastically lowering relative humidity and multiplying its water absorption capacity [RESEARCHED].
2. **Quality Control:** Microcontroller-driven PWM DC fans continuously purge moisture-laden air while maintaining internal temperatures between 40°C and 50°C, preserving delicate volatile aromatic compounds [RESEARCHED].
3. **Off-Grid Reliability:** A small 12V 15Ah LiFePO4 battery paired with a 40W solar panel ensures continuous drying operation even during monsoon rainstorms or nighttime cycles [INFERENCE].
4. **Eco-Packaging Integration:** A compact, low-power impulse sealer enables instant on-site packaging in eco-friendly aqueous-coated Kraft pouches, locking in aroma and blocking moisture re-entry [RESEARCHED].

### Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Air Intake & Pre-heating:** Ambient fresh air enters the solar thermal collector collector channel. Solar radiation passing through the top polycarbonate sheet heats the corrugated black aluminum absorber plate, warming incoming air by 10°C to 15°C and dropping its relative humidity (RH) by 25–35% [RESEARCHED].
2. **Chamber Circulation & Sensing:** Warm, dry air enters the base of the insulated chamber and flows upward through perforated aluminum trays holding wet agarbatti sticks. Dual DHT22 sensors measure internal chamber RH ($RH_{chamber}$) and temperature ($T_{chamber}$), alongside ambient conditions ($RH_{ambient}, T_{ambient}$) [INFERENCE].
3. **Smart Actuation Loop:**
   * If $T_{chamber} > 52^\circ\text{C}$ (fragrance danger zone), the ESP32 activates the servo motor to open top vent dampers and increases fan PWM to 100% to purge heat [INFERENCE].
   * If $RH_{chamber} > 60\%$ and $T_{chamber} < 45^\circ\text{C}$, the controller throttles fan speed to retain thermal mass while pulsing an auxiliary 12V 30W PTC heating element (powered by solar/battery) [INFERENCE].
   * When $RH_{chamber}$ drops below $25\%$ and stabilizes (indicating stick moisture has reached ~8-10%), the system triggers the green completion LED and sounds the piezo buzzer [INFERENCE].
4. **Sealing Execution:** The artisan removes dry sticks and feeds aqueous-coated Kraft paper pouches into the benchtop impulse sealer. Pressing the mechanical lever closes a microswitch, delivering a precise 1.8-second 12V DC heating pulse to seal the pouch airtight [INFERENCE].
5. **Data Logging:** Operational stats (drying duration, final RH, batch count) are saved into ESP32 SPIFFS flash memory and synced via BLE to the artisan's smartphone app when within range [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Proposed AI/ML Component: Edge-Based Drying Time Predictor
* **Purpose:** To predict the remaining drying duration (in minutes) for a batch of wet agarbatti based on initial moisture, chamber temperature, ambient humidity, and solar thermal flux [INFERENCE].
* **Model Type:** Lightweight Polynomial Regression / Decision Tree Regressor converted to C++ via `emlearn` or `TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers` [INFERENCE].
* **Input Features:** Chamber Temp ($T_c$), Chamber RH ($RH_c$), Ambient Temp ($T_a$), Ambient RH ($RH_a$), Elapsed Time ($t$), RH Decay Rate ($\Delta RH / \Delta t$) [INFERENCE].
* **Output:** Estimated Time Remaining (e.g., "75 minutes until completion") [INFERENCE].
* **Data Required:** 30–50 lab test drying runs measuring weight loss vs. temperature/humidity over time [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI/ML is Preferable to Simple Rules:** While a simple threshold rule ($RH_c < 25\%$) can detect *completion*, it cannot estimate *remaining time* early in the drying cycle. A trained regression model accurately fits non-linear exponential drying curves under fluctuating solar weather [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation Metrics:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE < 15 minutes) [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode & Fallback:** If sensor readings drop out or ML inference outputs anomalous numbers (< 0 or > 12 hours), the system falls back to a deterministic threshold rule ($RH_c < 25\%$ AND $T_c > 40^\circ\text{C}$ for 30 consecutive minutes) [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight:** The artisan can visually inspect a test stick (flexible bend test) before removing the batch, overriding system alerts [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **Temperature Control & Over-Heat Safety:** Safety cutoffs and exhaust fan modulation MUST rely on hardcoded deterministic C++ threshold logic/PID loops (e.g., `if (temp > 52) trigger_vent();`). AI models must NEVER control thermal safety cutoffs to avoid unpredictable behavior [INFERENCE].
* **Impulse Sealer Duration:** Heating pulse timing (1.8 seconds) MUST be controlled by a hardware timer circuit or precise C++ microcontroller millisecond delay, NOT an AI inference loop [INFERENCE].

### Offline-First Synchronization & Low-Network Strategy
* **Local Caching:** All sensor readings and batch records are written directly to ESP32 SPIFFS flash memory or local SQLite database on the artisan's Android smartphone via BLE [INFERENCE].
* **Bandwidth Optimization:** Telemetry is sampled every 2 seconds for fan control, but stored locally only as 10-minute moving averages, reducing data size to < 20 KB per batch [INFERENCE].
* **Sync Mechanism:** When the phone detects an active 3G/4G/Wi-Fi connection (e.g., when the artisan visits a nearby market or town center), a background worker uploads pending batch logs to the SHG Central Dashboard via lightweight HTTP POST JSON payloads [INFERENCE].
* **Conflict Resolution:** Local database uses UUIDs (`batch_id`) with client-side timestamps. Server append-only architecture ensures zero write collisions [INFERENCE].
