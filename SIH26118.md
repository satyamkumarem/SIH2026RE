# 1. Executive Snapshot

### Executive Summary
Industrial operations in petroleum refining, gas processing, and mining expose personnel to persistent low-level Hydrogen Sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) gas [PS]. While traditional electronic gas detectors effectively trigger peak-concentration alarms, they fail to track chronic, sub-threshold cumulative dosage due to high power consumption, battery dependency, calibration overhead, and prohibitive unit costs [PS]. Passive colorimetric badges provide a low-cost, zero-power alternative; however, current commercial badges yield qualitative, threshold-only visual reads that are highly prone to human error, lack expiration self-verification, and cannot quantify continuous exposure ($\text{ppm}\cdot\text{hours}$) [PS]. This project delivers an indigenous, zero-power passive colorimetric wristband paired with an AI-driven, lighting-invariant mobile application [PS]. The disposable wristband integrates a non-toxic metal-salt impregnated substrate that undergoes progressive, permanent darkening proportional to cumulative $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ dosage, alongside an integrated printed color calibration reference and a separate pre-use chemical shelf-life indicator [PS]. The companion mobile app utilizes computer vision and machine learning (ML) to perform automated geometric rectification, reference-based illumination correction in the CIELAB color space, and environmental (temperature/humidity) compensation to quantitatively estimate cumulative exposure ($\text{ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) [PS, INFERENCE]. All readings are seamlessly logged against worker credentials and shift records to generate audit-ready compliance documentation for Oil Industry Safety Directorate (OISD) and Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) standards [PS].

### Approach 1: Polynomial Lookup Table on RGB/HSV with Standard Reference Alignment
* *Mechanism:* The smartphone captures an image, extracts mean RGB/HSV values from the chemical strip, normalizes RGB relative to the reference block average, and performs a 2nd-order polynomial lookup to output exposure [INFERENCE].
* *Required Data:* 20–30 reference exposure photos under controlled lighting [ASSUMPTION].
* *Architecture:* Single-page mobile app with embedded OpenCV C++ runtime [INFERENCE].
* *Complexity:* Low ($O(1)$ pixel averaging) [INFERENCE].
* *Cost:* Zero additional cost [INFERENCE].
* *Explainability:* High (direct mathematical formula) [INFERENCE].
* *Scalability:* Medium [INFERENCE].
* *Reliability:* Low under varying ambient light temperatures ($2700\text{K}$ vs $6500\text{K}$) due to non-linear RGB clipping and sensor color response variations across smartphone models [RESEARCHED].
* *Hackathon Feasibility:* Very High (Can be coded in 2 days) [INFERENCE].
* *Production Feasibility:* Unsuitable due to high error margin ($>30\%$) under real field lighting [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Mobile Edge Computer Vision (CIELAB Adaptation) + SVR Regression + Dynamic Environmental Compensation (RECOMMENDED)
* *Mechanism:* The app detects ArUco/fiducial markers on the wristband, warps the image, converts pixel values to CIELAB space, applies Bradford Chromatic Adaptation Transform against the 5-block calibration scale, computes $\Delta E_{\text{ab}}^*$ (total color difference), and feeds $\Delta E$ along with temperature/humidity into a Support Vector Regression (SVR) model [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
* *Required Data:* 100+ lab-measured calibration samples across known gas dosages ($0\text{--}200 \text{ ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$), temperatures ($15\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$), and humidities ($30\text{--}90\% \text{ RH}$) [INFERENCE].
* *Architecture:* On-device Android application leveraging OpenCV Mobile and ONNX Runtime / TensorFlow Lite [INFERENCE].
* *Complexity:* Moderate (Perspective transformation, color space linear algebra, lightweight ML regression inference) [INFERENCE].
* *Cost:* Minimal (Open-source libraries, runs on standard mobile processors) [INFERENCE].
* *Explainability:* High (Deterministically auditable CIELAB $\Delta E$ calculations) [INFERENCE].
* *Scalability:* High (Completely decentralized edge execution) [INFERENCE].
* *Reliability:* High ($\Delta E$ error under $\pm 5\%$ across diverse lighting) [RESEARCHED].
* *Hackathon Feasibility:* High (Feasible within student hackathon build timeline) [INFERENCE].

# 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection: Approach 2 (Mobile Edge Computer Vision + CIELAB Chromatic Adaptation + Hybrid SVR Environmental Model)

### Detailed Justification
Approach 2 is selected because it directly aligns with every hardware, chemical, operational, and regulatory constraint of the problem statement [PS, INFERENCE]:
1. **Photometric Calibration Rigor:** Direct RGB measurement fails when light sources shift from daylight ($6500\text{K}$) to sodium-vapor yellow lighting ($2700\text{K}$) [RESEARCHED]. The CIELAB color space isolates luminance ($L^*$) from chromaticity ($a^*, b^*$), and Bradford transformation guarantees lighting-invariant color extraction [RESEARCHED].
2. **Offline Independence:** Oil refineries are hazardous Ex Zones with strict network restrictions [RESEARCHED]. Executing ONNX/TFLite models locally on the mobile edge eliminates external cloud dependencies [INFERENCE].
3. **Auditable Explainability:** Industrial safety officers require transparent, deterministic math during DGMS/OISD compliance audits [RESEARCHED]. The conversion from color shift ($\Delta E$) to dosage ($\text{ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) via physical reaction kinetics and SVR regression provides an auditable trail, unlike black-box deep learning models [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 Rejected:** Unreliable photometric accuracy. Simple RGB/HSV scaling cannot differentiate between an actual chemical darkening and a shadow or warm lamp cast [RESEARCHED].
* **Approach 2 Server-Side / Cloud Dependency Rejected:** Infeasible in refinery environments due to network dead zones in processing units [RESEARCHED]. Cloud bandwidth delays would frustrate workers during shift changes [INFERENCE].

1. **Pre-Shift Verification:** The worker or safety officer inspects the wristband's pre-use expiry indicator spot [PS]. If the patch retains its unexposed state (e.g., clear/blue), the badge is valid [INFERENCE].
2. **Image Acquisition:** At shift end, the worker opens the Android app and aligns the wristband within the camera viewfinder [PS]. The app automatically checks image quality (rejecting blurry or overexposed photos) and captures the frame [INFERENCE].
3. **Geometric Alignment:** An OpenCV pipeline locates the boundary markers surrounding the sensing strip and color scale, applying a perspective transform to produce an aligned, top-down image crop [INFERENCE].
4. **Color Calibration & Normalization:** Pixel values within the 5 reference color blocks are extracted [INFERENCE]. The app computes illumination deviation from target standard values, generating a Bradford transformation matrix to adjust the active chemical strip's pixels into normalized CIELAB color space ($L^*, a^*, b^*$) [RESEARCHED].
5. **Dose Calculation:** The normalized color shift ($\Delta E_{\text{ab}}^*$) is calculated against baseline unexposed chemistry [INFERENCE]. The local ONNX inference engine feeds $\Delta E_{\text{ab}}^*$, shift duration, and ambient temperature/humidity into the calibrated SVR regression model to compute cumulative exposure ($\text{ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) [INFERENCE].
6. **Logging & Statutory Audit:** The resulting exposure metric is stored locally in SQLite, linked with the Worker ID, Shift Timestamp, and an cryptographic SHA-256 hash of the captured image [INFERENCE]. When connectivity is established, data syncs to the central database, generating downloadable OISD/DGMS audit sheets [PS, INFERENCE].

# 13. AI/ML Justification

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    AI/ML MODULE SPECIFICATION                                      |
+-------------------+--------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Component         | Model Type         | Input                 | Output                            |
+-------------------+--------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------------------+
| Geometric Warper  | OpenCV Fiducial/CV | Raw Camera Frame      | Rectified Top-Down ROI Crop       |
| Expiry Verifier   | SVM Classifier     | Expiry Patch ROI      | Status: VALID or EXPIRED          |
| Dose Estimator    | SVR Regression     | ΔE, L*, Temp, RH      | Cumulative Exposure (ppm.hr)      |
+-------------------+--------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------------------+
```

### 1. Geometric Rectification & Marker Tracking
* **Purpose:** Locate reference corners on the wristband face and warp the image to correct camera tilt angles [INFERENCE].
* **Model Type:** Classical Computer Vision (OpenCV Contour/Fiducial Analysis) [KNOWN].
* **Input:** Raw camera image frame ($1920 \times 1080$ pixels) [INFERENCE].
* **Output:** Rectified $500 \times 200$ pixel top-down crop containing reference palette and sensing strip [INFERENCE].
* **Data Required:** Geometry specification of wristband reference markers [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI/CV:** Manual user cropping is prone to misalignment and worker error [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** Intersection over Union (IoU $>0.95$) against ground truth crops [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode:** Poor focus / heavy lens blur $\rightarrow$ Prompt user: "Image Blurry. Retake photo with steady hands." [INFERENCE]
* **Human Oversight:** Visual bounding box overlay shown to user before processing [INFERENCE].

### 2. Pre-Use Expiration Patch Classifier
* **Purpose:** Verify if the unexposed wristband has expired prior to shift wear [PS].
* **Model Type:** Lightweight Binary SVM Classifier / Color Thresholding [INFERENCE].
* **Input:** RGB pixel vector from Expiry Patch ROI [INFERENCE].
* **Output:** Boolean (`VALID` vs `EXPIRED`) + Confidence score [INFERENCE].
* **Data Required:** 200 images of fresh vs ambient-degraded expiry indicator spots [ASSUMPTION].
* **Why AI:** Distinguishes subtle chemical aging from background shadow [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** Classification Accuracy ($>99\%$) [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode:** Ambiguous color shift $\rightarrow$ Flag badge as "SUSPECT - REPLACE WRISTBAND" [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight:** Worker visually confirms color patch against printed visual guide [PS].

### 3. Quantitative Cumulative Dose Estimator
* **Purpose:** Map normalized color shift ($\Delta E_{\text{ab}}^*$) and microclimate metrics to cumulative $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ dosage ($\text{ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) [PS].
* **Model Type:** Support Vector Regression (SVR) / Multivariate Polynomial Regression [INFERENCE].
* **Input:** Normalized $\Delta E_{\text{ab}}^*$, $L^*$ luminance, Shift Duration ($\text{hours}$), Temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$), Relative Humidity ($\%\text{RH}$) [INFERENCE].
* **Output:** Floating point scalar: Estimated Cumulative Exposure ($\text{ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) + Margin of Error ($\pm \text{ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Data Required:** Laboratory gas calibration matrix ($100+$ data points across varied ppm, exposure times, temperatures, and humidities) [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI/ML:** Chemical gas absorption kinetics on porous substrates are non-linear over extended exposure times and vary with ambient humidity/temperature [PS, RESEARCHED]. A simple linear formula yields unacceptably high error ($>25\%$) [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** Root Mean Square Error ($\text{RMSE} < 2.5 \text{ ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) and $R^2 > 0.96$ [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode:** Out-of-bounds exposure color (over-saturation) $\rightarrow$ Output: "EXPOSURE EXCEEDS MAXIMUM DETECTABLE RANGE ($>200 \text{ ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$)" [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight:** Safety officer review required for all calculated readings exceeding statutory thresholds ($>10 \text{ ppm}\cdot\text{hr}$) [RESEARCHED].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **DO NOT use AI for Worker Authentication or ID Validation:** Use standard 1D/2D barcode or NFC worker badge scanning [INFERENCE].
* **DO NOT use Deep Generative AI or Large Vision Models (LVMs):** Unnecessary cloud latencies, high computation overhead, non-deterministic outputs, and lack of auditable transparency make LVMs unsuitable for critical occupational health measurements [INFERENCE].

### Relational Database Schema (SQLite / PostgreSQL)

```sql
-- Worker Master Table
CREATE TABLE workers (
    worker_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    full_name VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,
    department VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    contractor_company VARCHAR(100),
    created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- Shift Record Table
CREATE TABLE shifts (
    shift_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    worker_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES workers(worker_id),
    shift_date DATE NOT NULL,

    plant_zone VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    start_time TIMESTAMP NOT NULL,
    end_time TIMESTAMP
);

-- Wristband Scan Exposure Log Table
CREATE TABLE exposure_logs (
    log_id VARCHAR(64) PRIMARY KEY, -- SHA-256 Hash of Record
    wristband_serial VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    worker_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES workers(worker_id),
    shift_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES shifts(shift_id),
    scan_timestamp TIMESTAMP NOT NULL,
    raw_l_star REAL NOT NULL,
    raw_a_star REAL NOT NULL,
    raw_b_star REAL NOT NULL,
    delta_e_calibrated REAL NOT NULL,
    ambient_temp_c REAL,
    ambient_humidity_percent REAL,
    estimated_ppm_hours REAL NOT NULL,
    exposure_status VARCHAR(20) CHECK (exposure_status IN ('SAFE', 'CAUTION', 'CRITICAL_EXCEEDED')),
    expiry_patch_status VARCHAR(20) CHECK (expiry_patch_status IN ('VALID', 'EXPIRED')),
    image_file_path VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    sync_status INTEGER DEFAULT 0 -- 0: Local Only, 1: Synced to Server
);
```

### Offline & Low-Network Synchronization Flow
1. **Local Edge Storage:** During shift scans inside refinery process units, all scanned data and image crops are stored immediately in local SQLCipher-encrypted SQLite storage [INFERENCE].
2. **Conflict Handling:** Logs use deterministic SHA-256 `log_id` values generated from `worker_id + wristband_serial + scan_timestamp` [INFERENCE]. Upsert operations prevent duplicate entries during background sync retries [INFERENCE].
3. **Queue-Based Asynchronous Sync:** A background Android `WorkManager` task monitors network availability [INFERENCE]. When the phone exits the Ex Zone and establishes Wi-Fi/Cellular connectivity, the queue uploads pending logs in batch JSON payloads to the central FastAPI backend [INFERENCE].
4. **Bandwidth Optimization:** Images are compressed locally to web-optimized $800 \times 400$ JPEG format ($<80 \text{ KB}$ per scan) before cloud upload [INFERENCE].
