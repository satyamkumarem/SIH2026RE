## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Executive Summary
Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in India's agricultural and food processing sectors face severe barriers when exporting perishable produce to global markets due to strict compliance requirements for international traceability standards, high cold-chain infrastructure costs, and frequent cellular connectivity loss in transit [PS, RESEARCHED]. Existing enterprise logging solutions are prohibitively expensive for SMEs, while cheap standard loggers lack tamper resistance and fail in remote blackouts [PS, INFERENCE]. The proposed **AgriNode Trust** system addresses this by delivering an ultra-low-cost (under ₹2,500 / $30 BOM), rugged, IP67-rated IoT sensor node equipped with an onboard hardware cryptographic secure element (Microchip ATECC608A) [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION], multi-source energy harvesting (solar photovoltaic and thermoelectric Peltier harvesting) [PS, RESEARCHED], and an encrypted offline store-and-forward engine [PS]. When transit vehicles pass through zero-connectivity rural zones, sensor readings (temperature, relative humidity, and ethylene gas concentration) are recorded, hashed, and signed at the edge [PS, INFERENCE]. Upon regaining cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity, the node automatically syncs lightweight Merkle-tree rooted sensor batches directly to a decentralized ledger via MQTT/TLS [PS], creating an immutable, tamper-proof digital paper trail from farm gate to retail fork [PS, KNOWN].

The explicit problem statement provided by MoFPI is broken down into atomic, traceable requirements below:

*   **REQ-A (Low-Cost Accessibility):** The solution must be affordable for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) who are currently priced out of expensive enterprise cold-chain monitoring systems [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Hardware Bill of Materials (BOM) must remain under ₹2,500 ($30 USD), and operational overhead (gas fees/cloud servers) must be near zero [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Utilize commodity microcontrollers (ESP32-S3 or STM32L4), low-cost MEMS/electrochemical sensors, and L2/permissioned blockchain networks with minimal transaction overhead [INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag:* [PS]

*   **REQ-B (Harsh Environment Survivability):** The hardware device must survive harsh agricultural and industrial environments, including extreme moisture, dust, and continuous mechanical vibrations during transit [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Enclosure must meet minimum IP67 dustproof/waterproof standards and incorporate shock-absorbing mounting [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Use polycarbonate injection-molded IP67 casing, silicone gasket sealing, potted electronics or conformal coating, and M12 industrial waterproof cable glands or wireless charging [INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag:* [PS]

*   **REQ-C (Multi-Metric Continuous Logging):** The node must continuously track and log ambient environmental metrics including temperature, relative humidity, and ethylene gas during transit [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Sensing must be continuous with configurable sampling intervals (e.g., every 1 to 5 minutes) specifically tuned to detect spoilage and premature ripening triggered by ethylene gas spikes [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Integrate calibrated digital temperature/humidity sensor (SHT31 / DHT22) and an electrochemical or MEMS ethylene sensor (Winsen ME3-C2H4 or GM-502B) with onboard ADC sampling [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag:* [PS]

*   **REQ-D (Local Cryptographic Offline Storage):** The device must possess local cryptographic storage to guarantee data integrity and prevent post-hoc tampering during cellular connectivity dropouts in remote agricultural regions [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Sensor payloads recorded offline must be encrypted and cryptographically signed at the exact moment of sampling using a hardware root of trust before saving to local non-volatile Flash memory [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Pair MCU with Microchip ATECC608A Secure Element (storing elliptic curve private keys in hardware) and maintain an append-only ring buffer on NOR Flash (SPIFFS/LittleFS) encrypted with AES-256 [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag:* [PS]

*   **REQ-E (Indefinite Energy Harvesting Power):** The device must feature energy-harvesting capabilities, such as ambient solar or thermal energy, to extend battery life indefinitely [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Power management subsystem must dynamically harvest energy from light or thermal gradients (e.g., temperature differential between refrigerated interior and ambient exterior) to charge an onboard lithium/supercapacitor storage unit [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Integrate TI BQ25570 Nano-Power Energy Harvester IC with Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT), connected to a 5V mini solar panel and/or a Peltier Thermoelectric Generator (TEG) module, backing up a LiFePO4 battery [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag:* [PS]

*   **REQ-F (Automated Lightweight Decentralized Sync):** Upon reconnecting to a cellular/Wi-Fi network, the node must automatically sync its encrypted and signed data directly to a decentralized ledger via lightweight protocols such as MQTT without human intervention [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Sync engine must detect network connectivity, flush stored offline logs using MQTT over TLS (MQTTS), and anchor batch hashes to a blockchain smart contract to verify zero unauthorized manipulation of cold-chain logs [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Deploy lightweight MQTT client with auto-reconnect logic; flush batched Merkle root cryptographic signatures to a smart contract on Hyperledger Fabric, Polygon L2, or IOTA Tangle [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag:* [PS]

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Geography & Terrain:** Transit routes across rural India (e.g., Nashik to JNPT port, or Himachal to Delhi) pass through Western Ghats, hilly terrains, and rural stretches with zero cellular coverage for 4 to 12 consecutive hours [KNOWN, INFERENCE].
*   **Connectivity Realities:** Cellular connectivity is non-contiguous. 2G/4G networks drop frequently in transit. The node cannot rely on continuous cloud streaming and MUST operate in complete isolation for days [PS, INFERENCE].
*   **Thermal Extremes & Harsh Field Conditions:** Ambient temperatures inside non-refrigerated farm loading zones reach +45°C, while cold rooms drop to -20°C [KNOWN]. High humidity (>90% RH) in reefer units causes heavy condensation [INFERENCE]. Diesel exhaust, dust, and continuous road vibration require mechanical isolation [PS, INFERENCE].
*   **Power Availability:** Reefer trucks may shut off engines during prolonged stops, cutting off auxiliary vehicle power. Battery replacement in rural packhouses is unfeasible [INFERENCE]. Energy harvesting must keep the node powered perpetually [PS].
*   **Digital Literacy & User Interface:** Truck drivers and rural packhouse operators cannot be expected to configure complex wireless settings or execute manual commands [INFERENCE]. The node MUST be 100% plug-and-play with Zero-Touch Provisioning (ZTP) [PS, INFERENCE].

## 6. Research Questions

1.  *Ethylene Sensing Thresholds:* What is the exact concentration range of ethylene ($C_2H_4$) that triggers rapid ripening in climacteric fruits (e.g., bananas, mangoes, grapes), and can low-cost sensors resolve sub-ppm levels?
    *   *Finding:* Climacteric fruits respond to ethylene levels as low as 0.1 to 1.0 ppm [4.3]. Low-cost electrochemical sensors like Winsen ME3-C2H4 offer 0.5 ppm resolution (range 0-100 ppm), which is sufficient for detecting spoilage spikes during transport [4.1, 4.4].
2.  *Thermal Energy Harvesting Feasibility:* Can a Peltier Thermoelectric Generator (TEG) generate sufficient microwatts/milliwatts inside a reefer truck?
    *   *Finding:* A Peltier module placed on the boundary wall between a cold storage interior (4°C) and external ambient air (30°C) provides a $\Delta T \approx 26^\circ\text{C}$, generating ~1.5 mW to 5 mW of power [INFERENCE]. Paired with TI BQ25570 (which cold-starts at 330 mV and operates down to 100 mV input), this is sufficient to maintain deep-sleep MCU cycles [1.2, 1.4].
3.  *Blockchain Gas Costs vs. Throughput:* How can low-cost nodes submit hundreds of periodic telemetry points to a public or permissioned blockchain without exorbitant transaction fees?
    *   *Finding:* Directly submitting every sensor reading to a smart contract is economically non-viable [INFERENCE]. The optimal pattern is to store raw payloads locally, construct an offline Merkle Tree of batched readings every hour, and submit only the 32-byte Merkle Root signed by the hardware secure element to the blockchain [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Simple Cellular IoT Logger with Centralized Cloud Sync
*   **Mechanism:** ESP32 node continuously reads sensors and streams raw HTTP/MQTT JSON packets to a AWS/Firebase database over 4G SIM.
*   **Pros:** Easy to build during a hackathon; cheap hardware; straightforward architecture.
*   **Cons:** Fails completely during cellular blackouts; central database is vulnerable to SQL injection/admin tampering; no cryptographic hardware security; high cellular data/cloud costs.
*   **Evaluation:** Fails explicit core requirements REQ-D (cryptographic offline storage) and REQ-F (decentralized ledger sync) [PS].

### Approach 2: Direct-to-Chain On-Demand Smart Contract Pushing Node
*   **Mechanism:** Microcontroller runs a lightweight Web3 library (e.g., `web3.js` port) and signs individual transactions using a software-stored private key, sending every reading directly to an Ethereum L2 or Solana RPC endpoint.
*   **Pros:** High decentralization; instant on-chain visibility when online.
*   **Cons:** Private key stored in vulnerable Flash memory (easily extracted via JTAG/power analysis); network drops crash the device; exorbitant gas fees for thousands of individual sensor readings; heavy processing overhead on MCU.
*   **Evaluation:** Architecturally flawed for low-power edge IoT and fails security standards [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3 (RECOMMENDED): Hybrid Store-and-Forward Edge-Signed Node with Hardware Secure Element & Merkle Batch Blockchain Sync
*   **Mechanism:** MCU interfaces with Microchip ATECC608A Secure Element. Sensor readings are sampled locally, hashed into a local Merkle Tree, and stored in an AES-encrypted Flash ring buffer. During network dropouts, logging continues uninterrupted. Upon network reconnection, the node connects to a lightweight MQTT broker via MQTTS, flushes stored logs, and submits the hardware-signed Merkle Root to a Smart Contract on Hyperledger Fabric / Polygon L2. Energy is managed by TI BQ25570 MPPT harvesting solar and thermal power.
*   **Pros:** 100% compliant with REQ-A through REQ-F [PS]; extremely low gas cost (1 transaction per batch/trip); complete offline resilience; hardware-enforced private key security; perpetual operation via dual harvesting.
*   **Cons:** Slightly higher firmware complexity (Merkle tree & ATECC608A driver implementation).
*   **Evaluation:** Superior approach across all technical, economic, and hackathon constraints [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
The recommended architecture is **Approach 3: Hybrid Store-and-Forward Edge-Signed Node with Hardware Secure Element & Merkle Batch Blockchain Sync**.

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
1.  **Approach 1 (Centralized Cloud Sync)** was rejected because it violates explicit MoFPI requirements: it lacks cryptographic local storage [PS], cannot guarantee data integrity during extended rural blackout zones [PS], and relies on a centralized cloud database that can be modified by system administrators, failing export tamper-proof audits [INFERENCE].
2.  **Approach 2 (Direct On-Chain Pushing)** was rejected due to extreme resource inefficiency: storing crypto private keys in software flash is vulnerable to physical tampering [3.9, 3.11], and attempting to execute individual Web3 transactions over shaky 2G/4G connections consumes excessive power and causes astronomical blockchain gas fees [3.7, 3.8].

### Why Approach 3 Wins
Approach 3 achieves the perfect balance for an SME-targeted hackathon project [PS]:
*   *Hardware Security:* Private keys never leave the ATECC608A secure element, preventing key theft even if the physical device is stolen or dismantled [3.1, 3.4].
*   *Offline Integrity:* Data collected during multi-hour cellular dropouts is cryptographically signed at the edge and stored in local encrypted NOR Flash [PS, INFERENCE].
*   *Data Efficiency:* Merkle tree batching reduces 1,000 sensor readings into a single 32-byte root hash signature, reducing cellular bandwidth by 98% and gas costs by 99.9% [INFERENCE].
*   *Energy Autonomy:* Dual solar + Peltier TEG harvesting powered by BQ25570 ensures indefinite battery life without manual maintenance [1.2, 1.4].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative
1.  **Sampling Phase:** Every 5 minutes (configurable), the ESP32-S3 wakes from light sleep. It triggers the I2C bus to read temperature and relative humidity from the SHT31 sensor, and samples the analog voltage from the Winsen ME3-C2H4 ethylene gas sensor circuit via a 16-bit ADC [RESEARCHED].
2.  **Cryptographic Signing Phase:** The ESP32 constructs a structured binary/JSON payload: `{ timestamp, temp, humidity, ethylene, location_hash, batch_id }`. It hashes this payload (SHA-256) and sends the digest over I2C to the Microchip ATECC608A hardware secure element [2.1]. The ATECC608A signs the digest using its internal private key (stored in isolated hardware slot 0) via ECDSA P-256 [3.1, 3.4]. The signature is returned to the ESP32.
3.  **Local Encrypted Storage Phase:** The ESP32 appends the payload, hash, and signature into an AES-256 encrypted ring buffer in SPIFFS Flash memory [INFERENCE].
4.  **Network Reconnection & Merkle Batching:** When the SIM7080G modem detects a cellular signal (or local Wi-Fi at a distribution center), the ESP32 initiates an MQTTS connection (TLS 1.2/1.3) to the gateway EMQX MQTT broker [2.4, 2.9]. It aggregates un-synced offline records into a Merkle Tree structure, generates a master Merkle Root Hash, and requests the ATECC608A to sign the master Merkle Root [INFERENCE].
5.  **Ledger Anchoring & Verification:** The backend service receives the MQTT payload, validates the device signature against the registered device public key on-chain, and executes a smart contract transaction anchoring the Merkle Root, timestamp, and consignment ID to Polygon L2 or Hyperledger Fabric [1.5, 3.1]. End consumers scanning a QR code on produce packages can instantly verify that the full trip log matches the immutable on-chain Merkle Root [1.5, 2.1].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### AI Component 1: Predictive Spoilage & Remaining Shelf Life (RSL) Estimation
1.  **Purpose:** Estimate the remaining shelf life (in days/hours) of fresh produce (e.g., Alphonso mangoes, table grapes) based on cumulative thermal exposure and ethylene gas concentration spikes during transit [RESEARCHED].
2.  **Model Type:** Lightweight Random Forest Regressor / Accelerated Kinetic Decay Model (converted to ONNX/TensorFlow Lite Micro) [INFERENCE].
3.  **Input:** Time-series array of Temperature ($T$), Relative Humidity ($RH$), Ethylene ($C_2H_4$ ppm), and Produce Type ID [4.3, 4.10].
4.  **Output:** Estimated Remaining Shelf Life (RSL in hours) and Spoilage Risk Score (0-100%) [INFERENCE].
5.  **Data Required:** Post-harvest respiration datasets correlating $C_2H_4$ ppm and degree-days with fruit firmness/brix loss [4.6, 4.10].
6.  **Why AI is Preferable:** Respiration kinetics in climacteric fruits are non-linear; simple static temperature thresholds fail when ethylene gas triggers exponential ripening even under cold conditions [4.3, 4.10].
7.  **Evaluation:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in hours compared to laboratory penetrometer / fruit firmness testing [INFERENCE].
8.  **Failure Mode:** Model overestimates shelf life, causing spoiled produce to reach retail shelves.
9.  **Human Oversight:** If Spoilage Risk Score exceeds 75%, an automated alert flag is submitted to the buyer/importer QC team to trigger manual physical inspection before accepting delivery [INFERENCE].

### Local Storage & Ring Buffer Management
*   **Storage Medium:** 16 MB onboard SPI NOR Flash partitioned with LittleFS [INFERENCE].
*   **Capacity Calculation:** Each signed telemetry sample occupies ~128 bytes. At a 5-minute sampling interval (12 samples/hour = 288 samples/day), one day consumes ~36.8 KB. A 16 MB Flash memory can store over **400 days** of continuous offline logs [INFERENCE].
*   **Conflict Resolution:** Append-only sequence numbers prevent duplicate submissions. When network drops occur, the node marks records as `UNSYNCED`. Upon connection, it flushes records in contiguous chronological blocks using MQTT Quality of Service level 1 (QoS 1) [INFERENCE].
*   **Bandwidth Reduction:** Instead of streaming individual readings, the node sends 1-hour compressed binary blocks and submits a single Merkle Root signature to the blockchain, cutting cellular data overhead by >95% [INFERENCE].
