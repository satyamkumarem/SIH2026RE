## 1. Executive Snapshot

Thunderstorms and lightning represent severe atmospheric hazards in India, causing over 2,000 casualties annually and causing extensive destruction to livestock, aviation, power grids, and agricultural infrastructure `[KNOWN]`. Traditional Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models require multi-hour computational cycles and fail to resolve the micro-scale convective initiation and rapid decay of localized thunderstorm cells within 0–3 hour lead times `[RESEARCHED]`. This dossier presents a production-grade, AI/ML-driven multimodal nowcasting architecture engineered specifically for the **Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) - India Meteorological Department (IMD)** under Problem Statement `SIH26072` `[PS]`. The proposed solution fuses high-frequency Doppler Weather Radar (DWR) reflectivity grids, INSAT-3D/3DR/3DS geostationary satellite multispectral imagery, Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) Lightning Location Network (LLN) stroke events, and WRF atmospheric instability fields into a hybrid Deep Spatiotemporal Vision Transformer with Physics-Guided Advection `[RESEARCHED]`. The resulting platform delivers 1 km × 1 km hyper-local thunderstorm reflectivity (dBZ) and lightning stroke probability forecasts updated every 15 minutes, with an automated Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) alerting engine `[INFERENCE]`.

## 6. Research Questions

To ensure technical rigor, the following critical research questions must be addressed during development:

1. **How to align multi-resolution spatial grids (1 km radar, 4 km satellite, 9 km WRF, continuous lightning points) without losing localized convective features?**
   * *Investigation Path:* Evaluate bilinear, nearest-neighbor, and bicubic feature map interpolation versus learned spatial downsampling via strided 2D convolutional layers `[RESEARCHED]`.
2. **What neural network loss function prevents temporal over-smoothing in multi-step reflectivity and lightning predictions?**
   * *Investigation Path:* Compare MSE/MAE loss (which causes blurry, washed-out forecasts at $T+2\text{ hours}$) against Focal Loss combined with Structural Similarity (SSIM) and Perceptual Loss `[RESEARCHED]`.
3. **How can the model effectively predict convective initiation (cell growth) in regions with zero initial radar reflectivity?**
   * *Investigation Path:* Analyze the role of satellite Cloud Top Temperature (CTT) drop rates ($\Delta \text{CTT}/\Delta t$) and WRF Convective Available Potential Energy (CAPE) gradients as early indicators before precipitation particles grow large enough for radar detection `[RESEARCHED]`.
4. **What is the operational availability and access protocol for real-time IMD DWR and IITM Damini data feeds for research/hackathon prototypes?**
   * *Investigation Path:* Investigate MOSDAC FTP/HTTP endpoints, IMD Open Data Portal, and public AWS/NOAA radar datasets as realistic hackathon surrogates `[RESEARCHED]`.

### 1. Optical Flow & TITAN (Thunderstorm Identification, Tracking, Analysis, and Nowcasting)
* **Description:** Classical radar extrapolation algorithms calculating optical flow velocity vectors from past reflectivity frames to advect storm cells forward in time `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Strengths:** Computationally lightweight, low latency ($< 5\text{ seconds}$ execution), high accuracy for short lead times ($0-30\text{ minutes}$) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Weaknesses:** Assumes constant velocity and intensity; fails completely to model convective initiation (new storm formation), cell growth, or decay `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Relevance & Gap:** Fails during rapid severe convective developments; cannot predict lightning initiation prior to radar echo formation `[INFERENCE]`.

2. High-Resolution WRF Numerical Weather Prediction (MoES/IMD)
* **Description:** Physics-based hydrodynamical modeling simulating atmospheric dynamics at $3\text{ km} - 9\text{ km}$ resolution on supercomputing clusters (ARCA / ARUNICA) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Strengths:** Excellent multi-day atmospheric representation grounded in thermodynamic equations `[KNOWN]`.
* **Weaknesses:** High computational latency (2–4 hours per run cycle); spin-up errors impair short-term ($0-2\text{ hour}$) nowcasting accuracy `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Relevance & Gap:** Too slow for real-time localized lightning early warnings; cannot process incoming 15-minute radar/satellite observations dynamically `[INFERENCE]`.

3. IITM Damini Mobile Application Architecture
* **Description:** Mobile alerting system driven by IITM Pune's 83-sensor Lightning Location Network, issuing GPS radius-based warnings (20 km / 40 km) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Strengths:** Accurate real-time detection ($< 500\text{ m}$) of actual occurred lightning strikes across India `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Weaknesses:** Primarily reactive (alerts after lightning strikes have begun occurring); limited predictive lead time ($< 15-20\text{ minutes}$ extrapolation); lacks radar/NWP multi-modal predictive fusion `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Relevance & Gap:** Existing system alerts on *current* lightning; the proposed system builds a *predictive* 0–3h nowcasting layer ahead of cell electrification `[INFERENCE]`.

4. Google MetNet / MetNet-2 / Global MetNet
* **Description:** Deep learning nowcasting neural networks developed by Google Research predicting precipitation up to 12 hours ahead using satellite and radar data `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Strengths:** High skill score (CSI) across multi-hour horizons; uses axial self-attention to process large spatial contexts without over-smoothing `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Weaknesses:** Designed for precipitation rate nowcasting; not fine-tuned for tropical extreme lightning electrification, regional Indian monsoon dynamics, or IITM ground sensor fusion `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Relevance & Gap:** Serves as architectural inspiration, but must be adapted for multi-modal Indian observations (INSAT-3D + DWR + IITM LLN) `[INFERENCE]`.

### Approach 1: Classical Feature Engineering + XGBoost/LightGBM Grid Classifier
* **Mechanism:** Discretize the geographic domain into a $0.01^\circ \times 0.01^\circ$ grid. For each grid cell, extract domain-engineered features from historical radar ($Z$, VIL), INSAT (CTT, $\Delta \text{CTT}/\Delta t$), LLN (flash counts in 10 km radius), and WRF (CAPE, CIN, Wind Shear). Train an XGBoost gradient boosted tree to classify whether $Z \ge 40\text{ dBZ}$ or Lightning $\ge 1\text{ stroke}$ occurs in the next 30/60/120 minutes `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Problem Fit:** Low-to-moderate; handles tabular non-linear interactions well but completely ignores multi-scale spatial cell shapes, advection velocity vectors, and neighborhood storm propagation `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Complexity & Cost:** Very low complexity; fast CPU training and sub-second inference `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Hackathon vs Production Feasibility:** High hackathon feasibility; moderate production skill score ceiling `[INFERENCE]`.

### Approach 2: End-to-End Spatiotemporal Deep Learning (ConvLSTM / 3D U-Net)
* **Mechanism:** Stack continuous multi-channel observations (Radar Reflectivity, INSAT TIR1, INSAT WV, gridded LLN flash density) into a 5D spatio-temporal tensor $[B, T_\text{past}, C, H, W]$. Pass tensor through an encoder-decoder ConvLSTM or 3D U-Net architecture to generate future sequence frames $[B, T_\text{future}, C_\text{out}, H, W]$ up to $T+12$ steps (3 hours) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Problem Fit:** High; directly learns atmospheric cell motion, growth, and decay patterns directly from observed image sequences `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Complexity & Cost:** High GPU computational requirements (NVIDIA RTX 4090 / A100 required for training); prone to spatial over-smoothing (loss of peak radar reflectivity and localized lightning spikes) at lead times $> 60\text{ minutes}$ `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Hackathon vs Production Feasibility:** Feasible for hackathon with cropped regional grids ($256 \times 256$ pixels); production deployment requires multi-GPU inference clusters `[INFERENCE]`.

### Approach 3: Hybrid Physics-Aligned Vision Transformer + Optical Flow Advection (RECOMMENDED)
* **Mechanism:** A dual-branch hybrid deep learning model:
  1. **Advection Branch:** Computes neural optical flow motion fields ($u, v$) from past radar and INSAT frames, explicitly advecting storm cell structures into future time steps to preserve sharp spatial gradients and prevent blurring `[RESEARCHED]`.
  2. **Physics & Electrification Branch:** A Cross-Attention Vision Transformer (Swin-UNet) that ingests 3D atmospheric parameters (WRF CAPE, CIN, Wind Shear) and satellite sounder vertical profiles, learning a residual intensity modulation field ($\Delta Z, P_\text{lightning}$) `[RESEARCHED]`.
  3. **Fusion:** The advected feature map is modulated by the residual intensity field to yield sharp, physically grounded reflectivity and lightning probability grids up to $T+3\text{ hours}$ `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Problem Fit:** Exceptional; combines the physical mass-conservation strength of optical flow (sharp cell boundaries) with deep learning's ability to model non-linear storm growth, decay, and electrification `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Complexity & Cost:** High algorithmic design effort; moderate GPU training cost; fast inference ($< 1.5\text{ seconds}$ per frame cycle) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Hackathon vs Production Feasibility:** Optimal for hackathon scoring due to state-of-the-art methodology, high visual accuracy, and strong physical interpretability `[INFERENCE]`.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection Statement
The **Hybrid Physics-Aligned Vision Transformer + Optical Flow Advection (Approach 3)** is selected as the winning architecture `[INFERENCE]`.

### Justification & Rejection of Alternatives
* **Why Approach 1 (XGBoost) was Rejected:** XGBoost evaluates each spatial cell independently or with coarse spatial buffers. It fails to represent advective storm motion, spatial shear, and contiguous convective cell shapes, resulting in noisy, fragmented hazard maps `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Why Pure Approach 2 (ConvLSTM) was Rejected:** Standard ConvLSTM models minimize Mean Squared Error (MSE), which causes progressive loss of high-frequency spatial details. By $T+90\text{ minutes}$, predicted storm cells blur into diffuse, low-reflectivity blobs, severely underestimating localized severe lightning events `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Why Approach 3 Wins:** Approach 3 explicitly decouples **motion** (where the storm is moving) from **thermodynamic development** (whether the storm is intensifying or dying). Optical flow handles motion without spatial blurring, while the Vision Transformer cross-attention module uses INSAT cloud-top cooling rates and WRF CAPE to predict lightning initiation and cell decay `[RESEARCHED]`. This approach achieves high Critical Success Index (CSI) scores while maintaining sharp operational warning boundaries `[INFERENCE]`.

====================================================================================================
3. HYBRID AI/ML NOWCASTING INFERENCE ENGINE
   ├─ Branch A: Neural Optical Flow Advection Network (Predicts Motion Vectors u, v)
   ├─ Branch B: Cross-Attention Vision Transformer (Ingests WRF CAPE/Shear + INSAT CTT)
   ├─ Feature Fusion & Advective Warping Layer
   └─ Parallel Prediction Heads:
        ├─ Head 1: Reflectivity Forecast Grid [T+15m ... T+180m] (dBZ Raster)
        └─ Head 2: Lightning Stroke Probability Map [T+15m ... T+180m] (P in [0,1])
====================================================================================================
4. GEOSPATIAL POST-PROCESSING & WARNING ENGINE
   ├─ Polygon Extraction: Marching Squares Contour Algorithm (Z >= 40 dBZ, P_light >= 0.7)
   ├─ District / Station Spatial Overlay Intersect Engine (PostGIS)
   └─ Common Alerting Protocol (CAP v1.2) XML/JSON Alert Generator
====================================================================================================
5. PRESENTATION & DELIVERY LAYER
   ├─ FastAPI REST API & WebSocket Gateway
   ├─ Interactive WebGIS Dashboard (Mapbox GL JS / React / Deck.gl)
   ├─ Mobile FCM / WhatsApp / SMS Alert Notification Service
   └─ Low-Bandwidth GeoJSON Vector Tile Server
====================================================================================================
```

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Ingestion & Parsing:** Async Python background workers continuously poll incoming FTP/S3/WebSocket feeds every 5 minutes `[INFERENCE]`. DWR reflectivity volumes are parsed via `PyART`, INSAT-3D HDF5 multispectral bands via `SatPy`, IITM LLN point stroke logs via `Pandas/Geopandas`, and WRF GRIB2 files via `xarray` `[RESEARCHED]`.
2. **Resampling & Tensor Stacking:** Heterogeneous observation grids are resampled to a uniform $1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km}$ regional grid over the geographic target domain using bilinear interpolation for continuous fields and spatial point-density accumulation for lightning strikes `[RESEARCHED]`. The aligned arrays are normalized and stacked into a 5D PyTorch tensor `[INFERENCE]`.
3. **AI Inference Pipeline:** The tensor is passed into the PyTorch Triton-optimized model container. The Advection branch calculates motion vectors and warps current reflectivity forward in time `[RESEARCHED]`. Simultaneously, the Cross-Attention ViT calculates growth/decay factors based on satellite cloud-top temperature drops and WRF atmospheric instability `[RESEARCHED]`.
4. **Post-Processing & Polygon Generation:** Predicted reflectivity and lightning probability grids for $T+15, T+30, \dots, T+180\text{ minutes}$ undergo vector contouring via Marching Squares `[RESEARCHED]`. Hazard polygons ($> 40\text{ dBZ}$ / $> 70\%$ lightning risk) are intersected with administrative boundaries (districts, sub-districts, airport buffers) in PostGIS `[INFERENCE]`.
5. **Alert Dissemination:** If new hazard polygons intersect populated zones, the alert gateway formats a CAP-compliant XML payload and pushes real-time WebSocket notifications to the WebGIS dashboard and mobile notification services within $< 15\text{ seconds}$ of inference completion `[INFERENCE]`.

```

│  • Performs backward advective warping    │   │  • Predicts cell growth/decay residual ΔZ │

└────────────────────────────┬──────────────┘   └────────────────────────────┬──────────────┘

                             │    DUAL PREDICTION HEADS        │

### Component 1: Neural Optical Flow Advection Branch
* **Purpose:** Track storm cell velocity vectors and extrapolate physical motion up to 3 hours ahead without spatial blurring `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Model Type:** Deep Optical Flow Network (RAFT / PIV-Net based) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Input:** Past 4 consecutive frames ($T-60, T-45, T-30, T-0$) of Radar Reflectivity and INSAT TIR1 imagery `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Output:** Dense 2D motion vector field maps $[u(x,y), v(x,y)]$ representing pixel displacement per 15-minute interval `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Why AI over Rule/Classical?** Classical Lucas-Kanade optical flow assumes brightness constancy and uniform motion, failing when storm cells deform, rotate, or split. Deep neural optical flow learns non-linear fluid dynamics and multi-scale atmospheric motion `[RESEARCHED]`.

### Component 2: Cross-Attention Vision Transformer (Growth, Decay & Electrification Branch)
* **Purpose:** Predict where existing storm cells will intensify/decay and where *new* convective initiation and lightning strikes will emerge `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Model Type:** Swin-Transformer Encoder-Decoder with Cross-Attention Fusion `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Input:** Warped motion tensor fused with INSAT Cloud Top Temperature (CTT), Water Vapor channel, IITM Lightning stroke density, and WRF CAPE/CIN/Shear maps `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Output:** Residual reflectivity modulation matrix $\Delta Z(x,y,t)$ and Lightning Probability Matrix $P_\text{lightning}(x,y,t) \in [0,1]$ for $T+15$ to $T+180\text{ minutes}$ `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Why AI over Rule/Classical?** Lightning electrification involves complex microphysical interactions between ice crystals, graupel, and supercooled water droplets above the freezing level. Non-linear cross-attention captures complex correlations between CTT cooling rates ($<-50^\circ\text{C}$), high CAPE ($>2000\text{ J/kg}$), and electrical discharge initiation `[RESEARCHED]`.

### Model Evaluation Metrics
* **Critical Success Index (CSI):** $\text{CSI} = \frac{\text{Hits}}{\text{Hits} + \text{False Alarms} + \text{Misses}}$ evaluated at thresholds $Z \ge 35\text{ dBZ}$ and Lightning Stroke Probability $\ge 0.5$ `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Fractions Skill Score (FSS):** Evaluates spatial correctness across varying neighborhood scales ($1\text{ km}$ to $20\text{ km}$), preventing harsh spatial displacement penalties `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Probability of Detection (POD) & False Alarm Ratio (FAR):** Target operational benchmark: $\text{POD} > 0.75$, $\text{FAR} < 0.25$ for 30-minute lead time `[RESEARCHED]`.

### Model Failure Modes & Human Oversight
* **Failure Mode 1 (False Convective Initiation):** Unusually rapid satellite cloud top cooling in non-precipitating cirrus clouds could trigger false lightning warnings `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Failure Mode 2 (Oographic Radar Blocking):** In mountain valleys with beam blockage, radar inputs are missing, causing potential false negatives `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Human Oversight Mechanism:** The WebGIS dashboard includes an explicit "Forecaster Override" toggle. IMD meteorologists can review AI-generated confidence scores and manually adjust or suppress automatically generated CAP alert polygons prior to public broadcast `[INFERENCE]`.

### Explicit Non-AI Rule Enforcement
* **Rule:** Simple distance buffering ($< 10\text{ km}$ from active strike) and linear thresholding ($Z \ge 45\text{ dBZ}$) are used for immediate $0-15\text{ minute}$ tactical alerts `[INFERENCE]`. AI is **NOT** used for immediate strike echoing, preserving sub-second alert response `[INFERENCE]`.

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Spatial Database Schema (PostgreSQL / PostGIS)

```sql
-- Active Weather Radar Observations Grid Table
CREATE TABLE radar_observations (
    obs_id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    station_id VARCHAR(10) NOT NULL,
    observation_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    max_reflectivity_raster RASTER NOT NULL,
    created_at TIMESTAMPTZ DEFAULT NOW()
);
CREATE INDEX idx_radar_obs_time ON radar_observations(observation_time);

-- Lightning Stroke Point Logs (IITM Network Ingestion)
CREATE TABLE lightning_strokes (
    stroke_id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    stroke_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    location GEOMETRY(Point, 4326) NOT NULL,
    peak_current_ka REAL,
    stroke_type VARCHAR(5) CHECK (stroke_type IN ('CG', 'IC'))
);
CREATE INDEX idx_lightning_geom ON lightning_strokes USING GIST(location);
CREATE INDEX idx_lightning_time ON lightning_strokes(stroke_time);

-- AI Generated Forecast Polygons
CREATE TABLE forecast_hazard_polygons (
    polygon_id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    issue_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    valid_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    lead_time_minutes INT NOT NULL,
    hazard_type VARCHAR(20) CHECK (hazard_type IN ('THUNDERSTORM', 'LIGHTNING', 'HAIL')),
    risk_level VARCHAR(10) CHECK (risk_level IN ('YELLOW', 'ORANGE', 'RED')),
    geom GEOMETRY(Polygon, 4326) NOT NULL,
    confidence_score REAL CHECK (confidence_score BETWEEN 0 AND 1)
);
CREATE INDEX idx_hazard_poly_geom ON forecast_hazard_polygons USING GIST(geom);
```

### Low-Network & Offline Architecture
* **Vector Tile Optimization:** Instead of streaming heavy PNG raster tiles over cellular networks, the backend converts predicted hazard grids into ultra-compact Mapbox Vector Tiles (MVT / `.pbf` format) `[KNOWN]`. File size per tile is reduced by $> 85\%$ ($< 15\text{ KB}$ per tile) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Progressive Web App (PWA) Offline Caching:** The mobile/web dashboard uses Service Workers and IndexedDB to cache base geographic maps, district boundaries, and recent warning logs `[KNOWN]`. When connectivity drops, users can still view the last downloaded 3-hour forecast trend and local emergency guidelines `[INFERENCE]`.
* **SMS Fallback Gateway:** In regions with severe network degradation (2G/GPRS), the platform defaults to sending concise SMS text alerts containing district name, risk level, estimated time of arrival (ETA), and safety instructions `[INFERENCE]`.
