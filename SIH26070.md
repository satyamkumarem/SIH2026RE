# SIH26070 Solution Research Dossier: AI/ML System for Tropical Cyclone Pattern Identification, Classification, and Prediction

---

## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Tropical cyclones forming over the North Indian Ocean (Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea) represent severe natural hazards that devastate coastal communities, infrastructure, and marine operations [KNOWN]. Traditional operational cyclone tracking and intensity estimation primarily depend on manual or semi-automated application of the Dvorak Technique using satellite infrared imagery, combined with numerical weather prediction (NWP) models [RESEARCHED]. While effective, the Dvorak technique suffers from subjective interpreter variability, delays during rapid intensification events, and limited capacity to integrate multi-sensor satellite observations (e.g., thermal infrared, water vapor, microwave, and scatterometer wind vectors) in real time [RESEARCHED]. This dossier details the end-to-end strategy for a student hackathon team to architect, develop, and demonstrate an automated, operational-grade AI/ML framework. The proposed solution ingests multi-source satellite data (INSAT-3D/3DR/3DS geostationary imagery, OceanSat-3 scatterometer wind fields, and passive microwave data), detects cyclogenesis, classifies tropical cyclone intensity categories according to India Meteorological Department (IMD) standards, and predicts cyclone track trajectory and intensity evolution for lead times up to 24–72 hours [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Geographic Coverage:** The North Indian Ocean (NIO) basin, comprising the Bay of Bengal (BoB) and the Arabian Sea (AS), accounts for ~7–10% of global tropical cyclones [RESEARCHED]. The regional characteristics feature rapid coastal landfalls, shallow ocean dynamics, and strong monsoon background flows [KNOWN].
2. **Satellite Temporal & Spatial Resolution:** 
   * **INSAT-3D / 3DR / 3DS:** Geostationary imager provides TIR-1 (10.2–11.2 µm) and TIR-2 (11.5–12.5 µm) at 4 km spatial resolution every 15–30 minutes (and 4-minute rapid scan mode during active cyclones) [RESEARCHED].
   * **OceanSat-3 / SCATSAT-1:** Polar-orbiting Ku-band scatterometer providing sea surface wind vectors (12.5 km grid), but only 1–2 passes per day over a given ocean area [RESEARCHED].
3. **Data Missingness & Gaps:** Satellite downlinks frequently suffer from missing frames, corrupted scanlines, or spatial misalignment during orbital maneuvers [RESEARCHED]. Operational systems must handle irregular time intervals and missing satellite scenes without crashing [INFERENCE].
4. **Compute Constraints at Operational Weather Centers:** While MoES/IMD operates High-Performance Computing (HPC) facilities (e.g., Aditya / Pratyush / Mihir 22 PetaFLOPS systems) [RESEARCHED], local forecasting stations and emergency response units operate under standard desktop hardware with intermittent internet connections [INFERENCE].
5. **Connectivity & Bandwidth in Field Offices:** District emergency offices during severe cyclone landfalls frequently experience network throttling or power grid failures [KNOWN]. Offline caching of lightweight forecast vectors (JSON/GeoJSON) is required [INFERENCE].

## 8. Existing Solutions & Gap Analysis

### Existing Solutions Comparison

1. **Operational Dvorak Technique (Subjective / ADT)**
   * **Description:** Dvorak Technique uses visual pattern matching on geostationary IR imagery (Curved band, Shear, Eye pattern, CDO) to assign T-numbers (T1.0–T8.0) [RESEARCHED]. Advanced Dvorak Technique (ADT v9.0 at CIMSS) automates eye/cloud temperature routines [RESEARCHED].
   * **Strengths:** Globally accepted standard for 50+ years; well understood by forecasters [KNOWN].
   * **Weaknesses:** Subjective manual interpretation varies between forecasters; struggles with weak or asymmetric tropical depressions; dependent solely on single IR band thresholds [RESEARCHED].
   * **Relevance:** Serves as the primary operational baseline that the AI model must complement [INFERENCE].
   * **Gap:** Does not natively integrate multi-sensor scatterometer wind fields or non-linear spatial feature extractors [RESEARCHED].
   * **Confidence:** [RESEARCHED]

2. **Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) Models (e.g., IMD GFS, NCMRWF Unified Model, ECMWF IFS)**
   * **Description:** Physics-based dynamical atmospheric models solving Navier-Stokes and thermodynamic equations on supercomputers [RESEARCHED].
   * **Strengths:** Rigorous physical conservation laws; excellent medium-range track guidance (3–7 days) [KNOWN].
   * **Weaknesses:** High computational cost (requires hours on PetaFLOPS supercomputers); initial vortex positioning errors; frequently underpredicts rapid intensification (RI) rates [RESEARCHED].
   * **Relevance:** Primary guidance tool used alongside satellite observations [KNOWN].
   * **Gap:** Slow execution time (cannot update continuously every 15 minutes as new geostationary imagery arrives) [INFERENCE].
   * **Confidence:** [RESEARCHED]

3. **Pure Deep Learning Track & Intensity Baselines (e.g., DeepCyclone, ConvLSTM research models)**
   * **Description:** Academic research models (e.g., VGG16/ResNet CNNs + ConvLSTM) trained on single-satellite IR datasets over specific basins [RESEARCHED].
   * **Strengths:** High inference speed (<1 second per frame); strong pattern feature learning [RESEARCHED].
   * **Weaknesses:** Academic prototypes lack multi-source data fusion (ignore scatterometer winds and ERA5 fields); lack operational GIS forecaster interfaces; fail when satellite frames have missing timestamps [RESEARCHED].
   * **Relevance:** Provides the foundational ML architecture components [INFERENCE].
   * **Gap:** Lack end-to-end operational readiness, missing frame handling, multi-sensor alignment, and explainable meteorological diagnostic tools [INFERENCE].
   * **Confidence:** [RESEARCHED]

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 3: Multi-Modal Spatiotemporal Deep Learning Framework (YOLOv8-Geo + ConvLSTM-ViT Hybrid Engine)** [INFERENCE].

### Justification
1. **Multi-Source Mandate:** Explicitly satisfies REQ-01 [PS] by fusing geostationary multi-spectral imagery (INSAT-3D TIR1/TIR2/WV) with polar-orbiting surface wind observations (OceanSat-3 / SCATSAT-1) and atmospheric reanalysis fields (ERA5) [RESEARCHED].
2. **Spatiotemporal Continuity:** Cyclones are dynamic thermodynamic systems. Single-frame 2D CNNs (Approach 2) miss temporal intensification rates ($\frac{dP}{dt}$) and trajectory momentum [RESEARCHED]. ConvLSTM sequence layers effectively learn temporal cloud-pattern evolution and eyewall organization [RESEARCHED].
3. **Robustness to Missing Satellite Scenes:** Incorporates a generative temporal interpolation layer (ConvLSTM-U-Net) to fill gaps in historical INSAT-3D archives (gap sizes of 1–3 missing frames) [RESEARCHED].

### Reasons for Rejecting Alternatives
* **Approach 1 (XGBoost/RF):** Rejected because hand-engineered scalar features collapse complex spatial cloud top morphology (curved bands, central dense overcast, concentric eyewalls), losing critical spatial signals required for accurate classification [INFERENCE].
* **Approach 2 (Pure 2D Single-Frame CNN):** Rejected because it cannot forecast future track trajectory or predict rapid intensification, as trajectory and temporal pressure changes inherently require sequence history ($t-3h, t-2h, t-1h, t_0$) [RESEARCHED].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Ingestion:** The ingestion controller polls the MOSDAC data repository and NOAA IBTrACS feed every 15 minutes [RESEARCHED]. Newly arrived INSAT-3D/3DR TIR1, TIR2, and WV NetCDF files are downloaded and placed into MinIO Object Storage [INFERENCE].
2. **Preprocessing & Fusion:** The `xarray` and `rasterio` engine reads raw brightness temperatures, clips the North Indian Ocean bounding box (5°N–30°N, 50°E–100°E), normalizes temperature values ($180\text{ K} - 310\text{ K} \rightarrow [0, 1]$ grayscales), and co-locates available OceanSat-3 scatterometer wind vector grids and ERA5 environmental fields onto a unified spatial raster stack [RESEARCHED].
3. **Missing Scene Reconstruction:** If timestamps contain missing satellite observations (common in historical archives), the ConvLSTM frame-inpainting model generates synthetic intermediate IR scenes to maintain sequence continuity [RESEARCHED].
4. **Identification:** The preprocessed raster sequence is passed to `YOLOv8-Geo`. The model detects cyclonic cloud organization, outputting bounding box coordinates and the precise cyclonic Center-Fix (Latitude, Longitude) [RESEARCHED].
5. **Classification & Feature Attribution:** The cropped storm region is fed into the Vision Transformer (ViT) classifier. It outputs the estimated Current Intensity (CI) number, Maximum Sustained Surface Wind (MSW in knots), Estimated Central Pressure (ECP in hPa), and assigned IMD storm category [RESEARCHED]. Simultaneously, Grad-CAM generates spatial attention maps highlighting active eyewall convection [RESEARCHED].
6. **Spatiotemporal Forecast Generation:** A sequence of the past 6 hours of storm crops, along with motion vector history, is processed by the `ConvLSTM` trajectory and intensity predictor. It outputs forecasted Lat/Lon coordinates and MSW/ECP values for +6h, +12h, +24h, and +48h lead times [RESEARCHED].
7. **Dissemination:** Outputs are serialized to PostGIS and Redis, and pushed via WebSocket to the MapLibre GL frontend, rendering interactive track cones, layer toggles, and downloadable IMD SOP bulletins [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: YOLOv8-Geo (Cyclone Detection & Center Localization)
1. **Exact Problem AI Solves:** Identifies whether a tropical disturbance/cyclone exists in full-disk satellite scenes and pinpoints its exact center coordinates (Lat/Lon) [RESEARCHED].
2. **Input:** Full-disk or regional INSAT-3D TIR-1 raster image ($3207 \times 3062$ or downsampled $1024 \times 1024 \times 1$) [RESEARCHED].
3. **Output:** Bounding box $[x_{\min}, y_{\min}, x_{\max}, y_{\max}]$, confidence score $[0, 1]$, and extracted Center-Fix $[Lat, Lon]$ [RESEARCHED].
4. **Why Simpler Rule Fails:** Simple thresholding on minimum brightness temperature fails because non-cyclonic convective thunderstorms or Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) cloud clusters display similar cold cloud tops without cyclonic vortex structure [RESEARCHED].
5. **Data Required:** ~5,000 annotated INSAT-3D/3DR scenes with bounding boxes and IMD best-track center fixes [RESEARCHED].
6. **Evaluation:** Mean Average Precision ($mAP@0.5$), Center Distance Error in kilometers ($\text{Distance Error} < 35\text{ km}$) [RESEARCHED].
7. **Failure Mode:** False positive on heavy monsoon convective clusters. **Mitigation:** Threshold confidence at $>0.75$ and require persistence across 2 consecutive 30-min frames [INFERENCE].
8. **Requires Training:** Yes, trained from scratch / fine-tuned from YOLOv8 weights [RESEARCHED].
9. **Fine-Tuning:** Fine-tuned on NIO satellite dataset [RESEARCHED].
10. **RAG:** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision:** Yes, 2D Convolutional Object Detection [RESEARCHED].
12. **Speech Processing:** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting:** No (Detection only) [INFERENCE].
14. **Human Oversight:** Required. Forecaster can manually adjust center-fix if satellite eye is obscured [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Dual-Branch Vision Transformer Classifier (Intensity & T-Number Classification)
1. **Exact Problem AI Solves:** Maps storm cloud morphology to IMD intensity categories (D to SuCS), Maximum Sustained Winds (MSW in knots), and Estimated Central Pressure (ECP in hPa) [RESEARCHED].
2. **Input:** Cropped storm ROI ($512 \times 512 \times 3$ channels: TIR1, TIR2, WV) centered on the detected vortex [RESEARCHED].
3. **Output:** Intensity Class (7 categories), MSW numerical value (knots), ECP (hPa), Dvorak T-number [RESEARCHED].
4. **Why Simpler Rule Fails:** Manual Dvorak rules rely on rigid lookup tables and human visual matching of pattern types (curved band, CDO, eye), which introduces high inter-observer variance [RESEARCHED].
5. **Data Required:** Historical INSAT-3D storm crops paired with IMD Best Track MSW/ECP labels (2013–2024) [RESEARCHED].
6. **Evaluation:** Classification Accuracy (%), Mean Absolute Error ($\text{MAE} \le 5\text{ knots}$), Root Mean Square Error ($\text{RMSE} \le 7\text{ knots}$) [RESEARCHED].
7. **Failure Mode:** Misclassification during cloud-filled eyes or sheared systems. **Mitigation:** Output probability distribution over categories and flag low-confidence predictions ($<0.70$) for manual forecaster review [INFERENCE].
8. **Requires Training:** Yes [RESEARCHED].
9. **Fine-Tuning:** Pre-trained ViT / ResNet fine-tuned on cyclone crops [RESEARCHED].
10. **RAG:** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision:** Yes (ViT / CNN image classification) [RESEARCHED].
12. **Speech Processing:** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting:** No (Current state estimation) [INFERENCE].
14. **Human Oversight:** Required before publishing official IMD bulletins [INFERENCE].

### Component 3: ConvLSTM Spatiotemporal Predictor (Track Trajectory & Intensity Forecasting)
1. **Exact Problem AI Solves:** Predicts storm track movement (future Lat/Lon) and future intensity trends for +6h, +12h, +24h lead times [RESEARCHED].
2. **Input:** Sequence of past 4–6 storm raster crops ($t-3h$ to $t_0$) + motion vector tensor + ERA5 vertical wind shear / SST grids [RESEARCHED].
3. **Output:** Predicted future center coordinates $[Lat_{t+n}, Lon_{t+n}]$ and forecasted MSW/ECP [RESEARCHED].
4. **Why Simpler Rule Fails:** Linear extrapolation of past motion fails during sudden track recurvatures (e.g., Cyclone Biparjoy) or rapid intensification caused by ocean heat content variations [RESEARCHED].
5. **Data Required:** Time-series sequences of historical storms in the NIO basin (2013–2024) [RESEARCHED].
6. **Evaluation:** Track Position Error in kilometers ($\text{PE} \le 50\text{ km}$ at 12h, $\le 120\text{ km}$ at 24h), Intensity Forecast MAE [RESEARCHED].
7. **Failure Mode:** Underpredicting rapid intensification rates ($>30\text{ knots}$ increase in 24 hours). **Mitigation:** Highlight RI risk warning whenever thermodynamic indicators cross critical thresholds [INFERENCE].
8. **Requires Training:** Yes [RESEARCHED].
9. **Fine-Tuning:** Yes [RESEARCHED].
10. **RAG:** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision:** Yes (Spatiotemporal video/sequence prediction) [RESEARCHED].
12. **Speech Processing:** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting:** Yes (Spatiotemporal trajectory & intensity forecasting) [RESEARCHED].
14. **Human Oversight:** Mandatory. AI track guidance serves as input to forecaster ensemble decision-making [INFERENCE].

### Explicit Non-AI Decision
* **SOP Bulletin Generation & Alert Routing:** Will NOT use Generative LLMs for deterministic emergency alert generation [INFERENCE]. Standardized text bulletins (IMD SOP formats) must be generated using deterministic Template Generators (Jinja2/Python) to prevent hallucination of wind speeds, landfall locations, or evacuation deadlines [INFERENCE].

CREATE TABLE cyclone_events (

    confidence_score NUMERIC(4,3),
    raw_satellite_tile_path VARCHAR(255)
);

CREATE TABLE cyclone_forecasts (
    forecast_id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    obs_id INTEGER REFERENCES cyclone_observations(obs_id),
