## 1. Executive Snapshot

This technical dossier presents an end-to-end product strategy, hardware-software architecture, data model, and implementation roadmap for **SIH26040: Smart Water Purification and Quality Monitoring System for Rural and Mining-Affected Areas** [PS]. Designed specifically for the Department of Higher & Technical Education, Government of Jharkhand [PS], the system addresses the severe contamination of groundwater and surface water in mining belts (such as Dhanbad, West Singhbhum, Ramgarh, and Jharia) caused by suspended coal dust, acid mine drainage, dissolved total solids (TDS), iron, heavy metals, and bacterial pathogens [RESEARCHED]. The proposed solution, named **JalDrishti**, combines a low-power, 4-stage energy-efficient purification hardware unit (sediment filtration, activated carbon, heavy-metal selective ion-exchange resin, and ultrafiltration with UV-C LED sterilization) [INFERENCE] with an ESP32-based IoT sensing module (pH, turbidity, TDS, temperature, flow, differential pressure) [INFERENCE]. Data is evaluated locally using the Weighted Arithmetic Water Quality Index (WAWQI) against Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS IS 10500:2012) benchmarks [KNOWN] and synchronized over LoRaWAN/GSM to a web dashboard and mobile app with offline-first caching [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Geography & Terrain:** Mining regions in Jharkhand (e.g., East/West Singhbhum, Dhanbad, Giridih) feature rugged plateau terrain, acidic mine runoff, and elevated groundwater iron ($>3\text{ mg/L}$) and fluoride ($>1.5\text{ mg/L}$) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Connectivity Realities:** Rural habitations suffer from weak or patchy 2G/4G cellular coverage. Broadband fiber is nonexistent in remote hamlets [KNOWN]. Edge devices must buffer up to 30 days of sensor readings locally in SPIFFS/Flash memory and sync via MQTT upon reconnection [INFERENCE].
*   **Power Supply:** Rural grid power availability is intermittent (8–12 hours/day with frequent brownouts) [KNOWN]. System must be powered by a $12\text{V}$, $24\text{Ah}$ LiFePO4 battery recharged by a $50\text{W}$ solar panel [INFERENCE].
*   **Digital Literacy & Language:** Local operators (Jal Sahiyas) may have limited literacy. The physical interface must use clear color-coded status lights (Green = Safe, Yellow = Service Required, Red = Unsafe/Cut-off) and voice alerts in Hindi/Santhali via an onboard audio module [INFERENCE].
*   **Water Matrix Variability:** Feedwater turbidity fluctuates drastically during monsoon seasons (rising from $10\text{ NTU}$ to $>300\text{ NTU}$) due to mine runoff silt [RESEARCHED]. System requires a washable/reusable pre-filter screen to prevent rapid clogging of fine cartridges [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Solar-Powered High-Pressure Reverse Osmosis (RO) + Cellular IoT Transducer
*   **Mechanism:** Feedwater is pressurized to $70\text{ PSI}$ using a heavy-duty DC diaphragm pump through a 0.0001-micron RO membrane, paired with continuous sensor telemetry.
*   **Pros:** Removes 98% of all dissolved salts and heavy metal ions regardless of water chemistry [KNOWN].
*   **Cons:** Consumes $80\text{W}$ power, requiring large $200\text{W}$ solar panels and heavy $50\text{Ah}$ batteries; generates $65\%$ reject wastewater, which is unacceptable in water-scarce rural habitations [INFERENCE].
*   **Hackathon Feasibility:** Low (costly, heavy, difficult to power in portable demo).

### Approach 2: Low-Pressure Modular Ultrafiltration (UF) + Heavy-Metal Selective Resin + Solar IoT Edge Monitor (RECOMMENDED)
*   **Mechanism:** Multi-stage low-pressure purification: (1) Washable 50-micron mesh pre-filter, (2) 5-micron PP sediment + Katalox-Light media (for iron/manganese/coal dust), (3) Heavy-metal selective chelating ion-exchange resin (for $\text{Pb}^{2+}$, $\text{Cd}^{2+}$, $\text{Fe}^{3+}$), (4) 0.01-micron hollow-fiber UF membrane + 275nm UV-C LED chamber. Sensors read pH, turbidity, TDS, temperature, and pressure differential.
*   **Pros:** Operates under low pressure ($15\text{ PSI}$, $<15\text{W}$ DC power); 95% water recovery rate; highly portable; cost-effective; solar PV runnable [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons:** TDS reduction is moderate (20-30%); highly saline water ($>2000\text{ mg/L}$) requires secondary dilution or specialized media [INFERENCE].
*   **Hackathon Feasibility:** High (affordable, compact, low power, easily demonstrateable).

### Approach 3: Chemical Coagulation-Flocculation Dosing Unit + Microcontroller Sensor Array
*   **Mechanism:** Automated micro-dosing of alum/polyaluminium chloride (PAC) to settle mining silt, followed by gravity sand filter and chlorination.
*   **Pros:** Effective for extremely turbid surface mine pit water ($>500\text{ NTU}$) [KNOWN].
*   **Cons:** Requires handling liquid chemical reagents in remote villages; risk of chemical over-dosing; high mechanical maintenance [INFERENCE].
*   **Hackathon Feasibility:** Medium (chemical handling risks during live demonstration).

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

**Selected Approach:** **Approach 2 (Hybrid Modular Ultrafiltration + Heavy-Metal Ion-Exchange Resin + Low-Power Solar ESP32 IoT Edge System)** [INFERENCE].

### Justification
1.  **Energy Efficiency & Portability:** Mining-affected rural areas in Jharkhand frequently suffer from total grid failure. Approach 2 operates on a small $12\text{V}$ DC booster pump ($12\text{W}$) and UV-C LED ($3\text{W}$), totaling $<15\text{W}$. The entire system can run continuously off a compact $50\text{W}$ solar panel and $12\text{V}$ LiFePO4 battery pack [INFERENCE].
2.  **Specific Contaminant Targeting:** Mining runoff in Jharkhand contains high suspended solids, iron, manganese, and heavy metals rather than extreme sea-water salinity [RESEARCHED]. Katalox-Light media and selective chelating resin directly eliminate iron ($>95\%$) and heavy metals without the massive water wastage of RO systems [INFERENCE].
3.  **Water Conservation:** Generates $<5\%$ flush waste, compared to RO systems that waste $65\text{–}70\%$ of raw water [KNOWN].
4.  **Rejection of Alternatives:** Approach 1 (RO) was rejected due to high power consumption ($80\text{W}+$), high cost, and unacceptable water wastage in drought-prone rural mining blocks. Approach 3 was rejected due to the severe logistical hazard of supplying and handling chemical liquid reagents in unassisted rural hamlets [INFERENCE].

### Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative

1.  **Water Ingress & Sensing:** Raw water enters the intake port. Before entering the primary purification stack, a pre-sensing chamber measures raw pH, turbidity, TDS, and temperature using analog probes connected to the ESP32 ADC pins [INFERENCE].
2.  **Edge Calibration & WAWQI Calculation:** The ESP32 converts raw analog voltages into physical units using stored polynomial calibration curves. The embedded C++ firmware calculates the Weighted Arithmetic Water Quality Index (WAWQI) score in real time [INFERENCE].
3.  **Safety Rule Execution & Actuation:**
    *   If WAWQI is within safe drinking limits ($WQI \le 50$, pH $6.5\text{--}8.5$, Turbidity $<1\text{ NTU}$, TDS $<500\text{ mg/L}$), the ESP32 energizes the inline $12\text{V}$ solenoid valve, allowing water to flow through the 4-stage filter stack and UV-C LED chamber to the outlet tap [INFERENCE].
    *   If any parameter breaches critical BIS 10500 limits (e.g., Turbidity $>5\text{ NTU}$ or pH $<6.5$), the valve automatically closes, preventing unsafe consumption. A red LED illuminates and an onboard piezo alarm sounds [INFERENCE].
4.  **Telemetry Sync & Offline Buffering:** Telemetry packets formatted in JSON are transmitted via MQTT to the cloud broker. If cellular signal is lost, telemetry records are appended to an offline SPIFFS flash buffer on the ESP32. Upon cellular link restoration, the buffer flushes sequentially [INFERENCE].
5.  **Cloud Ingestion & Visualization:** The Node.js backend ingests MQTT payloads, writes time-series logs to PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB, and pushes live updates via WebSockets to the React GIS Web Dashboard and Flutter Mobile App [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### AI/ML Evaluative Assessment

*Is AI strictly required for basic water potability classification?* **NO.** Water potability classification is governed deterministically by Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS IS 10500:2012) mathematical rules using the Weighted Arithmetic Water Quality Index (WAWQI) [KNOWN]. Employing an LLM or complex neural network for simple potability classification introduces hallucination risks, latency, and unnecessary computational cost [INFERENCE].

### Justified AI Component 1: Anomaly Detection & Sensor Calibration Drift Prediction
*   **Purpose:** Detect physical sensor fouling, cable degradation, or artificial probe tampering by flagging abnormal parameter combinations (e.g., sudden TDS drops to zero while conductivity remains high) [INFERENCE].
*   **Model Type:** Lightweight Isolation Forest or Microcontroller TinyML (e.g., TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers) [INFERENCE].
*   **Input:** Multi-variable time-series window (pH, TDS, Turbidity, Temp, Delta Time, Flow Rate) [INFERENCE].
*   **Output:** Anomaly Score ($0\text{--}100$) and Sensor Health State (`OK` / `DRIFT_DETECTED` / `FAULTY`) [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required:** 5,000 synthetic and historical sensor drift sequences [ASSUMPTION].
*   **Why AI is Preferable:** Fixed static threshold rules fail to detect subtle multivariate sensor calibration drift over time [INFERENCE].
*   **Evaluation Metric:** F1-score ($>0.90$) on synthetic test datasets [INFERENCE].
*   **Failure Mode Handling:** If anomaly score $>80\%$, flag reading for manual re-calibration; do NOT trigger false water valve shut-off unless primary raw thresholds (BIS IS 10500) are explicitly breached [INFERENCE].
*   **Human Oversight:** Local Jal Sahiya performs physical buffer-solution calibration when flagged by the anomaly alert [INFERENCE].

### Justified AI Component 2: Predictive Filter Clogging & Remaining Useful Life (RUL)
*   **Purpose:** Predict when the sediment filter and heavy-metal resin cartridges will clog, enabling preemptive maintenance dispatch [INFERENCE].
*   **Model Type:** Linear Regression / Gradient Boosting Regressor (LightGBM) running on the cloud backend [INFERENCE].
*   **Input:** Accumulated flow volume (Liters), average inlet turbidity, total differential pressure ($\Delta P$), and operating hours [INFERENCE].
*   **Output:** Estimated Remaining Useful Life (RUL) in days and Liters [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required:** Bench test pressure drop curves across varying turbidity levels [INFERENCE].
*   **Why AI is Preferable:** Clogging rate is non-linear and depends heavily on monsoon turbidity spikes and usage throughput [INFERENCE].
*   **Evaluation Metric:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE $<3\text{ days}$) [INFERENCE].
*   **Failure Mode Handling:** Fall back to a fixed volumetric replacement limit ($5,000\text{ Liters}$) if predictive model confidence drops [INFERENCE].
*   **Human Oversight:** PHED district engineer validates replacement schedule before dispatching spare cartridges [INFERENCE].

### Explicit Exclusion of AI
AI/ML must **NOT** be used to alter, bypass, or predict physical water purification processes or override safety shut-off valves. Safety valve shut-off MUST remain governed by deterministic C++ firmware logic running locally on the ESP32 [INFERENCE].

### Offline & Low-Network Synchronization Design

1.  **Local Storage Engine:** The ESP32 utilizes a $1.5\text{ MB}$ SPI Flash File System (SPIFFS) partition structured as a ring-buffer file storing binary telemetry records (`Struct` size: 32 bytes) [INFERENCE].
2.  **Capacity & Storage Calculation:**
    *   Sampling Rate: 1 record per 15 minutes = 96 records/day = $3.07\text{ KB/day}$ [INFERENCE].

    *   The edge device monitors signal availability using AT commands (`AT+CSQ`) [INFERENCE].
