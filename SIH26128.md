## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Livestock farming forms the backbone of Maharashtra’s rural economy, yet disease outbreaks such as Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD), Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD), Brucellosis, and Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR) inflict severe economic losses and threaten public health through zoonotic transmission [KNOWN]. Current animal health reporting in rural Maharashtra is hampered by delayed symptom reporting, fragmented health and vaccination histories, long distances to veterinary diagnostic facilities, and unreliable network connectivity in remote tribal hamlets (*padas*) [INFERENCE]. To address these challenges, we propose **PashuRakshak AI**—a unified, offline-first, multilingual livestock health surveillance and decision-support platform tailored for the Government of Maharashtra. **PashuRakshak AI** integrates multi-channel report ingestion (Mobile App, Web, offline PWA, and IVR/SMS via toll-free 1962 integration) with rule-based and AI-assisted clinical triage, geospatial epidemiology mapping (Kriging and spatial autocorrelation), automated sample referral tracking, and real-time administrative decision dashboards [INFERENCE].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The problem description provided by the Government of Maharashtra [PS] contains explicit operational challenges and required system features.

### A. Explicit Challenges Identified in Source Text
1. **Lack of Unified, Real-Time Mechanism**: Livestock owners, field veterinarians, para-veterinary workers, and government departments lack a single integrated platform to track emerging animal health risks at village, block, and district levels [PS].
2. **Delayed Symptom Reporting**: Disease symptoms and animal mortality events are frequently reported late, leading to delayed disease containment [PS].
3. **Distant Diagnostic Infrastructure**: Veterinary diagnostic facilities and referral laboratories are physically distant from rural farms [PS].
4. **Incomplete Health Records**: Vaccination, treatment, and medical histories of individual animals or herds remain incomplete or unavailable [PS].
5. **Fragmented Information Systems**: Data across farms, veterinary dispensaries, diagnostic laboratories, mass vaccination drives, and state surveillance programs operate in silos [PS].
6. **Severe Downstream Consequences**: Delayed action leads to higher livestock mortality, reduced milk/meat productivity, increased risk of zoonotic disease spillover to humans, and depressed farmer income [PS].
7. **Infrastructure Limitations**: System deployment must function seamlessly in low-connectivity and zero-network rural zones [PS].

### B. Explicit Solution Requirements Identified in Source Text
The target solution must be a scalable animal-health surveillance and decision-support system capable of:
* **Req-1**: Capturing symptom and mortality reports from livestock owners (farmers) and field workers (veterinarians/paravets) [PS].
* **Req-2**: Applying rule-based or AI-assisted triage to automatically flag suspected disease outbreaks [PS].
* **Req-3**: Integrating geospatial risk mapping, local weather data, and historical disease trends [PS].
* **Req-4**: Maintaining animal-level or herd-level health, vaccination, and treatment records linked to identification [PS].
* **Req-5**: Issuing multilingual advisories, risk alerts, and preventive guidelines [PS].
* **Req-6**: Supporting sample collection tracking, laboratory referral workflows, and escalation of severe cases [PS].
* **Req-7**: Providing decision-support analytics and monitoring dashboards for state/district veterinary officials [PS].
* **Req-8**: Operating flexibly through mobile app, web application, Interactive Voice Response (IVR), SMS, and offline-enabled synchronization channels [PS].

### C. Inferred Technical & Operational Requirements
* **Inf-1**: Universal Identifier Compatibility: System must interface with India's 12-digit ear-tag identifier ("Pashu Aadhaar") established under the Bharat Pashudhan / NDLM database [RESEARCHED].
* **Inf-2**: Marathi Language Primacy: System UI, voice prompts, and notification engines must support Marathi as the primary language alongside Hindi and English [KNOWN].
* **Inf-3**: Strict Data Synchronization Protocol: Local SQLite/IndexedDB databases must queue offline field reports and sync via transactional delta-compression when network connectivity is restored [INFERENCE].
* **Inf-4**: Epidemic Containment Protocols: Automated generation of 5 km / 10 km containment zone rings around confirmed contagious outbreaks (e.g., FMD or African Swine Fever) [RESEARCHED].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Geographic Diversity across Maharashtra**: Maharashtra spans distinct agro-climatic zones—from heavy-rainfall coastal Konkan to drought-prone Marathwada and forest/tribal belts of Vidarbha (e.g., Gadchiroli, Melghat) [KNOWN]. System must adapt to localized environmental vector risk factors (e.g., high humidity boosting tick-borne diseases like Babesiosis in Konkan vs waterborne anthrax in drought-struck areas) [RESEARCHED].
2. **Network Connectivity Realities**: Rural hamlets (*padas*) frequently experience 2G-only or zero-cellular coverage [KNOWN]. **Constraint**: All core field reporting, ear-tag lookups, and triage functions must run 100% offline on client devices, syncing asynchronously when 3G/4G/Wi-Fi becomes available [INFERENCE].
3. **Hardware Limitations**: Field paravets and farmers possess low-cost Android smartphones (2GB–3GB RAM, limited storage) [KNOWN]. **Constraint**: AI vision models and client applications must be ultra-lightweight (<25MB APK/PWA size; ONNX/TensorFlow Lite models <8MB) [INFERENCE].
4. **Linguistic & Digital Literacy Realities**: Over 65% of rural livestock owners in Maharashtra prefer Marathi audio/visual communication over text-dense screens [INFERENCE]. **Constraint**: UI must feature iconographic workflows, Marathi voice input/output (STT/TTS), and simple IVR fallback for basic phone users [INFERENCE].
5. **Power Reliability**: Rural dispensaries and field locations suffer frequent load shedding [KNOWN]. **Constraint**: Mobile apps must feature aggressive battery preservation and minimal CPU-intensive background tasks [INFERENCE].

## 8. Existing Solutions & Gap Analysis

### 1. Bharat Pashudhan (formerly INAPH / NDLM)
* **Description**: Government of India’s official national database managing animal identification (Pashu Aadhaar), mass vaccination logging (under NADCP), breeding, and owner records [RESEARCHED].
* **Strengths**: Universal reach (over 38 crore tagged animals), standardized 12-digit ear tags, central repository [RESEARCHED].
* **Weaknesses**: Primary design is transactional record-keeping; lacks real-time automated AI triage, lacks dynamic symptom-based early warning, experiences network latency in offline rural areas [RESEARCHED].
* **Relevance**: Core identity layer for Indian livestock [RESEARCHED].
* **Remaining Gap**: Lacks instant farmer-facing diagnostic triage, voice-based IVR reporting, and dynamic micro-level geospatial outbreak prediction [INFERENCE].
* **Source**: Department of Animal Husbandry & Dairying (DAHD) [1.2].
* **Confidence**: [RESEARCHED]

### 2. ICAR-NIVEDI NADRES 3.0 (National Animal Disease Referral Expert System)
* **Description**: AI/ML-enabled national forecasting portal developed by ICAR-NIVEDI that provides 2-month advance district-level risk forecasts for 13 major livestock diseases [RESEARCHED].
* **Strengths**: Robust epidemiological models, integration with IMD weather data, proven scientific credibility [RESEARCHED].
* **Weaknesses**: Macro-level forecasting (district scale); lacks real-time village/block-level field reporting ingestion, lacks direct farmer/paravet referral workflows [RESEARCHED].
* **Relevance**: Gold standard for disease forecasting algorithms in India [RESEARCHED].
* **Remaining Gap**: Micro-level (village/farm scale) early warning and real-time field escalation mechanics are unaddressed [INFERENCE].
* **Source**: ICAR-NIVEDI [2.4, 2.5].
* **Confidence**: [RESEARCHED]

### 3. Maharashtra 1962 Mobile Veterinary Unit (MVU) Call Center
* **Description**: State toll-free helpline operating out of Commissionerate of Animal Husbandry (Pune) providing doorstep veterinary care via MVUs [RESEARCHED].
* **Strengths**: Highly accessible via basic voice phone call; strong ground presence [RESEARCHED].
* **Weaknesses**: Call-center operations are manual; lacks automated symptom triage, automated spatial clustering, or digital sample tracking [INFERENCE].
* **Relevance**: Essential communication channel for non-smartphone farmers [RESEARCHED].
* **Remaining Gap**: Absence of intelligent digital backend to automatically cluster incoming calls into spatial epidemic alerts [INFERENCE].
* **Source**: Department of Animal Husbandry, Govt of Maharashtra [3.11].
* **Confidence**: [RESEARCHED]

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

We select **Approach 2: Hybrid AI-Assisted Multilingual Triage + Spatial-Temporal Risk Engine + Offline-First Architecture** [INFERENCE].

### Justification
1. **Why Approach 1 was modified/rejected**: While deterministic rule-based systems are explainable, they fail when dealing with non-literate farmers who cannot fill text forms, and they cannot process lesion images or spoken Marathi voice descriptions [INFERENCE].
2. **Why Approach 3 was rejected**: Installing IoT collars across India's 30+ crore livestock population is economically and logistically infeasible for smallholder farmers owning 2–5 cattle [INFERENCE].
3. **Why Approach 2 Wins**:
   * It combines the mathematical precision and regulatory safety of **clinical rule engines** with the accessibility of **multilingual AI voice processing (Whisper/Bhashini)** and **edge image classification (YOLOv8-lite)** [INFERENCE].
   * Its **offline-first PWA architecture** guarantees uninterrupted field operation in zero-connectivity tribal blocks [INFERENCE].
   * It directly fulfills every explicit requirement (Req-1 through Req-8) from the Government of Maharashtra problem statement [PS].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative
1. **Report Generation (Field)**: A livestock owner or paravet observes high fever and oral lesions in cattle in a remote village in Ahmednagar. Opening the **PashuRakshak Mobile App** (or calling the 1962 IVR), the user records a Marathi voice note and captures a photograph of the lesions [INFERENCE].
2. **Offline Edge Triage**: If offline, the app’s embedded ONNX model checks the lesion image, while a client-side decision tree categorizes symptoms. The report is stored locally in SQLite with a cryptographic timestamp and queued for upload [INFERENCE].
3. **Background Sync**: As soon as the device connects to a cellular or Wi-Fi network, the Service Worker executes a delta-sync, sending compressed JSON payloads to the API Gateway [INFERENCE].
4. **Server Triage & Pashu Aadhaar Validation**: The backend validates the 12-digit ear tag against the Pashu Aadhaar registry schema, executes the master clinical rule engine, and assigns a suspicion triage score (e.g., *High Risk: Suspected Foot-and-Mouth Disease*) [INFERENCE].
5. **Geospatial Outbreak Clustering**: The PostGIS spatial engine aggregates reports within a 5 km radius over a sliding 72-hour window. If report frequency exceeds the statistical baseline (Spatial Scan Statistics / Anselin Local Moran's I), an **Outbreak Cluster Event** is generated [INFERENCE].
6. **Alert Dispatch & Lab Referral**:
   * An automated alert is sent to the District Veterinary Officer’s dashboard [INFERENCE].
   * An SMS/WhatsApp advisory in Marathi is broadcast to all registered farmers within a 10 km radius [INFERENCE].
   * A digital QR-coded Sample Collection Order is pushed to the local field vet's app for blood/swab collection and transmission to the RDDL lab in Pune [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### AI Component 1: Computer Vision Lesion & Disease Symptom Classifier
1. **Exact Problem Solved**: Rapidly flags visual signs of diseases (e.g., Lumpy Skin Disease nodular lesions, FMD foot/mouth erosions) from field photos taken by farmers or paravets [INFERENCE].
2. **Input**: Digital image (JPEG/PNG) from mobile camera [INFERENCE].
3. **Output**: Bounding box object detection, lesion class label, and confidence score (e.g., *LSD Lesion: 88% confidence*) [INFERENCE].
4. **Why Simpler System Fails**: Farmers often struggle to describe lesion shapes and severity in text forms [INFERENCE].
5. **Data Required**: 1,500+ annotated images of healthy vs. diseased livestock skin/mouth/feet [ASSUMPTION].
6. **Evaluation Metrics**: Mean Average Precision (mAP@0.5) > 0.85, Inference time <200ms on mobile CPU [INFERENCE].
7. **When Wrong**: If confidence <70%, system flags report as "Uncertain - Requires Vet Verification" rather than issuing definitive triage [INFERENCE].
8. **Training Required**: Yes, fine-tuning pre-trained YOLOv8-Nano [INFERENCE].
9. **Fine-Tuning**: Fine-tuned on veterinary dermatology datasets [INFERENCE].
10. **RAG**: Not applicable [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision**: Yes [INFERENCE].
12. **Speech Processing**: No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting**: No [INFERENCE].
14. **Human Oversight**: Mandatory. AI provides decision support; field vet makes final clinical assessment [INFERENCE].

### AI Component 2: Multilingual Speech-to-Text & Intent Extractor
1. **Exact Problem Solved**: Converts Marathi spoken audio (via IVR or app voice notes) into structured clinical symptom tags [INFERENCE].
2. **Input**: Audio file (WAV/MP3/Ogg) in Marathi / Hindi [INFERENCE].
3. **Output**: Transcribed text + extracted clinical entities (e.g., *Species: Cow, Symptom: High Fever, Duration: 3 Days*) [INFERENCE].

### Offline Synchronization & Conflict Handling Protocol
1. **Local Storage Engine**: Web PWA uses **IndexedDB via Dexie.js**; Mobile React Native app uses **SQLite via WatermelonDB** [INFERENCE].
2. **Sync Mechanism**:
   * Uses an asynchronous **Vector Clock & Timestamped Delta Sync** algorithm [INFERENCE].
   * Reports created offline receive a client-generated UUID `report_id` and `sync_status = 'PENDING'` [INFERENCE].
   * When network connectivity returns, a background service worker posts pending payloads to `/api/v1/sync/upload` [INFERENCE].
3. **Conflict Handling Strategy**:
   * **Field Reports**: Appends-only architecture; no conflict possible (each report is an immutable timeline event) [INFERENCE].
   * **Animal Health Passbook Updates**: Server-wins timestamp resolution for vaccination status; client receives updated state on next pull [INFERENCE].
4. **Bandwidth Optimization**:
   * Field images are compressed on-device (WebP format, max 1024x768, <150KB) before queueing [INFERENCE].
   * JSON sync payloads use gzip/brotli compression [INFERENCE].

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

* **Personal Data Protection (DPDP Act 2023 Compliance)**: Farmer phone numbers, names, and Aadhaar-linked identities are classified as Sensitive Personal Data. All PII is encrypted at rest using **AES-256-GCM** and anonymized on public-facing epidemic maps [INFERENCE].
* **Location Privacy**: Public heatmaps fuzz exact farm locations using a 1 km grid obfuscation algorithm to prevent social stigma or illegal commercial actions against affected farmers [INFERENCE].
* **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)**:
  * *Farmer*: View/manage own livestock records; submit reports; receive advisories [INFERENCE].
  * *Paravet / LSS*: Create field reports, scan ear tags, issue lab sample referrals [INFERENCE].
  * *Veterinary Officer*: Review block/district triage logs, validate lab results, authorize advisories [INFERENCE].
  * *State Admin*: Access state-wide analytics, manage system configurations [INFERENCE].
* **Audit Trail & Governance**: Every clinical override, quarantine decision, and laboratory result entry is recorded in an append-only immutable `system_audit_logs` table with user identity and IP/GPS metadata [INFERENCE].
