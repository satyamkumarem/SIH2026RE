## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Radio Frequency & Regulatory Constraints:**
   * In India, transmitting over unlicensed ISM bands (433 MHz / 868 MHz / 2.4 GHz) is restricted in power (typically ≤ 10mW to 100mW depending on band WPC guidelines) [RESEARCHED].
   * Amateur radio bands (144–146 MHz, 435–438 MHz) require HAM radio licenses for operations [KNOWN]. For hackathon field testing, ISM 433 MHz / 868 MHz LoRa modules operating within legal power limits are selected [ASSUMPTION].

2. **Field Deployment Conditions:**
   * Drop tests from high altitudes (drones, balloons, or high buildings) involve high vibration, wind drift, and potential structural impact upon landing [KNOWN].
   * The hardware structure must be mechanically shock-resistant, with reinforced 3D printed PETG/PLA+ or aluminum framing [ASSUMPTION].

3. **Power & Thermal Budget:**
   * Battery power is limited during flight operations [KNOWN].
   * Microcontrollers and transmitters must operate with low power consumption, utilizing power-saving sleep modes and efficient transmission cycles [INFERENCE].
   * Direct sunlight during drop/flight tests can cause sensor thermal drift [KNOWN].

4. **Connectivity Realities during Field Operations:**
   * Field testing sites (e.g., open grounds, launch fields) often suffer from unstable cellular data [KNOWN].
   * The ground station software must function completely offline on a local WiFi/LAN hot-spot generated by a Raspberry Pi or laptop [ASSUMPTION].

4. **[RQ-04] AI Anomaly Detection Feasibility:** Can an ML model (Isolation Forest or Autoencoder) accurately distinguish between normal sensor noise during tumbling and actual power failure or structural malfunction in high-frequency flight data streams?

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

We evaluate three potential solution architectures for the SIH team:

### Approach 1: Pure Software Simulation & Virtual Ground Station
* **Mechanism:** A web-based 3D software simulator displaying a virtual satellite orbit, synthetic telemetry generation, and a mock ground station dashboard using Three.js and CesiumJS.
* **Problem Fit:** Low. Fails to meet the mandatory SIH **Hardware Category** requirement [PS-02].
* **Complexity:** Low (Software only).
* **Cost:** Nearly zero.
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High, but high risk of disqualification or low judging score due to lack of physical hardware [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Static Hardware CanSat Payload with Handheld Receiver
* **Mechanism:** A basic 3D printed cylinder with an Arduino Nano, IMU sensor, and 433 MHz RF module sending telemetry to a handheld LCD receiver board.
* **Problem Fit:** Moderate. Fulfills basic hardware requirement but represents a generic beginner project with minimal engineering differentiation [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity:** Low.
* **Cost:** ~₹3,000–₹5,000.
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High, but lacks automated ground tracking, open ground station software, active mechanical actuation, and AI analytics [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Integrated Hardware-in-the-Loop Space System (Recommended)
* **Mechanism:** A multi-layered architecture featuring:
  1. **AstraNode (Space Segment):** Standardized 1U modular stack featuring dual-core ESP32-S3/STM32 OBC, EPS with solar charging & voltage monitoring, 9-DOF IMU + Baro + GPS sensor suite, 433 MHz LoRa transceiver, and servo-activated payload deployment mechanism.
  2. **GroundSight (Ground Segment):** Automated 2D Pan-Tilt antenna tracking rotator driven by dual NEMA-17 steppers and Arduino, RTL-SDR receiver module, and Raspberry Pi/laptop host.
  3. **AstraIQ Engine (Software & AI Layer):** Local Python backend receiving telemetry, calculating SGP4/GPS pointing angles, training an Isolation Forest model for real-time anomaly detection, and serving a 3D Web Dashboard.
* **Problem Fit:** High. Delivers a complete hardware-software co-designed system that satisfies space engineering standards [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity:** Moderate-High.
* **Cost:** ~₹10,000–₹14,000 [ASSUMPTION].
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High for a 4–6 student multidisciplinary team (electronics, software, mechanical) [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

We select **Approach 3: Integrated Hardware-in-the-Loop Space System ("AstraNode & GroundSight")**.

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected:
* **Approach 1** was rejected because pure software does not fulfill the mandatory SIH **Hardware** category requirement [PS-02].
* **Approach 2** was rejected because a simple handheld RF receiver lacks the technical complexity, automated ground station tracking, and AI anomaly detection needed to stand out in a competitive national hackathon [INFERENCE].

### Why Approach 3 Wins:
1. **End-to-End System Demonstration:** Demonstrates the complete space mission life cycle: Design -> Manufacturing -> Telemetry Transmission -> Ground Antenna Tracking -> AI Anomaly Processing [INFERENCE].
2. **High Judge Impact:** A live demo featuring a physical antenna rotator dynamically tracking a moving flight payload while real-time 3D orientation and ML anomaly scores stream to a web dashboard creates a compelling showcase [INFERENCE].
3. **Clear Division of Labor:** Allows a 4–6 student team to split cleanly into sub-teams: Mechanical/3D Printing, Firmware/PCB, RF/Ground Station, and Software/AI [INFERENCE].

### Data Flow Narrative

1. **Data Acquisition (Space Segment):**
   * The OBC reads accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer values from the BNO055/MPU6050 via I2C at 20 Hz [ASSUMPTION].
   * Pressure, temperature, and altitude are sampled from the BMP280 via I2C at 5 Hz [ASSUMPTION].
   * Latitude, longitude, altitude, and UTC time are read from the NEO-6M GPS via UART at 1 Hz [ASSUMPTION].
   * Battery voltage and charge current are monitored via analog ADC pins [ASSUMPTION].

2. **Telemetry Formatting & Local Storage:**
   * Raw telemetry values are packed into a binary struct/packet (reducing bandwidth) or lightweight JSON frame [ASSUMPTION].
   * Telemetry is simultaneously logged to the on-board MicroSD card for black-box data recovery [ASSUMPTION].

3. **RF Downlink Transmission:**
   * The SX1278 LoRa module transmits encoded data packets over 433 MHz at 2 Hz [ASSUMPTION].

4. **Ground Station Signal Reception & Rotator Tracking:**
   * The ground station receiver (ESP32 Gateway or RTL-SDR) picks up the packet [ASSUMPTION].
   * The GPS coordinates extracted from the packet are passed to the Ground Sight Rotator Controller [ASSUMPTION].
   * The controller calculates the target Azimuth and Elevation angles relative to the ground station's fixed coordinates and sends pulse commands to the NEMA-17 stepper motors, pointing the high-gain antenna directly at the flying payload [ASSUMPTION].

5. **Backend Ingestion & AI Processing:**
   * Telemetry packets are sent via local WebSocket to the Python FastAPI backend [ASSUMPTION].
   * The **AstraIQ AI Engine** passes the incoming feature vector `[Voltage, Temp, Gyro_X, Gyro_Y, Gyro_Z, Altitude_Delta]` through an Isolation Forest anomaly detection model [ASSUMPTION].
   * An anomaly score (0 to 100%) and flag status (NORMAL / WARNING / CRITICAL) are calculated [ASSUMPTION].

6. **Real-Time UI Visualization:**
   * The Web Dashboard renders real-time time-series plots of altitude and power, updates the live 3D orientation model in Three.js, plots trajectory on an interactive map, and displays AI health diagnostics [ASSUMPTION].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

| Parameter | AI Anomaly Detection Engine (AstraIQ) Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Exact Purpose** | Automatically identify operational anomalies in flight telemetry (e.g., sudden power drainage, sensor freezing, rapid tumbling, or abnormal altitude decay) during flight operations [ASSUMPTION]. |
| **Model Type** | **Isolation Forest** (Unsupervised Anomaly Detection) [ASSUMPTION]. |
| **Input Features** | 6-feature vector: `[Battery_Voltage, Temperature, Gyro_Magnitude, Accel_Magnitude, Pressure_Delta, GPS_Satellite_Count]` sampled at 2 Hz [ASSUMPTION]. |
| **Output** | Anomaly Score (-1 for Anomaly, 1 for Normal) and Normalized Anomaly Probability Score (0% to 100%) with feature attribution flags [ASSUMPTION]. |
| **Data Required** | ~5,000 samples of baseline "healthy" flight telemetry generated via desktop bench testing and nominal synthetic drop profiles [ASSUMPTION]. |
| **Why AI over Rules?** | Simple static thresholds fail during complex dynamic states (e.g., high rotational acceleration during descent might look like a failure, but is normal unless accompanied by voltage drops or rapid pressure changes). AI learns complex multi-variate non-linear relationships across power, thermal, and kinematic features simultaneously [INFERENCE]. |
| **Evaluation Metrics** | Precision, Recall, and F1-Score on synthetically injected fault sets (e.g., simulated battery short, sensor stall, freefall tumbling) [ASSUMPTION]. |
| **Failure Mode** | High false-positive rate under unexpected turbulent flight maneuvers [INFERENCE]. |
| **Human Oversight** | Ground operators retain override controls. The AI engine provides visual alerts and diagnostic recommendations on the dashboard, but does **NOT** trigger irreversible mechanical fail-safes autonomously [ASSUMPTION]. |

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used:
* **Critical Parachute / Recovery Deployment Trigger:** The mechanical release trigger must be governed strictly by deterministic, deterministic hard-coded firmware rules (e.g., `if (altitude < 100m && descent_rate > 5m/s) deploy()`) or physical barometric switches [ASSUMPTION]. AI must **NEVER** control critical flight termination or parachute release mechanisms due to non-deterministic failure risks [INFERENCE].

#### 2. Ground Station Database Schema (SQLite / Time-Series Table)
```sql
CREATE TABLE telemetry_logs (
    id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
    packet_id INTEGER,
    received_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    payload_id VARCHAR(32),
    battery_voltage REAL,
    temperature REAL,
    pressure REAL,
    altitude REAL,
    gyro_x REAL, gyro_y REAL, gyro_z REAL,
    accel_x REAL, accel_y REAL, accel_z REAL,
    latitude REAL, longitude REAL, gps_alt REAL,
    ai_anomaly_score REAL,
    ai_status VARCHAR(16)
);
```

### Offline & Low-Network Operation
* **Local Hotspot Architecture:** The Raspberry Pi ground station acts as its own local WiFi access point (`AstraGround_Hotspot`). Laptops and tablets connect locally without needing external internet access [ASSUMPTION].
* **On-Board SD Logging (Black Box):** In case of RF communication blockage or signal loss, the OBC logs all high-frequency data (20 Hz) to an SPI MicroSD card module [ASSUMPTION].
* **Bandwidth Optimization:** RF payload frames use fixed-point binary integer encoding (struct packing) rather than plain text JSON, reducing transmission size from ~220 bytes to 48 bytes per frame [ASSUMPTION].
