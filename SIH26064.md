## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Executive Summary
The National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (NCPOR) under the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES), Government of India, requires a low-cost, deployable ocean-bottom sensor system to detect and map metal-rich seabed mineral deposits—specifically polymetallic nodules (PN), hydrothermal polymetallic sulphides (PMS), cobalt-rich ferromanganese crusts, and rare-earth-element (REE)-bearing sediments [PS]. Deep-ocean exploration currently relies on multi-million dollar Remotely Operated Vehicles (ROVs), Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs), or heavy towed arrays, which are expensive to operate and limit survey spatial coverage [RESEARCHED]. This dossier proposes **HydroDetect-OBN** (Ocean Bottom Node), an autonomous, free-fall deployable seafloor sensor lander and mapping payload equipped with multi-modal non-destructive sensing—combining Active Electromagnetic Induction (EMI) for conductivity/metal proximity, Self-Potential (SP) Ag/AgCl non-polarizable electrode pairs for hydrothermal sulphide identification, and 3-axis Magnetometry for magnetic anomaly detection [INFERENCE]. Designed for rapid release from research vessels (e.g., ORV *Sagar Kanya* or ORV *Sagar Nidhi*) [KNOWN], the system logs multi-sensor geophysical data at the ocean bed and communicates acoustic status pings back to the vessel before recovering via a galvanic burn-wire / electromagnetic release drop-weight system [INFERENCE]. For the SIH build, a functional tabletop/shallow-water physical prototype with full embedded acquisition, multi-sensor calibration, real-time telemetry bridge, and 3D bathymetric/mineral probability GIS mapping software will be built and demonstrated [ASSUMPTION].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The explicit problem statement provided by MoES / NCPOR is deconstructed into the following atomic requirements:

*   **Requirement A (Deployability & Deployment Mechanism)**:
    *   *Source Text*: "...low-cost deployable ocean-bottom sensor that can be released from a research vessel during surveys..." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation*: The sensor system must be modular, lightweight enough for manual or deck-crane launch, and capable of free-fall descent to the seafloor without tethering to allow unassisted vessel operation during broad-area surveys [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Hydrodynamic ballast design with positive buoyancy syntactic foam/glass spheres, coupled with an automatic or acoustic command drop-weight release system for surface recovery [INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag*: `[PS]`

*   **Requirement B (Seafloor Metal & Mineral Target Diversity)**:
    *   *Source Text*: "...to detect and map metal-rich seabed deposits, including polymetallic nodules, hydrothermal sulphides, cobalt-rich crusts and rare-earth-element-bearing sediments..." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation*: A single geophysical detection modality (e.g., simple metal detector coil) is insufficient. Polymetallic nodules require inductive conductivity/permittivity sensing; hydrothermal sulphides generate galvanic Self-Potential (SP) redox voltages; cobalt crusts and ferromanganese deposits exhibit distinct magnetic susceptibilities [RESEARCHED].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Tri-modal sensing payload integration: (1) Active Pulsed Electromagnetic Induction (EMI) coil array, (2) Differential Self-Potential (SP) Ag/AgCl electrode array, and (3) Fluxgate/TMR 3-axis Magnetometer [INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag*: `[PS]`

*   **Requirement C (Mapping & Spatial Resolution)**:
    *   *Source Text*: "...detect and map metal-rich seabed deposits..." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation*: Raw sensor values are useless without time-synchronized geo-referencing. The system must pair internal sensor logs with drop-location GPS, acoustic ultra-short baseline (USBL) positioning, or dead-reckoning IMU data to produce 2D/3D geospatial heatmaps of deposit distribution [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Onboard high-precision Real-Time Clock (RTC) synced via GNSS prior to deployment; post-processing interpolation software creating GeoTIFF / CSV mineral density maps overlaid on bathymetry [INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag*: `[PS]`

*   **Requirement D (Low-Cost & Rapid Exploration Economics)**:
    *   *Source Text*: "...providing a rapid and cost-effective tool for deep-ocean mineral exploration." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation*: Bill-of-materials (BOM) must be orders of magnitude lower than standard AUVs ($1M+) or deep-towed sensors ($500k+) to allow multi-node swarm deployment [RESEARCHED].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Use COTS (Commercial Off-The-Shelf) pressure-tolerant microcontrollers, custom PCB analog front-ends (AFE), and modular acrylic/aluminium pressure housings rated for test depths (50–300m for SIH demo, scaleable to 4000m for production) [INFERENCE].
    *   *Confidence Tag*: `[PS]`

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Hydrostatic Pressure**: Deep-sea mineral deposits occur at depths from 1,000m (cobalt crusts) to 4,000–6,000m (polymetallic nodules in CIOB) [RESEARCHED]. Pressure increases by ~1 bar per 10m depth (400 bar / 40 MPa at 4000m) [KNOWN]. *Hackathon Constraint*: Testing at 400 bar requires expensive hyperbaric chambers; MVP must use acrylic/aluminium pressure housing rated for 5–10 bar (50–100m water depth) while proving scaleable pressure housing calculations [ASSUMPTION].
*   **Electromagnetic Attenuation in Seawater**: High-frequency RF (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Cellular, GPS) attenuates within a few centimeters in conductive seawater ($\sigma \approx 4\text{ S/m}$) [KNOWN]. *Constraint*: No wireless communications (GPS/Wi-Fi) underwater; all position/sensor data must be logged internally or transmitted via low-frequency acoustic waves (~10–30 kHz) [INFERENCE].
*   **Corrosion & Biofouling**: Seawater causes rapid galvanic corrosion of exposed metals and biofouling over long deployments [KNOWN]. *Constraint*: Pressure housing must use marine-grade Anodized Aluminum 6061-T6 / Titanium Grade 5 or POM Delrin, with sacrificial zinc anodes [INFERENCE].
*   **Temperature Variations**: Deep ocean water (below 1,000m) remains at 1°C to 4°C, whereas surface tropical waters (Indian Ocean) reach 28°C–30°C [KNOWN]. *Constraint*: Temperature drift severely affects analog sensor front-ends (magnetometers, SP electrodes, EMI oscillators); hardware must include precision temperature compensation sensors (DS18B20 / PT100) and software calibration matrices [INFERENCE].
*   **Darkness & Visibility**: Zero ambient sunlight below 200m depth [KNOWN]. *Constraint*: Optical cameras (if used for ground-truth validation) require high-efficiency LED strobe lights and consume significant battery power [INFERENCE].
*   **Vessel Operating Conditions**: Survey vessels experience high sea states (Pitch, Roll, Yaw) and unpredictable ocean currents during drop operations [KNOWN]. *Constraint*: Node must have hydrodynamic stability and bottom-heavy ballast to land upright on muddy abyssal sediments [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Optical / Computer Vision Only (Autonomous Camera Lander)
*   **Mechanism**: High-intensity LED flash lights with high-resolution underwater camera recording seafloor video; computer vision (YOLOv8) counts nodules on surface [INFERENCE].
*   **Required Data**: Annotated seafloor image dataset of nodules and sediment [INFERENCE].
*   **Architecture**: Raspberry Pi 4 + Camera Module + High-power LED strobe inside transparent acrylic pressure sphere [INFERENCE].
*   **Complexity**: Low to Moderate.
*   **Explainability**: High (visual evidence).
*   **Cost**: Low ($500–$800).
*   **Scalability**: Poor (suffers from high water turbidity, zero sub-surface detection, cannot detect buried sulphides/crusts) [INFERENCE].
*   **Hackathon Feasibility**: 90/100.
*   **Production Feasibility**: 35/100 (Useless in turbid water or for buried deposits).
*   **Risks**: Turbidity blinds camera; high power draw for LEDs.

### Approach 2: Pure Acoustic / Sonar Backscatter Lander
*   **Mechanism**: High-frequency (450 kHz) imaging sonar or single-beam acoustic backscatter transponder measuring target reflectivity [INFERENCE].
*   **Required Data**: Acoustic impedance profiles of nodules vs sediment [INFERENCE].
*   **Architecture**: STM32 + Sonar Transducer Front-End + MicroSD logging [INFERENCE].
*   **Complexity**: Moderate.
*   **Explainability**: Moderate.
*   **Cost**: Moderate ($1,200–$2,500).
*   **Scalability**: Moderate (detects surface roughness, but cannot distinguish metallic nodules from non-metallic gravel/rocks) [INFERENCE].
*   **Hackathon Feasibility**: 70/100.
*   **Production Feasibility**: 50/100 (High false positive rate with non-metallic rocks).
*   **Risks**: False positives from non-metallic seabed topography.

### Approach 3: Multi-Modal Geophysical Ocean Bottom Node (EMI + SP + Magnetometer + Depth/Temp) [RECOMMENDED]
*   **Mechanism**: Combines Active Pulsed Electromagnetic Induction (for metallic conductivity), Differential Self-Potential Ag/AgCl electrodes (for hydrothermal sulphide electrochemical signatures), and 3-axis Magnetometry (for magnetic anomalies) [INFERENCE].
*   **Required Data**: Multi-channel analog voltage signals, temperature, pressure/depth, GPS drop coordinates [INFERENCE].
*   **Architecture**: Microcontroller (STM32H7 / ESP32-S3) + Custom Precision AFE (24-bit ADC) + MicroSD Logger + Acoustic Transducer + Galvanic Release + GIS Dashboard [INFERENCE].
*   **Complexity**: High (requires precise analog design and calibration).
*   **Explainability**: Extremely High (multi-physics correlation eliminates false positives).
*   **Cost**: Low-to-Moderate ($1,200 for MVP hardware; $7,500 for deep-sea production node).
*   **Scalability**: Excellent (nodes can be deployed in swarms to map square kilometers).
*   **Hackathon Feasibility**: 85/100.
*   **Production Feasibility**: 92/100.
*   **Risks**: Electromagnetic noise from onboard electronics; electrode polarization drift.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selected Solution
**Approach 3: Multi-Modal Geophysical Ocean Bottom Node (HydroDetect-OBN)** is selected [INFERENCE].

### Justification & Rejection of Alternatives
1.  **Why Approach 1 (Optical Only) was rejected**: Optical vision fails completely when seafloor sediments are disturbed (turbidity plume during lander impact) and cannot detect metal deposits buried beneath 5–50 cm of marine mud or sediment layer [RESEARCHED].
2.  **Why Approach 2 (Pure Acoustic) was rejected**: Acoustic backscatter measures surface acoustic impedance and acoustic roughness, but cannot differentiate a metallic manganese nodule from a basaltic rock or coral rubble of identical shape [RESEARCHED].
3.  **Why Approach 3 Wins**:
    *   *Direct Metal & Chemical Sensitivity*: Active EMI detects metallic conductivity directly; SP detects electrochemical redox reactions associated with hydrothermal sulphides; Magnetometry detects magnetic minerals [RESEARCHED].
    *   *Multi-Sensor Fusion*: Cross-verifying EMI spikes with SP negative anomalies (-50 mV to -200 mV) eliminates false positives from non-metallic rocks [RESEARCHED].
    *   *Low Power & Deployability*: Passive SP and magnetic sensors draw minimal microamps; EMI operates in short pulsed bursts, allowing months of battery operational life on simple lithium-ion batteries [INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative
1.  **Deployment Phase**: The survey vessel navigates to target coordinates. The OBN lander is powered on, syncs high-precision clock time via GPS, performs sensor zero-calibration, and is dropped into the sea [INFERENCE].
2.  **Descent Phase**: As the node sinks at ~1.2 m/s under heavy steel ballast, the MS5837 pressure sensor records depth changes. When depth exceeds a pre-set threshold (e.g., >10m), the MCU switches into active geophysical logging mode [INFERENCE].
3.  **Seafloor Settlement & Sensing Phase**:
    *   Upon seabed contact (detected by 3-axis IMU acceleration shock and constant depth profile), the node initiates its sensing sequence [INFERENCE].
    *   *Self-Potential (SP)*: The non-polarizable Ag/AgCl electrode pair measures natural galvanic potential differences across seawater/sediment. A 24-bit ADC (ADS1256) captures microvolt-level signals [RESEARCHED].
    *   *Active EMI*: The MCU pulses a 20 kHz current through the transmit coil. Secondary eddy currents induced in nearby metallic deposits generate a decay voltage in the receive coil, amplified and sampled by the ADC [INFERENCE].
    *   *Magnetometer & Environment*: The 3-axis magnetometer reads local magnetic vector alterations; temperature and depth sensors record ambient conditions [INFERENCE].
    *   Data is timestamped and written to an onboard industrial SPI MicroSD flash buffer [INFERENCE].
4.  **Acoustic Ping Telemetry Phase**: Every 60 seconds, an acoustic pulse (18–22 kHz FSK) encodes node ID, current depth, battery status, and a summarized mineral anomaly index (0–100%) to confirm operation to the surface vessel [INFERENCE].
5.  **Recovery Phase**: Upon command from surface acoustic trigger or auto-timer expiration, the MCU fires a MOSFET gate, sending high current through a sacrificial nickel-chromium burn-wire. The steel ballast weight drops off, positive buoyancy causes the node to float to the surface, and an onboard LED strobe / GPS beacon aids recovery [INFERENCE].
6.  **Data Ingestion & Mapping**: The recovered MicroSD card is plugged into the surface laptop. The HydroDetect Python/GIS engine imports CSV logs, merges drop coordinates with USBL/pressure depth, applies kriging spatial interpolation, and renders 3D heatmaps of metal deposit concentration [INFERENCE].

### Local Storage & Synchronization Strategy
1.  **High-Frequency Local Buffering**: Readings captured at 50 Hz are stored in an onboard RAM ring-buffer and flushed to the MicroSD card in 512-byte binary blocks every 5 seconds to minimize SD write wear and power consumption [INFERENCE].
2.  **Zero-Network Seafloor Operation**: The OBN functions entirely offline without surface connectivity. All data is written to redundant local storage (MicroSD + onboard Flash EEPROM backup for critical settings) [INFERENCE].
3.  **Data Extraction Protocol**:
    *   *Primary*: Physical extraction of MicroSD card upon lander recovery [INFERENCE].
    *   *Secondary (Backup)*: USB-C waterproof port on top cap allowing fast offload without opening the pressure hull [INFERENCE].
4.  **Acoustic Low-Bandwidth Compression**: For live status updates, 32 bytes of status data are compressed into a 12-byte payload using delta-encoding, transmitting node status over the 20 kHz acoustic link [INFERENCE].
