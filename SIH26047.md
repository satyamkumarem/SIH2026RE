# 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
The Indian healthcare system suffers from extreme outpatient department (OPD) congestion in government tertiary centers, registering 4,000 to 10,000 patients daily [PS]. This high volume restricts doctor-patient consultation times to an average of 2 to 5 minutes [PS] (with primary care consultations averaging ~2 minutes as documented in BMJ Open 2017) [RESEARCHED]. Within this brief window, clinicians must take a comprehensive history, conduct physical examinations, review scattered physical records, and formulate diagnostic and treatment plans [PS]. The problem is compounded in Ayurvedic institutions (such as the All India Institute of Ayurveda), where clinical history requires extensive assessment across Trividha, Ashtavidha, and Dashavidha Pariksha frameworks [PS]. Furthermore, historical patient data remains trapped in unstructured, handwritten paper records [PS]. 

**MediKiosk** is a multimodal, multilingual, patient-facing digital intake and document digitization software platform designed to run on self-service hospital kiosks and tablets [PS]. Using Indian-language Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR via Bhashini/AI4Bharat), a constrained clinical dialogue engine, high-accuracy handwritten medical OCR, and ABDM FHIR R4 interoperability, MediKiosk captures structured medical histories and digitizes prior paper records before the patient enters the consultation room [PS]. This transforms unstructured intake into a physician-ready clinical summary, restoring the consultation room for examination and clinical reasoning [PS].

### Fundamental System Objective
To provide an accessible, multimodal, voice-and-touch AI platform that empowers low-literacy and non-tech-savvy patients to independently record structured clinical histories and digitize legacy health records prior to consultation, generating FHIR R4-compliant, physician-ready summaries linked to ABHA [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Acoustic Environment (60–80 dB Noise):** Government hospital OPD waiting halls are extremely loud due to thousands of shouting patients, public address announcements, and crowd movement [INFERENCE]. Microphones without directional beamforming or noise-cancellation hardware will produce high Word Error Rates (WER) [INFERENCE].
2. **Connectivity & Network Latency:** Public hospital Wi-Fi and mobile networks in India experience severe congestion and frequent dropouts [KNOWN]. The platform must operate with local edge fallback and robust retry queues rather than depending on uninterrupted high-bandwidth cloud connections [INFERENCE].
3. **Digital Literacy & User Cognition:** Over 60% of public hospital OPD attendees in rural/semi-urban India have low text literacy or high digital apprehension [INFERENCE]. The UI cannot present dense text screens, drop-down menus, or complex navigation [PS].
4. **Hardware Hygiene & Throughput:** Touchscreens in public OPDs face hundreds of interactions hourly [INFERENCE]. Physical terminals require ruggedized, washdown-capable IP65 front glass, non-mechanical physical document feed slots, or overhead camera scanners [INFERENCE].
5. **Operating System & Hardware Cost Constraints:** Hospitals cannot deploy high-end GPU workstations at every registration desk [INFERENCE]. The software stack must run on affordable industrial Android/Linux kiosks or mini-PCs (e.g., Intel NUC or ARM SBCs) with central server offloading [INFERENCE].
6. **Linguistic Diversity:** A single OPD in a major metro or national apex institute (like AIIA Delhi) sees patients speaking Hindi, Punjabi, Bengali, Bhojpuri, Haryanvi, and South Indian languages [KNOWN].

### Approach 1: Pure Rule-Based Touchscreen Kiosk
* **Mechanism:** Static, decision-tree driven touchscreen interface with pre-translated audio prompts.
* **Pros:** Highly predictable; runs on low-cost hardware; 100% offline; zero clinical hallucination risk [INFERENCE].
* **Cons:** Rigid interaction; cannot handle free-form spoken chief complaints; fails for patients unable to navigate menus [INFERENCE].
* **Feasibility:** High for hackathon, but fails explicit PS requirement for natural voice conversation [PS].

### Approach 2: Pure Generative LLM Voice Agent (Cloud-First)
* **Mechanism:** End-to-end cloud voice pipeline streaming audio to Whisper/Bhashini and passing transcripts to an unconstrained LLM (e.g., GPT-4o / Claude) for diagnostic dialogue.
* **Pros:** Highly conversational; natural language understanding [INFERENCE].
* **Cons:** High latency over hospital Wi-Fi; expensive API costs; risk of clinical hallucination or off-topic dialogue loops; high risk of failing during peak network outages [INFERENCE].
* **Feasibility:** Vulnerable to network dropouts and hallucination risks in clinical triage [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Hybrid Multimodal Architecture (Constrained Dialogue Manager + Edge OCR + Bhashini ASR/TTS) -- RECOMMENDED
* **Mechanism:** A hybrid system utilizing local edge processing for touch UI and state management, Bhashini streaming ASR/TTS for regional Indian voice processing, a deterministic Finite State Machine (FSM) guardrailing a lightweight Clinical Micro-LLM for history extraction, and an edge-optimized Vision OCR pipeline.
* **Pros:** Combines natural voice flexibility with strict clinical guardrails; dual-mode voice/touch redundancy; robust offline local fallback for touch intake; zero hallucination on critical red-flag rules [INFERENCE].
* **Cons:** Requires careful software integration between state machine and voice engine [INFERENCE].
* **Feasibility:** Optimal balance for hackathon demonstration and production deployment [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The **Hybrid Multimodal Architecture (Approach 3)** is selected [INFERENCE].

### Justification
1. **Compliance with Explicit PS Requirements:** Fully satisfies voice + touch duality (`REQ-08`), Indian language ASR (`REQ-05`), and low-literacy audio guidance (`REQ-06`) [PS].
2. **Clinical Safety & Zero Hallucination:** By anchoring the dialogue engine to a deterministic Finite State Machine (FSM) constrained by standard SOCRATES and Dashavidha decision trees, the system eliminates LLM hallucination risks during critical triage [INFERENCE].
3. **Resilience to Hospital Realities:** If cloud network connectivity fluctuates, the kiosk gracefully degrades to local touchscreen + pre-recorded voice prompt mode without shutting down intake [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 (Static Touch Kiosk)** was rejected because it violates explicit requirements for natural voice interaction and conversational SOCRATES symptom probing [PS].
* **Approach 2 (Pure Cloud LLM Voice Agent)** was rejected due to high network vulnerability, latency spikes in crowded hospitals, high API operating costs, and unacceptable clinical hallucination risks during red-flag detection [INFERENCE].

### End-to-End Data Flow Narrative

1. **Step 1: Patient Authentication & Consent (Identify):**
   * Patient approaches kiosk, taps screen or speaks. Audio prompt plays in selected language.
   * Patient enters ABHA ID / Aadhaar number or scans ABHA QR code. System verifies via ABDM M1 API.
   * Kiosk plays audio-guided DPDP consent explanation; patient taps "Agree" or speaks confirmation.
2. **Step 2: Conversational Multimodal Intake (Converse):**
   * Kiosk asks chief complaint in selected language (e.g., *"Aapko kya takleef hai?"*).
   * Patient speaks naturally or selects icon. Audio is captured, filtered via WebRTC VAD/Noise cancellation, and streamed to Bhashini ASR.
   * Text intent is passed to FSM Dialogue Manager. If chief complaint is "Chest Pain", SOCRATES protocol activates (asking onset, radiation, intensity).
   * Parallel AYUSH intake executes if in Ayurvedic OPD (capturing Agni, Koshtha, Prakriti markers).
   * Red-Flag Engine continuously scans responses. If acute emergency symptoms (e.g., chest pain radiating to arm with dyspnea) are detected, an immediate priority alert bypasses the queue and notifies nursing staff.
3. **Step 3: Document Upload & Intelligence Processing (Scan):**
   * Patient feeds physical paper records (prescriptions, lab reports) into scanner slot.
   * Document AI pipeline executes OCR (PaddleOCR for printed, TrOCR fine-tuned for handwritten notes).
   * Medical NER identifies diagnoses, medications, and lab values, matching them against LOINC and reference ranges. Out-of-range values and drug interaction risks are flagged.
4. **Step 4: Summary Synthesis & ABDM/HIS Routing (Summarize & Route):**
   * Summarizer Engine synthesizes conversational history and OCR entities into a dual-format JSON payload.
   * Generator emits: (a) Local language audio summary played to patient for verbal confirmation; (b) Standard English/Hindi clinical draft mapped into an NRCeS FHIR R4 OPConsultRecord Bundle.
   * Payload is pushed over secure ABDM Gateway to hospital HIS and linked to patient's ABHA account.
   * Kiosk purges all ephemeral session data from memory and prints/displays consultation queue token.
5. **Step 5: Physician Review & Consultation (Consult):**
   * Patient enters consultation room. Physician screen automatically displays structured intake summary upon token selection.
   * Physician reviews structured HPI, Dashavidha parameters, digitized timeline, flagged lab outliers, and drug warnings in <15 seconds.
   * Physician edits/confirms draft, adds physical exam notes, and saves directly to HIS/EMR.

### Detailed AI Component Analysis

#### 1. Bhashini Speech Recognition (ASR)
* **Purpose:** Converts spoken patient responses into structured text transcripts across Indian languages [PS].
* **Model Type:** IndicConformer / Streaming ASR [RESEARCHED].
* **Input:** Raw PCM audio stream (16kHz) from kiosk microphone array.
* **Output:** Local language text string with confidence scores.
* **Why AI:** Rule-based audio matching cannot handle unpredictable speech patterns, multi-accent variations, or free-form narration [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** Word Error Rate (WER) and Character Error Rate (CER) target <15% in 65 dB noise background [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode & Human Oversight:** If ASR confidence drops below 0.60, kiosk automatically switches UI to icon-driven touchscreen selection mode [INFERENCE].

#### 2. Clinical History Dialogue Engine & FSM
* **Purpose:** Dynamically probes symptoms using SOCRATES and Dashavidha frameworks [PS].
* **Model Type:** Finite-State Machine (FSM) guardrailed by a quantized Intent Classification Model [INFERENCE].
* **Input:** Patient transcript text + current conversation state.
* **Output:** Next structured question + voice prompt ID + UI option buttons.
* **Why AI:** Static questionnaires are rigid, whereas intent-driven FSM permits natural language answers while retaining deterministic diagnostic pathways [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** Intent classification F1 score > 0.90; zero invalid state transitions.
* **Failure Mode:** If intent is unrecognized, system plays audio clarification prompt: *"Did you mean pain started today or earlier?"* with on-screen touch buttons [INFERENCE].

#### 3. Medical Document Intelligence (OCR + NER)
* **Purpose:** Extracts diagnoses, drugs, dosages, and lab test values from paper records [PS].
* **Model Type:** PaddleOCR (printed) + TrOCR Transformer (handwritten) + BioBERT/Clinical NER [RESEARCHED].
* **Input:** High-resolution document image scan (300 DPI).
* **Output:** Chronologically organized JSON schema containing mapped drugs (RxNorm/SNOMED) and lab values (LOINC).
* **Why AI:** Rule-based template matching fails across heterogeneous, non-standard handwritten Indian prescriptions [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** Precision/Recall on clinical entity extraction > 85%.
* **Failure Mode:** Low-confidence OCR extractions are displayed in yellow on physician dashboard with original image side-by-side for manual verification [INFERENCE].

#### 4. Structured Clinical Summarizer & Red-Flag Classifier
* **Purpose:** Synthesizes intake history into standard HPI summary and flags emergencies [PS].
* **Model Type:** Med-Gemma-2B / Llama-3-8B Fine-Tuned + Regex Emergency Filter [INFERENCE].
* **Input:** Full interview transcript + parsed OCR JSON payload.
* **Output:** Structured draft clinical summary + Red-Flag triage priority tag.
* **Why AI:** Summarizing unstructured dialogue into standard clinical headers requires natural language generation [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation:** ROUGE-L score against expert clinician summaries; 0% false negatives on critical red-flag rules.
* **Failure Mode:** Physician retains 100% edit control. System presents summary as a draft requiring explicit click-to-confirm [PS].

### Where AI MUST NOT Be Used
1. **Red-Flag Emergency Priority Logic:** Initial triage detection must utilize deterministic keyword/regex threshold rules (e.g., "chest pain" AND "shortness of breath") rather than probabilistic LLM outputs to guarantee zero non-deterministic triage drops [INFERENCE].
2. **ABHA Authentication & OTP Verification:** Cryptographic identity verification must execute strictly through deterministic security protocols [PS].
3. **FHIR R4 Bundle Validation:** Mappings to NRCeS FHIR schemas must follow strict deterministic serialization to prevent invalid API payload rejections [RESEARCHED].

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Database Schema (PostgreSQL / JSON Engine)

```sql
-- Ephemeral Kiosk Session Table (Cleared post-submission)
CREATE TABLE kiosk_sessions (
    session_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    kiosk_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    abha_number VARCHAR(17),
    selected_language VARCHAR(10) DEFAULT 'hi',
    consent_granted BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,
    consent_timestamp TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE,
    current_state VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    is_red_flag BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    created_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT NOW()
);

-- Clinical History Intake Responses
CREATE TABLE clinical_responses (
    response_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    session_id UUID REFERENCES kiosk_sessions(session_id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    question_key VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL, -- e.g., 'CHIEF_COMPLAINT', 'SOCRATES_ONSET'
    raw_audio_path VARCHAR(255),
    transcript_text TEXT,
    structured_value JSONB, -- Mapped SNOMED / NAMASTE code
    input_mode VARCHAR(10) CHECK (input_mode IN ('VOICE', 'TOUCH', 'HYBRID'))
);

-- Extracted Document Intelligence Records
CREATE TABLE digitized_documents (
    doc_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    session_id UUID REFERENCES kiosk_sessions(session_id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    document_type VARCHAR(50), -- 'PRESCRIPTION', 'LAB_REPORT', 'DISCHARGE_SUMMARY'
    doc_date DATE,
    raw_ocr_text TEXT,
    extracted_entities JSONB, -- {medications: [], diagnoses: [], lab_results: []}
    abnormal_flags JSONB
);

-- Formatted Physician Summary (Pushed to HIS)
CREATE TABLE clinical_summaries (
    summary_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    session_id UUID REFERENCES kiosk_sessions(session_id),
    patient_abha VARCHAR(17) NOT NULL,
    chief_complaint TEXT NOT NULL,
    hpi_structured JSONB NOT NULL,
    ayush_dashavidha JSONB, -- Dashavidha Pariksha object
    past_history JSONB,
    timeline_summary JSONB,
    fhir_bundle_json JSONB NOT NULL,
    physician_status VARCHAR(20) DEFAULT 'DRAFT' -- 'DRAFT', 'ACCEPTED', 'AMENDED'
);
```

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

1. **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 Compliance:**
   * **Data Minimization:** Kiosk collects only data necessary for the upcoming clinical encounter [PS].
   * **Explicit Audio Consent:** Low-literacy patients receive audio explanations of data usage before intake begins [PS].
   * **Ephemeral Session Purge:** Immediate wiping of voice recordings, temporary document scans, and PII memory buffer as soon as session completes or timeouts occur [PS].
2. **ABDM Consent Framework & Identity Security:**
   * Patient authentication via ABHA ID / Aadhaar OTP or demographic validation [PS].
   * OAuth 2.0 authorization tokens utilized for all ABDM Gateway API requests [RESEARCHED].
3. **Data Encryption & Cryptographic Safeguards:**
   * **In Transit:** Mandatory TLS 1.3 encryption for all internal server-kiosk communications [INFERENCE].
   * **At Rest:** AES-256 bit encryption applied to any temporary disk buffer on the kiosk or hospital server [INFERENCE].
4. **Access Control & Governance:**
   * Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) ensuring only authenticated attending OPD physicians assigned to the patient's queue can view clinical summaries [INFERENCE].
5. **Medical Liability Disclaimer:**
   * Software explicitly operates as a *draft intake assistant*, never providing autonomous clinical diagnoses [PS].
   * Physician screen explicitly requires human verification and acceptance before saving to the official EMR [PS].
