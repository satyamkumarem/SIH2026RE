## Problem Statement SIH26043: Digital Platform for Crowdsourcing Societal Challenges and Collaborative Problem Solving

---

## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
The state of Jharkhand faces diverse local challenges across rural and urban domains, including water management, sanitation, rural livelihoods, agriculture, and public service delivery [PS]. While citizens, Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs), and Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) are the first to identify these ground-level issues, India currently lacks a structured, AI-driven mechanism to convert crowdsourced community grievances into structured research problems for Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) and co-funded innovation projects with industry/MSMEs [PS, RESEARCHED]. This dossier details the architecture, strategy, and execution plan for the **Societal Innovation Collaboration Portal (SICP)**—a multi-tenant, offline-first, AI-assisted platform designed for the Department of Higher & Technical Education, Government of Jharkhand [PS]. SICP seamlessly connects community problem crowdsourcing with university research teams, industry mentors, and state government monitoring analytics [PS].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The explicit problem statement supplied by the Department of Higher & Technical Education, Government of Jharkhand, is broken down into atomic requirements below [PS]:

### Explicit Requirement (a): Multi-Stakeholder Problem Submission
* **Requirement**: Allow citizens, community organizations, Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs), Urban Local Bodies (ULBs), and government departments to submit societal challenges through an intuitive web and mobile interface [PS].
* **Source Text**: *"Allowing citizens, community organizations, local bodies, and government agencies to submit societal challenges through an intuitive web and mobile interface, supported by photographs, videos, location details, and relevant documents."* [PS]
* **Interpretation**: The ingestion engine must support multi-channel inputs (web application + progressive mobile application) with multimedia attachments (JPEG, MP4, PDF) and automatic GPS location geotagging [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Implementation Implication**: Frontend requires offline multimedia caching, EXIF location extraction, audio/voice-to-text input for low-literacy users, and multipart file upload pipelines [INFERENCE].
* **Confidence Tag**: `[PS]`

### Explicit Requirement (b): AI-Enabled Categorization, Deduplication, & Routing
* **Requirement**: Automatically categorize submitted problems across thematic domains, prioritize them, eliminate duplicate submissions, and route validated challenges to appropriate universities [PS].
* **Source Text**: *"Automatically categorizing submitted problems based on thematic domains such as education, agriculture, healthcare, water resources, environment, energy, urban development, accessibility, public administration, and rural livelihoods using AI-enabled classification techniques... prioritizing, deduplication, and routing validated challenges to appropriate universities based on subject expertise and institutional capabilities."* [PS]
* **Interpretation**: An AI pipeline must classify submissions into 10+ specific domains, generate semantic embeddings to detect duplicate complaints within geographic radii, rank severity/urgency, and map problems to institutional capability matrices [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Implementation Implication**: Fine-tuned NLP text classification model (e.g., IndicBERT / DistilBERT) [ASSUMPTION], vector similarity search (e.g., Pgvector / FAISS) for deduplication [ASSUMPTION], and a capability matching engine comparing problem tags against university department profiles [INFERENCE].
* **Confidence Tag**: `[PS]`

### Explicit Requirement (c): University Collaboration & Multidisciplinary Team Formation
* **Requirement**: Enable Higher Education Institutions (HEIs) to evaluate assigned challenges, form multidisciplinary student and faculty teams, and submit research or solution proposals [PS].
* **Source Text**: *"Routing validated problem statements to appropriate universities... Enabling universities to evaluate submitted challenges, constitute multidisciplinary student and faculty teams, and prepare solution proposals or research projects."* [PS]
* **Interpretation**: Universities need a dedicated portal workspace with role-based access for Nodal Officers, Department Heads, Faculty Mentors, and Students to review routed problems, assemble cross-departmental teams, and draft structured proposals [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Implementation Implication**: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) supporting multi-tier academic hierarchies, collaborative proposal editors, and credit-linking workflows compliant with NEP 2020 [INFERENCE].
* **Confidence Tag**: `[PS]`

### Explicit Requirement (d): Industry, MSME, Startup, & CSR Co-Creation Module
* **Requirement**: Facilitate partnerships between universities and external industry entities, startups, MSMEs, CSR units, and research labs for mentorship, funding, prototyping, testing, and deployment [PS].
* **Source Text**: *"Facilitating collaboration between universities and industry partners, startups, MSMEs, CSR organizations, research laboratories, and innovation ecosystems for mentorship, funding, prototyping, testing and deployment of solutions."* [PS]
* **Interpretation**: Industry partners need a marketplace view to explore academic proposals, offer co-funding or CSR grants, assign industry mentors, and sign digital non-disclosure agreements or technology transfer agreements [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Implementation Implication**: Pitch deck showcase, industry sponsorship bidding/matching module, escrow/milestone fund tracking, and intellectual property agreement management [INFERENCE].
* **Confidence Tag**: `[PS]`

### Explicit Requirement (e): End-to-End Workflow & Project Lifecycle Management (PLM)
* **Requirement**: Provide comprehensive project lifecycle management covering problem review, institutional allocation, milestone tracking, documentation, testing, and solution validation [PS].
* **Source Text**: *"Providing workflow management for problem review, institutional allocation, project monitoring, stakeholder communication, milestone tracking, and solution validation... project lifecycle management system for monitoring milestones, deliverables, approvals, documentation, testing outcomes, intellectual property generation, and implementation status."* [PS]
* **Interpretation**: System must track a state machine from state `SUBMITTED` -> `VALIDATED` -> `ROUTED` -> `PROPOSAL_APPROVED` -> `DEVELOPMENT` -> `PILOT_TESTING` -> `DEPLOYED` [INFERENCE].

* **Source Text**: *"Generating dashboards and analytics for government departments to monitor the number of challenges received, domain-wise distribution, institutional participation, industry engagement, project progress, and measurable social outcomes."* [PS]
* **Interpretation**: Government officials (State Higher Education Department, District Magistrates) require high-level aggregated visual dashboards with district-level GIS mapping, patent trackers, and social impact metrics [PS, INFERENCE].

### Explicit Requirement (g): Multi-Stakeholder Notification & Communication Engine

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

### Regional & Geographical Context
* Jharkhand has 24 districts with diverse topography ranging from the Chota Nagpur Plateau to dense forested and tribal rural belts (e.g., West Singhbhum, Latehar, Santhal Parganas) [KNOWN, RESEARCHED].
* Rural digital penetration is uneven; while urban centers like Ranchi and Jamshedpur have robust 4G/5G, interior Gram Panchayats often experience spotty 2G/3G connectivity [KNOWN, INFERENCE].

### Connectivity & Offline Operation
* **Reality**: Field workers, Gram Pradhans, and rural citizens cannot rely on continuous high-speed internet [INFERENCE].
* **Design Mandate**: The mobile application/PWA must support complete **offline-first data capture** using IndexedDB [ASSUMPTION]. Photos, voice notes, and GPS coordinates must queue locally and automatically sync when connectivity is restored [INFERENCE].

### Device Hardware & Literacy Constraints
* **Citizen Hardware**: Basic Android smartphones (2GB RAM, low storage) [INFERENCE].
* **College Hardware**: Standard desktop PCs in computer labs with variable internet bandwith [INFERENCE].
* **Language & Literacy**: High prevalence of regional vernacular languages (Hindi, Santali, Ho, Mundari, Kurukh) [KNOWN, RESEARCHED].
* **Design Mandate**: Interface must support voice-guided inputs, minimal text typing, high-contrast UI, and vernacular audio prompts [INFERENCE].

### Governance & Academic Realities
* Jharkhand's Higher Education Gross Enrollment Ratio (GER) stands at **17.0%** compared to the national average of 27.3% (AISHE 2021-22) [RESEARCHED - Source: Govt of Jharkhand Higher & Technical Education Dept] [3.1, 3.4].
* Higher Educational Institutions (HEIs) in Jharkhand include 9 state public universities (e.g., Ranchi University, Vinoba Bhave University, Jharkhand University of Technology) [RESEARCHED - Source: Chancellor Portal Jharkhand] [1.2, 1.3].
* The system must align with NEP 2020 guidelines regarding credit accumulation via the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) and National Credit Framework (NCrF) to incentivize student participation [RESEARCHED - Source: UGC / PIB] [3.6, 3.11].

2. **Deduplication Thresholds**: What semantic similarity threshold (Cosine/Euclidean) balances merging identical local issues (e.g., two citizens reporting the same broken check dam) without grouping distinct issues from adjacent villages? [INFERENCE]
3. **University Routing Optimization**: What scoring algorithm fairly distributes problem statements across state universities while matching specialized faculty expertise and incubation center infrastructure? [INFERENCE]
4. **Industry & CSR Engagement**: What tax/regulatory incentives or institutional recognition will motivate MSMEs and industrial houses in Jharkhand (e.g., steel, mining, manufacturing) to co-fund student projects on this platform? [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE]
5. **IP Rights Ownership**: Who owns the Intellectual Property (IP) generated when a student team solves a crowdsourced challenge using industry mentorship and state university infrastructure? [INFERENCE]

### Approach 1: Monolithic Web Portal with Rule-Based Routing
* **Mechanism**: A centralized web application where citizens fill a standard text form. Routing to universities is handled via hardcoded category dropdowns and manual administrative assignment [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data**: Basic text submissions, manual dropdown selections, static university mapping tables [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: PHP/Laravel monolith with MySQL database hosted on a single VM [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Cost**: Very low complexity; minimal initial cost [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability & Reliability**: 100% deterministic rule matching [INFERENCE].
* **Limitations**: Fails on low literacy (no audio/photo deduplication), high administrative burden on government staff, no offline mobile support, zero AI intelligence [INFERENCE].
* **Feasibility**: High for Hackathon; Very Low for Production scale in Jharkhand [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Mobile-First PWA + Classical ML (TF-IDF + SVM) Routing Engine
* **Mechanism**: Progressive Web App (PWA) allowing photo/text submission. Uses TF-IDF vectorization and Support Vector Machines (SVM) for category prediction. Uses simple geographic distance matching to assign problems to the nearest university [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data**: Pre-categorized grievance text datasets, latitude/longitude boundary matrices [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: Node.js REST API backend, React frontend, Scikit-Learn Python microservice, PostgreSQL DB [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Cost**: Moderate complexity; moderate cost [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability**: High (SVM feature weights can be inspected) [INFERENCE].
* **Limitations**: Weak text classification on non-standard Hinglish/vernacular text; cannot detect duplicate photos/audio; naive geographic routing ignores university specialization [INFERENCE].
* **Feasibility**: Moderate for Hackathon; Sub-optimal for Production [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3 (RECOMMENDED): Offline-First Microservices PWA + Hybrid AI Engine (IndicBERT + Pgvector Deduplication + Graph/Matrix Matching Engine)
* **Mechanism**: Multi-tenant Progressive Web App supporting offline audio/photo capture. AI pipeline uses fine-tuned **IndicBERT** for multilingual domain classification, **Pgvector cosine similarity** on sentence embeddings for semantic deduplication, and a **Multi-Criteria Decision Matrix (MCDM)** matching algorithm balancing institutional expertise, faculty ratings, and proximity [ASSUMPTION]. Complete Project Lifecycle Management (PLM) engine with industry marketplace and executive GIS analytics [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Required Data**: Multilingual problem text/audio, vector embeddings, spatial GeoJSON, university capability matrices, industry CSR profiles [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: React / Next.js PWA frontend, Python FastAPI AI microservice, Node.js workflow backend, PostgreSQL + Pgvector DB, Redis cache, Object Storage (S3/MinIO) [ASSUMPTION].
* **Complexity & Cost**: High technical capability, but highly optimized for open-source stack (zero commercial license fees) [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability**: AI classification provides confidence scores; matching matrix outputs transparent factor weights [INFERENCE].
* **Scalability & Reliability**: Horizontal scaling via Docker containers; asynchronous job queues (Celery/Redis) handle media processing without lag [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: High (Core modules demonstrable in 48 hours using lightweight pre-trained models) [ASSUMPTION].
* **Production Feasibility**: Exceptionally high, directly satisfying all implicit and explicit requirements of SIH26043 [PS, INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selected Solution
**Approach 3: Offline-First Microservices PWA with Hybrid AI Engine & Lifecycle Workflow System** [ASSUMPTION].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
1. **Approach 1 (Monolithic Rule-Based) Rejected Because**:
   * It relies on manual government categorization and routing, creating a massive bottleneck when thousands of complaints arrive annually [PS, INFERENCE].
   * It excludes low-literacy rural citizens who cannot type detailed formal text complaints in English/Hindi [INFERENCE].
   * It lacks automated deduplication, leading to university fatigue from multiple reports of identical regional issues [INFERENCE].

2. **Approach 2 (Classical ML) Rejected Because**:
   * TF-IDF models break down when dealing with colloquial Hindi, code-switched text (Hinglish), and local Jharkhand vernacular phrasing [INFERENCE].
   * Distance-only geographic routing routes agricultural issues to specialized urban engineering colleges simply because they are geographically closer [INFERENCE].

### Why Approach 3 Wins
* ** Solves Low-Literacy & Connectivity Realities**: Offline PWA with voice recording and camera integration allows a Gram Panchayat worker or citizen to submit an issue in 30 seconds [INFERENCE].
* ** Eliminates Redundancy**: AI semantic deduplication automatically merges similar issues reported across neighboring villages into a single consolidated regional challenge [PS, INFERENCE].
* ** Smart Academic Matching**: Ensures a water contamination problem in West Singhbhum is routed to the Environmental Engineering department at Jharkhand University of Technology or NIT Jamshedpur based on proven research capability rather than random allocation [PS, INFERENCE].
* ** Complete Incentive Alignment**: Integrates NEP 2020 credit tracking for students, CSR deployment tracking for industry, and governance analytics for the Department of Higher & Technical Education [PS, RESEARCHED].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Submission Phase**:
   * A citizen or Gram Panchayat official opens the SICP PWA on a smartphone.
   * If offline, the app captures a voice note, photo, and device GPS coordinates, storing them in browser `IndexedDB`.
   * When network connectivity is established, the service worker pushes the queued payload to the `/api/v1/challenges/submit` endpoint [INFERENCE].

2. **AI Processing & Ingestion Phase**:
   * The backend receives the multipart request and forwards audio to the Python AI service.
   * **Whisper / Bhashini API** transcribes regional speech to text [ASSUMPTION].
   * **IndicBERT** analyzes the text and assigns probability scores across 10 domains (e.g., Water Resources: 0.92, Agriculture: 0.08) [ASSUMPTION].
   * The text is converted into a 384-dimensional vector embedding using `Sentence-Transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2)` [ASSUMPTION].
   * **Pgvector / PostGIS** performs a spatial-semantic search: checks if an issue in the same domain exists within a 5 km radius with >0.82 cosine similarity [ASSUMPTION].
   * If a duplicate is found, the new submission is linked as a "Supporting Report" to increment the existing problem's **Priority Score** [INFERENCE]. If unique, a new `Master_Problem` record is initialized [INFERENCE].

3. **Validation & Routing Phase**:
   * District/Department Nodal Officers receive a notification to perform a 1-click administrative validation [INFERENCE].
   * Upon validation, the **Institutional Matching Engine** calculates a composite match score for state universities based on:
     $$\text{Match Score} = (0.4 \times \text{Domain Expertise}) + (0.3 \times \text{Incubation/IIC Rating}) + (0.3 \times \text{Geographic Proximity})$$ [INFERENCE]
   * The problem is routed to the Top-3 matching universities' Nodal Officer dashboards [INFERENCE].

4. **Academic Proposal & Team Formation Phase**:
   * University Nodal Officer accepts the challenge and assigns a Lead Faculty Mentor [INFERENCE].
   * Faculty Mentor forms a multidisciplinary team of 3–6 students across relevant departments [INFERENCE].
   * The team drafts a structured Solution Proposal (Objectives, Methodology, Budget, Timeline) on the portal and submits it [PS, INFERENCE].

5. **Industry Co-Development & Funding Phase**:
   * The approved university proposal appears on the **Industry Marketplace** [PS].
   * CSR managers or MSME owners review the pitch deck and commit mentorship, equipment, or co-funding [PS].
   * Digital MoU / Agreement templates are signed on-platform [INFERENCE].

6. **Lifecycle Execution & Deployment Phase**:
   * Team executes project milestones (Prototype -> Lab Testing -> Field Pilot) [PS].
   * Each milestone requires uploading evidence (test reports, photos, citizen feedback) [PS].
   * Upon successful field pilot validation by local PRI/ULB, the project is marked `DEPLOYED` [INFERENCE].
   * Student credit points are pushed to the Academic Bank of Credits (ABC) portal, and results update the State Visual Analytics Dashboard [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE] [3.11].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    AI / ML PIPELINE MATRIX                                       |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
| AI Component      | Underlying Model           | Input Data            | Core Function           |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
| 1. Categorizer    | Fine-tuned IndicBERT       | Vernacular text/audio | Predict 10 domains      |
| 2. Deduplicator   | SBERT + Pgvector           | 384-d vector embedding| Cosine similarity search|
| 3. Prioritizer    | Multi-Factor Scorer        | Upvotes, age, spatial | Rank urgency (1-100)    |
| 4. Matchmaker     | Multi-Criteria Matrix      | Domain + Capability   | Rank Top-3 Universities |
+-------------------+----------------------------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
```

### Component 1: Multilingual Problem Categorizer
* **Purpose**: Automatically classify raw, unstructured crowdsourced complaints into 10 thematic domains (education, agriculture, healthcare, water, environment, energy, urban dev, accessibility, public admin, rural livelihoods) [PS].
* **Model Type**: Fine-tuned **IndicBERT** (multilingual Transformer model trained on 12 Indian languages) [ASSUMPTION].
* **Input**: Transcribed text string (Hindi / English / Hinglish) [INFERENCE].
* **Output**: Primary domain classification label + softmax confidence score (0.0 to 1.0) [INFERENCE].
* **Data Required**: ~2,000 labeled samples of Indian municipal and rural complaints [ASSUMPTION].
* **Why AI Is Necessary**: Citizens use informal, non-standard language (e.g., *"hamare gaon ke talab ka paani peene layak nahi hai, ganda badboo aa raha hai"*). Hardcoded keywords miss context; NLP understands semantic intent (Water Resources / Sanitation) [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation Metric**: Macro F1-Score (Target: >0.85) [ASSUMPTION].
* **Failure Mode & Oversight**: If top prediction confidence is <0.60, the system flags the issue as `NEEDS_MANUAL_REVIEW` on the Nodal Officer dashboard [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Semantic Problem Deduplicator
* **Purpose**: Identify whether a newly submitted challenge is a duplicate of an existing active challenge in the same geographical area [PS].
* **Model Type**: **Sentence-Transformers (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`)** producing 384-dimensional dense vector embeddings, indexed via **Pgvector** HNSW [ASSUMPTION].
* **Input**: New problem description text + GPS coordinates [INFERENCE].
* **Output**: Array of matched duplicate problem IDs with similarity scores [INFERENCE].
* **Data Required**: Vectorized database of existing active problem statements [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI Is Necessary**: Two citizens describe the same broken culvert differently: *"naala tut gaya hai"* vs *"bridge damaged near main road"*. Keyword matching fails; vector cosine similarity identifies semantic equivalence [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation Metric**: Cosine Similarity Threshold (Set at 0.82) [ASSUMPTION].
* **Failure Mode & Oversight**: Citizens can click "Not a Duplicate - Submit as New Issue" if the system incorrectly links distinct local problems [INFERENCE].

### Component 3: Problem Prioritization Scorer
* **Purpose**: Rank validated problem statements by social urgency to ensure high-impact issues receive immediate university attention [PS].
* **Model Type**: Hybrid Heuristic & Gradient Boosting Scorer [ASSUMPTION].
* **Input**: Citizen upvotes/supporting reports ($N$), days pending ($T$), affected population size ($P$), keyword severity flags (e.g., "outbreak", "contamination", "flood") [INFERENCE].
* **Output**: Priority Score (Integer from 1 to 100) [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI/ML Is Preferable**: Multi-variable weighting prevents older minor complaints from burying critical public health emergencies [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode & Oversight**: District Magistrates can manually override priority scores to `URGENT` [INFERENCE].

### Component 4: University & Faculty Institutional Matchmaker
* **Purpose**: Route validated challenges to appropriate universities based on academic disciplines, research expertise, innovation centers, and faculty specialization [PS].
* **Model Type**: Multi-Criteria Decision Matrix (MCDM) with Vector Matching [INFERENCE].
* **Input**: Problem domain vector + location vs. University department taxonomy vectors, NIRF/IIC ranking, active faculty research keywords, current student capacity [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Output**: Ranked list of Top 3 recommended universities with match percentage [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI Is Preferable**: Ensures optimal resource utilization across 9 state universities without bias [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode & Oversight**: State Higher Education Department retains manual re-assignment controls [INFERENCE].

###  EXPLICIT NEGATIVE SPECIFICATION: Where AI MUST NOT Be Used
1. **Final Institutional Allocation Approvals**: AI suggests top matches, but human Nodal Officers must click "Accept Allocation" [INFERENCE].
2. **Monetary Grant & CSR Disbursement**: Fund releases must rely strictly on verified milestone sign-offs, never on automated AI predictions [INFERENCE].
3. **Intellectual Property (IP) Transfer Approval**: Legal contracts and IP rights assignments must be reviewed and signed by university/industry legal heads [INFERENCE].

### Conflict Handling & Retry Strategy
* **Conflict Resolution**: Client payload includes a local timestamp `client_submitted_at` and UUID. If the server receives duplicate UUID payloads, it idempotently returns the existing `challenge_id` [INFERENCE].

* **Bandwidth Optimization**: Mobile images automatically compressed to WebP (max 1280x720, 80% quality) on the client side before upload; audio compressed to AAC/Opus [INFERENCE].
