## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Industrial facilities (petrochemical complexes, oil refineries, steel plants, power stations, mines, and LNG terminals) produce significant thermal signatures detectable by satellite sensors [PS]. However, satellite systems like NASA FIRMS raise generic "thermal anomaly" alerts without differentiating between routine industrial heat (e.g., gas flaring, kiln operation), accidental industrial fires/explosions, agricultural stubble burning, and wildfires [PS]. This creates a critical intelligence gap for monitoring national critical infrastructure and emergency response [INFERENCE]. This dossier presents **AGIS-FlareGuard** (Automated Geospatial Intelligence System for Thermal Source Classification), an end-to-end geospatial platform that ingests real-time NASA FIRMS hotspot feeds, cross-references them against OpenStreetMap (OSM) industrial footprints and ESA WorldCover land use maps, extracts multi-spectral reflectance signatures (Sentinel-2 SWIR B11/B12), and applies a hybrid Machine Learning & Spatio-Temporal Temporal-Persistence model to segregate industrial fires from routine operational heat and natural fires [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

To build an automated, real-time geospatial intelligence system that ingests NASA FIRMS thermal detections, cross-analyzes them with OSM industrial infrastructure boundaries and Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery, and uses AI/ML to classify thermal anomalies into industrial fires, persistent operational heat, wildfires, and agricultural burns, presenting actionable layers on a GIS dashboard [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Satellite Sensor Resolution & Swath Overpass Times [KNOWN]:**
  * VIIRS (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21) provides 375m spatial resolution with ~2 daytime and ~2 nighttime overpasses per day per satellite [RESEARCHED].
  * MODIS (Terra/Aqua) provides 1km spatial resolution [RESEARCHED].
  * Sentinel-2 (MSI) offers 10m-20m resolution but has a 5-day revisit cycle (or 2-3 days with both 2A and 2B operational) [RESEARCHED].
  * **Constraint Implication:** Real-time continuous thermal monitoring from space is constrained by satellite revisit intervals (~3 to 6 hours between VIIRS passes). The system must highlight "time of observation" and cannot claim instantaneous zero-latency fire detection without geostationary data (e.g., INSAT-3D/3DR, which have lower thermal resolution) [INFERENCE].
* **Cloud Cover & Atmospheric Interference [KNOWN]:**
  * Heavy cloud cover, monsoonal storms, and dense smoke obscure optical and thermal sensors (3.9µm / 11µm / SWIR), causing missing detections or attenuated Brightness Temperatures [KNOWN].
  * **Constraint Implication:** The system must incorporate cloud-mask metadata (`confidence` score in FIRMS, Sentinel-2 Scene Classification Layer - SCL) and flag obstructed regions [INFERENCE].
* **Positional Smear & Pixel Center Offset [RESEARCHED]:**
  * A VIIRS hotspot coordinate represents the center of a 375m x 375m pixel (which broadens up to ~800m off-nadir) [RESEARCHED].
  * **Constraint Implication:** Direct point-in-polygon checks against OSM plant outlines will fail if a flare stack is on the edge of a facility. Spatial buffering (e.g., 500m to 1km radius around industrial centroids) is mandatory [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **OpenStreetMap Data Completeness in Developing Regions [RESEARCHED]:**
  * While major Indian refineries and thermal power plants are mapped in OSM, small-scale chemical plants or illegal industrial units in rural/peri-urban belts may lack mapped boundaries (`landuse=industrial`) [INFERENCE].
  * **Constraint Implication:** The system must fall back to land-cover classification (built-up vs cropland vs forest) and multi-year historical thermal persistence analysis when OSM metadata is absent [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 6. Research Questions

1. **How can we mathematically distinguish between a routine gas flare and an accidental industrial explosion using VIIRS data?**
   * *Investigation focus:* Evaluate Fire Radiative Power (FRP), Brightness Temperature $T_{i4}$ (3.74µm), and $T_{i5}$ (11.45µm) variance against a 90-day rolling baseline for that specific location [INFERENCE].
2. **What OSM tags comprehensively capture industrial thermal emitters across India?**
   * *Investigation focus:* Standardize Overpass queries for `industrial=*`, `landuse=industrial`, `man_made=works`, `man_made=flare`, `power=plant`, `building=industrial` [RESEARCHED].
3. **What is the optimal satellite imagery source for verifying active industrial fires when Sentinel-2 revisit is unavailable?**
   * *Investigation focus:* Evaluate Landsat 8/9 OLI/TIRS (100m thermal, 30m SWIR) and PlanetScope (commercial, out-of-scope for raw hackathon feed, but valid as fallback) [RESEARCHED].
4. **How do we handle false positives caused by solar glint off metal roofs of industrial sheds?**
   * *Investigation focus:* Analyze FIRMS solar zenith angle attributes, daytime vs nighttime pass flags, and SWIR Band 11/12 ratios (solar glint reflects strongly in NIR/SWIR1, whereas high-temperature combustion spikes exponentially in SWIR2 Band 12) [RESEARCHED].

## 8. Existing Solutions & Gap Analysis

### 1. NASA FIRMS (Fire Information for Resource Management System)
* **Description:** Global active fire monitoring platform operated by NASA LANCE using MODIS and VIIRS satellite instruments [RESEARCHED].
* **Strengths:** Low-latency NRT ingestion (~3 hours from satellite pass), global coverage, free access, reliable FRP calculations [RESEARCHED].
* **Weaknesses:** No contextual awareness. Marks every thermal anomaly as a generic "hotspot". Does not distinguish between a forest fire, a crop stubble burn, an oil refinery flare, or an accidental industrial explosion [PS].
* **Relevance:** Core upstream data provider for our system [PS].
* **Remaining Gap:** Lack of automated spatial attribution, land-use contextualization, and machine learning classification [INFERENCE].
* **Source:** `firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov` [PS] | **Confidence:** [PS]

### 2. NOAA Earth Observation Group (EOG) - VIIRS Nightfire (VNF)
* **Description:** Specialized processing algorithm developed by Colorado School of Mines / NOAA for detecting combustion sources at night using multi-spectral VIIRS bands [RESEARCHED].
* **Strengths:** Fits Planck curves to NIR/SWIR/MWIR/LWIR bands to estimate source temperature (Kelvin) and surface area; highly effective at identifying persistent gas flares (>1600 K) [RESEARCHED].
* **Weaknesses:** Nighttime observations only; requires raw VIIRS sensor data processing (large bandwidth/compute); no real-time emergency alerting dashboard for daytime accidental fires [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
* **Relevance:** Provides theoretical foundation for operational flare temperature thresholds [RESEARCHED].
* **Remaining Gap:** Operational real-time daytime + nighttime pipeline for accidental industrial fire alerts [INFERENCE].
* **Source:** `eogdata.mines.edu/products/vnf/` [RESEARCHED] | **Confidence:** [RESEARCHED]

### 3. Global Forest Watch (GFW) Fires
* **Description:** Online platform monitoring forest fires and land clearance using satellite data [KNOWN].
* **Strengths:** Excellent UI for deforestation and forest loss analytics [KNOWN].
* **Weaknesses:** Focused strictly on forestry and conservation; ignores heavy industry, petrochemical complexes, and industrial safety compliance [INFERENCE].
* **Relevance:** Example of specialized geospatial alerting UX [INFERENCE].
* **Remaining Gap:** Total lack of industrial domain intelligence, plant layout integration, and hazard buffer assessment [INFERENCE].
* **Source:** `fires.globalforestwatch.org` [KNOWN] | **Confidence:** [KNOWN]

### Approach 1: Spatial Buffer & Rule-Based Heuristic System
* **Mechanism:** Ingest FIRMS hotspots. Perform a PostGIS spatial lookup (`ST_DWithin` 500m) against OSM industrial polygons and ESA WorldCover rasters. If point falls in `landuse=industrial` or near `man_made=flare`, classify as Industrial. Else, if landcover = `cropland`, classify as Agricultural; if landcover = `tree_cover`, classify as Wildfire.
* **Pros:** Extremely fast to implement; lightweight compute requirements; 100% explainable rules.
* **Cons:** High false positive/negative rate. Cannot distinguish an *accidental industrial explosion* from a *routine operational gas flare* because both fall inside the same industrial polygon [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Spatio-Temporal Persistence + XGBoost Hybrid Machine Learning System (RECOMMENDED)
* **Mechanism:**
  1. Ingest FIRMS hotspots.
  2. Compute **Spatio-Temporal Persistence Features**: Cluster historical FIRMS points (last 180 days) using HDBSCAN. Calculate recurrence frequency, average FRP baseline, temperature variance, and active days ratio.
  3. Compute **Spatial Context Features**: Distance to nearest OSM industrial facility, distance to forest boundary, ESA WorldCover class probability.
  4. Compute **Spectral Features**: Sample Sentinel-2 SWIR B11/B12 reflectances and Normalized Burn Ratio 2 ($NBR2 = (B11-B12)/(B11+B12)$) [RESEARCHED].
  5. Feed tabular feature vector into an **XGBoost Classifier** that outputs probabilities across 5 classes: `Accidental Industrial Fire`, `Persistent Operational Heat/Flare`, `Wildfire`, `Agricultural Stubble Burning`, `Mining/Quarry Activity`.
* **Pros:** Accurately isolates accidental industrial fires by detecting sudden FRP/brightness temperature spikes over the historical operational baseline [INFERENCE]. Highly scalable, fast inference, robust under sparse satellite coverage.
* **Cons:** Requires engineering clean feature pipelines and constructing a labeled training dataset.

### Approach 3: End-to-End Multimodal Deep Learning (Swin Transformer + Sentinel-2 SWIR Vision)
* **Mechanism:** Directly download Sentinel-2 multi-spectral image patches (12 bands x 128x128 pixels) around every FIRMS detection. Feed image patches into a Vision Transformer (Swin-S) combined with a temporal LSTM processing historical thermal time series.
* **Pros:** Learns rich visual spatial context (smoke plume shapes, facility layouts, surrounding vegetation burn scars).
* **Cons:** Downloading high-resolution Sentinel-2 GeoTIFF rasters in real-time during live inference creates massive network bottlenecks and latency (several gigabytes per batch); heavy GPU dependencies; prone to failure during a live hackathon demo if cloud quotas or satellite APIs stall [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection: Approach 2 (Spatio-Temporal Persistence + XGBoost Hybrid System)

#### Why Alternatives Were Rejected:
* **Approach 1 (Rule-Based Only)** was rejected because it fails the core objective of the prompt: segregating *industrial fires* from *persistent industrial operational sources* [PS]. A static spatial rule marks every flare stack as an industrial fire, generating hundreds of false alarms per day for industrial security operators [INFERENCE].
* **Approach 3 (End-to-End Deep Multimodal Vision)** was rejected due to hackathon feasibility and network latency risks [INFERENCE]. Downloading full 12-band Sentinel-2 L2A granules for every hotspot in real-time introduces 30-120 seconds of latency per alert, violating near-real-time operational monitoring requirements [INFERENCE].

#### Victory Justification for Approach 2:
Approach 2 achieves the optimal balance between high classification precision and real-time inference speed [INFERENCE]. By decoupling vector feature extraction (FIRMS properties + PostGIS OSM distance + 180-day persistence metrics) from heavy raster downloads, the backend can classify thousands of global thermal anomalies in **<2 seconds** [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION]. Sentinel-2 SWIR imagery is fetched asynchronously on-demand only when a user selects a specific high-priority alert on the frontend GIS dashboard, ensuring seamless UX and rock-solid demo reliability [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

### Data-Flow Narrative Step-by-Step

1. **Ingestion:** Every 30-60 minutes, the `FIRMS Stream Ingester` worker issues an HTTP GET request to the NASA FIRMS API (`/api/area/csv/MAP_KEY/VIIRS_SNPP_NRT/sub-area`) covering the target region (e.g., India bounding box: `68.0, 6.0, 97.0, 37.0`) [RESEARCHED].
2. **Geospatial Cross-Referencing:** Incoming hotspots (latitude, longitude, brightness $T_{i4}$, FRP, acq_date, acq_time) are inserted into PostGIS. A spatial query checks:
   * Distance to nearest OSM industrial boundary (`ST_Distance`).
   * Underlying land cover classification code from ESA WorldCover raster layer (e.g., Class 50 = Built-up, Class 10 = Tree cover, Class 40 = Cropland) [RESEARCHED].
3. **Persistence Lookup:** The engine queries TimescaleDB for historical hotspots within a 500-meter radius over the prior 180 days [INFERENCE].
   * If $\ge 15$ historical detections exist in the past 180 days with consistent FRP $\rightarrow$ Marked as **High Persistence (Operational Source)** [INFERENCE].
   * If $0-2$ historical detections exist, but current FRP is $5\times$ higher than surrounding average $\rightarrow$ Flagged as **Thermal Anomaly Spike** [INFERENCE].
4. **ML Inference:** The engineered feature vector is passed to the trained XGBoost model. The model computes softmax probabilities. If classified as `Accidental Industrial Fire` with confidence $>0.85$, an emergency event record is created [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
5. **Asynchronous Imagery Verification:** For high-confidence industrial fire alerts, a background worker queries the Copernicus STAC API for the latest clear Sentinel-2 scene, extracts SWIR Band 11/12 tiles, and attaches false-color infrared thumbnail overlays to the alert record [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
6. **Visualization & Alerting:** The Next.js frontend updates via WebSockets. Mapbox GL JS renders the active hotspot vector tile with color-coded classification icons (e.g., Red Flashing Flame = Industrial Fire; Orange Diamond = Gas Flare; Green Leaf = Wildfire; Yellow Stubble = Agricultural) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

```
                                ├── Class 4: Agricultural Stubble Burn (Probability)
                                └── Class 5: Mining / Quarry Activity (Probability)
                                │
                                ▼
                   [ Rule-Based Anomaly Guard ]
          IF Class == Industrial AND FRP > (Baseline_Mean + 3.0 * Baseline_Std)
          --> TRIGGER PRIORITY EMERGENCY ALERT [RED]
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### AI Component Details

#### 1. Purpose & Model Type
* **Model:** Multi-Class XGBoost Gradient Boosted Classifier + Z-Score Anomaly Detector [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Purpose:** Segregate thermal anomalies into 5 distinct operational and natural fire classes [PS].

#### 2. Input & Output
* **Input Features (14 Dimensions):** `bright_ti4` (K), `bright_ti5` (K), `frp` (MW), `scan`, `track`, `daynight` (0/1), `dist_to_osm_industrial_meters`, `osm_feature_type_code`, `esa_landcover_class`, `persistence_30d_count`, `persistence_180d_count`, `frp_historical_mean`, `frp_historical_std`, `frp_zscore` [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Output:** 5-class softmax probability distribution + Alert Severity Tag (`CRITICAL`, `ROUTINE`, `ENVIRONMENTAL`) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

#### 3. Why Simpler Rules Fail
Rule-based distance checks fail because industrial complexes contain *both* routine thermal sources (flare stacks, power plant cooling/boilers) and potential accidental fires [INFERENCE]. A rule saying "if inside industrial polygon = industrial" cannot detect an accidental explosion at a refinery storage tank 200m away from the flare stack [INFERENCE]. The ML model evaluates the **FRP Z-score anomaly** relative to historical thermal baselines at that exact lat/long [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

#### 4. Training Data & Fine-Tuning
* **Data:** 15,000 historical FIRMS hotspot records over South Asia annotated with ground-truth land-use data and historical industrial accident reports (e.g., Vizag gas leak/fire, Baghjan blowout, forest fire archives from FSI, agricultural burning logs from Punjab/Haryana) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Fine-tuning:** Hyperparameter tuning via Optuna (optimizing log-loss and multi-class F1-score) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

#### 5. Evaluation Metrics
* **Target Metrics:** Macro F1-score $> 0.90$; Precision on `Accidental Industrial Fire` $> 0.95$ (minimizing false alarms for emergency responders) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

#### 6. Failure Modes & Human Oversight
* **Failure Mode:** False Positive caused by a high-intensity flare stack coming online after maintenance (triggering an FRP spike).
* **Mitigation / Human Oversight:** The platform includes an "Analyst Verification Workflow". Alerts flagged as `Accidental Industrial Fire` are marked as `PENDING VERIFICATION` until an operator clicks to inspect high-resolution Sentinel-2 SWIR imagery or confirms with local facility contacts [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

#### 7. Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **DO NOT use Large Language Models (LLMs) or generative vision models for the core spatial classification task.** Core thermal physics and spatial proximity are best modeled by deterministic spatial math and gradient boosting [INFERENCE]. LLMs should only be used as a secondary UX assistant to generate automated text summaries of incident reports [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

```sql
    frp NUMERIC(8,2),            -- Fire Radiative Power (MW)
    confidence VARCHAR(20),       -- 'low', 'nominal', 'high'
    satellite VARCHAR(20),        -- 'Suomi NPP', 'NOAA-20', 'NOAA-21'
    daynight CHAR(1),            -- 'D', 'N'
    -- Processed Features & Classification Results
    dist_to_industrial_m NUMERIC(10,2),
    esa_landcover_code INT,
    persistence_score INT,
    classified_category VARCHAR(50), -- 'Industrial Fire', 'Operational Flare', 'Wildfire', etc.
    classification_confidence NUMERIC(4,3),
    PRIMARY KEY (hotspot_id, acquisition_timestamp)
);

-- Convert to TimescaleDB Hypertable partitioned by time
SELECT create_hypertable('thermal_hotspots', 'acquisition_timestamp');
CREATE INDEX idx_hotspots_location ON thermal_hotspots USING GIST(location);

-- 3. Facility FRP Thermal Baselines
CREATE TABLE facility_thermal_baselines (
    facility_id INT REFERENCES industrial_facilities(facility_id),
    avg_frp_mw NUMERIC(8,2),
    std_frp_mw NUMERIC(8,2),
    historical_hotspot_count INT,
    last_updated TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    PRIMARY KEY (facility_id)
);
```

### Low-Network & Offline Field Strategy
* **Vector Tile Caching:** The frontend client caches Mapbox Vector Tiles (MVT) for key industrial zones in IndexedDB using Service Workers [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Bandwidth Reduction:** Rather than streaming heavy GeoTIFF imagery to field teams, the API transmits compressed Protobuf vector records containing only point coordinates, category tags, and FRP metrics (~2 KB per alert vs 15 MB for image rasters) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Offline Alert Queue:** When deployed on low-connectivity field tablets, incoming SMS alerts are parsed locally via an Android PWA background receiver [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
