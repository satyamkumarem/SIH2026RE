## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Executive Summary
The AI-Powered Email Threat Detection, GeoLocation and Forensic Intelligence Platform is a multi-layered cybersecurity and digital forensics solution engineered for educational institutions, government departments, banking, and enterprise email environments `[INFERENCE]`. Standard email security gateways primarily focus on filtering or blocking malicious messages based on static rules, signatures, or basic domain reputation `[PS]`. They frequently fail against sophisticated threats such as Business Email Compromise (BEC), AI-generated social engineering, display-name spoofing, obfuscated URLs, and complex SMTP relay chains `[PS]`. Furthermore, standard solutions lack post-detection forensic tracing and origin correlation capabilities `[PS]`. 

This platform addresses these critical deficiencies by integrating Natural Language Processing (NLP) content classification, deep RFC 5322 header parsing, protocol alignment validation (SPF/DKIM/DMARC/ARC), IP reputation and geolocation mapping, WHOIS/RDAP domain intelligence, and graph-based threat correlation `[INFERENCE]`. The platform processes raw email streams (`.eml`/`.msg`), extracts indicators of compromise (IOCs), reconstructs transmission paths, estimates the origin's geographic location, and generates legal-grade forensic reports with cryptographic chain-of-custody tracking `[INFERENCE]`.

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The problem statement provided by AICTE Cyber Security Cell is deconstructed into atomic requirements preserving explicit wording and structural integrity `[PS]`:

### Requirement Category A: Fraudulent Email Detection Engine
* **a.1 [PS]**: NLP-based analysis of email subject lines, body text, urgency cues, impersonation language, and social engineering patterns.
* **a.2 [PS]**: Detection of phishing indicators such as spoofed sender addresses, deceptive domains, suspicious attachments, malicious links, and obfuscated URLs.
* **a.3 [PS]**: AI/ML models to classify emails as legitimate, suspicious, impersonated, phishing, or fraud-related.
* **a.4 [PS]**: Identification of Business Email Compromise (BEC) patterns such as payment diversion, fake invoice requests, credential harvesting attempts, and executive impersonation.

### Requirement Category B: Email Header and Protocol Analysis Module
* **b.1 [PS]**: Deep analysis of email headers including `Return-Path`, `Received` headers, `Message-ID`, `Reply-To`, DKIM signatures, SPF alignment, and DMARC status.
* **b.2 [PS]**: Detection of anomalies in mail routing, forged sender fields, relay manipulation, and spoofed transmission records.
* **b.3 [PS]**: Validation of whether the email was sent through authorized infrastructure or suspicious relay paths.

### Requirement Category C: Origin Traceability and Location Analysis
* **c.1 [PS]**: Extraction of originating IP addresses from header chains and identification of the earliest reliable sending node.
* **c.2 [PS]**: IP geolocation mapping to estimate the likely country, region, city, ISP, hosting provider, or proxy service associated with the email source.
* **c.3 [PS]**: Correlation with VPN, TOR, open relay, botnet, or cloud-hosted infrastructure indicators where applicable.
* **c.4 [PS]**: Domain intelligence analysis using WHOIS/RDAP data, DNS records, MX records, hosting fingerprints, and registrar details to identify suspicious sender infrastructure.

### Requirement Category D: Identity Correlation and Attribution Support
* **d.1 [PS]**: Correlation of email indicators with known threat intelligence, blacklists, previous incidents, domain clusters, and repeated fraud campaigns.
* **d.2 [PS]**: Graph-based relationship analysis between sender domains, IP addresses, aliases, reply chains, and linked infrastructure.
* **d.3 [PS]**: Confidence-based investigative assessment to assist in revealing probable sender identity, associated infrastructure, or campaign-level attribution patterns.
* **d.4 [PS]**: Support for flagging whether the email likely originated from a compromised account, spoofed domain, anonymized infrastructure, or direct malicious actor environment.

### Requirement Category E: Alerting, Dashboard, and Forensic Reporting
* **e.1 [PS]**: Real-time alerts for high-risk emails before user interaction or administrative approval.
* **e.2 [PS]**: Analyst dashboard showing fraud score, spoofing indicators, sender trace path, geolocation map, and attribution confidence.
* **e.3 [PS]**: Generation of structured forensic reports for institutional action, legal review, cyber incident response, and support to law enforcement agencies (LEAs).
* **e.4 [PS]**: Searchable case management view for grouping related fraudulent emails into campaigns.

### Requirement Category F: Privacy, Legal, and Compliance Safeguards
* **f.1 [PS]**: Controlled handling of personal data and metadata in accordance with organizational privacy policies.
* **f.2 [PS]**: Logging, evidence preservation, and chain-of-custody support for investigation purposes.
* **f.3 [PS]**: Configurable retention and masking mechanisms for sensitive communication data.

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

### Operational & Infrastructure Realities in India
* **Institutional Heterogeneity**: Educational institutions (AICTE colleges) and government offices run varied email setups—ranging from cloud-hosted Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 to legacy on-premise Postfix, Sendmail, Exim, or NIC (National Informatics Centre) mail infrastructure `[KNOWN]`.
* **High Email Throughput**: Medium-to-large academic campuses process between 50,000 to 500,000 emails daily `[INFERENCE]`. Processing must not introduce discernible latency into delivery streams (<500ms overhead per email) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Network & API Rate Limits**: Public threat intelligence APIs (e.g., AbuseIPDB, VirusTotal) impose strict rate limits on free tiers `[KNOWN]`. Production systems must use local caching (Redis) and fallback offline databases (MaxMind GeoLite2 local `.mmdb`) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Legal & Regulatory Compliance**: Indian cybersecurity initiatives operate under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP) 2023 `[KNOWN]`. Email body logging must strictly handle personally identifiable information (PII). In digital forensics, electronic evidence must satisfy Section 63B of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023 (formerly Section 65B Indian Evidence Act) for court admissibility `[KNOWN]`.
* **Header Manipulation & Anonymization Realities**: Threat actors frequently route messages through open relays, compromised SOCKS proxies, or TOR exit nodes `[KNOWN]`. Headers can be forged up to the first mail transfer agent (MTA) controlled by the receiving domain `[KNOWN]`.

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Monolithic Heuristic & Rule-Based Gateway with Static API Enrichments
* **Mechanism**: Python-based script utilizing regular expressions for body analysis, combined with standard library header parsing and direct REST API calls to external services (AbuseIPDB, WHOIS) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Problem Fit**: Low. Handles standard headers but fails against modern AI-generated social engineering `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Accuracy Potential**: Low to Moderate (High false positives on text heuristics) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Data Requirements**: Minimal local storage; relies entirely on live external APIs `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Complexity**: Low.
* **Explainability**: High (Direct rule matching).
* **Cost**: Low initial cost, but vulnerable to API rate limit overages `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Scalability**: Poor. Synchronous API lookups block processing queues `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: Extremely High.
* **Production Feasibility**: Unviable due to latency and low detection capability against BEC `[INFERENCE]`.

### Approach 2: Deep Learning Pure Transformer Architecture
* **Mechanism**: Heavy Transformer-based LLM (e.g., Llama-3-8B / Mistral) processing entire email texts and header blocks as raw tokens in a single prompt pipeline `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Problem Fit**: Moderate. High text understanding, but poor exact protocol logic execution (LLMs hallucinate SPF/DKIM alignment rules) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Accuracy Potential**: High for text, unreliable for cryptographic header verification `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Data Requirements**: High computational infrastructure (GPUs) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Complexity**: High.
* **Explainability**: Low ("Black box" neural outputs without precise RFC verification breakdowns) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Cost**: Very High (Requires dedicated GPU instances) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Scalability**: Low throughput without expensive cluster scaling `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: Low to Moderate (GPU resource limits during live demo) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Production Feasibility**: Low due to high latency and hardware costs `[INFERENCE]`.

### Approach 3 (Recommended Hybrid Approach): Multi-Layered Asynchronous Microservice Architecture
* **Mechanism**: Combines a fast-path RFC 5322 header parser, fine-tuned lightweight NLP model (DeBERTa-v3-small for body/subject analysis), local GeoIP database lookups, async Redis threat intelligence enrichment workers, and a Neo4j graph database for attribution correlation `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Problem Fit**: Ideal. Delivers exact protocol validation via deterministic code, high NLP precision via transformer embeddings, fast response times via caching, and campaign attribution via graph queries `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Accuracy Potential**: Very High (>98% detection across headers and content) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Data Requirements**: Moderately structured (Local MaxMind DB, pre-trained NLP model weights, Redis cache, PostgreSQL metadata DB) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Complexity**: Moderate.
* **Explainability**: High. Each layer returns explicit sub-scores (Header Score, Auth Score, Content Score, Geo/IP Score) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Cost**: Low to Moderate (Runs efficiently on standard CPU nodes) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Scalability**: Excellent. Microservices scale horizontally; Redis decouples slow API enrichments from main mail flow `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: High (Feasible build for a 4-6 student team) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Production Feasibility**: High (Enterprise-ready architecture) `[INFERENCE]`.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 3: Multi-Layered Asynchronous Microservice Architecture** is selected as the optimal architecture `[INFERENCE]`.

### Justification & Rejection of Alternatives
1. **Why Approach 1 was rejected**: Pure rule-based heuristics cannot reliably detect modern Business Email Compromise (BEC) and AI-generated phishing messages, which lack traditional spam keywords or blacklisted URLs `[PS]`. Furthermore, synchronous API calls create massive bottlenecking in email queues `[INFERENCE]`.
2. **Why Approach 2 was rejected**: Relying purely on Large Language Models to evaluate technical headers is fundamentally flawed. LLMs are non-deterministic and prone to hallucinations when calculating domain alignment, IP netmask matching, or DKIM signature verification `[INFERENCE]`. Additionally, LLM inference introduces intolerable latency and excessive operational costs `[INFERENCE]`.
3. **Why Approach 3 wins**: It strictly separates **deterministic tasks** (RFC header parsing, cryptographic DKIM check, SPF alignment, GeoIP table lookups) from **probabilistic tasks** (NLP intent analysis, urgency detection, graph campaign clustering) `[INFERENCE]`. This guarantees 100% precision on technical protocol checks while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on social engineering content, all within an asynchronous pipeline that maintains high throughput `[INFERENCE]`.

### Data-Flow Narrative
1. **Ingestion**: Raw emails arrive via direct file upload (`.eml`/`.msg`), an administrative REST API endpoint, or an inline SMTP proxy filter `[INFERENCE]`.
2. **Parsing & Queueing**: FastAPI validates the incoming payload, calculates an initial SHA-256 cryptographic hash of the raw message, stores the raw message in an S3-compatible object store (MinIO), and pushes an analysis job to Redis `[INFERENCE]`.
3. **Parallel Analysis Execution**:
   - **Header Worker**: Parses header fields, evaluates `Authentication-Results`, validates SPF alignment against `Return-Path`, checks DKIM signatures against DNS public keys, extracts the `Received:` chain, and filters internal/RFC-1918 IPs to locate the earliest reliable public sending IP `[RESEARCHED]`.
   - **NLP Worker**: Extracts plain text and HTML bodies, cleans HTML tags, passes text tokens to a quantized DeBERTa-v3 ONNX runtime engine to predict probabilities for phishing, BEC, and impersonation `[RESEARCHED]`.
   - **Enrichment Worker**: Queries the local MaxMind database for IP geolocation, checks AbuseIPDB for IP reputation scores, and performs RDAP queries to fetch domain creation age and registrar data `[RESEARCHED]`.
4. **Correlation & Scoring**: The Core Risk Engine aggregates sub-scores into an overall composite Risk Score (0-100). If the score exceeds defined thresholds, an alert is published. Concurrently, extracted entities (Sender Domain, Originating IP, Reply-To, Target) are written to Neo4j to update campaign graphs `[INFERENCE]`.
5. **Presentation & Action**: The analyst dashboard receives real-time updates via WebSockets. Investigators can view the hop-by-hop map, inspect domain alignment, explore the Neo4j campaign graph, or export a legally formatted PDF report `[INFERENCE]`.

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Components Utilizing AI/ML

#### 1. Social Engineering & Intent Classifier
* **Purpose**: Detect deceptive intent, urgency language, credential harvesting, and payment diversion requests within body and subject text `[PS]`.
* **Model Type**: Fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-small (quantized to ONNX format) `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Input**: Decoded plain text / cleaned HTML body and Subject string.
* **Output**: Probability scores across categories: `[Legitimate, Phishing, BEC_Payment_Diversion, Executive_Impersonation, Credential_Harvesting]`.
* **Required Data**: Balanced corpus of legitimate corporate emails and annotated phishing datasets (e.g., Nazario, Enron, Kaggle 2026 Corpus) `[2.1, 2.2]`.
* **Why AI is Preferable**: Static keyword lists (e.g., flagging "URGENT" or "WIRE TRANSFER") produce excessive false positives and fail against novel, AI-generated phrasing `[PS]`.
* **Evaluation**: F1-Score, Precision-Recall AUC on benchmark phishing evaluation sets `[2.7]`.
* **Failure Mode**: False Positives on urgent legitimate administrative notices. Mitigated by context-aware thresholding and white-listing known internal senders `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Human Oversight**: High-risk BEC alerts require analyst confirmation before outbound payment processing rules are triggered `[INFERENCE]`.

#### 2. Domain Homoglyph & Display-Name Impersonation Detector
* **Purpose**: Identify lookalike domains (e.g., `aiicte-india.org` vs `aicte-india.org`) and display name spoofing (e.g., claiming to be "AICTE Chairman" from a generic Gmail address) `[PS]`.
* **Model Type**: String Distance Heuristics (Levenshtein Distance / Jaro-Winkler) + Character Homoglyph Mapping `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Input**: `From:` header display name, envelope `From:` address, and institutional executive directory list.
* **Output**: Impersonation Confidence Index (0 - 1.0).
* **Required Data**: Institutional roster (names, designated titles, official email addresses).
* **Why AI/Heuristics Preferable**: Visual domain tricks (using Cyrillic characters) bypass standard domain blacklists `[RESEARCHED]`.
* **Evaluation**: Accuracy against synthesized homoglyph test sets.
* **Failure Mode**: Flagging external users with names identical to internal executives. Mitigated by checking external tag indicators `[INFERENCE]`.

### Explicit Exclusions: Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **Header Structure Validation**: Checking whether headers conform to RFC 5322 standard syntax must be executed via **deterministic parsing code**, not AI models `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Authentication Checks**: SPF pass/fail, DKIM signature verification, and DMARC alignment policy checks rely strictly on **cryptographic math and DNS record lookup logic**. AI must never be used to "predict" SPF/DKIM validity `[INFERENCE]`.
* **IP Geolocation**: Translating an IP address to geographic coordinates must be executed via **deterministic database index matching** (MaxMind GeoLite2), not AI inference `[INFERENCE]`.

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Database Schema (PostgreSQL Relational Core)

```sql
-- Table: raw_emails
CREATE TABLE raw_emails (
    email_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    sha256_hash VARCHAR(64) UNIQUE NOT NULL,
    received_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
    s3_object_key VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    sender_address VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,
    return_path VARCHAR(255),
    subject TEXT,
    overall_risk_score INT CHECK (overall_risk_score BETWEEN 0 AND 100),
    verdict VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL -- Legitimate, Suspicious, Phishing, BEC
);

-- Table: header_analysis
CREATE TABLE header_analysis (
    analysis_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    email_id UUID REFERENCES raw_emails(email_id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    spf_result VARCHAR(20), -- PASS, FAIL, NEUTRAL, NONE
    spf_alignment BOOLEAN,
    dkim_result VARCHAR(20), -- PASS, FAIL, NONE
    dkim_alignment BOOLEAN,
    dmarc_result VARCHAR(20), -- PASS, FAIL, NONE
    earliest_public_ip INET,
    hop_count INT,
    raw_headers JSONB NOT NULL
);

-- Table: ip_geolocation
CREATE TABLE ip_geolocation (
    ip_address INET PRIMARY KEY,
    country_code VARCHAR(5),
    country_name VARCHAR(100),
    city_name VARCHAR(100),
    latitude NUMERIC(8,5),
    longitude NUMERIC(8,5),
    isp VARCHAR(150),
    abuse_score INT DEFAULT 0,
    is_proxy_vpn_tor BOOLEAN DEFAULT FALSE,
    updated_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

### Graph Schema (Neo4j Entity Relationship Model)
* **Nodes**:
  * `:Email {id, subject, timestamp, risk_score}`
  * `:Domain {name, creation_date, registrar}`
  * `:IPAddress {ip, country, abuse_score, is_vpn}`
  * `:SenderIdentity {email_address, display_name}`
* **Relationships**:
  * `(:Email)-[:ORIGINATED_FROM]->(:IPAddress)`
  * `(:Email)-[:CLAIMS_SENDER]->(:SenderIdentity)`
  * `(:SenderIdentity)-[:BELONGS_TO_DOMAIN]->(:Domain)`
  * `(:IPAddress)-[:BELONGS_TO_ASN]->(:ASN)`

### Offline & Low-Network Resiliency
* **Local Caching Layer**: All WHOIS/RDAP queries and IP reputation scores are cached in Redis with a 7-day Time-To-Live (TTL) to minimize external network requests `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Offline GeoIP Operation**: Geolocation lookups utilize a locally hosted MaxMind `.mmdb` database file updated monthly. The core trace mapping operates entirely offline without requiring internet access `[1.1, 3.2]`.
* **Queue Buffering**: In the event of internet connectivity failure during external threat intel checks, Celery workers automatically retry API enrichments with exponential backoff while allowing local header and NLP analysis to complete instantly `[INFERENCE]`.

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

### Personal & Sensitive Data Handling (DPDP Act 2023 Compliance)
* **Email Body Masking**: Before storing email body content in persistent databases, an automated PII anonymization engine uses regular expressions and Named Entity Recognition (NER) to mask sensitive data (e.g., Aadhaar numbers, PAN numbers, bank account numbers, phone numbers) `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Configurable Retention**: System administrators can set data retention policies (e.g., purge benign email bodies after 7 days; retain high-risk forensic logs for 90 days) `[PS]`.

### Evidentiary Standards & Chain of Custody (BSA 2023 Section 63B)
* **Cryptographic Hashing**: Upon ingestion, the exact raw message file (`.eml`) is hashed using SHA-256 `[INFERENCE]`. The calculated hash is stored immutably in PostgreSQL and embedded into generated forensic reports `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Audit Logging**: Every action performed on an email investigation (viewing, re-scoring, exporting report, flagging) is logged in an append-only audit trail containing analyst UUID, UTC timestamp, and source IP `[PS]`.

### Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
* **Tier 1 SOC Analyst**: View dashboard, inspect risk scores, read masked email bodies, re-assign cases `[INFERENCE]`.
* **Tier 2 Forensic Lead**: Unmask PII for authorized active legal investigations, export signed PDF forensic reports, modify platform scoring thresholds `[INFERENCE]`.
* **System Administrator**: Manage system integrations, API keys, retention schedules, and user roles `[INFERENCE]`.
