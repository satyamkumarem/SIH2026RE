## 1. Executive Snapshot

This dossier presents an end-to-end technical blueprint for **SIH26174: AI Human Activity Recognition for On-board BAS Experiments**, sponsored by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). In microgravity environments on board the Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS) and future deep-space crewed missions, high-latency and restricted bandwidth to Earth prohibit streaming continuous high-definition video to ground control [PS]. The proposed solution is **AstraCheck-HAR**, an edge-native, offline AI assistance system that continuously monitors fixed-payload video feeds, tracks experimental protocols, validates step sequences via hand-object interaction (HOI) and orientation-agnostic 3D Human Mesh Recovery (HMR), provides real-time offline voice guidance and anomaly alerts, logs structured execution telemetry, streams local IP feeds, and renders a real-time monitoring dashboard—all operating locally on space-constrained edge compute hardware [PS].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The problem statement has been broken down into atomic, explicit requirements [PS]. Every explicit item from the official description and dataset notes is preserved without weakening or silent alteration [PS].

* **Requirement [a] - Offline Standalone Edge Operation:** System must process video feeds locally on edge hardware with zero dependency on cloud or ground control connectivity [PS].
* **Requirement [b] - Fixed-Payload Camera Input:** Ingestion must accept real-time video feeds from fixed cameras positioned around experimental payload racks [PS].
* **Requirement [c] - Custom Synthetic & Focused Dataset Generation:** The solution requires creating a focused local synthetic/replicated dataset (e.g., using webcams and physical prop boxes: outer container, red box, yellow box) for object detection, pose estimation, and hand-object interaction tracking [PS].
* **Requirement [d] - Real-Time Sequence Recognition & Tracking:** The AI model must recognize active human-object interaction steps and continuously track the progress of a pre-defined multi-step experiment [PS].
* **Requirement [e] - Predictive Next-Step Guidance:** At the start of the experiment and immediately after each completed step, the system must suggest the next step to be performed [PS].
* **Requirement [f] - Voice-Based Out-of-Sequence & Skip Alerts:** The system must trigger immediate voice alerts when an experiment step is skipped or performed out of sequence [PS].
* **Requirement [g] - Timestamped Structured Telemetry Logging:** System must continuously generate a lightweight, timestamped, structured text file (JSON/CSV) capturing executed steps, status, durations, and step outcomes [PS].
* **Requirement [h] - Local Storage & Dual-IP Streaming:** Video streams must be recorded to local edge storage while simultaneously streaming live feeds over a specific local IP address [PS].
* **Requirement [i] - Real-Time Graphical User Interface (GUI):** A local desktop/web GUI must display active step status, live video overlay, camera status, next-step recommendations, and log streams [PS].
* **Requirement [j] - Deliverable Model & Executable:** The final deliverable must be a trained offline AI model packaged into an offline standalone application [PS].
* **Requirement [k] - (Optional / Advanced) Orientation-Agnostic 3D Human Mesh Recovery (HMR):** The model should perform body tracking relative to the payload rack rather than a terrestrial ground/floor plane, accommodating microgravity 0G orientations where "up" and "down" do not exist [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Spatial & Orientation Realities (Microgravity):** In microgravity (0G), astronauts float at arbitrary angles (upside down, sideways) relative to cameras [PS, KNOWN]. Terrestrial pose estimators relying on vertical gravity vectors fail [PS]. Body posture tracking must be mapped relative to fixed payload rack reference frames [PS].
* **Network & Data Limitations:** LEO and space station communications suffer from bandwidth bottlenecks, intermittent line-of-sight contacts, and high latency [PS, KNOWN]. All AI processing, logging, and voice synthesis must occur completely air-gapped on the edge [PS].
* **Compute & Power Budgets:** On-board payload computers (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin Industrial or space-qualified embedded systems) operate under tight thermal and power caps (15W–40W) [KNOWN]. Models must run efficiently at 15–30 FPS on TensorRT/ONNX Runtime without consuming excessive VRAM [INFERENCE].
* **Optical & Environmental Variations:** Payload rack lighting can change due to orbital solar angles, cabin illumination adjustments, or physical self-shadowing by astronauts [INFERENCE]. Astronauts wear gloves that alter hand geometry, requiring robust hand-object contact detection [RESEARCHED].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach A: Pure Rule-Based Heuristic with 2D Bounding Box Overlap

* **Mechanism:** YOLOv11 detects hands and boxes. If bounding boxes overlap for $N$ consecutive frames, a step completion is registered.
* **Pros:** Extremely fast (<10 ms latency), zero GPU requirement, trivial implementation effort.
* **Cons:** Fails under occlusion; cannot verify hand posture or grasp state; highly sensitive to camera angle changes; zero orientation awareness.
* **Evaluation:** High hackathon feasibility, but fails the core requirement for robust protocol validation.

### Approach B: Hybrid Architecture – Edge YOLOv11 + Rack-Relative 3D Mesh Normalization + Hysteresis Finite State Automaton (RECOMMENDED)

* **Mechanism:** YOLOv11 detects payload objects and hands; a lightweight 3D HMR (e.g., HMR2.0-S / CLIFF) extracts 3D SMPL body joint coordinates; 3D coordinates are transformed relative to detected payload rack corner fiducials; a deterministic Finite State Automaton (FSA) validates step sequence transitions and triggers local offline voice synthesis.
* **Pros:** Highly robust to 0G rotation; low edge compute footprint (<30ms end-to-end latency); zero state hallucination due to deterministic FSA; fully offline executable.
* **Cons:** Requires initial synthetic dataset generation and ArUco/fiducial marker setup.
* **Evaluation:** Perfect fit for hackathon MVP and production deployment requirements.

### Approach C: End-to-End Multimodal Vision-Language Action Model (VLA / Edge VLM)

* **Mechanism:** A quantized local VLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL 2B or PaliGemma) ingests video clips and generates step prediction text directly.
* **Pros:** High flexibility; no manual state machine coding needed.
* **Cons:** High VRAM usage (>6GB); slow frame rate (<3 FPS on edge hardware); non-deterministic output risks false positive/negative protocol validations; high hallucination risk.
* **Evaluation:** Unsuitable for mission-critical space payload operations.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

**Selected Strategy:** **Approach B (Hybrid Architecture)** [ASSUMPTION].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected

* **Approach A Rejected:** Too fragile. Simple 2D bounding box overlap triggers massive false positives whenever an astronaut's hand passes *over* a box without actually picking it up or interacting with it [INFERENCE].
* **Approach C Rejected:** Edge VLMs are non-deterministic [KNOWN]. In space payload monitoring, hallucinating a step completion when an astronaut skipped a vital safety procedure can destroy an experiment or endanger personnel [INFERENCE]. Furthermore, VLMs cannot maintain 30 FPS processing on low-power edge platforms [KNOWN].

### Victory Factors of Approach B

1. **Deterministic Safety:** Combining perception (AI) with a rule-based State Machine (Deterministic Engine) guarantees zero hallucinated state transitions [INFERENCE].
2. **0G Rotation Invariance:** Transform matrix converts camera-space 3D coordinates to Rack-Space coordinates using 4 corner fiducial markers on the payload rack [INFERENCE].
3. **Edge Optimization:** Runs smoothly at 30 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin or standard laptop GPU using ONNX Runtime / TensorRT [INFERENCE].

### Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative

1. **Ingestion:** Fixed payload cameras capture 1080p video frames at 30 FPS and feed them into the Edge Perception pipeline [INFERENCE].
2. **Object & Hand Detection:** YOLOv11-Nano extracts 2D bounding boxes and confidence scores for `outer_box`, `lid_open`, `lid_closed`, `red_box`, `yellow_box`, `slot_A`, `slot_B`, and `astronaut_hand` [ASSUMPTION].
3. **0G Spatial Transformation:** ArUco markers placed on the payload rack frame define the 3D local coordinate system $(X_{rack}, Y_{rack}, Z_{rack})$. The predicted hand and body 3D keypoints are transformed into rack-relative space, eliminating camera/astronaut tilt orientation errors [INFERENCE].
4. **Interaction Distance & Contact Analysis:** Hand-object 3D spatial distance and IoU contact thresholds determine physical interaction states (`HAND_GRASP_RED_BOX`, `RED_BOX_IN_SLOT_A`, etc.) [INFERENCE].
5. **State Machine Validation:** The FSA compares detected interaction events against the pre-defined JSON experiment workflow [INFERENCE]:
   - If event matches expected active step: Advance state, set status `COMPLETED`, prompt next step via voice and GUI [INFERENCE].
   - If event violates sequence or skips a step: Retain active state, trigger instant offline voice alert (e.g., *"Warning: Step 2 skipped! Please place Red Box in Slot A before proceeding."*) [INFERENCE].
6. **Logging & Streaming:** The logging module writes structured telemetry to a local JSON file, while GStreamer encodes video to disk (`/data/video_archive.mp4`) and broadcasts an RTSP feed to `rtsp://192.168.1.100:8554/live` [ASSUMPTION].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### AI Component 1: YOLOv11 Object & Hand Detection Model

* **Purpose:** Detect astronaut hands, outer experiment box, box lid status, red box, yellow box, slot A, slot B [PS].
* **Model Type:** YOLOv11-Nano fine-tuned on custom experiment dataset [ASSUMPTION].
* **Input:** 1920x1080 / 640x640 RGB video frame [ASSUMPTION].
* **Output:** Bounding box coordinates $[x_1, y_1, x_2, y_2]$, class label, confidence score [KNOWN].
* **Data Required:** 1,500 annotated synthetic/replicated experiment images with varied lighting and roll/pitch rotations [ASSUMPTION].
* **Why AI is Necessary:** Rule-based color thresholding or fixed background subtraction fails under changing lighting, shadows, and floating hands [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation Metric:** mAP@0.5:0.95 > 0.88 [ASSUMPTION].
* **Failure Mode & Mitigation:** False detection due to glare/shadows $\rightarrow$ Hysteresis filter requires bounding box persistence across 5 consecutive frames before triggering state transition [INFERENCE].

### AI Component 2: Rack-Relative 3D Pose / Mesh Estimator

* **Purpose:** Track 3D body keypoints and hand vectors relative to payload rack fiducials, overcoming 0G orientation shifts [PS].
* **Model Type:** Lightweight 3D Pose Estimator (MediaPipe 3D World Landmarks / HMR2.0-Small) [RESEARCHED].
* **Input:** Cropped astronaut body/hand bounding box RGB frame [RESEARCHED].
* **Output:** 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$ for body joints and hand fingertips [KNOWN].
* **Data Required:** Pre-trained 3D body datasets + ArUco calibration matrices [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI is Necessary:** Analytical 2D projection cannot recover 3D depth or spatial orientation relative to arbitrary rack coordinates [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation Metric:** Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) < 45mm [RESEARCHED].
* **Failure Mode & Mitigation:** Full occlusion by astronaut back $\rightarrow$ System falls back to object state transition tracking until pose is re-established [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used

* **Sequence Logic & Error Verification MUST NOT use AI [INFERENCE].** Step sequence verification, skip detection, out-of-order detection, and next-step recommendations are governed strictly by a deterministic Finite State Automaton (FSA) [INFERENCE]. Relying on an LLM or AI model for rule verification risks hallucinating step completion and causing mission failure [INFERENCE].

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

* **Personal & Astronaut Data Protection:** Video footage contains biometrics of Vyomanauts [KNOWN]. In accordance with ISRO spaceflight governance, all processed video remains strictly on local space station storage; ground transmission is limited to lightweight encrypted JSON logs [INFERENCE].
* **Local Encryption:** Local video storage and SQLite telemetry files are encrypted using AES-256 with key storage bound to hardware TPM modules [ASSUMPTION].
* **Air-Gapped Security & Integrity:** System executable has zero listening network sockets exposed to public interfaces; local RTSP streaming is restricted to authenticated mission control IP subnets [INFERENCE].
* **System Misuse & False Alert Safety:** Voice alerts are advisory; astronauts retain manual override capability via physical rack switches or GUI controls to suppress alerts if an unscripted emergency step is required [INFERENCE].

3. **Microgravity 0G Simulation:**
   - To demonstrate **orientation-agnostic tracking**, the webcam will be mounted on a rotating arm or physically flipped upside down / 90 degrees sideways during live testing [ASSUMPTION].
   - ArUco corner markers on the experiment prop box will dynamically normalize the 3D frame, demonstrating that sequence validation remains 100% accurate regardless of camera tilt [INFERENCE].
4. **Live Sequence & Anomaly Simulation:**
   - *Phase 1 (Normal Execution):* Open box $\rightarrow$ Pick Red Box $\rightarrow$ Place Slot A $\rightarrow$ Pick Yellow Box $\rightarrow$ Place Slot B $\rightarrow$ Close Box. GUI shows green status, voice prompts next step [PS].

   - *Phase 3 (Out-of-Sequence Anomaly):* Attempt to close box while Yellow Box is outside. System alerts: *"Warning! Experiment incomplete. Yellow Box not placed in Slot B."* [PS].
5. **Network & Storage Verification:** Demonstrate live RTSP video playback on a second laptop connected over local Wi-Fi/LAN, and show real-time writing of `experiment_log.json` [ASSUMPTION].
