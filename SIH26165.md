## 1. Executive Snapshot

Oil India Limited (OIL) captures significant volumes of Unsafe Act / Unsafe Condition (UA/UC) observations, near-misses, and incident reports across onshore and offshore installations via its Health, Safety, Security, and Environment (HSSE) platform [PS]. However, these safety logs are currently triaged manually at periodic intervals (monthly or quarterly) [PS]. This batch-processing latency creates a critical safety vulnerability: high-energy hazards with fatal potential remain unaddressed until after manual review [INFERENCE]. Modern safety science (DEKRA Martin & Black 2015; EEI SIF Precursor Model; VelocityEHS 2024 PSIF Classifier) proves that non-fatal minor accidents and fatal incidents stem from fundamentally different root causes [PS]. While non-fatal US industrial accidents decreased by 51% over 15 years, fatalities fell by only 25.5% [PS]. Leading oil and gas operators separately isolate and prioritize the ~20–25% of safety reports that contain genuine Serious Injury and Fatality (SIF) potential [PS]. This proposal presents **"SIF-Guard AI"**, an intelligent, offline-capable NLP engine and spatial-temporal safety dashboard designed for OIL [ASSUMPTION]. SIF-Guard AI automatically ingests free-text safety reports, classifies SIF-potential vs. non-SIF-potential incidents, maps records to the official 9 IOGP Life-Saving Rules [RESEARCHED], pinpoints barrier failures, and ranks operational assets by precursor density to enable real-time, proactive HSE interventions [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Geographic & Field Conditions:** OIL's primary operations are concentrated in Assam (Duliajan, Moran, Digboi), Arunachal Pradesh, Tripura, KG Basin, and offshore assets [RESEARCHED]. Operating environments include dense jungles, heavy rainfall regions, riverine areas, and active drill sites with classified hazardous zones (Zone 0, Zone 1, Zone 2 explosion risks) [KNOWN].
2. **Connectivity Constraints:** Remote production wells, seismic survey sites, and exploratory drilling rigs frequently suffer from non-existent or patchy 2G/cellular connectivity [KNOWN]. Centralized cloud-only inference will fail in field conditions. Edge caching and offline-first capabilities are strictly required [INFERENCE].
3. **Language & Literacy Realities:** Field technicians, contractor labor, and roustabouts report incidents in mixed languages—Hinglish, Assamese, Benglish, or non-standard English with local oilfield terminology (e.g., "derrick man", "BOP pressure", "mud pump spill", "LOTO not applied") [INFERENCE].
4. **Hardware Constraints:** Field personnel use low-to-mid-tier Android smartphones (2GB–4GB RAM) or shared ruggedized industrial tablets [KNOWN]. AI models cannot run raw multi-billion parameter LLMs locally on field devices; lightweight web-based sync and efficient cloud server-side inference (or lightweight edge models) are required [INFERENCE].
5. **Regulatory & Statutory Constraints:** Oil Industry Safety Directorate (OISD) guidelines (OISD-STD-105) and Petroleum & Natural Gas (PNG) Safety Rules 2008 mandate strict records for "High Potential (Hi-Po) Near Misses" [RESEARCHED]. All data must reside within sovereign Indian cloud infrastructure or OIL on-premise servers [KNOWN].

### Approach 1: Deterministic Keyword & Rule-Based Regex Engine
* *Mechanism:* String matching, dictionary lookups (e.g., "height", "gas release", "crane"), and IF-THEN heuristic decision trees based on EEI rules.
* *Problem Fit:* Low. Field safety reports use non-standard phrasing, regional jargon, and spelling errors ("scafold unsecure", "gas leak near DG set").
* *Accuracy Potential:* Poor (<60% F1-score) [INFERENCE]. High false negatives for subtle SIF precursors.
* *Data Requirements:* Minimal (dictionary lists).
* *Complexity:* Very Low.
* *Explainability:* 100% transparent.
* *Cost:* Very Low ($0).
* *Scalability:* High speed, but brittle maintainability.
* *Hackathon Feasibility:* High.
* *Production Feasibility:* Unacceptable due to poor accuracy.

### Approach 2: Pure Cloud LLM API Pipeline (e.g., GPT-4o / Claude 3.5 Prompting)
* *Mechanism:* Direct REST API calls passing safety reports to a commercial cloud LLM with structured JSON output prompts.
* *Problem Fit:* Medium. High language understanding, but introduces data privacy and connectivity bottlenecks.
* *Accuracy Potential:* High (~85–90% zero-shot accuracy) [INFERENCE].
* *Data Requirements:* Zero training data required; requires prompt engineering.
* *Complexity:* Low implementation effort, high operational complexity (network dependency).
* *Explainability:* Low (black-box API).
* *Cost:* High (recurring per-token API fees across thousands of daily OIL reports) [INFERENCE].
* *Scalability:* Limited by API rate limits and network bandwidth at remote rig sites.
* *Hackathon Feasibility:* High.
* *Production Feasibility:* Unacceptable due to OIL data sovereignty policies, recurring API costs, and lack of offline connectivity in field operations [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3 (RECOMMENDED): Hybrid Architecture — Fine-Tuned Transformer (DeBERTa-v3 / RoBERTa) + Deterministic High-Energy Heuristic Rule Engine
* *Mechanism:* A dual-stage hybrid engine. Stage 1 uses an explicit rule engine implementing the EEI Safety Classification & Learning (SCL) decision tree to flag obvious high-energy precursors (e.g., falls > 1.8m, voltage > 50V, unisolated pressure > 100 PSI). Stage 2 uses a fine-tuned, quantized lightweight NLP Transformer (DeBERTa-v3-base) trained on domain-specific safety text to perform multi-task SIF-potential classification and IOGP LSR multi-label tagging. Stage 3 utilizes HDBSCAN & Sentence-Transformers for clustering recurring precursor patterns.
* *Problem Fit:* Perfect fit for OIL requirements.
* *Accuracy Potential:* Excellent (>90% Precision/Recall on SIF potential) [INFERENCE].
* *Data Requirements:* Requires ~1,000–2,000 labeled safety report training samples (synthesized/augmented for hackathon).
* *Complexity:* Medium-High.
* *Explainability:* High (Rule stage is 100% transparent; ML stage outputs confidence scores & attention highlight maps).
* *Cost:* Low (Open-source model deployed on local GPU/edge server; zero recurring API token fees).
* *Scalability:* High throughput (>100 reports/second per GPU node) [INFERENCE].
* *Hackathon Feasibility:* High (Can be trained using Google Colab/Kaggle GPUs and deployed via FastAPI + ONNX Runtime).
* *Production Feasibility:* Excellent. Fully compatible with OIL on-premise servers and offline edge deployments.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The **Hybrid Architecture (Approach 3)** is selected as the winning approach for the SIH solution.

### Justification:
1. **Safety-Critical Reliability:** Pure machine learning models can occasionally hallucinate or miss critical edge cases. Integrating deterministic EEI High-Energy Rule filters ensures that explicit fatal hazards (e.g., "worker inside vessel without gas test") are flagged 100% of the time, regardless of NLP confidence scores [INFERENCE].
2. **Data Sovereignty & On-Premise Compliance:** OIL is a Navratna Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) under the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas [RESEARCHED]. Transferring operational hazard reports containing exact rig locations and personnel names to external commercial APIs violates national data sovereignty guidelines [INFERENCE]. Approach 3 runs completely on-premise or on sovereign Indian cloud instances.
3. **Offline Field Capability:** Fine-tuned Transformer models compressed via ONNX Runtime / INT8 quantization can run locally on field laptops or edge gateways situated at remote drilling sites without active internet access [INFERENCE].
4. **Superior Cost-Efficiency:** Eliminates recurring per-token cloud API bills, allowing OIL to process millions of historical reports cost-effectively.

### Rejection of Alternatives:
* *Approach 1 (Rules Only)* was rejected because regex/keywords fail to capture complex semantic context, syntax errors, and informal field descriptions, leading to dangerously high false-negative rates [INFERENCE].
* *Approach 2 (Cloud LLM API)* was rejected due to data security policy violations, high recurring operational costs, latency, and complete failure in remote, zero-bandwidth oilfield locations [INFERENCE].

### Detailed Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative:

1. **Ingestion:** A field worker submits an Unsafe Act/Unsafe Condition (UA/UC) report narrative (e.g., *"Worker seen operating welding torch on gas pipeline without hot work permit and gas test near Derrick Rig #3"*) via the offline-first Progressive Web App (PWA) or batch CSV upload [INFERENCE].
2. **Preprocessing & Anonymization:** The FastAPI backend receives the report. The PII Anonymizer strips personnel names, phone numbers, and employee IDs using Spacy NER. Text is standardized, removing typos and translating regional Assamese/Hindi terms to standardized English [INFERENCE].
3. **Stage 1 — Rule-Based Filtering:** The text is passed through the EEI High-Energy Heuristic Rule Filter. It checks for hardcoded critical keywords and energy thresholds (e.g., "hot work", "gas pipeline", "no permit"). If an explicit life-threatening rule violation is triggered, it is instantly tagged as `SIF-Potential = High (Rule-Triggered)` [INFERENCE].
4. **Stage 2 — Deep Transformer Inference:** Concurrently, the preprocessed text is tokenized and processed by the fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3 ONNX model.
   * *Head A:* Predicts probability of SIF Potential ($P_{SIF} \in [0,1]$). If $P_{SIF} \ge 0.5$, classified as SIF-Potential [INFERENCE].
   * *Head B:* Multi-label classification predicts applicable IOGP Life-Saving Rules (e.g., `Hot Work`, `Work Authorisation`) [INFERENCE].
5. **Stage 3 — Information & Barrier Extraction:** The NER engine extracts structured entities:
   * `Activity:` Welding / Hot Work
   * `Location:` Derrick Rig #3
   * `Barrier Failure:` Missing Work Permit & Omission of Gas Test [INFERENCE].
6. **Storage & Analytics Ingestion:** Results are committed to PostgreSQL (relational metadata + PostGIS location tags) and vector embeddings are stored in `pgvector`/Qdrant for similarity clustering [INFERENCE].
7. **Precursor Density Calculation:** The analytics engine recalculates the SIF Precursor Density Metric for "Derrick Rig #3" and the "Welding/Hot Work" activity category:
   $$\text{SIF Precursor Density} = \frac{\sum \text{SIF-Potential Reports in Last 30 Days}}{\text{Total Reports in Asset Cluster}} \times 100$$
8. **Real-time Alerting & Visualization:** If the asset's precursor density exceeds the threshold (>25%), an automated alert is pushed to the Installation HSE Manager via SMS/WhatsApp, and the location glows red on the Executive GIS Dashboard [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### 1. SIF-Potential Binary Classifier
* *Purpose:* Classify whether an unstructured safety report description carries genuine fatal potential (~20-25%) or is a low-severity event [PS].
* *Model Type:* Fine-Tuned Transformer (`microsoft/deberta-v3-base`) [RESEARCHED].
* *Input:* Unstructured text string (e.g., *"Scaffolding plank slipped from 15ft height while worker was passing tools without safety harness"*).
* *Output:* SIF-Potential Flag (`True`/`False`) + Probability Score (e.g., `0.92`).
* *Data Required:* ~2,000 safety report descriptions annotated with ground-truth SIF labels based on EEI SCL criteria [ASSUMPTION].
* *Why AI is Preferable:* Simple rules fail to comprehend linguistic variations, implied context, and complex interactions between hazards and missing controls [INFERENCE].
* *Evaluation Metric:* Precision, Recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC (Target: Recall $\ge 90\%$ to minimize false negatives on fatal risks) [INFERENCE].
* *Failure Mode & Mitigation:* If model yields a false negative on an ambiguous report, the Stage 1 Rule Filter acts as a safety net [INFERENCE].
* *Human Oversight:* All reports flagged as SIF-Potential require mandatory sign-off/review by the Installation HSE Manager [INFERENCE].

### 2. Multi-Label IOGP Life-Saving Rules Classifier
* *Purpose:* Automatically map safety reports to one or more of the 9 official IOGP Life-Saving Rules [PS].
* *Model Type:* Multi-Label Classification Transformer Head attached to DeBERTa-v3 [RESEARCHED].
* *Input:* Unstructured text narrative.
* *Output:* Binary vector representing presence of 9 IOGP LSRs (e.g., `[Energy Isolation: 0, Hot Work: 1, Confined Space: 0, Line of Fire: 1, ... ]`).
* *Data Required:* Multi-label dataset categorized by certified HSE domain experts [ASSUMPTION].
* *Why AI is Preferable:* Safety reports frequently involve multiple overlapping rules (e.g., Hot Work in a Confined Space). Standard keyword search cannot accurately distinguish primary context [INFERENCE].
* *Evaluation Metric:* Micro/Macro F1-score, Hamming Loss [INFERENCE].
* *Failure Mode & Mitigation:* Low confidence predictions (<0.4) trigger a prompt for manual tagging by the HSE reviewer [INFERENCE].
* *Human Oversight:* HSE Officers can manually modify auto-tagged LSR labels on the dashboard UI [INFERENCE].

### 3. Precursor Entity & Pattern Extraction (NER & Clustering)
* *Purpose:* Extract specific Entities: Activity, Location, Barrier Failure, and cluster recurring patterns [PS].
* *Model Type:* Custom Spacy Named Entity Recognition (NER) + Sentence-BERT (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) + HDBSCAN Density-Based Clustering [RESEARCHED].
* *Input:* Text narrative.
* *Output:* Extracted Entity JSON + Cluster ID (e.g., `Cluster #12: Recurring Gas Detector Calibration Failures in Moran Rig #2`).
* *Data Required:* Entity-annotated safety text + pre-computed semantic embeddings.
* *Why AI is Preferable:* Automatically discovers emerging systemic failures without predefined categories [INFERENCE].
* *Evaluation Metric:* Silhouette Coefficient, Cluster Density Score [INFERENCE].
* *Failure Mode & Mitigation:* Outlier reports assigned to Noise Cluster (-1) for manual inspection [INFERENCE].
* *Human Oversight:* HSE Directors review auto-generated cluster summaries [INFERENCE].

### Explicit Non-AI Component Identification:
* **Precursor Density Calculation:** Pure mathematical calculation ($\frac{\text{SIF Reports}}{\text{Total Reports}}$) executed directly in PostgreSQL SQL queries. AI MUST NOT be used for statistical aggregation [INFERENCE].
* **User Permission & Access Control:** Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) managed strictly via standard JWT authentication logic. AI MUST NOT participate in security decisions [INFERENCE].

              |                                                   |                       |

1. **Local Storage Mechanism:** The PWA UI uses Dexie.js (IndexedDB wrapper) to store unsubmitted reports locally on field smartphones/tablets [INFERENCE].
2. **Bandwidth Reduction:** Inbound text reports are light (~1–2 KB per record). Attachments/photos are compressed client-side (WebP format, max 800x600 resolution) before queueing [INFERENCE].
3. **Synchronization & Conflict Resolution:**
   * Uses a background ServiceWorker (`BackgroundSync` API) that detects network restoration [KNOWN].
   * Reports are sent in compressed batch JSON payloads (`gzip`).
   * Conflict Handling: Every offline report generates a client-side UUID and timestamp. Server accepts all unique client UUIDs. Duplicate timestamps from the same user are deduplicated automatically [INFERENCE].
4. **Retry Strategy:** Exponential backoff with jitter (initial retry 5s, doubling up to max 5 minutes) to avoid overloading gateway upon network reconnection [INFERENCE].
