## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
The **Supervisory Analytics Tool for SOC Assessment (SAT-SA)** is an air-gapped, supervisory intelligence platform designed for the National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre (NCIIPC) [PS]. While Critical Sector Entities (CSEs) maintain operational Security Operations Centres (SOCs) and report compliance metrics, manual audits repeatedly reveal operational weaknesses—specifically **Execution Gaps** (superficial or gaming behaviour despite policy compliance) and **Negative Space** (absent security telemetry or missing alert categories) [PS]. SAT-SA ingests batch-submitted, multi-entity SOC metadata (alert metadata, case records, escalation logs, asset inventories) without collecting raw logs or packet captures [PS]. Operating 100% offline within NCIIPC’s controlled environment [PS], SAT-SA leverages statistical benchmarking, vector-based natural language processing (NLP), machine learning anomaly detection, and a localized quantized Small Language Model (SLM) to prioritize high-risk CSEs, flag superficial investigation templates, detect coverage blind spots, and generate fully traceable, explainable supervisory evidence for human examiners [PS, INFERENCE].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

### Explicit Requirements
* **Primary Mandate**: Provide a deployable Supervisory Analytics Tool for SOC Assessment (SAT-SA) that assists NCIIPC human examiners in analyzing SOC alert and case-management data at scale [PS].
* **Core Capabilities [PS]**:
  1. Identify entities requiring supervisory attention.
  2. Prioritize alert samples and investigations for manual review.
  3. Detect operational weaknesses and cyber resilience concerns.
  4. Improve efficiency, consistency, and scalability of supervisory assessments.
  5. Support human examiners without replacing supervisory judgment.

### Out of Scope Exclusions [PS]
* The tool shall **NOT** function as or replace a SOC for CSEs [PS].
* The tool shall **NOT** perform real-time monitoring [PS].
* The tool shall **NOT** act as a SIEM platform [PS].
* The tool shall **NOT** act as a centralized SOC for multiple entities [PS].
* The tool shall **NOT** continuously collect logs or telemetry from CSEs [PS].
* The tool shall **NOT** serve as a national cyber monitoring platform [PS].

### Data Environment Explicit Guidelines [PS]
* Periodic submission ingestion of:
  * (i) Alert metadata [PS]
  * (ii) Case management records [PS]
  * (iii) Investigation workflow data [PS]
  * (iv) Escalation records [PS]
  * (v) Alert disposition and closure information [PS]
  * (vi) Asset and system inventory information (where available) [PS]
* Tool must minimize dependence on raw logs, packet captures, customer information, or sensitive operational data [PS].

### Core Supervisory Conditions to Detect [PS]
* **Category A: Execution Gaps [PS]**: Documented controls look effective, but operational evidence shows failure:
  * (i) Alerts acknowledged but not meaningfully investigated [PS].
  * (ii) Cases closed unusually quickly [PS].
  * (iii) Critical alerts closed without escalation [PS].
  * (iv) Repetitive or template-driven investigations [PS].
  * (v) Controls deployed but not effectively monitored [PS].
  * (vi) Operational behavior designed to satisfy metrics without reducing risk [PS].
* **Category B: Negative Space [PS]**: Absence of expected operational evidence:
  * (i) Missing telemetry from critical systems [PS].
  * (ii) Absence of expected alert categories [PS].
  * (iii) Missing investigations or escalation records [PS].
  * (iv) Unexpectedly low activity levels [PS].
  * (v) Monitoring blind spots [PS].
  * (vi) Absence of evidence expected in comparable environments [PS].

### Functional Requirements (1–17) [PS]
* **Data Ingestion**:
  1. Ingest structured data from multiple CSEs [PS].
  2. Support common formats: CSV, JSON, database exports, and APIs where available [PS].
  3. Support analysis of large datasets spanning multiple entities and time periods [PS].
* **Supervisory Analytics**:
  4. Identify indicators of detection, investigation, and escalation weaknesses [PS].
  5. Detect potential execution gaps [PS].
  6. Detect potential negative space [PS].
  7. Identify anomalies, outliers, and suspicious operational patterns [PS].
  8. Perform peer comparison and benchmarking across entities [PS].
  9. Generate entity-level supervisory risk indicators [PS].
  10. Prioritize entities, controls, processes, and alert samples for manual review [PS].
* **Explainability**:
  11. Provide clear rationale for findings [PS].
  12. Present supporting evidence [PS].
  13. Support traceability and auditability of results [PS].
  14. Allow supervisors to understand why an entity or activity was flagged [PS].
* **Reporting**:
  15. Generate supervisory dashboards and reports [PS].
  16. Support trend analysis across entities and time periods [PS].
  17. Enable drill-down from supervisory findings to underlying evidence [PS].

### Deployment Requirements (Air-Gapped Environment) [PS]
* Fully air-gapped network under NCIIPC control [PS].
* No Internet connectivity [PS].
* No dependency on cloud services or SaaS platforms [PS].
* No dependency on externally hosted AI models or APIs [PS].
* Support local deployment and local data processing [PS].
* Required AI specifications if used: Model architecture, hardware requirements, offline training/inference approach, model update mechanism, explainability controls, auditability controls [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Air-Gapped Physical & Network Security**: NCIIPC operates in high-security, network-isolated environments [PS, KNOWN]. The system cannot rely on external DNS, cloud storage (AWS, Azure), SaaS platforms, or remote AI APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic) [PS].
2. **Hardware Constraints**: Supervisory laptops or local workstations utilized by examiners may range from mid-tier laptops (16 GB RAM, 8-core CPU, no discrete GPU) to dedicated audit workstations (64 GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX GPU) [ASSUMPTION]. AI models must run efficiently on CPU-only or modest GPU setups [INFERENCE].
3. **Data Heterogeneity & Quality**: CSEs use diverse SIEM/SOAR platforms (Splunk, Elastic, IBM QRadar, Sentinel, ArcSight) with varying field naming conventions [KNOWN, INFERENCE]. Ingested data may have missing fields, inconsistent time formats, or sanitized asset identifiers [INFERENCE].
4. **Confidentiality & Non-Attribution**: Sectoral peer benchmarking must maintain operational privacy across competing or strategic CSEs [INFERENCE]. Raw asset identifiers must be hash-anonymized in supervisory cross-entity views while remaining traceable within single-entity audits [INFERENCE].
5. **Human-in-the-Loop Imperative**: The system must not act as an automated decision-maker or enforcement tool [PS]. All outputs are advisory evidence artifacts to augment human examiner judgment [PS].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Deterministic Rule-Based & Statistical Heuristic Engine
* **Mechanism**: A suite of SQL queries, REGEX rules, and hardcoded threshold checks (e.g., Alert Closure Time < 30 seconds = Flagged; Critical Severity + Escalation False = Flagged) [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: Python backend parsing CSV/JSON files into an SQLite database, executing rule triggers, and outputting static HTML/PDF audit reports [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity**: Very Low [INFERENCE].
* **Cost**: $0 [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability**: 100% deterministic and easy to audit [INFERENCE].
* **Scalability**: High performance, low computational overhead [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: 100% buildable in 48 hours [INFERENCE].
* **Production Feasibility**: Poor flexibility; fails to detect novel/unknown execution gaps or subtle semantic metric gaming [INFERENCE].
* **Risks**: High false-positive rate; rigid thresholds can be easily gamed if CSEs learn the exact rules [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Pure Unsupervised Machine Learning Anomaly Detection
* **Mechanism**: Ingests numerical features extracted from alerts/cases (closure speed, alert frequency, word count, escalation ratios) into unsupervised ML algorithms (Isolation Forest, DBSCAN, Autoencoders) [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: Python data pipeline (scikit-learn, PyTorch) vectorizing data, running multi-dimensional outlier detection, and clustering suspicious behaviors [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity**: Medium-High [INFERENCE].
* **Cost**: Low (Runs on local CPU/GPU) [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability**: Poor (Black-box anomaly scores; difficult to explain to a human examiner why an alert was flagged) [INFERENCE].
* **Scalability**: Moderate (Scales with feature dimensionality) [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: Feasible for a student team [INFERENCE].
* **Production Feasibility**: Fails NCIIPC’s mandatory explainability and auditability requirement (FR-11 to FR-14) [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Risks**: High false alarm rate; examiners cannot verify evidence without interpretable feature attribution [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Hybrid Supervisory Intelligence Engine (Deterministic Rules + Vector NLP + Anomaly Detection + Local SLM)
* **Mechanism**: Combines deterministic rules (for clear violations), vector similarity + NLP embeddings (for copy-paste investigation template detection), statistical peer-group benchmarking (for negative space and low telemetry), and a locally hosted quantized Small Language Model (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct GGUF) for natural language rationale synthesis [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: React/Tailwind frontend, FastAPI Python backend, SQLite/DuckDB local analytical database, ChromaDB vector store for sentence embeddings, and `llama-cpp-python` for local offline SLM inference [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity**: Moderate-High [INFERENCE].
* **Cost**: $0 open-source stack running 100% air-gapped on standard hardware [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability**: Extremely high (Combines explicit rule/vector evidence with clear text summaries grounded in exact case numbers) [INFERENCE].
* **Scalability**: Excellent for batch supervisory uploads [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: High (Core modules buildable within hackathon timeframe) [INFERENCE].
* **Production Feasibility**: Excellent; fully compliant with all deployment, explainability, and air-gapped constraints [PS, INFERENCE].
* **Risks**: SLM inference latency on CPU-only hardware (mitigated by quantized 4-bit GGUF models and caching) [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
1. **Approach 1 (Pure Rules)** was rejected because hardcoded thresholds cannot catch subtle metric gaming (e.g., an analyst waiting exactly 31 seconds before auto-closing to bypass a 30-second rule) or identify semantically repetitive investigation notes written in different words [INFERENCE].
2. **Approach 2 (Pure Unsupervised ML)** was rejected because black-box anomaly scores fail NCIIPC's explicit requirement for trace-back auditability and clear rationale (FR-11 to FR-14) [PS]. Examiners cannot issue regulatory warnings based purely on an unexplained Isolation Forest distance score [INFERENCE].

### Data Flow Narrative
1. **Ingestion & Normalization**: The examiner uploads periodic SOC export batches (CSV/JSON/SQLite) via the local web UI [PS, INFERENCE]. The Ingestion Pipeline parses heterogeneous fields into the **Standardized Supervisory SOC Schema (S3S)** and generates salted cryptographic hashes for sensitive asset/analyst names [INFERENCE].
2. **Deterministic & Vector Processing**: Data is routed concurrently to:
   - **Rule Engine**: Evaluates time-to-close metrics, severity escalations, and SLA compliance [INFERENCE].
   - **Vector NLP Store (ChromaDB)**: Embeds investigation text notes using a local sentence-transformer model (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) to detect cluster density of identical/templated responses [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
   - **Negative Space Engine**: Cross-references asset inventory lists against alert streams to pinpoint zero-telemetry assets and absent ATT&CK categories [INFERENCE].
3. **Statistical Benchmarking & Risk Scoring**: The Benchmarking Engine clusters CSEs by peer group (e.g., Banking vs. Power) and calculates z-scores for alert distribution, MTTR variance, and Gini coefficients of investigation depth [INFERENCE]. A composite **Supervisory Risk Score (0-100)** is computed per entity [INFERENCE].
4. **Offline SLM Rationale Generation**: For top-ranked suspicious samples, structured finding parameters (e.g., `Entity: PowerCorp_01`, `Finding: 45 critical alerts closed in <20s with template text X`) are passed into `llama-cpp-python` running a quantized Llama-3-8B-Instruct model [INFERENCE]. The SLM outputs a concise, factual supervisory summary [INFERENCE].
5. **Presentation & Audit Trail**: Findings populate the Supervisory Dashboard [PS]. Examiners click on any flagged metric to inspect the exact underlying case IDs, original text, and timestamp evidence chain [PS, INFERENCE].

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Standardized Supervisory SOC Schema (S3S)
The system normalizes incoming heterogeneous CSE exports into a local DuckDB schema:

```sql
-- Core Table: Ingested Alerts Metadata
CREATE TABLE s3s_alerts (
    alert_id VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
    cse_id VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    timestamp TIMESTAMP NOT NULL,
    rule_name VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    severity VARCHAR CHECK (severity IN ('LOW', 'MEDIUM', 'HIGH', 'CRITICAL')),
    mitre_tactic VARCHAR,
    mitre_technique VARCHAR,
    source_asset_id VARCHAR,
    dest_asset_id VARCHAR,
    raw_status VARCHAR
);

-- Core Table: Case Management & Investigations
CREATE TABLE s3s_cases (
    case_id VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
    alert_id VARCHAR REFERENCES s3s_alerts(alert_id),
    cse_id VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    created_at TIMESTAMP NOT NULL,
    closed_at TIMESTAMP,
    resolution_time_seconds INTEGER,
    assigned_analyst_hash VARCHAR,
    escalation_status VARCHAR CHECK (escalation_status IN ('NONE', 'TIER_2', 'CISO', 'NCIIPC', 'CERT_IN')),
    disposition VARCHAR CHECK (disposition IN ('TRUE_POSITIVE', 'FALSE_POSITIVE', 'BENIGN_ANOMALY', 'UNRESOLVED')),
    investigation_notes TEXT
);

-- Core Table: Critical Asset Inventory
CREATE TABLE s3s_asset_inventory (
    asset_id VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
    cse_id VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    asset_name_hash VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    asset_type VARCHAR, -- e.g., 'OT_SCADA', 'DOMAIN_CONTROLLER', 'SWIFT_GATEWAY'
    criticality_tier INTEGER CHECK (criticality_tier IN (1, 2, 3)),
    ip_subnet VARCHAR
);

-- Core Table: Derived Supervisory Findings (Audit Trail)
CREATE TABLE s3s_supervisory_findings (
    finding_id VARCHAR PRIMARY KEY,
    cse_id VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    category VARCHAR CHECK (category IN ('EXECUTION_GAP', 'NEGATIVE_SPACE', 'ANOMALY', 'BENCHMARK_DEVIATION')),
    rule_code VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    severity VARCHAR NOT NULL,
    rationale_text TEXT NOT NULL,
    supporting_evidence_json JSON NOT NULL,
    flagged_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

### Local Storage & Air-Gapped Optimization
* **In-Memory Query Engine**: DuckDB executes analytical queries directly against local Parquet/SQLite storage, yielding sub-second aggregation across millions of rows without network overhead [RESEARCHED].
* **Offline Vector Indexing**: ChromaDB indexes investigation note embeddings locally using HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World) graph trees saved to local disk [RESEARCHED].
* **Bandwidth & Offline Packaging**: The entire system (UI, backend, DuckDB, ChromaDB, GGUF SLM model file) is packaged as a portable 4.8 GB offline bundle that can be transferred via encrypted USB drive to an air-gapped NCIIPC audit workstation [ASSUMPTION].
