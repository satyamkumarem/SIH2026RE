# SIH 2026 Comprehensive Solution Dossier & Technical Architecture

## 1. Executive Snapshot

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a highly prevalent, debilitating musculoskeletal disorder that disproportionately impacts rural, elderly, and agrarian populations in India's North Eastern Region (NER) due to terrain demands, heavy physical labor, and severe shortages of orthopaedic specialists [PS] [KNOWN]. Existing diagnostic pathways rely on costly hospital-based X-rays, MRI scans, and specialist evaluations that are largely inaccessible at the primary healthcare level in hilly, remote hamlets [PS] [RESEARCHED]. This project proposes **ArthriSense-NER**, a low-cost, portable, hardware-assisted, and offline-first AI screening solution [INFERENCE] [ASSUMPTION]. ArthriSense-NER combines a low-cost Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) knee strap with mobile computer vision (MediaPipe Pose) and a digital clinical questionnaire (WOMAC/KOOS) to capture joint kinematics, gait symmetry, and mobility indicators in field conditions [INFERENCE]. Operating completely offline on standard Android smartphones used by Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs) and Auxiliary Nurse Midwives (ANMs), the system computes an explainable OA Risk Score and severity indication, generates ABDM-compliant FHIR R4 screening records, and syncs automatically with district health dashboards upon re-establishing connectivity [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

```
[NER Field Operational Environment]
 ├── Geography: Rugged, mountainous terrain; isolated hamlets (Arunachal, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Mizoram)
 ├── Connectivity: 0 to 2G intermittent signal in hills; broadband restricted to urban centers
 ├── Power: Frequent power grid outages (6-12 hours/day); require battery/power bank operation
 ├── Language: High linguistic diversity (Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Meitei, Nagamese)
 ├── Digital Literacy: Low among elderly patients; moderate (basic smartphone usage) among ASHA/ANM workers
 └── Field Conditions: High humidity, rain, dust; screening performed in open village community halls/camps
```

1. **Geographical & Terrain Isolation:** The North Eastern Region features dense forest coverage and mountainous topography [KNOWN]. Transportation between rural hamlets and District Hospitals takes 4–10 hours [RESEARCHED]. Screening tools must fit in a backpack and weigh less than 1.5 kg [INFERENCE] [ASSUMPTION].
2. **Connectivity Deficit:** Cellular connectivity in rural NER is highly inconsistent, with frequent total signal blackouts in hill valleys [RESEARCHED]. Cloud-dependent AI architectures (e.g., streaming video to AWS/GCP) will fail completely [INFERENCE]. 100% of diagnostic inference must occur locally on the smartphone [INFERENCE].
3. **Power Reliability:** Rural Primary Health Sub-Centres frequently experience prolonged power outages [RESEARCHED]. Hardware devices must operate on rechargeable Li-ion batteries providing at least 10 hours of continuous field screening, chargeable via standard USB-C/power banks [INFERENCE] [ASSUMPTION].
4. **Linguistic Diversity:** Over 220 languages/dialects are spoken across the 8 NER states [RESEARCHED]. Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Khasi, Garo, Mizo, and Meitei represent major regional blocks [RESEARCHED]. Text-only interfaces are insufficient; audio instructions in native dialects are mandatory [INFERENCE].
5. **Low Digital Literacy:** Rural elderly citizens cannot navigate complex touchscreen apps [KNOWN]. ASHA workers must act as proxy operators, guiding the patient through physical movements while the app provides step-by-step visual and verbal cues [INFERENCE].

## 6. Research Questions

1. **Kinematic Accuracy:** How closely do smartphone-based MediaPipe Pose estimation and single-strap BLE IMU sensors correlate with gold-standard optical motion capture (Vicon/OptiTrack) for measuring knee flexion/extension and gait asymmetry in field settings?
2. **Clinical Correlation:** Can an ensemble ML model trained on spatiotemporal gait parameters and WOMAC scores reliably differentiate early-stage OA (Kellgren-Lawrence Grade 1–2) from healthy control joints without immediate radiologic imaging?
3. **Hardware BOM Optimization:** What is the minimal viable sensor configuration (e.g., dual 6-axis MPU6050 vs single 9-axis ICM-20948) that maintains acceptable knee joint angle measurement accuracy (< 3° error) while keeping total kit cost under ₹2,500?
4. **Offline Sync & Conflict Resolution:** What data synchronization protocol guarantees zero data loss or duplication when 500+ ASHA workers upload cached patient reports simultaneously after weeks of offline field operation?
5. **ABDM Compliance:** How can FHIR R4 JSON bundles containing raw IMU kinematic time-series and CV pose vectors be structured to comply with National Health Authority (NHA) digital health record standards?

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selected Solution: Hybrid BLE IMU Motion Strap + Smartphone Computer Vision + Edge ML Risk Classifier (Approach 2)

#### Why Approach 2 Wins:
1. **Fulfills the Hardware Category Mandate:** Problem Statement `SIH26004` is explicitly listed under the **Hardware** category and requires a "portable assessment interface or sensor-assisted screening mechanism" [PS]. Approach 1 (software-only) violates the category mandate and fails under low-light/traditional clothing conditions in field camps [INFERENCE].
2. **Dual-Sensor Complementarity:** IMU sensors measure precise micro-rotations, angular velocity, and flexion angles directly on the joint without occlusion issues [1.1, 1.5]. Computer Vision captures macro-body posture, walking speed, cadence, and step symmetry [2.2, 2.10]. Combining both inputs yields a clinical accuracy profile exceeding 88% sensitivity for early OA indicators [1.1, 2.8].
3. **100% Offline Capability:** All data processing—from BLE telemetry parsing and MediaPipe pose estimation to LightGBM ML inference and report compilation—occurs locally on an off-the-shelf $120 Android phone without needing cloud connectivity [INFERENCE].
4. **Extreme Affordability:** The total hardware Bill of Materials (BOM) cost is under ₹2,000 ($24), allowing state health departments to equip every ASHA worker across NER at negligible cost [INFERENCE] [ASSUMPTION].

#### Rejection of Alternatives:
- **Approach 1 (Pure Computer Vision App) Rejected:** High error rates in knee flexion angle estimation when patients wear traditional loose attire (e.g., Mekhela Chador in Assam, Phanek in Manipur) or when screening in poorly lit village huts [INFERENCE]. Software-only approach fails the SIH Hardware category criteria [PS].
- **Approach 3 (Cloud X-Ray AI Platform) Rejected:** Over 85% of rural primary sub-centres in NER lack functioning diagnostic X-ray infrastructure [PS] [RESEARCHED]. Furthermore, transmitting heavy radiological files over 2G network connections in mountain districts is infeasible [INFERENCE]. X-rays only detect structural changes in moderate-to-late OA (KL Grade 2+), missing early functional/kinematic risk markers [KNOWN].

### Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative:
1. **Patient Registration:** ASHA worker launches the offline mobile app in a village health camp, selects the local language (e.g., Khasi), and enters basic demographic data (age, gender, BMI, occupation) or scans the patient's ABDM QR code to pull their 14-digit ABHA ID [INFERENCE].
2. **Hardware Pairing & Calibration:** The worker straps the lightweight ESP32 BLE IMU unit onto the patient's knee. The mobile app automatically pairs via BLE 5.0 and performs a 3-second static standing calibration to establish the baseline zero-angle position [INFERENCE].
3. **Multimodal Screening Protocol:**
   - **Active ROM Test:** Patient performs 3 active knee extension/flexion movements. Dual MPU6050 sensors stream raw acceleration and angular velocity data at 50 Hz to the phone. The BLE parser calculates precise joint angle changes ($\Delta \theta$) [INFERENCE].
   - **5-Meter Gait Test:** Patient walks 5 meters along a straight path. Smartphone camera records video. MediaPipe Pose extracts skeletal keypoints (hip, knee, ankle) in real time to calculate gait speed, cadence, stance-phase symmetry, and dynamic knee valgus [2.10, 4.5].
   - **Digital WOMAC Questionnaire:** ASHA worker administers a 10-question visual/voice questionnaire capturing pain, stiffness, and daily function difficulty [1.1].
4. **On-Device Edge AI Scoring:** The feature extraction engine computes 18 kinematic and clinical metrics. The local ONNX LightGBM model processes the feature vector in < 150 milliseconds, generating an OA Risk Probability (0–100%), Severity Tier (Low, Moderate, High), and SHAP attribution breakdown (identifying primary risk drivers) [INFERENCE].
5. **Report Generation & Local Storage:** The app generates a structured PDF report and localized voice explanation for the patient. The complete record is encrypted (AES-256) and saved to local SQLite storage [INFERENCE].
6. **Cloud Sync & ABDM Publishing:** When the ASHA worker returns to a PHC with mobile connectivity, the Delta Sync Engine pushes encrypted records to the FastAPI backend and publishes ABDM-compliant FHIR R4 bundles to the National Health Authority (NHA) gateway [INFERENCE] [RESEARCHED].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Model 1: MediaPipe Pose Kinematic Feature Extractor
1. **Exact Problem Solved:** Tracks lower-limb skeletal joints (Hip, Knee, Ankle keypoints) from smartphone walking video to extract dynamic gait parameters [2.10, 4.5].
2. **Input:** 1080p / 720p 30 FPS monocular video frame buffer [2.10].
3. **Output:** 3D coordinates $(x, y, z)$ for keypoints 23 (Left Hip), 24 (Right Hip), 25 (Left Knee), 26 (Right Knee), 27 (Left Ankle), 28 (Right Ankle) with confidence scores [4.1, 4.10].
4. **Why Simpler System Fails:** Manual frame-by-frame angle measurement (e.g., Kinovea) takes 15 minutes per patient and requires expert biomechanists [2.5, 4.7].
5. **Data Required:** 2D/3D video frames across diverse body shapes and rural clothing [4.7].
6. **Evaluation Metric:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in knee angle degrees compared to optical motion capture (target MAE < 4.5°) [2.5, 2.10].
7. **When Wrong:** False keypoint detection under heavy skirts/pants. Mitigated by confidence thresholding ($> 0.7$) and primary reliance on BLE IMU readings [INFERENCE].
8. **Training Required:** Pre-trained COCO/BlazePose weights fine-tuned on lower-limb gait trajectories [INFERENCE].
9. **Fine-Tuning:** Not required for MVP; pre-trained MediaPipe Pose model achieves high relative reliability (ICC > 0.89) [4.7].
10. **RAG:** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision:** Yes (Object Detection & Pose Estimation) [2.10, 4.5].
12. **Speech Processing:** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting:** No [INFERENCE].
14. **Human Oversight:** ASHA worker reviews skeleton overlay on screen before submitting video analysis [INFERENCE].

### Model 2: LightGBM Early OA Risk & Severity Classifier
1. **Exact Problem Solved:** Predicts OA risk probability and severity tier by fusing joint ROM, gait speed, asymmetry index, age, BMI, and WOMAC pain score [1.1, 2.8].
2. **Input:** 18-element normalized feature vector [IMU ROM, Peak Flexion, Stance Time Ratio, Cadence, Gait Speed, Asymmetry Index, WOMAC Pain, WOMAC Stiffness, WOMAC Function, Age, Gender, BMI, Occupational Class] [INFERENCE].
3. **Output:** OA Risk Index (0–100%), Categorical Risk Tier (Low / Moderate / High), SHAP Feature Importance Values [INFERENCE].
4. **Why Simpler System Fails:** Fixed clinical threshold rules (e.g., "if WOMAC > 15 then high risk") miss non-linear combinations (e.g., moderate pain + severe gait asymmetry in a 45-year-old farmer indicates early structural degradation) [1.1, 2.8].
5. **Data Required:** OAI NIH tabular dataset (4,796 patients) merged with sensor-based knee gait datasets [1.2, 1.3].
6. **Evaluation Metric:** Area Under ROC Curve (AUC-ROC > 0.88), Sensitivity (> 85%), Specificity (> 82%) [1.1, 2.8].
7. **When Wrong:** False positive over-refers to PHC; false negative misses early intervention. System explicitly displays confidence intervals and mandates doctor review [INFERENCE].
8. **Training Required:** Yes (Supervised classification on historical cohort data) [1.1, 2.8].
9. **Fine-Tuning:** Periodic retraining when field validation data is uploaded to central servers [INFERENCE].
10. **RAG:** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision:** No (Tabular Gradient Boosting) [INFERENCE].
12. **Speech Processing:** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting:** No [INFERENCE].
14. **Human Oversight:** Essential. Results are flagged as "Preliminary Risk Screening" requiring PHC Medical Officer verification [PS] [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used:
- **No AI for Final Clinical Diagnosis:** AI must NEVER issue an absolute medical diagnosis of Osteoarthritis. Final diagnosis requires clinical examination and radiological correlation by a licensed physician [PS] [INFERENCE].
- **No AI for Patient Consent:** Consent for screening and ABDM health record linking must be obtained via explicit user interaction, never automated AI decision-making [3.4, 4.6].
- **No Generative AI for Medical Advice:** Prevention advice must be retrieved from deterministic, pre-approved medical templates validated by orthopaedic specialists, avoiding LLM hallucination risks [INFERENCE].

### Local Offline Data Persistence:
- **Local Storage Engine:** WatermelonDB backed by SQLite with SQLCipher AES-256 database encryption [INFERENCE].
- **Sync Status Flags:** Every local record contains a `sync_status` flag: `0` (Unsynced), `1` (Syncing), `2` (Synced), `3` (Conflict) [INFERENCE].

### Synchronization & Conflict Resolution Protocol:
1. **Vector Clock Timestamping:** Every record mutation is tagged with a client UUID and monotonically increasing sequence number [INFERENCE].
2. **Delta Compression:** To minimize bandwidth over 2G connections, the app transmits compressed JSON delta payloads containing only unsynced screening records (`sync_status = 0`) [INFERENCE].
3. **Conflict Handling Rule:** If a patient record is modified on both client and server, the server applies **Last-Write-Wins (LWW)** based on NTP-validated timestamps, storing superseded versions in an audit table [INFERENCE].
4. **Exponential Backoff Retry Strategy:** Failed upload requests retry at intervals of $2^n$ seconds (5s, 10s, 20s, 40s, max 15 mins) to conserve phone battery during network outages [INFERENCE].
5. **Bandwidth Reduction:** Raw 50 Hz IMU CSV telemetry and MediaPipe video frames are processed locally into summary metrics (~2 KB JSON packet per screening). Raw video files are discarded immediately after feature extraction to respect phone storage limits [INFERENCE].
6. **Offline UX Indicators:** A persistent sync status banner informs the ASHA worker: `"12 Screenings Saved Offline - Will Auto-Upload When Network Available"` [INFERENCE].

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

1. **Personal Data & PII Protection:** Patient names, phone numbers, and addresses are encrypted at rest using AES-256-GCM encryption keys derived from Android Keystore System [INFERENCE]. The app strictly complies with India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 [4.6].
2. **Location Data Governance:** GPS coordinates recorded during screening are blurred to town/village center level to protect patient privacy while providing sufficient spatial accuracy for MDoNER GIS heatmaps [INFERENCE].
3. **Authentication & Access Control:** Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) enforces strict privileges:
   - **ASHA / ANM:** Can create patient records, run screening protocols, and view local patient reports [INFERENCE].
   - **PHC Doctor:** Can review full diagnostic reports, confirm/override risk tiers, and issue official referrals [INFERENCE].
   - **MDoNER Administrator:** Accesses aggregated, anonymized district-level epidemiological analytics [INFERENCE].
4. **Encryption Standards:** All data in transit between mobile devices and central servers uses TLS 1.3 with Certificate Pinning to prevent Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attacks [INFERENCE].
5. **ABDM Consent Framework:** Integrates NHA ABDM Consent Manager APIs [4.1, 4.6]. Patient explicit opt-in consent (captured via Aadhaar OTP or digital signature) is required before linking screening reports to their 14-digit ABHA ID [4.1, 4.6].
6. **Regulatory Validation Disclaimer:** The dossier explicitly labels the hackathon system as a **"Prototype Decision Support Tool for Screening"**. It is not a certified Class B/C medical device under Indian Medical Devices Rules (MDR) 2017 until formal clinical trials and CDSCO approval are completed [INFERENCE].
