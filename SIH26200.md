## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Connectivity Limitations**: Rural Indian microgrid sites and agricultural feeders (PM-KUSUM) frequently suffer from intermittent cellular networks (2G/EDGE or lossy 4G) [KNOWN]. *Impact*: System architecture must be offline-first, using local MQTT/SQLite queuing with compact Protobuf/JSON payloads.
* **Hardware Availability & Cost**: Small microgrids (<50 kW) cannot afford $5,000+ commercial SCADA gateways or thermal imaging drones [KNOWN]. *Impact*: Software must run on low-cost single-board computers (Raspberry Pi 4 / ESP32 edge gateways) and accept virtualized sensor feeds.
* **Operating Environment & Soiling**: Dust accumulation in arid/semi-arid Indian regions (e.g., Rajasthan, Gujarat, Western UP) degrades PV output by 15-30% within weeks if uncleaned [RESEARCHED]. *Impact*: Software must feature automated soiling loss estimation to trigger cleaning schedules.
* **Language & Digital Literacy**: Field technicians and agricultural users require intuitive interfaces with minimal text density, high icon usage, and regional language support (Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil) [KNOWN].
* **Power Quality & Grid Fluctuation**: Rural distribution feeders experience wide voltage variations (180V–260V) and frequent unannounced load shedding [KNOWN]. *Impact*: Energy management software must monitor grid voltage limits before executing grid-export routines.

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Static Rule-Based & Threshold Energy Management System
* **Mechanism**: Fixed threshold rules (e.g., IF Battery SoC > 80% AND Solar > Load THEN Export to Grid; IF Solar < Load THEN Discharge Battery).
* **Problem Fit**: Moderate for basic microgrids, poor for variable solar conditions and dynamic ToU tariffs.
* **Accuracy Potential**: Low (does not look ahead at weather forecasts).
* **Data Requirements**: Minimal (real-time SoC and instantaneous generation).
* **Complexity**: Very Low (simple `if-else` execution).
* **Explainability**: 100% transparent.
* **Cost**: Extremely low.
* **Scalability**: High execution speed, but low adaptability across diverse sites.
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: 100% (can be built in 1 day).
* **Production Feasibility**: Poor (causes sub-optimal battery cycling and higher operational costs).
* **Risks**: Misses peak tariff savings; frequently over-discharges batteries during unexpected rain/cloud cover.

### Approach 2: Pure End-to-End Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) Dispatch
* **Mechanism**: Deep Q-Network (DQN) or PPO agent trained in a Gym environment to control BESS charge/discharge based on reward function.
* **Problem Fit**: High theoretically, low practically due to sample inefficiency and unexplainable actions.
* **Accuracy Potential**: High in simulation, unpredictable in real-world edge cases.
* **Data Requirements**: Massive historical time-series for training.
* **Complexity**: Very High.
* **Explainability**: Low ("Black Box" decision-making, dangerous for electrical grid safety).
* **Cost**: Medium-High (training compute).
* **Scalability**: Requires re-training or fine-tuning per microgrid topology.
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: Low (hard to stabilize training within hackathon window).
* **Production Feasibility**: Very Low (utility operators reject unexplainable control algorithms).
* **Risks**: Hallucinated control signals violating physical battery voltage or inverter thermal limits.

### Approach 3: Physics-Informed ML Forecasting + MILP Optimization (Recommended Hybrid)
* **Mechanism**:
  1. *Generation Modeling*: `pvlib-python` computes clear-sky baseline yield from tilt/azimuth [1.2].
  2. *Forecasting*: LightGBM/XGBoost models predict 24-hour GHI and power output using satellite weather forecasts (Open-Meteo/NASA POWER) [3.8, 4.2].
  3. *Dispatch*: Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (`PyPSA` / `SciPy.optimize.linprog`) optimizes BESS scheduling and load prioritization to minimize energy cost subject to SoC and degradation constraints [2.3].
  4. *Diagnostics*: Compares actual IoT yield against `pvlib` physics baseline to calculate soiling ratio and detect inverter degradation [1.2].
* **Problem Fit**: Perfect fit for software-driven management and generation efficiency.
* **Accuracy Potential**: High (MAE < 8-12% for day-ahead solar forecasting) [3.9].
* **Data Requirements**: Public satellite APIs + local sensor streams [3.2].
* **Complexity**: Medium-High, but modular.
* **Explainability**: High (MILP output provides explicit mathematical proof of optimal cost breakdown).
* **Cost**: Low (open-source python libraries) [1.2, 2.2].
* **Scalability**: High (parametrization by PV capacity and battery size).
* **Hackathon Feasibility**: High (realistic for a 4-6 student team using Python backend).
* **Production Feasibility**: Excellent (aligns with modern utility EMS architectures).
* **Risks**: Dependency on satellite API availability (mitigated by fallback persistence forecast).

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selected Solution
**Approach 3: Physics-Informed ML Forecasting + MILP Optimization Hybrid**.

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 (Rule-Based)** was rejected because static thresholds cannot optimize for upcoming weather events. For example, if a severe rainstorm is forecasted in 2 hours, a rule-based system will discharge the battery now, leaving the microgrid stranded later.
* **Approach 2 (Pure DRL)** was rejected due to lack of explainability, high training risk, and potential safety violations in grid control. Grid operators require hard operational constraints (e.g., never discharge BESS below 15% SoC) which MILP guarantees, whereas DRL can occasionally violate constraints during policy exploration.

### Core Winning Differentiator
By combining physical equations (`pvlib`) with statistical ML (XGBoost) and deterministic math (MILP via `PyPSA`), GridPulse ensures **100% safety guarantees**, **transparent operational reasoning**, and **maximum generation yield detection** without requiring expensive hardware pyranometers [1.2, 2.3].

### Detailed Data Flow Narrative
1. **Telemetry Ingestion**: Every 10 seconds, the Edge Gateway (or IoT Simulator) reads grid voltage, PV array current/voltage, active power, and BMS battery parameters. If online, it streams JSON payloads over MQTT/WebSocket to the FastAPI backend. If offline, it appends records to a local SQLite buffer.
2. **Weather & Solar Feature Extraction**: Every 1 hour, the `Forecast Service` queries Open-Meteo API [4.2] for 15-minute resolution Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI), Cloud Cover, and Temperature for the site's latitude/longitude.
3. **Yield Prediction**: The XGBoost regression model ingests weather features and predicts solar power generation for the next 24 hours ($P_{solar}(t)$).
4. **Optimal Dispatch Execution**: The `Dispatch Service` passes $P_{solar}(t)$, forecasted load demand $P_{load}(t)$, BESS capacity ($E_{cap}$), and DISCOM ToU rates into a `PyPSA` linear optimal power flow solver [2.3]. The solver outputs hourly target commands: $P_{charge}(t)$, $P_{discharge}(t)$, $P_{grid\_import}(t)$, and $P_{grid\_export}(t)$.
5. **Real-time Fault Detection**: Simultaneously, `pvlib` calculates the expected theoretical yield $P_{theoretical}(t)$ under clear-sky conditions [1.2]. The `Diagnostic Service` calculates $Ratio = \frac{P_{actual}}{P_{theoretical}}$. If $Ratio < 0.75$ during clear sky periods for >2 consecutive hours, a "Soiling / String Fault Alert" is published to Redis and pushed to the technician's PWA via WebSocket.
6. **Data Storage**: High-frequency telemetry and dispatch logs are written to TimescaleDB hyper-tables for long-term analytics.

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: Short-Term Irradiance & Solar Output Forecaster
* **Purpose**: Predict PV array AC power output ($kW$) for 15-minute intervals over a rolling 24-hour horizon.
* **Model Type**: XGBoost Gradient Boosted Decision Trees.
* **Input Features**: GHI ($W/m^2$), Temperature ($^\circ C$), Cloud Cover (%), Humidity (%), Sun Azimuth/Elevation, Hour of Day, Day of Year, Lagged Generation (t-15m, t-1h, t-24h).
* **Output**: Predicted AC Active Power Output ($kW$) for time steps $t+1 \dots t+96$.
* **Data Required**: 1-3 years of historical hourly solar irradiance and weather data from NASA POWER / Open-Meteo [3.2, 4.2].
* **Why AI is Preferable**: Solar irradiance is non-linear and heavily affected by cloud cover patterns. Simple linear regression or clear-sky physical models overestimate generation by 30-80% during overcast or partially cloudy days.
* **Evaluation Metrics**: Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Normalized MAE ($nMAE < 8\%$).
* **Failure Mode & Fallback**: If weather API calls fail, system falls back to a **Physics-Based Persistence Model** ($P_{pred}(t) = P_{clearsky}(t) \times \frac{P_{actual}(t-24h)}{P_{clearsky}(t-24h)}$).
* **Human Oversight**: Operators can manually set a "Generation Discount Factor" (0.5 - 1.0) during extreme local weather events (e.g., severe dust storm).

### Component 2: Anomaly & Soiling Diagnostic Engine
* **Purpose**: Identify whether reduced generation is due to weather (clouds) or operational faults (soiling, micro-cracks, inverter tripping).
* **Model Type**: Physics-Informed Residual Analysis (`pvlib` Clear-Sky Baseline vs. Filtered Sensor Stream) [1.2].
* **Input**: Measured Irradiance, Ambient Temp, PV Array DC Output, Inverter AC Output, `pvlib` Theoretical Output [1.2].
* **Output**: Soiling Derate Factor ($0.0 - 1.0$), Inverter Efficiency Index (%), Fault Classification Tag (`NORMAL`, `SOILING_ALERT`, `INVERTER_EFFICIENCY_DROP`, `STRING_OFFLINE`).
* **Why AI/Statistical Thresholding is Preferable**: Distinguishes between cloud shading (transient drop across all sensors) and string failure (drop in specific DC currents while irradiance remains high).
* **Evaluation**: Precision/Recall on simulated fault injection dataset.
* **Failure Mode**: Defaults to "Sensor Data Unreliable" warning if ambient temperature or irradiance sensors drift outside physical ranges.
* **Human Oversight**: PWA alerts maintenance manager with recommended action ("Schedule Manual Washing", "Inspect Inverter Fuse").

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **BESS Safety Dispatch & Relay Control**: Battery charge/discharge execution must NEVER be delegated to a black-box machine learning model (e.g., Deep RL). Safety limits (Over-voltage, Under-voltage, Max C-rate, Temperature Cut-off) must be enforced via **hard-coded deterministic safety interlocks** and **MILP mathematical constraints** [2.3].

```sql
-- Generation Forecasts
CREATE TABLE generation_forecasts (
    forecast_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,

    target_time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    predicted_pv_kw REAL NOT NULL,
    model_version VARCHAR(30)

SELECT create_hypertable('generation_forecasts', 'forecast_time');
```

### Offline / Low-Network Strategy
* **Edge Buffering**: The edge gateway stores incoming Modbus readings in a local SQLite database table (`buffered_telemetry`).
* **Synchronization Manager**: A background daemon checks network ping every 15 seconds. When connection is restored, it batches stored records into gzip-compressed payloads (up to 500 records per HTTP batch) and POSTs them to `/api/v1/telemetry/sync`.
* **Conflict Resolution**: Timestamps are generated by the edge gateway RTC (Real-Time Clock) synced via NTP. Server accepts records using `ON CONFLICT (time, site_id) DO UPDATE`.
* **Bandwidth Reduction**: Data payloads use Protobuf or compact JSON with short key names (`t`, `p_pv`, `p_ld`, `soc`). In low-bandwidth mode, telemetry frequency dynamically drops from 10s to 60s.

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

* **Personal & Location Data Protection**: Exact latitude/longitude of critical energy infrastructure (microgrids, military/institutional feeders) is sensitive. Coordinate values in public dashboards are fuzzied (rounded to 2 decimal places ~1.1km) for general users, with full precision restricted to authenticated Site Admins.
* **Authentication & Authorization**: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) implemented via JWT tokens with RSA-256 signatures. Roles: `SUPER_ADMIN`, `DISCOM_OPERATOR`, `PLANT_TECHNICIAN`, `VIEWER`.
* **Transport Encryption**: All edge-to-cloud communications enforce TLS 1.3 (HTTPS / MQTTS).
* **Data Ownership**: Site owners retain full ownership of raw operational telemetry. Public aggregates used for grid planning are anonymized.
* **AI Liability & Regulatory Compliance**: Generated dispatch recommendations are advisory for grid-tied systems unless explicitly authorized by the local DISCOM under Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC) grid code guidelines [2.1].
