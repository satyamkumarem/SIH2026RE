## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
This dossier outlines a complete technical, hardware, data, and software architecture for an **Automated High-Current Short-Circuit Test System** engineered to comply with **IEC 60898-1:2015** [PS]. Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs) rated from 0.5A to 63A across single-pole to four-pole configurations require rigorous testing at short-circuit fault levels up to 10,000A [PS]. Manual test methods introduce imprecise circuit impedance ($R$ and $X_L$), poor power factor control, extended setup times, and severe arc-flash and electrical hazards for testing personnel [PS]. The proposed system automates test circuit impedance selection via an automatically switched $R$-$X_L$ load bank, integrates a transformer-based high-current power source (10,000A), enforces safety via a universal pneumatic test station with a specialized arc chute, and captures microsecond-level fault waveforms ($I_p$, $I^2t$) using an Industrial PC / PLC Data Acquisition (DAQ) architecture with automated IEC-compliant PDF report generation [PS]. For the Smart India Hackathon (SIH), where a live 10kA discharge is infeasible and unsafe, a scaled physical hardware control jig paired with a high-speed DAQ waveform engine and lab simulation mode will be demonstrated [ASSUMPTION].

To automate low-voltage Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) short-circuit breaking capacity testing up to 10,000A per IEC 60898-1:2015 by controlling circuit impedance ($R$ and $X_L$), capturing high-speed waveforms ($I_p$, $I^2t$), and executing safe, repeatable test sequences with zero human hazard during fault initiation [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Extreme Energy Hazard:** A 10,000A fault at 240V produces up to 2.4 MW of peak instantaneous power [KNOWN]. The physical equipment must withstand high magnetic repulsion forces ($F \propto I^2$) on busbars and heavy thermal stress during the 3–10ms clearing window [KNOWN].
* **Power Factor Tolerances per IEC 60898-1:** The standard mandates strict power factor ranges based on short-circuit current level [RESEARCHED]:
  * $I \le 1500\text{ A}: \cos \phi = 0.93 \text{ to } 0.98$ [RESEARCHED]
  * $1500\text{ A} < I \le 3000\text{ A}: \cos \phi = 0.80 \text{ to } 0.90$ [RESEARCHED]
  * $3000\text{ A} < I \le 4500\text{ A}: \cos \phi = 0.70 \text{ to } 0.80$ [RESEARCHED]
  * $4500\text{ A} < I \le 6000\text{ A}: \cos \phi = 0.50 \text{ to } 0.70$ [RESEARCHED]
  * $6000\text{ A} < I \le 10000\text{ A}: \cos \phi = 0.45 \text{ to } 0.50$ [RESEARCHED]
* **Hackathon Demonstration Constraint:** Generating a live 10,000A prospective arc fault inside an SIH hackathon venue is physically dangerous, strictly prohibited by venue electrical capacity, and unachievable on standard student budgets [KNOWN]. The hackathon team MUST demonstrate a scaled physical model (~24V AC/DC control circuit with real pneumatic clamping, automated load switching, and physical interlocks) integrated with a high-speed DAQ software engine and validated waveform replay capability [ASSUMPTION].
* **Electromagnetic Interference (EMI):** High-current switching creates severe electromagnetic pulses (EMP) and ground bounce [KNOWN]. DAQ instrumentation, PLC control lines, and signal cables must be optically isolated and housed in shielded metal enclosures (Faraday cage design) [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Centralized PLC Control with Hardwired Heavy Contactor $R$-$X_L$ Matrix & Standalone Digital Oscilloscope
* **Mechanism:** A Siemens S7-1500 PLC manages test sequencing ($O - t - CO$) and controls heavy AC air contactors to switch pre-calculated resistor and inductor banks. An external Keysight/Tektronix digital storage oscilloscope (DSO) captures waveforms via Ethernet/LXI and transfers CSV files to a PC for offline Python analysis.
* **Problem Fit:** High industrial reliability, but fragmented data acquisition pipeline.
* **Accuracy Potential:** Moderate ($10\text{ kS/s}$ Ethernet transfers may bottleneck raw data stream).
* **Data Requirements:** Calibrated $R/X_L$ relay lookup tables.
* **Complexity:** Medium hardware complexity, low software integration complexity.
* **Explainability:** High (Standard industrial PLC ladder logic).
* **Cost:** High (Expensive standalone oscilloscope + heavy contactors).
* **Scalability:** Moderate.
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** Moderate (Requires real PLC and oscilloscope).
* **Production Feasibility:** Good for traditional industrial labs, but lacks seamless real-time processing.

### Approach 2: Industrial PC (IPC) + High-Speed PCIe/EtherCAT DAQ + Thyristor-Switched $R$-$X_L$ Bank + Point-on-Wave Synchronous Switching (RECOMMENDED)
* **Mechanism:** An Industrial PC running a real-time Linux OS (or TwinCAT) interfaces with high-speed EtherCAT DAQ modules (sampling at $1\text{ MS/s}$). Solid-state thyristor switches handle both precise point-on-wave current initiation ($0^\circ-90^\circ$ voltage closing) and fast $R$-$X_L$ power factor tuning. Universal pneumatic actuators clamp the MCB into a sealed arc chute chamber.
* **Problem Fit:** Excellent. Directly addresses precise $R/X_L$ control, high-energy safety, and microsecond waveform capture ($I_p$, $I^2t$).
* **Accuracy Potential:** Extremely high ($\pm 0.5\%$ current waveform accuracy).
* **Data Requirements:** Microsecond voltage/current arrays, calibration curves, automated report templates.
* **Complexity:** High hardware and software integration.
* **Explainability:** High (Mathematical peak and trapezoidal integration algorithms clearly defined by IEC 60898-1).
* **Cost:** Moderate-High (Optimized industrial components without redundant standalone equipment).
* **Scalability:** High (Modular EtherCAT nodes enable scaling from 1P to 4P testing).
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High when implemented as a scaled physical jig + IPC DAQ software simulator.
* **Production Feasibility:** Highest accuracy, speed, and safety compliance.

### Approach 3: Embedded Microcontroller Architecture (ESP32/STM32) + Relay Board + Local Web HMI
* **Mechanism:** Dual STM32 microcontrollers handle low-level relay switching and SPI-based ADC waveform sampling ($100\text{ kS/s}$). An ESP32 hosts a local Wi-Fi Web HMI for parameter entry and basic waveform rendering.
* **Problem Fit:** Poor for 10kA high-power production, but feasible for low-cost educational prototypes.
* **Accuracy Potential:** Low-Moderate (ADC bit depth and buffer limitations in low-cost MCUs).
* **Complexity:** Low cost, but fragile in high-EMI short-circuit environments.
* **Cost:** Very Low.
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** Very High for basic physical proof-of-concept.
* **Production Feasibility:** Rejected for production due to noise vulnerability, lack of SIL safety rating, and limited high-speed processing.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 2 (Industrial PC + High-Speed EtherCAT/PCIe DAQ + Automated $R$-$X_L$ Load Bank + Universal Pneumatic Safety Station)** is selected as the optimal architecture.

### Justification
1. **Precise Power Factor Control:** Thyristor/Contactor switched $R$-$X_L$ load banks under real-time IPC control enable automated impedance setting within $\pm 1\%$ tolerance, satisfying IEC 60898-1 Table 17 requirements across all current ranges (0.5A to 63A) [RESEARCHED].
2. **Synchronous Point-on-Wave Closing:** Integrated thyristor closing control allows initiation of the short-circuit fault at precise electrical phase angles (e.g., $0^\circ$ for maximum asymmetrical peak $I_p$, or $90^\circ$ for symmetrical test), eliminating test variance [RESEARCHED].
3. **Microsecond Waveform Analysis:** $1\text{ MS/s}$ simultaneous DAQ guarantees precise calculation of peak let-through current $I_p$ and let-through energy $I^2t = \int i(t)^2 dt$, which occur within $3\text{ ms} - 10\text{ ms}$ of arc initiation [RESEARCHED].
4. **Complete Human Isolation:** Universal pneumatic clamping and enclosed arc chute eliminate human physical intervention during high-energy discharges, satisfying DoCA safety mandates [PS].

### Rejection of Alternatives
* **Approach 1 (PLC + External Oscilloscope)** was rejected because standalone oscilloscopes introduce data transfer bottlenecks, require manual configuration file management, and increase total system cost without providing automated closed-loop parameter tuning [INFERENCE].
* **Approach 3 (Microcontroller MCU)** was rejected for production because standard microcontrollers lack the noise immunity, high-speed multi-channel buffer depth, and SIL-2 safety certification required for 10kA arc-flash test bays [INFERENCE]. However, low-cost MCU principles are selectively utilized for the low-voltage Hackathon control jig model [ASSUMPTION].

### Data Flow Narrative
1. **Configuration:** The test engineer enters MCB parameters (e.g., 16A Type C, 3-Pole, $I_{cn} = 10,000\text{A}$, Target $\cos \phi = 0.48$) via the IPC HMI [PS].
2. **Impedance Computation & Switching:** The IPC controller calculates required $R$ and $X_L$ values from internal calibration tables and signals the Switched Load Bank via EtherCAT/PLC output lines [INFERENCE]. Pneumatic contactors select the target resistor and inductor taps [INFERENCE].
3. **Pneumatic Clamping & Safety Verification:** Universal pneumatic actuators clamp the test MCB onto the copper busbars inside the arc chute enclosure [PS]. The Safety PLC verifies door lock signals, enclosure pressure sensors, and emergency stop continuity [INFERENCE].
4. **Synchronous Fault Execution:** The point-on-wave thyristor switch waits for the precise voltage phase angle ($0^\circ$) and triggers the high-current transformer source, dumping prospective short-circuit current through the MCB [INFERENCE].
5. **High-Speed Waveform Acquisition:** Simultaneous $1\text{ MS/s}$ sampling captures current $i(t)$ via Rogowski coils and arc voltage $v(t)$ via isolated differential probes during the 3–10ms clearing process [RESEARCHED].
6. **Data Processing & Compliance Analysis:** The IPC math engine calculates peak let-through current $I_p$, computes Joule integral $I^2t$, extracts arc duration, verifies trip timing, and logs raw array data into Parquet/TDMS format [INFERENCE].
7. **Report Generation:** The report engine generates an official IEC 60898-1 compliance PDF certificate with embedded oscillograms, pass/fail status, and timestamped metadata, saved to local storage and mirrored to the cloud database [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification
