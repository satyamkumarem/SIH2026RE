## 1. Executive Snapshot

Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL) imports over 15–20 million metric tonnes of metallurgical coking coal annually to support its East Coast integrated steel plants [RESEARCHED]. The current procurement model relies heavily on daily, reactive spot chartering in highly volatile global dry bulk markets [PS]. This manual approach leads to missed cost-saving opportunities, improper vessel selection, and vessel idle time caused by strict physical draft, Length Overall (LOA), and beam limitations across East Coast Indian ports (Paradip, Vizag, Gangavaram, Gopalpur, Dhamra, Sagar-Sandheads, and Haldia) [PS]. 

To solve this, the proposed solution—**NaviFreight-AI**—is a predictive dry bulk chartering decision support system. It combines hybrid machine learning time-series forecasting (XGBoost/LightGBM + Temporal Fusion Transformer) [INFERENCE] with a Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) constraint solver [INFERENCE] and discrete-event port congestion simulation [INFERENCE]. NaviFreight-AI shifts SAIL from reactive single spot contracts to proactive short-to-medium term Contract of Affreightment (COA) / time-charter strategy [PS].

   - **Gangavaram Port:** Deep draft (~19.5m draft) capable of handling Capesize and VLOC [RESEARCHED].

   - **Dhamra Port:** Deepwater (~17.5m draft), Capesize compliant [RESEARCHED].
   - **Gopalpur Port:** Medium draft (~12.5m–14.5m), Panamax/Supramax compliant [RESEARCHED].
3. **PSU Enterprise & Connectivity Environment:**
   - **Strict Cybersecurity:** SAIL intranet environments enforce strict air-gapping, proxy firewalls, and data governance policies. External cloud tools must operate over secure HTTPS REST APIs with OAuth2/JWT authentication [INFERENCE].
   - **Audit Compliance:** Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) in India must adhere to CVC (Central Vigilance Commission) guidelines. Every procurement recommendation must be mathematically explainable and logged for audit trails [KNOWN].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Deterministic Rule-Based Heuristic Solver + Statistical ARIMA/GARCH Forecasting
- **Mechanism:** Statistical ARIMA time-series models for spot rate forecasting combined with hardcoded rule-based lookup tables for vessel-port physical matching.
- **Complexity:** Low.
- **Explainability:** Very High (standard statistical formulas).
- **Pros:** Fast implementation, highly transparent for PSU audits.
- **Cons:** Fails to capture non-linear market shocks, geopolitical events, or complex multi-variable interactions (bunker prices + coal demand + port queueing).

### Approach 2: Deep Learning (LSTM / PatchTST) + Mixed Integer Linear Programming (MILP)
- **Mechanism:** Deep Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM/GRU) or Transformer-based time series models (PatchTST) to predict multi-route freight indices, coupled with Pyomo/OR-Tools MILP solver for vessel allocation.
- **Complexity:** High.
- **Explainability:** Medium-Low (Deep learning black-box requires SHAP/LIME explanation).
- **Pros:** Captures complex non-linear temporal trends.
- **Cons:** Requires massive historical training datasets; prone to overfitting during black-swan freight market shocks.

### Approach 3 (RECOMMENDED): Hybrid Machine Learning (Ensemble LightGBM/XGBoost + Temporal Fusion Transformer) + MILP Constraint Solver + Discrete Event Congestion Simulator
- **Mechanism:** Ensemble ML (LightGBM + Prophet/TFT) incorporating exogenous indicators (bunker prices, coal demand, seasonal weather indices) to forecast freight rates with prediction confidence bounds. Physical port constraints, parcel splitting (lighterage at Sandheads), and contract selection (Spot vs. 6-month COA) are solved using Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (PuLP/OR-Tools). Real-time queueing and idle time risk are calculated via a discrete-event SIMPY queue simulator.
- **Complexity:** Medium-High.
- **Explainability:** High (Feature importance SHAP scores + explicit mathematical MILP constraints).
- **Pros:** High predictive accuracy, transparent decision logic, realistic port handling modeling, robust against noisy data.
- **Cons:** Requires integration of multiple ML and optimization libraries.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The recommended approach is **Approach 3: Hybrid ML Forecasting Engine + MILP Constraint Solver + Discrete Event Congestion Simulator**.

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected:
- **Approach 1 Rejected:** Pure statistical models (ARIMA) cannot adapt to violent freight market swings caused by global geopolitical disruptions, monsoon seasonality, or rapid fuel price spikes [INFERENCE].
- **Approach 2 Rejected:** Standalone deep learning models lack physical port constraint awareness and risk failing PSU audit standards due to "black-box" decisions without explicit constraint validation [INFERENCE].

### Why Approach 3 Wins:
1. **Separation of Forecasting & Decision Logic:** Machine Learning is used where it excels (predicting volatile price trends from noisy multi-source data), while Mathematical Optimization (MILP) is used where strict compliance is required (draft, LOA, beam, parcel size hard constraints) [INFERENCE].
2. **PSU Audit Compliance:** The MILP mathematical formulation produces deterministic, auditable proofs for why a specific vessel class (e.g., Panamax vs. Capesize lightered at Sandheads) was selected over another [INFERENCE].
3. **Hackathon Feasibility:** Can be developed within the timeline using open-source Python frameworks (`lightgbm`, `pulp`, `simpy`, `scikit-learn`, `FastAPI`, `React`) [INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Ingestion Step:** Automated background ETL scripts fetch daily Baltic freight proxies, VLSFO bunker fuel prices, metallurgical coal indices, and AIS vessel queue updates into PostgreSQL/DuckDB [INFERENCE].
2. **Forecast Step:** The user inputs a cargo procurement request (e.g., 150,000 MT coking coal from Hay Point, Australia to East Coast India within a 45-day window). The LightGBM/Prophet ML engine generates daily predicted freight rates for Capesize (C5 route) and Panamax routes over the next 90 days, returning mean predictions along with P10/P90 confidence bands [INFERENCE].
3. **Constraint & Optimization Step:** The request and forecast curves are passed to the MILP Optimization Engine. The engine evaluates candidate vessel strategies:
   - **Option A:** Single Capesize vessel (150k MT) direct to deepwater port (Gangavaram or Paradip) [INFERENCE].
   - **Option B:** Single Capesize vessel with lighterage/top-off at Sagar-Sandheads anchorage down to 8.5m draft, then Haldia discharge [INFERENCE].
   - **Option C:** Two Panamax vessels (75k MT each) shipped directly to Vizag/Gopalpur [INFERENCE].
   - **Option D:** 6-Month Time Charter / COA contract vs. Spot market execution [INFERENCE].
4. **Simulation Step:** Selected routes run through the SimPy Discrete-Event Queue Simulator, using current port berth congestion levels to estimate laytime, waiting days, and expected demurrage costs ($/day) [INFERENCE].
5. **Presentation Step:** The FastAPI backend returns the optimal recommended charter window, vessel type, contract structure (COA vs Spot), total estimated cost breakdown ($/MT), and risk alerts to the React frontend [INFERENCE].

- **Progressive Web App (PWA):** Frontend caches UI assets and static port constraint databases locally via Service Workers and IndexedDB [INFERENCE].

- **Background Synchronization:** In field conditions with spotty network (e.g., dockside operations), local cargo requests are queued in IndexedDB and synchronized automatically upon reconnecting [INFERENCE].

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

- **Data Privacy & Confidentiality:** SAIL internal procurement volumes, target bid prices, and stockyard levels are classified as Proprietary Commercial Information [PS]. Inputs are encrypted using AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.3 in transit [INFERENCE].

  - *Admin:* User management, system calibration.
