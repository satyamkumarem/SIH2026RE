## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Food processing safety relies heavily on retrospective culture testing, creating a multi-day lag during which contaminated poultry and produce enter supply chains, leading to public health risks and massive product recalls [PS]. This proposal presents an inline, non-destructive Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI) inspection and sorting system engineered for high-speed conveyor belts in food processing plants [INFERENCE]. By combining Near-Infrared (NIR) absorption spectroscopy and Ultraviolet-A (UV-A) induced fluorescence imaging [RESEARCHED], the system captures full spatial-spectral data cubes of 100% of products moving along the line [PS]. High-speed edge computing units (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin) embedded directly in the sensor enclosure execute optimized 1D/3D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and band-ratio algorithms to identify the unique spectral signatures of bacterial biofilms and pathogens (*Salmonella*, *Listeria*, *E. coli*) within milliseconds [PS, RESEARCHED]. Real-time digital signals immediately trigger downstream pneumatic air ejectors to isolate infected food items without interrupting conveyor movement [PS]. Designed for the Smart India Hackathon (SIH 2026) under the Ministry of Food Processing Industries (MoFPI) [PS], this dossier outlines a practical hardware-software prototype, a clear path to industrial IP67/HACCP deployment, and a rigorous validation framework [INFERENCE].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The explicit problem statement provided by MoFPI contains the following core requirements, preserved with their exact structural breakdown:

1. **Reactive Testing Elimination**: Replace traditional random sampling and multi-day laboratory culture testing with inline real-time detection to eliminate widespread recalls and public health hazards [PS].
2. **100% Inline Non-Destructive Scanning**: Build a non-destructive hardware sensing system capable of scanning 100% of products moving on an industrial conveyor without stopping or destroying the items [PS].
3. **Biofilm & Pathogen Detection**: Detect invisible bacterial biofilms and early-stage pathogens including *Salmonella*, *Listeria*, or *E. coli* directly on fresh food surfaces such as poultry and leafy greens [PS].
4. **Overhead Sensor Rig Placement**: Design an inline Hyperspectral Imaging (HSI) sensor rig positioned directly over industrial conveyor lines [PS].
5. **Specialized Illumination**: Integrate specialized Near-Infrared (NIR) or Ultraviolet (UV) light sources to capture distinct spectral signatures of biological contamination [PS].
6. **Embedded Edge Processing**: Integrate high-speed edge-computing processors directly into the sensor housing [PS].
7. **Millisecond-Level Detection Latency**: Process spectral data cubes and flag contaminated items within milliseconds [PS].
8. **Automated Downstream Actuation**: Instantly transmit alert signals to downstream sorting mechanisms (e.g., air ejectors, mechanical arms) to physically isolate hazardous products [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Conveyor Speed & Latency**: Industrial food conveyors move at speeds between 0.5 m/s and 2.0 m/s [KNOWN]. At 1.0 m/s, an item moves 1 mm every millisecond. The total pipeline delay (optical capture + spectral preprocessing + neural inference + GPIO signal propagation) must stay strictly below 30–50 ms to actuate downstream sorters accurately [INFERENCE].
* **Harsh Industrial Environment**: Poultry and leafy green processing plants involve ambient washdown (IP65/IP67 requirement), temperatures ranging from 4°C (chilled poultry rooms) to 35°C, high relative humidity (>85%), and suspended moisture droplets [KNOWN].
* **Optical Interference & Dirty Lenses**: Food splatter, water vapor, and organic residue can accumulate on light sources and optical lenses, skewing spectral reflectance profiles [INFERENCE]. Automatic reference calibration mechanisms (white/dark reference tiles) are mandatory [RESEARCHED].
* **Product Variability**: Leafy greens present variable leaf orientations, overlap, and variable chlorophyll density [RESEARCHED]. Poultry carcasses present natural fat, moisture, and collagen variations [RESEARCHED]. Algorithms must distinguish biological pathogen signatures from benign tissue variations [INFERENCE].
* **Hardware Cost Constraints**: Commercial off-the-shelf industrial hyperspectral cameras (e.g., Specim FX10) cost $25,000–$50,000 [RESEARCHED]. For an SIH hackathon team, hardware costs must be minimized through custom multispectral/diffraction-grating line-scan prototypes [ASSUMPTION].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Multi-Band Optoelectronic Strobe Array with Hardcoded Band-Ratios (Hardware Rule-Based)
* **Mechanism**: Custom optical sensor array using discrete LEDs at specific wavebands (e.g., 365 nm UV, 660 nm, 680 nm, 970 nm) and photo-diode/multispectral line-sensor arrays. Hardware logic calculates normalized difference band ratios (e.g., $(F_{665} - F_{680}) / (F_{665} + F_{680})$) [RESEARCHED].
* **Required Data**: Pre-calibrated spectral ratio thresholds for target substrates [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: Microcontroller (STM32/ESP32) + High-speed Multispectral Line Sensor + Analog Comparators [INFERENCE].
* **Pros**: Sub-millisecond latency (<2 ms), low hardware cost (<$300), minimal power [INFERENCE].
* **Cons**: Low spatial resolution, prone to false positives from natural substrate variation (e.g., leaf vein vs. biofilm), cannot adapt to complex non-linear spectral overlaps [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Pushbroom HSI + Embedded Edge AI (Spatial-Spectral 1D/3D-CNN) [RECOMMENDED]
* **Mechanism**: Line-scan hyperspectral camera capturing continuous spectra across a narrow line. Data streams into an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin Edge AI module. A 1D-CNN processes individual pixel spectra, while a 2D spatial context filter eliminates surface glare and boundary false positives [RESEARCHED]. Dual-excitation light source (UV-A 365 nm + NIR 850–950 nm) illuminates the scanline [RESEARCHED].
* **Required Data**: Hyperspectral data cubes of contaminated and uncontaminated produce/poultry [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: Pushbroom Spectrograph + CMOS Line Sensor + Dual NIR/UV Strobe + NVIDIA Jetson Orin + TensorRT C++ Engine + GPIO Driver [INFERENCE].
* **Pros**: High spatial and spectral resolution, high accuracy (>95%), adaptability to multiple pathogens and substrates, millisecond-level execution (10–25 ms) [RESEARCHED].
* **Cons**: Higher hardware complexity and moderate component cost ($1,200–$2,500) [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: High-Resolution HSI Capture with Cloud Offload Analytics
* **Mechanism**: High-resolution hyperspectral camera captures spatial-spectral cubes and streams raw data over 5G/Wi-Fi 6 to a cloud GPU cluster for deep 3D-ResNet inference [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data**: Large-scale continuous spectral video streams [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture**: HSI Camera + GigE Vision Interface + Local Frame Grabber + 5G Gateway + Cloud GPU (NVIDIA A100) [INFERENCE].
* **Pros**: Unlimited computational power, complex ensemble models [INFERENCE].
* **Cons**: Completely fails millisecond constraint due to network transmission latency (50–300 ms), high ongoing bandwidth cost, vulnerable to network drops in factory environments [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 2: Dual-Excitation Pushbroom Line-Scan HSI with TensorRT-Accelerated Edge AI on NVIDIA Jetson** [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 (Hardware Rule-Based)** was rejected because fixed band-ratios fail to account for non-uniform surface geometry, wet film reflection, and variable pigment density in fresh produce and poultry skin, leading to unacceptable false-positive rates [INFERENCE].
* **Approach 3 (Cloud Offload)** was rejected because streaming uncompressed hyperspectral line data (often >500 Mbps per line) introduces network latency ranging from 50 to 300 ms, violating the millisecond response time required to trigger downstream physical ejectors [PS, INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative
1. **Illumination & Surface Scanning**: As food items move under the overhead rig, synchronized 365 nm UV-A LEDs and 850–1050 nm NIR LEDs illuminate a 1 mm wide scanline across the conveyor belt [INFERENCE].
2. **Optical Capture**: Light reflected and emitted from the surface enters the line-scan optical bench, passing through a slit and diffraction grating that separates the light into 128 discrete spectral bands across the CMOS detector array [RESEARCHED].
3. **Edge Ingestion**: The raw frame (1024 spatial pixels $\times$ 128 spectral channels) is transferred via MIPI-CSI / GigE Vision directly into Jetson shared memory via Direct Memory Access (DMA) at 100 Hz line rate [INFERENCE].
4. **Radiometric Preprocessing**: Jetson GPU CUDA kernels subtract dark current background noise and normalize the raw count against pre-stored white reference panel responses [RESEARCHED].
5. **Spectral Inference**: The preprocessed line matrix is passed to C++ TensorRT engine. The 1D-CNN classifies each pixel into *Clean*, *Salmonella*, *Listeria*, *E. coli*, or *Background* [INFERENCE].
6. **Positional Queueing & Actuation**: If contiguous pathogen pixels exceed the spatial rejection threshold, the system flags a contamination event. Using rotary encoder timing, a hardware pulse is scheduled for GPIO pin activation precisely when the target reaches the pneumatic ejectors downstream [INFERENCE].
7. **Pneumatic Rejection**: The high-speed solenoid opens an air valve for 20 ms, blasting the contaminated item off the main line into an isolated waste bin [INFERENCE].
8. **Logging & Sync**: The event coordinates, spectral signature summary, timestamp, and camera snapshot are recorded to a local edge database and published to the plant floor MQTT broker [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### ML Component 1: 1D-CNN Spatial-Spectral Pixel Classifier
* **Purpose**: Classify each spatial pixel along the line-scan as clean food surface, *Salmonella*, *Listeria*, *E. coli*, or conveyor background [INFERENCE].
* **Model Type**: 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN) operating on 128-element spectral vectors per pixel [RESEARCHED].
* **Input**: Radiometrically calibrated spectral reflectance/fluorescence array $[1 \times 128]$ [INFERENCE].
* **Output**: Class probability vector $[P_{\text{clean}}, P_{\text{salmonella}}, P_{\text{listeria}}, P_{\text{ecoli}}, P_{\text{bg}}]$ [INFERENCE].
* **Data Required**: 50,000+ labeled spectral signatures acquired across diverse food samples and bacterial growth stages [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI Is Preferable**: Bacterial biofilm signatures overlap with host substrate spectra in complex, non-linear ways. Simple linear thresholding suffers high false-positive rates due to varying fat/water content [RESEARCHED].
* **Evaluation**: Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC-AUC) curve evaluated on independent food matrix validation sets [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode**: False Positive (clean food discarded) leading to minor yield loss; False Negative (contaminated item passes) posing health hazard [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight**: Periodic manual swab sampling and PCR audit verification of rejected vs. accepted batches [INFERENCE].

### Explicit Non-AI Callout
* **Spatial Rejection Timing & Rotary Encoder Scheduling**: **DO NOT USE AI**. Rejection timing must be governed strictly by deterministic spatial kinematics: $T_{\text{delay}} = \frac{D_{\text{camera-to-ejector}}}{V_{\text{belt}}}$. Utilizing machine learning for hardware delay estimation introduces non-deterministic jitter and physical timing failures [INFERENCE].

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Schema Design (Edge DuckDB / SQLite)

```sql
CREATE TABLE inspection_batches (
    batch_id VARCHAR(64) PRIMARY KEY,
    start_time TIMESTAMP,
    food_type VARCHAR(32), -- 'poultry', 'spinach', 'lettuce'
    target_speed_m_s REAL,
    total_items_scanned INTEGER DEFAULT 0,
    total_rejections INTEGER DEFAULT 0
);

CREATE TABLE spectral_events (
    event_id VARCHAR(64) PRIMARY KEY,
    batch_id VARCHAR(64) REFERENCES inspection_batches(batch_id),
    timestamp TIMESTAMP,
    spatial_x_mm REAL,
    pathogen_flag VARCHAR(32), -- 'Salmonella', 'Listeria', 'E_Coli'
    confidence_score REAL,
    peak_fluorescence_ratio REAL,
    raw_spectral_vector BLOB, -- Compressed 128-band array
    rejection_status VARCHAR(16) -- 'EJECTED', 'FAILED_ACTUATION'
);
```

### Offline & Low-Network Resiliency
* **Local Edge Autonomy**: The Jetson edge unit operates completely standalone. All ML models, C++ runtimes, and local event databases reside on onboard NVMe SSD storage [INFERENCE].
* **Buffering & Sync**: Network connectivity loss to plant server does not interrupt physical line scanning or ejection. Spectral logs buffer locally in DuckDB. When local network reconnects, an offline sync daemon pushes telemetry via MQTT in background batches [INFERENCE].
* **Bandwidth Reduction**: Raw high-speed spectral line video streams are NOT stored permanently. Only aggregated batch statistics and low-dimensional spectral BLOBs of confirmed rejection events are logged, reducing data generation from 20 GB/hour to <50 MB/hour [INFERENCE].
