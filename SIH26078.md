## 1. Executive Snapshot

Identifying and tracking extreme weather anomalies (such as severe cyclones, heat domes, and cold waves) across medium-range forecast horizons (3 to 10 days) [PS] using global Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) outputs is computationally intensive and currently relies on manual expert interpretation [PS]. Standard deterministic forecasts become unreliable over medium ranges due to atmospheric chaos [PS], while conventional deep learning architectures (e.g., standard CNNs or U-Nets) suffer from spectral smoothing, which artificially averages out localized high-intensity rainfall and wind peaks [PS]. This system solves these operational bottlenecks by pairing a Stage 1 Spherical Graph Neural Network (GNN) on an icosahedral mesh [PS] to automatically detect, track, and bound 4D extreme weather anomalies from 12 km NCMRWF Global Ensemble (NEPS-G) forecasts [PS], with a Stage 2 Physics-Constrained Generative Diffusion Model [PS] that downscales isolated threat bounding boxes to a hyper-local 5 km impact grid without suppressing extreme value amplitudes [PS]. The platform serves disaster response agencies like the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) [PS] and agricultural decision-makers with pinpoint 5 km radial alerts [PS], decreasing alert fatigue and enabling targeted proactive disaster mitigation [PS].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The explicit problem statement and technical design requirements provided in the source specification are deconstructed below into atomic, traceable units:

*   **[REQ-1] Computational & Manual Bottleneck in NWP Analysis**: Processing and tracking geographic footprints of extreme weather anomalies (e.g., cyclones, heat domes, cold waves) within multi-gigabyte global NWP outputs is computationally intensive and heavily reliant on manual visual inspection [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Forecasters must manually parse multi-member, multi-variable 4D NetCDF/GRIB2 ensemble arrays across lead times, delaying response times [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: The system must feature an automated batch ingestion and screening core that parses ensemble streams without human intervention [INFERENCE].
*   **[REQ-2] Medium-Range Forecast Uncertainty**: In 3- to 10-day forecast windows, atmospheric chaotic dynamics render single deterministic forecast runs highly uncertain [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Single-run predictions lose skill beyond 3 days; multi-member ensemble probability distributions must be processed directly [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: The ingestion pipeline must process 4D Ensemble Prediction Systems (EPS) data—specifically the 23-member NCMRWF Global Ensemble (NEPS-G) at 12 km resolution [PS], [RESEARCHED].
*   **[REQ-3] Spectral Smoothing Deficit in Standard AI**: Standard deep learning models (e.g., standard CNNs, U-Nets) suffer from spectral smoothing, averaging out spatial gradients and destroying extreme amplitudes (peak rainfall/wind) [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Minimizing mean squared error (MSE) losses causes models to output smooth average fields, underpredicting catastrophic extreme values [KNOWN].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Standard MSE loss U-Nets must be rejected for downscaling in favor of generative or probabilistic frameworks [INFERENCE].
*   **[REQ-4] Resolution Gap**: A critical operational gap exists between broad, coarse 12 km global ensemble grids and localized, high-fidelity threat tracking [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: A 12 km grid box (144 km²) cannot resolve localized flash floods or cloudburst footprints [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: The system must downscale coarse 12 km candidate anomaly regions to a 5 km subgrid impact zone [PS].
*   **[REQ-5] Stage 1 Spherical Anomaly Tracking (GNN)**: The system must map 12 km NEPS-G grids directly onto an icosahedral spherical mesh to eliminate geographic polar projections/distortions, calculate the Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) against a 30-year historical ERA5 baseline distribution, and output dynamic 4D bounding boxes around evolving anomalies [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Message-passing GNNs operate on non-Euclidean spherical meshes to calculate normalized deviation quantiles and draw 3D space + 1D time bounding boxes [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Use Deep Graph Library (DGL) or PyG to map GRIB2 lat/lon grids to an icosahedral graph (e.g., subdivision level 5/6) [RESEARCHED], [ASSUMPTION].
*   **[REQ-6] Stage 2 Amplitude-Preserving Downscaling (Diffusion)**: The cropped macro-scale bounding box must be passed to a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model to derive a 5 km subgrid array without flattening peak amplitudes [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Iterative generative score-matching generates high-resolution fields conditioned on the 12 km coarse bounding box and static topography [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Implement a conditional DDPM/EDM backbone via Hugging Face `diffusers` / PyTorch [PS], [RESEARCHED].
*   **[REQ-7] Physics-Informed Constraints**: Fluid dynamics and thermodynamic conservation laws must be embedded directly into the loss functions to penalize physically impossible states (e.g., severe rainfall lacking moisture convergence) [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Loss terms must calculate spatial derivatives (divergence, flux) via automatic differentiation or finite differences during training [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Integrate MetPy or custom PyTorch differential operators into the diffusion model training loss [PS], [INFERENCE].
*   **[REQ-8] Tooling & Framework Requirements**: The technical stack must utilize PyTorch/JAX (with custom physics losses), Deep Graph Library (DGL), Hugging Face Diffusers, Xarray, Dask, MetPy, and Cartopy [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Mandatory reliance on open-source scientific Python geospatial and AI stacks [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Design data layers strictly around Xarray dataset handles backed by Dask parallel chunking [INFERENCE].
*   **[REQ-9] Target Datasets**: System must use ERA5 / IMDAA reanalysis as historical baselines and historical NCUM (12 km deterministic) + NEPS-G (12 km global ensemble) datasets for training/testing on documented historical events (e.g., Cyclone Amphan, North India heatwaves) [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Models must be validated against real severe weather benchmark events over the Indian subcontinent [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication*: Data ingestion modules must handle GRIB2/NetCDF files from NCMRWF RDS or IMDAA archives [RESEARCHED].
*   **[REQ-10] Key System Deliverables**:
    *   a. *Tracking Core*: Production-ready Spatio-Temporal GNN module outputting 4D bounding boxes [PS].
    *   b. *Downscaling Core*: Generative diffusion module outputting 5 km arrays retaining extreme amplitudes [PS].
    *   c. *Visualization Dashboard*: Web UI translating 5 km centroid arrays into clean geographic layers [PS].
    *   d. *Alerting API*: REST API placing pinpoint coordinates at anomaly cores and issuing spatial alerts across a 5 km radius [PS].
*   **[REQ-11] Domain Impact**: Eliminate alert fatigue for NDRF first responders by transitioning from generic district-wide warnings to targeted 5 km radial warnings; provide rural farming communities with 3- to 10-day lead times for frost, hail, or heatwaves; and run low-latency live inference on standard cloud GPUs [PS].
    *   *Interpretation*: Inference must take seconds on standard single-GPU cloud nodes (e.g., NVIDIA T4 / A10G) to ensure low-cost deployment [PS], [ASSUMPTION].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Geographic & Polar Distortion Realities**: Standard equirectangular (latitude-longitude) grids distort grid box areas near the poles and northern Indian borders (Himalayan Arc), severely biasing convolution operations in standard CNNs [KNOWN]. Mapping data onto an icosahedral mesh preserves equal-area node relationships across the sphere [PS], [RESEARCHED].
*   **Computational Latency vs. Lead Time**: Operational medium-range forecasting requires post-processing to complete within minutes after NCMRWF publishes daily NEPS-G ensemble runs (00 UTC and 12 UTC) [RESEARCHED]. Traditional dynamical regional downscaling (e.g., running WRF at 4 km) takes hours on HPCs [KNOWN]. The AI pipeline must execute inference on standard cloud GPUs (e.g., single NVIDIA A10G/T4) in under 60 seconds [PS].
*   **Data Bandwidth & File Volume**: Daily NEPS-G outputs comprise 23 ensemble members (1 control + 22 perturbed) across multiple pressure and surface levels, yielding files exceeding 10–50 GB per run [RESEARCHED]. The pipeline must stream and crop data in-memory using Xarray/Dask to prevent disk IO bottlenecks [PS], [INFERENCE].
*   **Network & Connectivity Constraints for Field Users**: NDRF personnel and rural agricultural officers operate in remote regions where cellular bandwidth during disaster events is spotty (2G/3G or intermittent 4G) [KNOWN]. The Alerting API must transmit lightweight vector GeoJSON payloads (<50 KB) rather than heavy raster images [INFERENCE].
*   **Operational Reliability & Zero Misinformation**: False alarms lead to "alert fatigue" and non-compliance during actual emergencies [PS]. The system must maintain strict probabilistic confidence metrics and physics checks before elevating an alert to "Severe" [PS], [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach A: Flat-Grid Convolutional Transformer with MSE/Perceptual Loss
*   **Mechanism**: Regrid 12 km NEPS-G data onto flat 2D lat-lon regular grids. Use a Vision Transformer (ViT) to extract features and a UNet++ to output downscaled 5 km grids [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros**: Simple implementation; familiar PyTorch CV libraries [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons**: Suffer from severe polar projection distortion near northern Indian borders [KNOWN]; MSE loss causes spectral smoothing [PS]; lacks physical constraints [PS].
*   **Feasibility**: Easy for hackathon, but fails explicit problem constraints [INFERENCE].

### Approach B: Pure Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) Downscaler on Icosahedral Mesh
*   **Mechanism**: Map ensemble data to an icosahedral mesh. Use a Graph Generator and Discriminator to produce high-resolution spatial details [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros**: GANs preserve sharp spatial gradients better than MSE models [KNOWN].
*   **Cons**: Highly unstable training dynamics (mode collapse); prone to hallucinating extreme weather features where no physical trigger exists [KNOWN]; hard to embed fluid dynamic loss penalties [INFERENCE].
*   **Feasibility**: Medium hackathon effort; high risk of training instability [INFERENCE].

### Approach C (Proposed & Selected): Hybrid Two-Stage Pipeline — Spherical GNN Anomaly Tracker + Physics-Constrained Conditional Generative Diffusion Model
*   **Mechanism**:
    1.  *Stage 1*: Map 12 km NEPS-G onto a spherical icosahedral mesh using Deep Graph Library (DGL) [PS]. Execute message passing to calculate the Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) against a 30-year ERA5 baseline [PS]. Derive dynamic 4D spatiotemporal bounding boxes ($x, y, z, t$) isolating moving extreme anomalies over days 3–10 [PS].
    2.  *Stage 2*: Crop the 12 km coarse bounding box and feed it as conditioning input to a conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM/EDM) [PS]. The generative score-matching process derives a 5 km subgrid array [PS].
    3.  *Physics Loss Integration*: Enforce mass conservation ($\nabla \cdot \vec{v} = 0$) and moisture flux convergence equations as loss penalties during diffusion model training [PS].
*   **Pros**: Zero polar grid distortion [PS]; diffusion eliminates spectral smoothing and preserves peak amplitudes [PS]; physics constraints guarantee thermodynamic sanity [PS]; GNN cropping vastly reduces GPU compute load [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons**: Dual-stage AI pipeline requires structured orchestration during training [INFERENCE].
*   **Feasibility**: Fully achievable by a 4-6 student team using PyTorch, DGL, Hugging Face `diffusers`, and Xarray [PS], [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The recommended solution is **Approach C: Hybrid Spherical GNN Anomaly Tracker + Physics-Constrained Conditional Generative Diffusion Model** [PS].

### Justification & Rejection of Alternatives
1.  **Why Approach A was rejected**: Flat 2D rectangular lat-lon projections distort area metrics across latitudes. Furthermore, standard MSE-based neural architectures mathematically converge to the conditional mean of the dataset, causing **spectral smoothing** [PS]. This flattens the extreme peaks of wind speeds and precipitation rates—making it impossible for forecasters to issue actionable, hyper-local life-saving alerts [PS].
2.  **Why Approach B was rejected**: GAN architectures suffer from mode collapse and training instability when applied to complex multi-variable meteorological fields [KNOWN]. More critically, GAN discriminators often incentivize the generator to create visually plausible but physically impossible weather features ("hallucinated cloudbursts") that lack support from lower-level moisture convergence vectors [INFERENCE].
3.  **Why Approach C Wins**:
    *   The **icosahedral GNN mesh** handles the spherical nature of Earth effortlessly without polar coordinate singularities [PS], [RESEARCHED].
    *   The **GNN tracking core** calculates the Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) across 23 ensemble members against a 30-year ERA5 baseline, drawing automated 4D bounding boxes around evolving anomalies [PS], [RESEARCHED].
    *   By cropping the region of interest before downscaling, the computational burden on the generative stage drops by over 80%, enabling inference on a single standard cloud GPU in seconds [PS], [INFERENCE].
    *   The **conditional generative diffusion model** generates fine-grained 5 km spatial realizations that retain high-amplitude tails [PS].
    *   The **physics-informed loss function** acts as a hard scientific boundary, penalizing gradient updates whenever generated fields violate mass conservation or moisture continuity [PS].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1.  **Ingestion Phase**: Every 12 hours, raw 23-member 12 km NEPS-G ensemble datasets (GRIB2 format containing 2m temperature, 10m wind $u/v$ vectors, convective precipitation, specific humidity $q$, and geopotential height $Z500$) are loaded via `Xarray` and parallelized using `Dask` workers [PS], [RESEARCHED].
2.  **Icosahedral Mapping & EFI Calculation**: The ingestion worker maps the rectangular grid coordinates to nodes on an icosahedral spherical mesh generated in DGL [PS]. For each mesh node, the system computes the Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) by integrating the difference between the 23-member cumulative distribution function (CDF) and the 30-year historical ERA5/IMDAA model climate (M-climate) distribution [PS], [RESEARCHED].
3.  **Spatiotemporal GNN Bounding Box Tracking**: The graph message-passing network analyzes EFI threshold clusters ($EFI > 0.7$) across temporal forecast steps ($T+72\text{h}$ to $T+240\text{h}$) [PS], [RESEARCHED]. It predicts the temporal trajectory and draws a tightly cropped 4D bounding box around the localized anomaly footprint [PS].
4.  **Generative Diffusion Downscaling**: The cropped 12 km 4D tensor is passed as conditioning context into the Stage 2 Conditional Diffusion Model [PS]. Starting from Gaussian noise, the model performs iterative reverse denoising over 20 DDIM steps [RESEARCHED]. Static topographical features (SRTM 5 km DEM) are fed into cross-attention layers [INFERENCE].
5.  **Physics Loss Evaluation**: During inference step verification and training, custom differential operators evaluate mass conservation (divergence of horizontal wind fields) and moisture flux convergence ($\nabla \cdot (q\mathbf{u})$) [PS], [INFERENCE]. Non-physical noise states are penalized [PS].
6.  **Centroid Extraction & Alert Dispatch**: The downscaled 5 km output array is evaluated for extreme thresholds ($>99\text{th}$ percentile) [INFERENCE]. The mathematical centroid coordinates of the severe core are calculated, generating a 5 km geographical impact radius [PS].
7.  **API Serving & Visualization**: The FastAPI backend publishes GeoJSON alert structures to registered disaster management systems and updates the interactive Mapbox GL frontend dashboard for forecasters [PS], [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### 1. Stage 1: Spherical GNN Anomaly Tracker
*   **Exact Problem Solved**: Eliminates manual visual scanning of 23-member EPS outputs by automatically isolating 4D spatiotemporal extreme anomaly trajectories [PS].
*   **Input**: 4D NEPS-G 12 km grid tensor ($[B, \text{Members}=23, \text{Vars}=6, \text{Nodes}, \text{Time}=10\text{ days}]$) mapped to icosahedral mesh [PS].
*   **Output**: 4D bounding box coordinates ($[\text{Lat}_{\min}, \text{Lat}_{\max}, \text{Lon}_{\min}, \text{Lon}_{\max}, T_{\start}, T_{\end}]$) and spatial EFI probability heatmap [PS].
*   **Why Simpler Rule Fails**: Fixed thresholding fails because extreme weather anomalies are non-stationary, scale-dependent, and multi-variable (e.g., heatwaves combine high surface temperature and low humidity, while cyclones combine low pressure and high vorticity) [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required**: NEPS-G 12 km ensemble outputs and 30-year ERA5/IMDAA reanalysis baselines [PS].
*   **Evaluation Metric**: Bounding Box Intersection-over-Union (IoU) against expert meteorologist track analyses and CRPS (Continuous Ranked Probability Score) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Failure Mode & Oversight**: If GNN misses a subtle anomaly, rule-based fallback flags any grid node where raw EFI exceeds 0.85 [INFERENCE].

### 2. Stage 2: Physics-Constrained Conditional Generative Diffusion Downscaler
*   **Exact Problem Solved**: Derives hyper-local 5 km subgrid arrays from 12 km coarse bounding boxes without spectral smoothing or flattening extreme value amplitudes [PS].
*   **Input**: Cropped 12 km low-res anomaly tensor + SRTM 5 km Digital Elevation Model (DEM) conditioning vector [PS], [INFERENCE].
*   **Output**: Downscaled 5 km resolution subgrid tensor ($[\text{Vars}=3, H_{5\km}, W_{5\km}]$) [PS].
*   **Why Simpler Rule / Bilinear Interpolation Fails**: Linear interpolation or MSE-based UNets produce overly smooth averages, underestimating extreme rainfall intensities by up to 50% [PS], [KNOWN].
*   **Data Required**: Historical paired 12 km NCUM / NEPS-G inputs and high-resolution 4 km/5 km IMDAA / NEPS-R ground-truth targets [PS], [RESEARCHED].
*   **Evaluation Metric**: Power Spectral Density (PSD) matching across wavenumbers, High-Quantile Root Mean Square Error ($RMSE_{>95th}$), and Brier Score [RESEARCHED].
*   **Failure Mode & Oversight**: Physical loss terms trigger automatic alerts if generated fields violate mass conservation equations by $>5\%$ [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
*   **EFI Baseline Quantile Lookup**: Simple pre-computed array indexing against the 30-year ERA5 climate distribution [INFERENCE].
*   **Spatial Radial Geofencing**: Standard PostGIS geospatial buffer operations (`ST_Buffer`) to establish exact 5 km impact zones [INFERENCE].
*   **Alert Routing & Notifications**: Rule-based notification logic based on administrative boundaries [INFERENCE].
