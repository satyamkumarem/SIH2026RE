## 1. Executive Snapshot

Bovine mastitis causes annual economic losses exceeding ₹6,000–14,000 crore in India due to reduced milk yield, milk discarding, veterinary costs, and premature culling [KNOWN]. The traditional paradigm relies on post-symptomatic reactive treatment, which exacerbates antimicrobial resistance (AMR) and leaves dairy farmers with irreparable losses [PS]. This proposed system solves the early forecasting challenge by introducing an integrated hardware-software IoT and AI platform that captures multi-parameter physical, biological, and environmental signals to forecast individual animal and herd-level mastitis risk 7–14 days before clinical manifestation [PS]. Combining an affordable multi-sensor milk and physiological monitoring node with an offline-first mobile app and cloud-based predictive engine, the system delivers actionable preventive interventions, multilingual notifications, and GIS disease surveillance for smallholders, cooperatives, and veterinarians [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Intermittent Rural Connectivity:** Over 60% of rural Indian dairy pockets experience frequent 2G/3G fallbacks or total cellular outages [KNOWN]. *Impact:* Mobile apps must be offline-first, queuing telemetry locally in SQLite and syncing when connected [INFERENCE].
2. **Power Fluctuations:** Rural grid power supply is subject to frequent load-shedding (4–8 hours daily in remote regions) [KNOWN]. *Impact:* Hardware sensors must be battery-operated (Li-ion 18650) with solar micro-charging support [PS] [INFERENCE].
3. **Corrosive & Harsh Field Conditions:** Barn environments contain high humidity, ammonia, dust, animal dung, and water exposure during washdowns [KNOWN]. *Impact:* Sensor enclosures must meet IP67 ingress protection standards; milk probes must use food-grade 316 stainless steel electrodes [INFERENCE].
4. **Digital & Language Literacy:** Smallholder farmers may have limited textual literacy and limited familiarity with complex software interfaces [KNOWN]. *Impact:* UX must use color coding (Green/Yellow/Orange/Red), intuitive icons, voice-based readouts (Text-to-Speech), and local languages [PS] [INFERENCE].
5. **Cost Constraints:** Indian smallholder farmers operate on thin margins; hardware costs exceeding ₹2,000–3,000 per animal face severe adoption barriers [KNOWN]. *Impact:* Hardware design must utilize low-cost microcontrollers (ESP32) and shared inline cluster sensors rather than permanent individual per-cow inline meters [INFERENCE].
6. **Diverse Breeds & Animal Physiology:** Indian dairying involves both *Bos indicus* (indigenous cows like Gir, Sahiwal), *Bos taurus* crossbreds, and *Bubalus bubalis* (water buffaloes like Murrah) [KNOWN]. Normal baseline milk conductivity and SCC differ substantially across these species [RESEARCHED]. *Impact:* Predictive models must accept breed/species as a conditioning variable [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach A: Rule/Threshold-Based Local Hardware Sensor System
* **Mechanism:** Single inline probe measuring electrical conductivity and temperature during milking. Triggers a red LED and buzzer when conductivity exceeds a fixed threshold (e.g., >6.5 mS/cm) [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data:** Live EC and milk temperature [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture:** Standalone microcontroller (Arduino/ESP32) with local display [INFERENCE].
* **Pros:** Highly simple, low cost (~₹1,200), zero cloud dependency [INFERENCE].
* **Cons:** High false-alarm rate; cannot predict mastitis 7–14 days in advance (triggers only 1–3 days before symptoms when inflammation is severe); no multi-source data integration [RESEARCHED].
* **Feasibility:** Hackathon: High | Production: Low (fails core requirement of 7–14 day forecasting) [INFERENCE].

### Approach B: Computer Vision & Infrared Thermal Imaging System
* **Mechanism:** Thermal imaging camera attached to a smartphone or barn gantry captures udder skin surface thermograms. Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) detects temperature hot spots associated with udder tissue inflammation [RESEARCHED].
* **Required Data:** Udder thermal images labeled with clinical status [RESEARCHED].
* **Architecture:** Mobile app with embedded TFLite image classification model [INFERENCE].
* **Pros:** Non-invasive, quick cow-side check [INFERENCE].
* **Cons:** High hardware cost for thermal camera attachments (~₹25,000+); sensitive to ambient temperature and mud on udder; cannot measure milk composition or yield changes [RESEARCHED].
* **Feasibility:** Hackathon: Moderate | Production: Low (unaffordable thermal hardware for smallholders) [INFERENCE].

### Approach C (Recommended): Multi-Modal Edge-Cloud Hybrid IoT & Machine Learning Platform
* **Mechanism:** Low-cost multi-sensor node (Milk EC, pH, Temp + Collar Accelerometer) transmits telemetry to an offline-first mobile app. Mobile app aggregates data with manual hygiene logs and syncs with a Cloud ML engine. Temporal Gradient Boosting (LightGBM) & Random Forest models analyze 14-day longitudinal trends, animal metadata, and THI to output individual/herd risk scores 7–14 days ahead [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data:** Time-series milk EC, pH, temperature, yield, collar activity, parity, lactation stage, ambient THI, historical lab SCC [INFERENCE].
* **Architecture:** Microcontroller node + BLE/Wi-Fi + Mobile App (SQLite Edge Cache) + Cloud FastAPI Backend + PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB + LightGBM Predictive Engine + Leaflet GIS [INFERENCE].
* **Pros:** Meets ALL explicit requirements; achieves 7–14 day forecast window via multi-variable trend analysis; affordable hardware footprint; works offline; provides GIS tracking [INFERENCE].
* **Cons:** Higher technical complexity across IoT, ML, and mobile domains [INFERENCE].
* **Feasibility:** Hackathon: High (with modular hardware/simulation) | Production: High [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

**Selected Approach:** **Approach C (Multi-Modal Edge-Cloud Hybrid IoT & Machine Learning Platform)** [INFERENCE].

### Justification
Single-parameter threshold systems (Approach A) fail because electrical conductivity alone fluctuates due to diet, stage of lactation, and breed, causing unacceptable false-positive rates and detecting infection only 1–3 days prior to clinical onset [RESEARCHED]. Computer vision thermal systems (Approach B) are too expensive for smallholders and vulnerable to environmental fouling (dirt/dung on udders) [RESEARCHED].

Approach C combines multi-sensor physical telemetry (EC + pH + udder/body temp + yield) with contextual risk metadata (breed, lactation stage, farm hygiene) [INFERENCE]. By training a time-series Gradient Boosting model (LightGBM) on 14-day rolling feature vectors (e.g., standard deviation of EC across quarters, rate of pH drift, rumination drop rate), Approach C isolates subtle subclinical trajectories up to 14 days before overt clinical mastitis manifests [RESEARCHED] [INFERENCE].

### Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative

1. **Sensing & Data Ingestion:** During milking, the ESP32 hardware node samples milk electrical conductivity, pH, and temperature. The collar node streams accelerometer/gyroscopic activity data. Telemetry is formatted into JSON and transmitted via BLE to the farmer's mobile app or directly via GSM/MQTT to the cloud broker [INFERENCE].
2. **Local Edge Buffering (Offline Mode):** If the smartphone lacks internet access, the React Native app buffers the telemetry in an embedded SQLite database. Once connectivity is restored, an automated background service syncs queued data with the cloud API [INFERENCE].
3. **Data Normalization & Feature Engineering:** The FastAPI backend ingests the raw telemetry, joins it with historical animal metadata (breed, lactation stage, parity) stored in PostgreSQL, and computes time-series rolling features (e.g., 7-day EC moving average, quarter EC differential ratio, rumination drop index) in TimescaleDB [INFERENCE].
4. **AI Risk Prediction Engine:** The engineered feature vector is evaluated by the pre-trained LightGBM classification model. The model outputs a continuous risk score (0.0 to 1.0) and assigns the animal to one of four risk states (*No Risk*, *Low Risk*, *Moderate Risk*, *High Risk*) [INFERENCE]. Simultaneously, a secondary regression model estimates the Somatic Cell Count (SCC) range [INFERENCE].
5. **Alerting & Decision Support Generation:** If an animal transitions to *Moderate Risk* or *High Risk*, the Decision Engine generates actionable preventive guidelines (e.g., "Quarter 3 showing subclinical drift: Apply post-milking teat dip, administer vitamin E supplement, schedule CMT test"). An FCM push notification and fallback SMS are immediately dispatched to the farmer and assigned veterinarian [INFERENCE].
6. **GIS Aggregation & Surveillance:** Aggregated farm risk scores are pushed to the Web GIS Dashboard, displaying regional risk clusters and disease trends for milk union officials and DAHD authorities [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: 7–14 Day Mastitis Risk Predictive Model
* **Purpose:** Predict individual animal subclinical mastitis risk state 7–14 days before clinical symptoms [PS].
* **Model Type:** LightGBM Classifier (Gradient Boosted Decision Trees) [INFERENCE].
* **Input:** 14-day rolling window features: EC quarter differential, pH trend slope, body temp delta, rumination drop %, milk yield variance, breed, parity, lactation stage, ambient THI [INFERENCE].
* **Output:** Classification into 4 categories (*No Risk*, *Low Risk*, *Moderate Risk*, *High Risk*) + Probability score (0.0 to 1.0) [PS] [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data:** Longitudinal multi-parameter milk and health records from infected and healthy animals [INFERENCE].
* **Why AI:** Rule-based thresholds fail because individual baseline conductivity varies by breed, season, and lactation stage. ML captures complex non-linear interactions across 10+ time-varying signals [INFERENCE].
* **Evaluation Metrics:** ROC-AUC (>0.85 target), Sensitivity/Recall (>80% for High Risk), False Positive Rate (<10%) [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode & Fallback:** If sensor telemetry is incomplete, model degrades gracefully to baseline metadata rules; displays "Insufficient Data for 14-day Forecast" [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight:** High-risk flags prompt the veterinarian to perform a California Mastitis Test (CMT) or lab culture before administering medical therapy [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Subclinical Somatic Cell Count (SCC) Estimator
* **Purpose:** Estimate milk SCC levels ($cells/mL$) non-invasively using physical milk parameters [PS].
* **Model Type:** XGBoost Regressor [INFERENCE].
* **Input:** Milk electrical conductivity, pH, temperature, milk yield, days in milk (DIM) [INFERENCE].
* **Output:** Estimated SCC value (e.g., $250,000\ cells/mL$) and tier ($<200k$: Normal, $200k-400k$: Suspect, $>400k$: Subclinical Mastitis) [INFERENCE].
* **Required Data:** Paired milk physical telemetry and lab-verified Fossomatic SCC counts [RESEARCHED].
* **Why AI:** Direct SCC measurement requires expensive optical counters; regression models approximate SCC accurately from physical ionic leaks [RESEARCHED].
* **Evaluation Metrics:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE), $R^2$ Score ($>0.80$ target) [INFERENCE].
* **Failure Mode & Fallback:** Reverts to simple California Mastitis Test (CMT) score manual entry [INFERENCE].
* **Human Oversight:** Lab diagnostic validation recommended when estimated SCC exceeds $400,000\ cells/mL$ [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **Raw Sensor Calibration:** Hardware sensor calibration (e.g., pH/EC ADC voltage conversion) must use deterministic linear interpolation, NOT neural networks [INFERENCE].
* **User Authentication & Authorization:** Security, JWT token validation, and role-based access control must follow strict deterministic security protocols [INFERENCE].
* **Emergency Alert Dispatch Rules:** SMS triggering logic must follow strict deterministic boolean thresholds (e.g., `if risk == 'HIGH': dispatch_sms()`) to guarantee zero silent drops [INFERENCE].

### Core Database Schema (PostgreSQL / TimescaleDB)

```sql
-- Animals Master Table
CREATE TABLE animals (
    animal_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY, -- Pashu Aadhaar / Tag ID
    farm_id VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    breed VARCHAR(50) NOT NULL,
    species VARCHAR(20) CHECK (species IN ('COW', 'BUFFALO')),
    date_of_birth DATE,
    lactation_number INT,
    vaccination_status JSONB,
    created_at TIMESTAMP DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

-- Time-Series Telemetry Table (TimescaleDB Hypertable)
CREATE TABLE milk_telemetry (
    time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    animal_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES animals(animal_id),
    quarter VARCHAR(5) CHECK (quarter IN ('FL', 'FR', 'RL', 'RR', 'COMPOSITE')),
    electrical_conductivity NUMERIC(5,2), -- mS/cm
    milk_ph NUMERIC(3,2),
    milk_temperature NUMERIC(4,2), -- Deg C
    milk_yield_kg NUMERIC(4,2),
    PRIMARY KEY (time, animal_id, quarter)
);
SELECT create_hypertable('milk_telemetry', 'time');

-- Predictive Risk Output Table
CREATE TABLE mastitis_risk_predictions (
    prediction_id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    animal_id VARCHAR(50) REFERENCES animals(animal_id),
    risk_category VARCHAR(20) CHECK (risk_category IN ('NO_RISK', 'LOW_RISK', 'MODERATE_RISK', 'HIGH_RISK')),
    risk_score NUMERIC(3,2), -- 0.00 to 1.00
    estimated_scc INT,
    top_risk_factors JSONB, -- e.g. {"ec_drift": "+22%", "temp_spike": "1.2C"}
    recommended_action TEXT
);
```

### Offline Synchronization & Bandwidth Reduction Strategy
1. **Local Edge Caching:** The React Native app embeds an SQLite database using `WatermelonDB` / `TypeORM`. Telemetry captured in off-grid barns is saved locally with an `is_synced = false` flag [INFERENCE].
2. **Delta Sync Protocol:** When a network connection (Wi-Fi or cellular) is detected, the app initiates a background sync. It packages un-synced JSON records into compressed gzipped batches (reducing payload size by ~80%) [INFERENCE].
3. **Conflict Resolution Strategy:** Server-wins policy for master metadata (e.g., animal profile edits); Client-append policy for time-series sensor readings based on UTC timestamp [INFERENCE].
4. **Exponential Backoff & Retry:** Network requests utilize exponential backoff (e.g., retry after 2s, 4s, 8s, up to 5 minutes max) to prevent battery drain during persistent outages [INFERENCE].
