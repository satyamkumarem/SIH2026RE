## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Operational medium-range numerical weather prediction (NWP) models (such as the NCMRWF Unified Model - NCUM, and Global Ensemble Prediction System - NEPS-G) [RESEARCHED] frequently experience sudden, severe degradations in skill—termed **forecast busts** [PS]—during rapidly evolving, highly nonlinear meteorological regimes [PS]. These regimes include monsoon depressions, tropical cyclones, western disturbances, active/break monsoon transitions, and extreme heatwaves [PS]. When an NWP model suffers a forecast bust, operational forecasters at the National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF) [PS] and India Meteorological Department (IMD) [KNOWN] lack automated, real-time diagnostic indicators to identify that the model output for Day 1–10 lead times [PS] is untrustworthy. 

To resolve this critical operational vulnerability, we propose **BustGuard-AI**: a hybrid physics-informed spatiotemporal deep learning and historical error-retrieval platform. BustGuard-AI ingests current NWP model outputs (500 hPa geopotential height, 850 hPa wind vectors, precipitable water, Mean Sea Level Pressure [MSLP], and 24-hour accumulated precipitation) [RESEARCHED], extracts spatial anomaly patterns, compares them against a multi-decadal historical database of forecast error behaviors (using IMDAA regional reanalysis and IMD-NCMRWF gridded observations) [RESEARCHED], and predicts grid-wise forecast confidence (0–100%) and bust probability (0–1.0) for Day 1 to Day 10 lead times [PS]. Integrated with SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) [KNOWN] and domain-specific meteorological attribution, the system provides explainable diagnostic reasons (e.g., "rapid convective growth misrepresentation," "upper-level trough positioning error") [INFERENCE] through an operational web dashboard and REST API [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Geographic Domain & Resolution:** The operational domain covers South Asia and the North Indian Ocean (30°E – 120°E, 15°S – 45°N) [RESEARCHED]. Spatial resolution for medium-range models in India is typically 0.12° (~12 km) or gridded 0.25° (~25 km) [RESEARCHED]. Processing 10-day gridded forecasts over this domain involves millions of grid cells per parameter per lead time [INFERENCE].
*   **Operational Execution Latency:** Operational NWP models run twice daily (00 UTC and 12 UTC) [KNOWN]. The AI bust detection system must finish processing within 5–10 minutes after NWP outputs (GRIB2/NetCDF) are written to disk, ensuring forecasters receive alerts before issuing public bulletins [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Bandwidth & Format Constraints:** Meteorological model outputs are distributed in heavy GRIB2 or NetCDF4 formats [KNOWN]. Downloading raw multi-terabyte model files in real time is infeasible. The solution must use server-side slicing (xarray/cfgrib) and sub-region extraction [INFERENCE].
*   **Explainability Bar:** Operational meteorologists will not accept a "black box" prediction [KNOWN]. The system must frame outputs in standard meteorological language (e.g., "500 hPa Geopotential Height Anomaly", "850 hPa Moisture Flux Divergence") rather than raw ML weights [INFERENCE].
*   **Hardware Realities for Student Teams:** Live inference must run efficiently on standard cloud hardware (e.g., single NVIDIA T4 GPU or 8-core CPU) during hackathon evaluation [ASSUMPTION].

## 6. Research Questions

1.  *How is a "forecast bust" mathematically defined in medium-range weather forecasting over India?*
    *   *Findings:* In literature (e.g., Rodwell et al. 2013, ECMWF), a forecast bust is defined when the Anomaly Correlation Coefficient (ACC) of 500 hPa geopotential height drops below 0.40 (or 40%) at Day 5–6 lead times [RESEARCHED]. For precipitation and tropical cyclones, a bust occurs when categorical threat score drops near zero or track error exceeds 300 km at Day 3–5 [RESEARCHED].
2.  *What publicly available historical NWP forecast vs observation datasets exist for training?*
    *   *Findings:* The primary Indian high-resolution regional reanalysis is **IMDAA** (Indian Monsoon Data Assimilation and Analysis, 12 km resolution, 1979–2020) [RESEARCHED]. Historical ensemble forecasts are accessible via the **ECMWF TIGGE** (THORPEX International Grand Global Ensemble) database, which archives NCMRWF, ECMWF, and NCEP forecasts from 2006 to present [RESEARCHED]. Observed precipitation is available via **IMD-NCMRWF Merged Satellite-Gauge (NMSG)** gridded 0.25° dataset [RESEARCHED].
3.  *What are the primary dynamical drivers of forecast busts over the Indian subcontinent?*
    *   *Findings:* Key drivers include misrepresentation of convective heating in monsoon depressions [RESEARCHED], upper-level trough interaction with tropical moisture during Western Disturbances [RESEARCHED], latent heat release parameterization errors during rapid cyclone intensification [RESEARCHED], and land-surface moisture feedback errors during heatwaves [RESEARCHED].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Statistical Ensemble Spread & Anomaly Thresholding
*   **Mechanism:** Calculate standard deviation across ensemble members (e.g., NEPS-G 23 members) for each grid point and lead time. Flag regions where spread exceeds 2 standard deviations as "high risk" [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros:** Low computational complexity; no complex training needed; fully explainable [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons:** Fails during ensemble under-dispersion (when all ensemble members share identical physics parameterization errors) [RESEARCHED]. Cannot capture complex spatiotemporal error dynamics [INFERENCE].
*   **Feasibility:** Hackathon: High | Production: Medium.

### Approach 2: Pure Deep Learning Spatiotemporal Predictor (3D-UNet / ConvLSTM)
*   **Mechanism:** Feed multi-layer 3D grid stacks of live forecast fields into a 3D-UNet or ConvLSTM model trained end-to-end to output grid-wise RMSE maps and binary bust masks [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros:** High capacity to learn complex nonlinear atmospheric interactions [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons:** Pure "black box"; prone to overfitting on limited historical extreme event samples; lacks explainability required by meteorologists [INFERENCE].
*   **Feasibility:** Hackathon: Medium | Production: Low.

### Approach 3: Hybrid Physics-Informed ML Engine with Historical Error Retrieval & XAI (RECOMMENDED)
*   **Mechanism:** 
    1.  *Feature Extraction:* Extract dynamical anomaly features (e.g., Vorticity, Moisture Convergence, 500 hPa Height Anomalies, Thermal Advection) from current NWP runs [INFERENCE].
    2.  *Historical Memory Retrieval:* Pass spatial embeddings through a FAISS vector store to retrieve top-k historical NWP forecast patterns that exhibited similar synoptic setups [RESEARCHED].
    3.  *Dual-Head Prediction:* Pass combined features and historical error statistics to a Spatial Encoder-Decoder + XGBoost ensemble head predicting: (a) Grid Confidence (0–100%), (b) Bust Probability (0–1.0) for Days 1–10 [INFERENCE].
    4.  *XAI Engine:* Use TreeSHAP / Integrated Gradients to extract driver variables and map them to domain-specific meteorological rules [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros:** Combines historical empirical error ground truth with deep spatial pattern recognition; guaranteed explainability; highly reliable during ensemble under-dispersion [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons:** Requires building a structured vector store of historical forecast error patterns [INFERENCE].
*   **Feasibility:** Hackathon: High | Production: High.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

We select **Approach 3: Hybrid Physics-Informed ML Engine with Historical Error Retrieval & XAI** [INFERENCE].

### Justification
1.  **Direct Alignment with Problem Requirements:** The problem statement explicitly demands comparing "current NWP forecast patterns with historical forecast error behaviour" [PS] and providing "explainable output" [PS]. Approach 3 directly embeds historical error retrieval into the inference loop [INFERENCE].
2.  **Overcoming Ensemble Limitations:** Traditional ensemble spread fails when all members are identically biased [RESEARCHED]. Approach 3 identifies when a live synoptic pattern matches historical situations where the NWP model historically suffered a bust [INFERENCE].
3.  **Why Alternatives Were Rejected:**
    *   *Approach 1 (Statistical Spread)* was rejected because ensemble spread alone misses system-wide model parameterization failures (e.g., underestimating rapid tropical cyclone intensification or land surface dry bias) [RESEARCHED].
    *   *Approach 2 (Pure Black-Box Deep Learning)* was rejected because it fails the explicit requirement for "Explainable output - Key meteorological reasons for low confidence" [PS] and risks generating untrustworthy predictions on out-of-distribution weather events [INFERENCE].

### Data Flow Narrative
1.  **Ingestion:** The system periodically triggers an automated ingestion pipeline (via Celery/Redis) upon the completion of global NWP model runs (00 and 12 UTC) [INFERENCE].
2.  **Preprocessing:** Incoming GRIB2/NetCDF weather grids are ingested using `xarray` and `cfgrib`, clipped to the Indian region (30°E–120°E, 15°S–45°N), and standardized to a uniform 0.25° grid [RESEARCHED]. Dynamical meteorological quantities (vorticity, advection, moisture flux divergence) are computed [INFERENCE].
3.  **Vector Store Lookup:** The spatial feature vector of the current forecast is passed to a FAISS index containing pre-computed embeddings of 10+ years of historical forecasts. The top-K most similar historical synoptic patterns are retrieved along with their observed historical forecast errors [INFERENCE].
4.  **Model Inference:** Combined spatial features, ensemble spread, and retrieved historical error statistics are passed into a trained spatial prediction network (XGBoost/ResNet). The network produces two 2D grid outputs for each lead time (Day 1 to Day 10): (a) Bust Probability Map (0.0–1.0), (b) Confidence Score Map (0–100%) [PS].
5.  **Explainability:** For all high-probability bust clusters (>0.60), SHAP values compute the top 3 contributing meteorological parameters. The XAI module converts these into human-readable diagnostic statements [INFERENCE].
6.  **Visualization:** FastAPI packs grid outputs into Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs (COG) and GeoJSON layers, rendering interactive confidence heatmaps, bounding boxes, and explanation cards on the frontend dashboard [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: Synoptic Pattern & Historical Error Retriever (FAISS + Autoencoder)
*   **Purpose:** Find past historical weather days that match the current NWP forecast pattern [INFERENCE].
*   **Model Type:** Vector Autoencoder Embedding + FAISS Nearest Neighbors [INFERENCE].
*   **Input:** Normalized 500 hPa Geopotential Height & 850 hPa Wind vector grid (Indian domain) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Output:** Top-5 historical analogue dates + historical mean forecast error array [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required:** 10 years of ECMWF TIGGE / IMDAA reanalysis daily forecast errors [RESEARCHED].
*   **Why AI?:** Traditional atmospheric analog search across multi-terabyte arrays using brute force Euclidean distance takes hours; vector embeddings reduce search time to milliseconds [INFERENCE].
*   **Evaluation:** Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) of atmospheric regime classification [INFERENCE].
*   **Failure Mode:** Out-of-distribution climate anomaly (unseen extreme event); falls back to default ensemble variance [INFERENCE].
*   **Human Oversight:** Meteorologist can view the retrieved historical analog dates and verify relevance [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Grid-Wise Forecast Bust Classifier & Confidence Regressor
*   **Purpose:** Predict probability of forecast bust (ACC < 0.40 or Heavy Rainfall Miss > 64.5 mm) and confidence score (0–100%) for Days 1–10 [PS].
*   **Model Type:** Spatial Gradient Boosted Trees (XGBoost) / 3D-ResNet [INFERENCE].
*   **Input:** Current forecast fields + Ensemble spread + Historical analogue error stats + Orographic masks [INFERENCE].
*   **Output:** Grid array of Bust Probability [0.0–1.0] and Confidence Score [0–100%] across 10 lead times [PS].
*   **Data Required:** Historical paired NWP forecasts and verifying ground truth observations (2018–2024) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Why AI?:** Relationship between initial condition errors, convective instabilities, and downstream forecast busts is non-linear and cannot be captured by simple threshold rules [RESEARCHED].
*   **Evaluation:** Brier Score, ROC-AUC, Reliability Curve, Area Under Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) [INFERENCE].
*   **Failure Mode:** False positive bust warning during benign weather; mitigated by displaying confidence interval bounds [INFERENCE].
*   **Human Oversight:** Forecasters retain ultimate authority to override AI warnings before public bulletin issuance [INFERENCE].

### Component 3: Meteorological XAI Engine
*   **Purpose:** Provide domain-specific meteorological explanations for flagged low-confidence regions [PS].
*   **Model Type:** TreeSHAP / Feature Importance Attribution + Rule-Based Decision Tree [INFERENCE].
*   **Input:** Trained XGBoost model parameters + Local feature values of low-confidence grid cells [INFERENCE].
*   **Output:** Key meteorological driver tags (e.g., "High 850 hPa Moisture Convergence Misalignment", "Convective Parameterization Instability") [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required:** Feature contribution matrix [INFERENCE].
*   **Why AI?:** Automatically calculates mathematical contribution of each physical variable to the risk score [INFERENCE].
*   **Failure Mode:** Generates vague driver labels; mitigated by constraining output vocabulary to standardized IMD/WMO meteorological terminology [INFERENCE].

### Non-AI Components (Where AI is Explicitly Prohibited)
*   **GRIB2 / NetCDF Data Decoding:** Handled strictly via deterministic physical C/Fortran bindings (`eccodes`, `cfgrib`) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Spatial Regridding & Reprojection:** Handled deterministically using Climate Data Operators (CDO) and bilinear interpolation (`xarray`) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Map Tile Generation & Rendering:** Handled via standard Web Map Service (WMS) / COG protocols [INFERENCE].

```
|   - Key: `tile:{run_id}:{lead_day}:{z}:{x}:{y}`                                   |
|   - Value: PNG/Protobuf binary raster tile for UI map overlay                     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Offline & Low-Network Resiliency
*   **Bandwidth Reduction via Vector Clipping:** Instead of transmitting raw multi-megabyte gridded rasters to the frontend, the backend converts flagged low-confidence regions into lightweight GeoJSON polygon vectors (reducing payload size by >90%) [INFERENCE].
*   **Browser Caching:** Frontend leverages Service Workers and IndexedDB to cache map tiles and diagnostic explanations locally. Duty forecasters in remote regional centers with fluctuating internet can review previously fetched 10-day forecast confidence assessments offline [INFERENCE].
*   **Delta Synchronization:** Sync engine transmits only updated lead-time layers when a new NWP run completes [INFERENCE].
