# SIH 2026 Solution Research & Architecture Dossier

## 1. Executive Snapshot

This dossier provides an end-to-end technical, architectural, and operational roadmap for developing a **Hybrid AI–NWP Multi-Model Forecast Blending System** for the National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF), Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) [PS]. Atmospheric forecasting models exhibit spatially and temporally varying skill biases: physical Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models excel at satisfying physical conservation laws [KNOWN], ensemble systems capture probabilistic spread [KNOWN], and deep learning weather emulators (such as GraphCast and FourCastNet) deliver ultra-fast spatial pattern predictions [RESEARCHED]. This project establishes an automated, AI-driven blending framework that ingests heterogeneous forecasts, computes spatio-temporally dynamic model weight maps based on historical verification against ground-truth observational grids, applies extreme-value post-processing, and presents optimized forecasts and diagnostic weight maps via an operational forecaster dashboard [INFERENCE]. Designed for a 4–6 student team competing in the Smart India Hackathon (SIH) 2026, this plan balances rigorous meteorological science, state-of-the-art deep learning, spatial GIS visualization, and production-ready software engineering [INFERENCE].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

The explicit problem statement provided in the source-of-truth JSON is deconstructed into atomic requirements below:

*   **Requirement 1 (Multi-Source Ingestion & Dynamic Blending):** The system must combine forecasts from multiple distinct modeling paradigms, including physical deterministic NWP models, ensemble prediction systems, and AI/ML weather models [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* The ingestion pipeline must handle disparate spatial grids, temporal resolutions, and file formats (GRIB2, NetCDF4) from models such as NCUM, NEPS, and open AI emulators [INFERENCE].
*   **Requirement 2 (Spatio-Temporal Adaptive Weighting):** The blending engine must dynamically compute adaptive model weights conditioned on historical skill, forecast lead time (e.g., Day 1 to Day 10), geographic region/orography, season, and prevailing weather regime [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Static or spatially uniform model averaging is insufficient; weights must vary continuously per grid cell, lead time step, and atmospheric state [INFERENCE].
*   **Requirement 3 (Target Meteorological Variables):** The optimized forecast output must explicitly cover surface rainfall (Quantitative Precipitation Forecast), temperature (2-meter minimum/maximum), wind (10-meter vector components), and extreme weather indicators [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Variables with continuous Gaussian-like distributions (temperature) and non-Gaussian zero-inflated tail distributions (rainfall) require distinct blending loss functions and calibration methods [INFERENCE].
*   **Requirement 4 (Model Weight Maps Output):** The system must generate and export spatial model weight maps indicating which constituent model is most reliable for every specific grid point and lead time [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Weight maps provide model explainability and operational transparency for meteorologists, showing explicit spatial confidence distributions [INFERENCE].
*   **Requirement 5 (Quantifiable Skill Improvement):** The blended output must mathematically outperform every individual input model in standard meteorological verification metrics (e.g., RMSE, MAE, Threat Score/CSI, Continuous Ranked Probability Score) [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* The system must undergo rigorous backtesting against high-resolution observational datasets (e.g., IMD gridded observations or ERA5) [INFERENCE].
*   **Requirement 6 (Extreme Weather Guidance):** The framework must deliver improved signal sensitivity and reduced false-negative rates for extreme events, specifically heavy rainfall, heatwaves, and high-wind occurrences [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* Naive ensemble averaging smooths out spatial extremes; the system must incorporate extreme-value preservation or quantile-matching post-processing [INFERENCE].
*   **Requirement 7 (Operational Automated Workflow):** The final deliverable must include an automated execution script and interactive web dashboard for routine operational forecast blending cycles [PS].
    *   *Interpretation:* The production deployment requires scheduled job orchestration (e.g., triggering on 00:00 and 12:00 UTC model runs) with live dashboard rendering [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Geographic & Orographic Complexity:** The Indian subcontinent spans tropical, coastal, arid, and extreme mountainous terrain (Himalayas, Western Ghats) [KNOWN]. NWP models exhibit severe systematic biases over complex topography (e.g., overestimating monsoonal orographic rainfall) [RESEARCHED].
*   **Monsoonal Dynamics & High Variability:** Monsoonal convection exhibits non-linear, multi-scale interactions [KNOWN]. Physical parametrization schemes in NWP often struggle with convective initiation timing, whereas AI models trained on reanalysis (ERA5) may smooth extreme convective spikes [RESEARCHED].
*   **Operational Execution Latency:** NCMRWF operational cycles run twice daily (00:00 and 12:00 UTC) [RESEARCHED]. The forecast blending process must complete within 15–20 minutes after input forecast availability to remain useful for operational bulletin releases [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Heterogeneity & Grid Alignment:** Input models run on differing spatial grids (e.g., NCUM global 12 km, NCUM regional 4 km, AI models on 0.25° latitude-longitude) and vertical levels [RESEARCHED]. Regridding to a standardized 0.125° or 0.25° Indian domain grid must preserve conservative water-budget totals [INFERENCE].
*   **Bandwidth & Computing Constraints in Field Offices:** Regional weather forecasting offices (RMCs) and state disaster centers operate under variable internet bandwidth and varying hardware environments [INFERENCE]. Web dashboards must serve lightweight vector/raster tiles rather than raw multi-gigabyte GRIB2/NetCDF files [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach A: Sliding-Window Historical Skill Inverse Variance Weighting (Statistical Baseline)
*   **Mechanism:** Computes spatial Mean Squared Error (MSE) for each input model over a sliding historical window (e.g., past 15 or 30 days) against IMD gridded observations. Assigns grid-cell weight $W_i = \frac{1/MSE_i}{\sum (1/MSE_j)}$ per variable and lead time.
*   **Required Data:** Multi-model historical forecasts and daily IMD gridded observations for the past 30 days.
*   **Architecture:** Batch Python execution script using `xarray` and NumPy matrix algebra.
*   **Complexity:** Low ($O(N)$ grid operations).
*   **Explainability:** High (weights directly inverse to past 30-day MSE).
*   **Cost:** Minimal (CPU-only execution).
*   **Scalability:** High.
*   **Hackathon Feasibility:** 100% (Easy to build in 2 days).
*   **Production Feasibility:** Moderate (Lags during sudden monsoon onset or weather regime shifts due to sliding window memory inertia [RESEARCHED]).
*   **Risks:** High lag in responding to rapid synoptic weather shifts.

### Approach B: Grid-Wise Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) Meta-Learner
*   **Mechanism:** Trains local XGBoost regressors or Quantile Regression Forests per district/region. Inputs include multi-model forecasts, lead time, month/day-of-year, elevation, and synoptic features (e.g., Mean Sea Level Pressure gradients).
*   **Required Data:** 5+ years of daily multi-model reforecasts matched with IMD observations.
*   **Architecture:** Tabular ML pipeline utilizing `xgboost` or `LightGBM` parallelized across CPU cores.
*   **Complexity:** Medium.
*   **Explainability:** Medium (SHAP values provide feature importance per region).
*   **Cost:** Low to Moderate.
*   **Scalability:** Moderate (Managing thousands of separate regional models incurs storage overhead).
*   **Hackathon Feasibility:** High (Feasible within 1 week).
*   **Production Feasibility:** High.
*   **Risks:** Discontinuous boundary artifacts between separately trained spatial district models [INFERENCE].

### Approach C: Spatio-Temporal Hybrid Attention UNet with Extreme Quantile Calibration (Recommended)
*   **Mechanism:** A deep Convolutional UNet with Spatial and Channel Attention gates. Ingests 3D multi-model forecast tensors $(Batch, Models \times Variables, Lat, Lon)$, lead time embeddings, and atmospheric regime embeddings. Outputs continuous spatial weight maps $W_{i}(x,y,\tau) \in [0,1]$ constrained by Softmax ($\sum W_i = 1$). A secondary Quantile Residual Network adjusts the tail distributions for heavy precipitation (>64.5 mm) and temperature extremes.
*   **Required Data:** Multi-model gridded forecast fields (NCUM/GFS, NEPS/GEFS, GraphCast, FourCastNet) and IMD gridded target observations (2018–2024).
*   **Architecture:** End-to-end PyTorch deep learning architecture integrated with a FastAPI backend and MapLibre GL frontend.
*   **Complexity:** High.
*   **Explainability:** High (Model explicitly outputs interpretable softmax spatial weight maps per lead time).
*   **Cost:** Moderate (Requires single GPU for training; fast CPU/GPU inference).
*   **Scalability:** Very High (Processes full national spatial grids in single tensor forward passes without boundary artifacts).
*   **Hackathon Feasibility:** High for student team with AI/ML capabilities.
*   **Production Feasibility:** Excellent (Operational inference completed in seconds).
*   **Risks:** Requires robust preprocessed training dataset of aligned historical forecasts.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection: Approach C (Spatio-Temporal Hybrid Attention UNet with Extreme Quantile Calibration)
Approach C is selected because it directly solves every explicit requirement in SIH26081 while overcoming the severe limitations of statistical baselines [INFERENCE]. 

#### Explicit Rejection Rationale:
1.  **Why Approach A was rejected:** Pure sliding-window inverse variance weighting exhibits a fatal "memory lag" during rapid atmospheric shifts (e.g., cyclone formation or sudden monsoon onset) [RESEARCHED]. It cannot learn non-linear spatial dependencies or condition weights on atmospheric regimes [INFERENCE].
2.  **Why Approach B was rejected:** Training independent tabular XGBoost models for thousands of individual spatial grid points introduces sharp spatial discontinuities at region boundaries and fails to capture spatial context (such as surrounding moisture transport or mountain ridge effects) [INFERENCE].

#### Why Approach C Wins:
*   **Spatial Continuity:** Convolutional and attention mechanisms process full 2D spatial fields, maintaining smooth meteorologically physical transitions across terrain [INFERENCE].
*   **Explicit Weight Maps:** The softmax output layer guarantees that model weight maps are naturally bounded $[0,1]$ and sum to 100%, directly fulfilling Requirement 4 [PS].
*   **Extreme Weather Preservation:** The integrated Quantile Residual module explicitly addresses the "ensemble over-smoothing" problem, delivering sharp signals for heavy rainfall (>64.5 mm/day), heatwaves, and extreme winds [PS].

### Data-Flow Narrative
1.  **Ingestion:** Operational forecast feeds (NCUM/GFS deterministic, NEPS ensemble mean/spread, GraphCast/FourCastNet AI forecasts) are fetched daily upon release via automated S3/HTTP listeners [INFERENCE].
2.  **Regridding:** Raw GRIB2/NetCDF files pass through an `xarray` preprocessing worker that spatially aligns all forecasts onto the standardized IMD-compatible Indian grid ($0.25^\circ \times 0.25^\circ$, $135 \times 129$ matrix from $6.5^\circ\text{N}$ to $38.5^\circ\text{N}$ and $66.5^\circ\text{E}$ to $100.0^\circ\text{E}$) using conservative area-weighted interpolation [RESEARCHED].
3.  **Feature Extraction:** Derives synoptic atmospheric regime descriptors (e.g., 850hPa wind shear, precipitable water, season day-of-year, elevation topography) [INFERENCE].
4.  **Neural Blending Forward Pass:** The aligned tensor enters the Attention UNet. The network evaluates historical error distributions for the current regime and outputs spatial weight matrices $W_{k}(x, y, \tau)$ for each input model $k$ and lead time $\tau$ [INFERENCE].
5.  **Tail Extreme Calibration:** For cells where precipitation exceeds convective thresholds ($>35$ mm/day) or temperatures approach extreme percentiles, the Quantile Residual network applies extreme-value adjustment to prevent unweighted spatial smoothing [INFERENCE].
6.  **Storage & Tiling:** Blended outputs and weight maps are stored as compressed Zarr stores, and dynamic map tiles (COG - Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF / Mapbox Vector Tiles) are generated [INFERENCE].
7.  **Visualization:** Forecasters inspect the dashboard, interacting with lead-time sliders (Day 1 to 10), toggling layer visibility, analyzing model weight contributions per district, and exporting operational advisories [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: Spatio-Temporal Attention UNet (Dynamic Weight Generator)
1.  **What exact problem does AI solve?** Solves the non-linear assignment of spatially varying, lead-time dependent weights to input models based on atmospheric regime context [INFERENCE].
2.  **Input:** Multi-model forecast tensor $[K \times V \times H \times W]$, lead-time vector $\tau$, day-of-year, static elevation, and synoptic features (850hPa vorticity, 500hPa geopotential height) [INFERENCE].
3.  **Output:** Spatial weight map tensor $W_k(x, y, \tau) \in [0, 1]$ such that $\sum_{k=1}^K W_k(x, y, \tau) = 1.0$ [INFERENCE].
4.  **Why can a simpler system not solve it?** Fixed rules or static regression cannot account for complex spatial non-stationarity, land-sea contrast, orographic forced lifting, and regime-dependent model biases over South Asia [RESEARCHED].
5.  **Data Required:** Historical multi-model forecasts matched with IMD daily gridded observations (2018–2024) [RESEARCHED].
6.  **Evaluation:** Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS), RMSE reduction over equal-weighted mean, spatial Threat Score (CSI) [RESEARCHED].
7.  **What happens when it is wrong?** Softmax output structure guarantees weights remain bounded $[0,1]$; worst-case output degrades gracefully toward an equal-weighted ensemble mean [INFERENCE].
8.  **Does it require training?** Yes, offline supervised training on historical reforecasts [INFERENCE].
9.  **Fine-tuning?** Optional seasonal fine-tuning post-monsoon [INFERENCE].
10. **RAG required?** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision?** Yes, 2D Spatial Convolutional and Attention layers operate on gridded meteorological images [INFERENCE].
12. **Speech processing?** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Forecasting?** Yes, multi-lead-time spatial forecasting [INFERENCE].
14. **Human oversight?** Required; forecasters can inspect weight maps and override blended outputs during active cyclonic emergencies [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Quantile Residual Extreme Calibration (Extreme Signal Preservation)
1.  **What exact problem does AI solve?** Corrects the tail-distribution compression (underestimation of extreme heavy rainfall and temperature peaks) caused by weighted averaging [RESEARCHED].
2.  **Input:** Initial blended forecast field, ensemble spread variance, terrain slope [INFERENCE].
3.  **Output:** Residual extreme correction delta field $\Delta F(x, y)$ added to the blended baseline [INFERENCE].
4.  **Why can a simpler system not solve it?** Extreme precipitation obeys heavy-tailed distributions (e.g., Generalized Extreme Value) that linear combinations cannot reproduce [RESEARCHED].
5.  **Data Required:** High-resolution extreme events subset from IMD gridded rainfall (>64.5 mm/day) and temperature (>40°C) [RESEARCHED].
6.  **Evaluation:** Extreme Dependency Score (EDS), False Alarm Ratio (FAR) for heavy rainfall thresholds [RESEARCHED].
7.  **What happens when it is wrong?** Threshold gating restricts activation strictly to regions where ensemble probability of extreme weather exceeds 30% [INFERENCE].
8.  **Training?** Trained on extreme threshold subsets using Gradient Boosted Quantile Loss [INFERENCE].
9.  **Fine-tuning?** No [INFERENCE].
10. **RAG/Speech?** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Human oversight?** High-confidence extreme weather flags are highlighted in red on the dashboard for forecaster validation [INFERENCE].
