## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
India suffers from recurring climate and environmental disasters—ranging from flash floods in Assam and Kerala, to forest fires in Uttarakhand, severe urban smog in Delhi-NCR, and landslides in Western Ghats and Himalayas [KNOWN]. Existing monitoring relies heavily on centralized satellite feeds and sparse government stations, which lack localized granularity and fail during communications blackouts [INFERENCE]. This proposal details an **Environmental Intelligence Network (EIN)**: a resilient, solar-powered, hybrid edge-cloud solution designed for Qualcomm hardware architecture. Each distributed edge node leverages on-device AI models via the Qualcomm AI Stack (QNN Runtime on Hexagon NPU) to detect multi-hazard anomalies (floods, wildfires, industrial gas leaks, landslides, AQI spikes) in real time without continuous cloud connectivity [INFERENCE]. Using adaptive LPWAN protocols (LoRaWAN / NB-IoT) [PS], the system transmits low-bandwidth prioritized Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) alerts to regional command centers and NDMA SACHET-compatible citizen mobile apps [RESEARCHED]. This dossier provides a complete engineering, architecture, and deployment roadmap for a 4–6 student hackathon team targeted for completion before September 30, 2026 [PS].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

1. **Distributed Smart Sensor Nodes [PS]**

   - *Interpretation*: Hardware nodes must integrate multi-sensor interfaces (I2C, SPI, ADC, UART) with ultra-low power states, solar MPPT battery charging, and rugged IP67 enclosures suitable for remote outdoor deployment [INFERENCE].
   - *Implementation Implication*: Dual-board architecture—a low-power MCU (e.g., ESP32-S3 / STM32) for continuous sensor polling and power management, connected via UART to a high-efficiency Edge AI module (e.g., Qualcomm RB3 Gen 2 / QCS6490) activated for model inference upon local signal threshold trigger [INFERENCE].

   - *Implementation Implication*: Train lightweight multi-hazard ML models (TinyML / quantized INT8 ONNX/TFLite converted via Qualcomm QNN SDK) that compute risk scores locally [RESEARCHED]. If cloud connectivity drops, local decision logs are retained in non-volatile flash storage [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Cellular Connectivity Blackouts [KNOWN]**: During extreme disaster events (cyclones, heavy flooding, landslides), cellular towers lose power or backhaul fiber cuts occur.
   - *Impact*: Nodes cannot rely exclusively on 4G/5G/NB-IoT.
   - *Design Mitigation*: Mesh-capable LoRaWAN backhaul with local storage buffer on SD card/Flash until backhaul reconnects [INFERENCE].
2. **Extreme Outdoor Weather & Humidity [KNOWN]**: High temperature variations (+45°C in Indian summer to near 0°C in mountainous regions) and torrential monsoon rains (up to 100% RH).
   - *Impact*: Sensor degradation, lens condensation, water ingress, thermal throttling of processors.
   - *Design Mitigation*: IP67 weatherproof NEMA enclosures, conformal coating on PCB, anti-fungal optical windows, and passive heat-sinking [INFERENCE].
3. **Power Availability Constraints [KNOWN]**: Grid power is absent in remote forest reserves, riverbanks, and landslide-prone mountain slopes.
   - *Impact*: Must rely entirely on solar energy harvesting.
   - *Design Mitigation*: High-efficiency monocrystalline solar panels with MPPT charge controllers, LiFePO4 batteries (wide operating temperature range), and deep-sleep MCU duty cycling [INFERENCE].
4. **Digital Literacy & Language Diversity [KNOWN]**: Field personnel and rural citizens speak diverse regional languages (Assamese, Bengali, Hindi, Malayalam, Marathi, Tamil, etc.) and may have low smartphone literacy [RESEARCHED].
   - *Impact*: Text-heavy English dashboards will fail in real field conditions.
   - *Design Mitigation*: Color-coded danger scales (Green/Yellow/Orange/Red), pictorial hazard icons, audio voice warnings via text-to-speech in 12 Indian languages aligned with SACHET standards [RESEARCHED].
5. **Sensor Drift & Calibration Decay [KNOWN]**: Electrochemical gas sensors and optical dust sensors drift significantly over 6–12 months due to ambient accumulation and temperature swings.
   - *Impact*: High risk of false positives or false negatives over time.
   - *Design Mitigation*: On-device edge AI drift compensation algorithms using baseline offset adjustment based on multi-sensor correlation [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Centralized Cloud IoT Gateway System (Rule-Based Thresholding)
- **Mechanism**: Dumb hardware sensor nodes sample data every 10 seconds and transmit raw readings over cellular 4G to a central cloud server. Cloud applies static hardcoded threshold rules (e.g. `if water_level > 3.5m then alert`).
- **Required Data**: Raw sensor streams over cellular.
- **Evaluation**:
  - *Problem Fit*: Poor. Fails during network outages; high cellular bandwidth cost.
  - *Accuracy Potential*: Low (high false positive rate due to lack of multi-sensor contextual fusion).
  - *Complexity*: Low.
  - *Explainability*: Very High (if-else rules).
  - *Cost*: High recurring cellular data plans.
  - *Scalability*: Poor (central cloud bottleneck).
  - *Hackathon Feasibility*: High.
  - *Production Feasibility*: Unviable due to network dependence during disasters.

### Approach 2: Edge-Only Microcontroller Network (Localized Thresholds, No Cloud Analytics)
- **Mechanism**: Independent microcontrollers (ESP32) evaluate local thresholds and sound a local buzzer or send direct peer-to-peer RF alerts. No central GIS dashboard or historical analytics.
- **Required Data**: Local analog/digital sensor readings.
- **Evaluation**:
  - *Problem Fit*: Moderate (handles local alerts, fails macro-regional disaster governance).
  - *Accuracy Potential*: Moderate.
  - *Complexity*: Low.
  - *Explainability*: High.
  - *Cost*: Very Low.
  - *Scalability*: Poor (isolated islands of data).
  - *Hackathon Feasibility*: High.
  - *Production Feasibility*: Inadequate for State/National Disaster Authorities.

### Approach 3 (Recommended): Edge AI + Cloud Hybrid Intelligence Network (Qualcomm QNN Edge Acceleration + LPWAN Mesh + GIS Central Dashboard)
- **Mechanism**: Dual-processor node architecture. Low-power MCU continuously samples multi-hazard sensors. On anomaly signature detection, the MCU wakes the Qualcomm Edge processor (QCS6490 / RB3 Gen 2), running quantized multi-hazard ML models via Qualcomm AI Stack (QNN) on Hexagon NPU for multi-variate context validation [RESEARCHED]. Evaluated risk alerts are packaged into binary CBOR messages and sent via LoRaWAN / NB-IoT to a central GIS dashboard and SACHET-compatible CAP server [INFERENCE].
- **Required Data**: Local sensor arrays, local model weights, regional spatial layers.
- **Evaluation**:
  - *Problem Fit*: Excellent (fully addresses all explicit problem statement requirements [PS]).
  - *Accuracy Potential*: High (multi-variate temporal ML reduces false alarms).
  - *Complexity*: Moderate–High.
  - *Explainability*: High (SHAP-explainable risk scores + deterministic rule bounds).
  - *Cost*: Highly Cost-Effective (optimized LPWAN telemetry + low power consumption).
  - *Scalability*: High (hierarchical edge-gateway-cloud structure).
  - *Hackathon Feasibility*: High (realistic MVP build with simulated/physical nodes).
  - *Production Feasibility*: High.

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 3: Edge AI + Cloud Hybrid Intelligence Network** is selected [INFERENCE].

### Justification
1. **Direct Alignment with Sponsor & Problem Statement**: Qualcomm Inc. specifically requested on-device edge AI analytics operating without continuous cloud connectivity, multi-hazard support, and LPWAN/cellular flexibility [PS].
2. **Resilience During Blackouts**: By running quantized models locally on the Qualcomm NPU, nodes continue identifying flood thresholds or wildfire ignition signatures even when cellular networks fail [INFERENCE].
3. **Bandwidth Optimization**: Transmitting raw continuous sensor streams from thousands of nodes consumes massive bandwidth and power. Transmitting localized risk scores and compressed alerts reduces data transmission volume by over 98% [INFERENCE].
4. **Rejection of Alternatives**: Approach 1 was rejected due to single point of failure (cloud dependence during natural disasters) and high recurring cellular bandwidth costs. Approach 2 was rejected because it lacks central situational awareness, long-term trend forecasting, and regional GIS mapping required by disaster authorities [PS].

### Data Flow Narrative
1. **Acquisition**: The primary low-power MCU (ESP32-S3) wakes every 5 seconds, reads raw analog/digital signals from the sensor array (water level, smoke, gas, PM2.5, soil moisture, vibration), and performs baseline smoothing [INFERENCE].
2. **Local Edge Inference Trigger**: If any sensor exceeds a pre-set dynamic variance window, the MCU triggers the Qualcomm edge processing subsystem (QCS6490 via Hexagon NPU) via UART inter-processor interrupt [INFERENCE].
3. **On-Device AI Classification**: The Qualcomm QNN engine executes a multi-variate INT8 model (e.g. evaluating co-occurrence of rapid temperature rise + smoke gas drop + humidity drop to confirm wildfire ignition with 94% confidence) [INFERENCE].
4. **Local Alert Package Generation**: The node constructs a standardized, binary-encoded payload (CBOR format, ~45 bytes) containing: Node ID, Timestamp, Hazard Type Code, Severity Level (1–4), Confidence Score (0–100%), and Sensor Snapshot [INFERENCE].
5. **Resilient Transmission**: Payload is broadcast over LoRaWAN (IN865 MHz) to the nearest gateway. If LoRaWAN gateway is unreachable, the node buffers the payload to SPI Flash memory and retries via NB-IoT/Cellular fallback [INFERENCE].
6. **Cloud Processing & CAP Conversion**: EMQX MQTT broker receives payload, decodes CBOR, writes telemetry to TimescaleDB, updates PostGIS spatial index, and converts emergency events into CAP 1.2 XML payloads for SACHET integration [RESEARCHED].
7. **Dissemination**: Emergency alerts are pushed via WebSockets to the DDMA GIS Dashboard and via Web Push / SMS to citizen mobile apps in regional languages [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### 1. Water Level & Flash Flood Anomaly Model
- **Purpose**: Predict flash flood risk and rapid water level surge before river overflow occurs [INFERENCE].
- **Model Type**: Quantized INT8 Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) / LSTM [INFERENCE].
- **Input**: 24-step time-series history of water level (ultrasonic radar), rainfall rate (tipping bucket), and soil saturation index [INFERENCE].
- **Output**: Flood Risk Class (Normal, Advisory, Warning, Flash Flood Emergency) + Lead Time Estimate (minutes) [INFERENCE].
- **Data Required**: Historical river discharge station data from CWC NWDP portal & synthetic hydrological surge profiles [RESEARCHED].
- **Why AI**: Linear thresholds fail during complex multi-hour rainfall accumulation where soil saturation non-linearly accelerates runoff [INFERENCE].
- **Evaluation**: Precision, Recall, F1-score on holdout flood events; Target F1 > 0.90 [INFERENCE].
- **Failure Mode**: Ultrasonic sensor echo loss due to heavy surface debris.
- **Human Oversight**: Flagged warnings above 'Advisory' trigger manual visual confirmation or district officer approval before sounding regional sirens [INFERENCE].

### 2. Wildfire & Smoke Ignition Detector Model
- **Purpose**: Ultra-early identification of forest fire ignition at ground level [INFERENCE].
- **Model Type**: Multi-variate Gradient Boosted / Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) quantized to INT8 ONNX [RESEARCHED].
- **Input**: Ambient temperature, relative humidity, CO gas (ppm), VOC smoke density, and rate of temperature change ($\Delta T / \Delta t$) [INFERENCE].
- **Output**: Fire Probability Score (0.0 to 1.0) and Ignition Confidence [INFERENCE].
- **Data Required**: Synthesized smoke chamber gas profiles + open-source forest fire meteorological datasets [RESEARCHED].
- **Why AI**: Dust and ambient humidity spikes cause false alarms in simple smoke sensors; AI evaluates the simultaneous chemical combustion curve [INFERENCE].
- **Evaluation**: False Positive Rate (<1 per node-year target) [INFERENCE].
- **Failure Mode**: Sensor fouling due to dense spiderwebs or dust accumulation.
- **Human Oversight**: Forest ranger team alerted with geo-coords to inspect ground location [INFERENCE].

### 3. Air Quality Deterioration & Hazardous Gas Leak Classifier
- **Purpose**: Distinguish between normal seasonal AQI variation, toxic industrial chemical leaks (e.g. Ammonia, Chlorine, CO), and dust storms [INFERENCE].
- **Model Type**: Random Forest / Isolation Forest quantized for C execution [INFERENCE].
- **Input**: PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO2, SO2, temperature, humidity, and wind speed [INFERENCE].
- **Output**: Pollution Event Type (Dust, Agricultural Burning, Toxic Industrial Leak) + AQI Projection [INFERENCE].
- **Data Required**: CPCB CAAQMS 15-minute historical station logs [RESEARCHED].
- **Why AI**: Complex chemical interactions require multi-dimensional clustering to separate industrial leaks from atmospheric inversions [INFERENCE].
- **Evaluation**: Confusion matrix classification accuracy (>88%) [INFERENCE].
- **Failure Mode**: Chemical sensor saturation during extreme toxic events.
- **Human Oversight**: CPCB/SPCB alert dispatched automatically when toxic chemical indicators trigger [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
- **Raw Physical Safety Hard Cutoffs**: Direct hardcoded safety limits MUST remain non-ML (e.g., if CO concentration exceeds immediate lethal threshold `> 400 ppm` or water level touches raw physical bridge ceiling, trigger immediate raw emergency alert instantly without waiting for NPU model inference execution) [INFERENCE].
- **Battery & Solar Power Diagnostics**: Battery low-voltage cutoffs and solar charging state logic must be handled strictly by deterministic C++ firmware rules, not AI [INFERENCE].

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Edge Local Storage Schema (SQLite3 / SPI Flash Buffer)
```sql
-- Local Node Offline Circular Buffer Database
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS sensor_telemetry (
    id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
    timestamp INTEGER NOT NULL, -- Unix epoch seconds
    water_level_cm REAL,
    rain_mm_hr REAL,
    temp_c REAL,
    humidity_pct REAL,
    pm25 REAL,
    pm10 REAL,
    co_ppm REAL,
    soil_moisture_pct REAL,
    vibration_g REAL,
    battery_v REAL,
    synced_flag INTEGER DEFAULT 0 -- 0 = Pending Upload, 1 = Synced
);

CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS event_alerts (
    event_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
    timestamp INTEGER NOT NULL,
    hazard_type INTEGER, -- 1=Flood, 2=Fire, 3=AQI, 4=Landslide, 5=Gas
    severity_level INTEGER, -- 1=Info, 2=Advisory, 3=Warning, 4=Critical
    confidence_score INTEGER, -- 0 to 100
    payload_cbor BLOB,
    synced_flag INTEGER DEFAULT 0
);
```

### Bandwidth Reduction Strategy
1. **Binary Encoding (CBOR / Protocol Buffers)**: Raw JSON payload (`~350 bytes`) is encoded into Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR) (`~42 bytes`), reducing RF payload footprint by 88% [INFERENCE].
2. **Delta Encoding & Threshold-Based Reporting**:
   - *Normal State*: Node transmits a compact status summary packet once every 30 minutes [INFERENCE].
   - *Active Anomaly State*: Node switches immediately to 10-second priority transmission of event frames [INFERENCE].

### Offline Synchronization & Conflict Handling
- **Local Flash Circular Buffer**: The node maintains a 128 MB SPI Flash chip capable of storing up to 90 days of offline telemetry records [INFERENCE].
- **Reconnection Sync Protocol**: Upon network backhaul restoration, the node executes a Store-and-Forward protocol:
  1. Priority 1: Transmit all pending un-synced `event_alerts` records [INFERENCE].
  2. Priority 2: Transmit time-binned averaged `sensor_telemetry` records (1-hour averages instead of raw 5-second samples) to prevent gateway network congestion [INFERENCE].
- **Conflict Resolution**: Timestamps are generated using on-board RTC hardware synchronized via GPS or LoRaWAN MAC network time (`DeviceTimeReq`). Server accepts telemetry with `ON CONFLICT (node_id, timestamp) DO UPDATE` idempotency [INFERENCE].
