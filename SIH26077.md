## 1. Executive Snapshot

This dossier provides a comprehensive technical, architectural, and strategic roadmap for developing an **AI-Driven Hyper-Local Early Warning System for Severe Weather Nowcasting** for the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) and the National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF) [PS]. Traditional physics-based Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models suffer from high computational latency (often requiring hours to execute multi-node HPC simulations) and struggle to capture rapid, meso-beta and meso-gamma scale atmospheric fluctuations preceding severe localized weather [PS, KNOWN]. The proposed solution replaces computationally intensive thermodynamic simulations during the nowcasting window (2 to 6 hours) with a multi-modal spatiotemporal deep learning architecture (e.g., cross-attention multi-task spatiotemporal transformer) [PS, INFERENCE]. By ingesting real-time INSAT-3D/3DR geostationary satellite radiances (Water Vapor and Thermal Infrared channels), IMDAA high-resolution regional reanalysis thermodynamic baselines, and Digital Elevation Models (CartoDEM/SRTM), the engine simultaneously computes hyper-local probability risk maps for severe thunderstorms, cloudbursts, and flash floods [PS]. The platform combines a lightweight inference engine, an interactive GIS dashboard with Explainable AI (XAI) meteorological trigger heatmaps for disaster authorities, and an automated Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) compliant API for immediate first-responder dissemination [PS, INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Geographic & Orographic Realities:**  
   *Mountainous vs. Plain Terrain:* Cloudbursts occur primarily in steep Western Himalayan terrain (Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Jammu & Kashmir) and Western Ghats, where rapid orographic lift triggers explosive convection [KNOWN]. Elevation change across a single 10 km grid can exceed 2,000 meters, demanding high-resolution DEM modeling [KNOWN].

2. **Satellite Temporal & Spatial Resolution Constraints:**  
   *INSAT-3D/3DR Cadence:* Geostationary images from INSAT-3D/3DR Imager are acquired every 30 minutes in standard mode or 15 minutes in interleaved mode [RESEARCHED]. Spatial resolution is 1 km for Visible, 4 km for Thermal IR (TIR), and 8 km for Water Vapor (WV) channels [RESEARCHED].  
   *Resolution Mismatch:* Atmospheric predictors (WV channel at 8 km) must be fused with high-resolution DEM (CartoDEM at 30 m), requiring multi-scale spatial interpolation and grid alignment [INFERENCE].

3. **Reanalysis vs. Operational Data Latency:**  
   *IMDAA Reanalysis Limits:* IMDAA is a historical reanalysis dataset (1979–2020) [RESEARCHED]. In an operational nowcasting environment, real-time thermodynamic baselines must be sourced from operational NWP analysis feeds (such as NCMRWF NCUM operational analysis or IMD GFS analysis at 12 km resolution) [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].

4. **Field Connectivity & Infrastructure Constraints:**  
   *Last-Mile Connectivity:* High-altitude Himalayan valleys frequently lose cellular broadband (4G/5G) and grid power during severe convective storms [KNOWN].  
   *Impact:* Emergency alerts sent to first responders must rely on lightweight, highly compressed payloads (e.g., CAP SMS / Cell Broadcast) rather than bandwidth-heavy GIS video maps [INFERENCE].

5. **Digital Literacy & User Interface Realities:**  
   *Disaster Managers vs. Citizens:* Field workers and village disaster committees require simple color-coded threat levels (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red) and local language text (Hindi/State languages) rather than raw meteorological metric charts [KNOWN].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The recommended approach is **Approach 3: Multi-Task Spatiotemporal Cross-Attention Transformer Engine with Hydrological Topographic Coupling** [PS, INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected

1. **Rejection of Approach 1 (Heuristic Rule Engine):**  
   Severe convective events (cloudbursts > 100 mm/hr) are non-linear atmospheric instabilities [KNOWN]. Fixed threshold rules (e.g., "CAPE > 2000 AND CTT drop > 10°C") yield unacceptably high false positive rates because high CAPE often persists without triggering lift mechanism, or lift occurs under severe CIN capping [KNOWN, INFERENCE]. It fails the core problem objective [PS].

2. **Rejection of Approach 2 (Decoupled Separate Models):**  
   Running three separate machine learning pipelines (one for thunderstorms, one for cloudbursts, one for floods) creates redundant data ingestion loops, triples GPU memory overhead, and ignores the intrinsic physical feedback loops (e.g., cloudburst moisture flux directly drives flash flood hydrographs) [INFERENCE]. Furthermore, it violates the explicit PS requirement: *"To predict multiple extreme events simultaneously, the engine employs a multi-task learning approach"* [PS].

### Why Approach 3 Wins

- **Exact Problem Match:** Faithfully mirrors every explicit element in SIH26077 (IWV tracking, CTT drop rate, CAPE/CIN instability, low-level convergence, multi-task learning heads, DEM flood channeling, XAI visual dashboard) [PS].
- **Feature Synergy:** The shared encoder backbone learns foundational atmospheric dynamics (moisture accumulation, thermal instability, wind vector convergence) that benefit all three hazard heads simultaneously, improving generalized accuracy over single-task models [INFERENCE].
- **Inference Speed:** Executing a single forward pass on an ONNX-optimized transformer takes < 15 seconds on a single GPU (e.g., NVIDIA T4 / RTX 4090), providing true real-time nowcasting with zero computational bottleneck [ASSUMPTION].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative

1. **Ingestion & Preprocessing Phase:**  
   Every 15 minutes, automated cron workers fetch the latest INSAT-3D/3DR Imager files from MOSDAC SFTP/HTTPS mirrors [RESEARCHED]. The pipeline uses Python `satpy` and `gdal` to extract raw counts, calibrate to Brightness Temperatures (BT) for Water Vapor and TIR channels, and compute Integrated Water Vapor (IWV) proxies [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE]. Simultaneously, the system reads the corresponding IMDAA/NCUM thermodynamic grid frame (temperature, humidity, $U/V$ winds across 1000 to 200 hPa) [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].

2. **Alignment & Feature Vectorization:**  
   The raw multi-source rasters are resampled to a standardized $0.04^\circ \times 0.04^\circ$ spatial grid (~4 km resolution) covering the target geographic box [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION]. Temporal derivative kernels calculate $\Delta \text{CTT}/\Delta t$ (cooling rate) and $\Delta \text{IWV}/\Delta t$ (moisture convergence rate) across the preceding 4 satellite frames ($T-60\text{m}, T-45\text{m}, T-30\text{m}, T-0\text{m}$) [INFERENCE]. Low-level horizontal wind divergence ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$) and bulk wind shear between 850 hPa and 200 hPa are computed [INFERENCE].

3. **Multi-Task Neural Network Forward Pass:**  
   The aligned multi-channel tensor $[B, T=4, C=12, H, W]$ is fed into the Multi-Task Transformer [INFERENCE]. The shared encoder extracts joint spatiotemporal embeddings via 3D Convolutional Stem layers followed by Cross-Attention Transformer blocks [INFERENCE]. The representations branch into three output heads:
   - *Head 1 (Thunderstorm Head):* Predicts pixel-wise convective storm onset probability map for $T+2\text{h}$ to $T+6\text{h}$ [PS, INFERENCE].
   - *Head 2 (Cloudburst Head):* Predicts probability of extreme localized rainfall (>100 mm/hr rate) [PS, INFERENCE].
   - *Head 3 (Flash Flood Head):* Multiplies predicted rainfall probability by surface flow accumulation masks derived from CartoDEM (using HAND - Height Above Nearest Drainage index), yielding a topography-routed flash flood inundation risk map [PS, INFERENCE].

4. **XAI Attribution & Alert Dispatch:**  
   If any grid cell breaches predefined threat thresholds (e.g., Cloudburst risk > 70%), the XAI module computes feature saliency using Integrated Gradients [INFERENCE]. Saliency vectors (e.g., "75% risk driven by 12°C/hr CTT drop rate + IWV spike of +18%") are attached to the risk polygon [INFERENCE]. The post-processor converts risk rasters into GeoJSON vector polygons and publishes OASIS CAP v1.2 XML messages to the API endpoint for delivery to NDMA/SDMA dashboards [PS, INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Detailed AI Component Analysis

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        MULTI-TASK AIPredictive ARCHITECTURE                       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                  INPUT TENSOR
                    [Satellite Imagery + IMDAA Thermodynamic]
                                       |
                                       v
                    +-------------------------------------+
                    |   SHARED SPATIOTEMPORAL ENCODER     |
                    | (Cross-Attention Transformer Block) |
                    +------------------+------------------+
                                       |
           +---------------------------+---------------------------+
           |                           |                           |
           v                           v                           v
+---------------------+     +---------------------+     +---------------------+
| THUNDERSTORM HEAD   |     |   CLOUDBURST HEAD   |     |  FLASH FLOOD HEAD   |
| Binary Classifier   |     | Focal Loss Regressor|     | HAND Coupled Grid   |
| (Conv2D + Sigmoid)  |     | (>100mm/hr risk)    |     | (Topographic Mask)  |
+---------------------+     +---------------------+     +---------------------+
```

1. **Shared Encoder Backbone:**
   - *Purpose:* Extract unified 3D spatiotemporal atmospheric feature representations across satellite imagery and thermodynamic grids [PS].
   - *Model Type:* Spatiotemporal Cross-Attention Transformer (3D ConvStem + Swin/Transformer Blocks) [PS, INFERENCE].
   - *Input:* 5D Tensor $[B, T=4, C=12, H=256, W=256]$ (Channels: WV, TIR1, TIR2, IWV, CTT Drop, CAPE, CIN, $U_{10\text{m}}, V_{10\text{m}}$, Divergence, Vertical Shear, Elevation) [INFERENCE].
   - *Output:* Latent feature tensor $[B, C'=512, H/4, W/4]$ [INFERENCE].
   - *Data Required:* Synchronized historical INSAT-3D HDF5 files and IMDAA NetCDF reanalysis grids [PS, RESEARCHED].
   - *Why AI:* Atmospheric convection involves non-linear, high-dimensional fluid dynamics and thermodynamic interactions that physical NWP models take hours to compute; deep learning approximates these complex mappings in seconds [PS, KNOWN].
   - *Evaluation:* Spatial $F_1$-score, Critical Success Index (CSI), and Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) [INFERENCE].
   - *Failure Mode & Mitigation:* Missing satellite frames due to sensor outage; system falls back to linear temporal interpolation of previous frames and flags degraded confidence score in XAI panel [INFERENCE].
   - *Human Oversight:* Operational forecaster retains final authority to override automated red alert dispatches [INFERENCE].

2. **Thunderstorm Prediction Head:**
   - *Purpose:* Generate spatial probability map for convective storm onset 2–6 hours ahead [PS].
   - *Model Type:* 2D Transposed Convolutional / U-Net decoder head with Sigmoid activation [INFERENCE].
   - *Input:* Latent backbone feature tensor [INFERENCE].
   - *Output:* Pixel-wise probability grid $[0.0, 1.0]$ for severe thunderstorm presence [INFERENCE].
   - *Why AI:* Thunderstorms form rapidly when eroding CIN coincides with buoyant CAPE and localized low-level convergence; rules cannot capture spatial pattern evolution [PS, KNOWN].

3. **Cloudburst Prediction Head:**
   - *Purpose:* Forecast extreme localized rainfall events (>100 mm/hour over a small area) [PS, KNOWN].
   - *Model Type:* 2D Convolutional head trained with Focal Loss to handle extreme class imbalance [INFERENCE].
   - *Input:* Latent backbone feature tensor [INFERENCE].
   - *Output:* Probability mask of cloudburst precipitation intensity [INFERENCE].
   - *Why AI:* Cloudbursts are sub-grid microscale phenomena invisible to coarse NWP grids [PS, KNOWN].

4. **Flash Flood Prediction Head (Hydrological Coupling):**
   - *Purpose:* Predict ground surface inundation risk by channeling cloudburst precipitation along terrain slopes [PS].
   - *Model Type:* Topographic attention mask overlay multiplying predicted rainfall intensity by CartoDEM HAND (Height Above Nearest Drainage) index and D8 flow accumulation [PS, INFERENCE].
   - *Input:* Cloudburst Head probability grid $\times$ Static CartoDEM HAND Raster Layer [PS, INFERENCE].
   - *Output:* Flash flood risk probability map [PS, INFERENCE].
   - *Why AI + GIS Hybrid:* Atmospheric predictions provide the volume of water; static topographic elevation models channel the water. Pure AI without DEM physics ignores gravity routing; pure GIS without AI lacks precipitative lead time [PS, INFERENCE].

### Non-AI Components (Where AI Should NOT Be Used)

- **Unit Conversions & Radiometric Calibration:** Raw satellite counts converted to Brightness Temperature using official lookup tables (LUT) must use deterministic physics formulas, NOT neural networks [INFERENCE].
- **Spatial CRS Reprojections:** Converting satellite lat-long projection grids to EPSG:4326 must use deterministic GDAL/Proj library transformations [INFERENCE].
- **OASIS CAP Alert Generation:** Parsing alert triggers into XML schemas must be done via deterministic standard template formatters to guarantee zero syntax hallucination [INFERENCE].

## 14. Data Architecture & Offline/Low-Network Design

### Schema Architecture (PostGIS Spatial Entities)

```sql
-- 1. STATIONS & GRIDS TABLE
CREATE TABLE spatial_grids (
    grid_id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    grid_code VARCHAR(32) UNIQUE NOT NULL,
    geom GEOMETRY(Polygon, 4326) NOT NULL,
    elevation_mean FLOAT,
    hand_index_mean FLOAT,
    drainage_basin_id INT
);

-- 2. LIVE INFERENCE RUNS TABLE
CREATE TABLE inference_runs (
    run_id UUID PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
    timestamp_utc TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE NOT NULL,
    lead_time_hours INT NOT NULL, -- 2, 4, or 6 hours
    model_version VARCHAR(16) NOT NULL,
    status VARCHAR(16) CHECK (status IN ('RUNNING', 'COMPLETED', 'FAILED'))
);

-- 3. MULTI-HAZARD PREDICTIONS TABLE
CREATE TABLE hazard_predictions (
    pred_id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    run_id UUID REFERENCES inference_runs(run_id),
    grid_id INT REFERENCES spatial_grids(grid_id),
    thunderstorm_prob FLOAT CHECK (thunderstorm_prob BETWEEN 0 AND 1),
    cloudburst_prob FLOAT CHECK (cloudburst_prob BETWEEN 0 AND 1),
    flash_flood_prob FLOAT CHECK (flash_flood_prob BETWEEN 0 AND 1),
    risk_level VARCHAR(8) CHECK (risk_level IN ('GREEN', 'YELLOW', 'ORANGE', 'RED')),
    xai_saliency_json JSONB -- Stores top driver attributions (e.g. {"IWV_surge": 0.45, "CTT_drop": 0.35})
);

-- Spatial index for sub-millisecond query performance
CREATE INDEX idx_hazard_pred_geom ON spatial_grids USING GIST (geom);
CREATE INDEX idx_hazard_risk_level ON hazard_predictions (risk_level);
```

### Offline & Low-Network Resiliency Design

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        OFFLINE / LOW-NETWORK SYNC ARCHITECTURE                    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

   +-----------------------+                    +-----------------------+
   |   CENTRAL CLOUD/HPC   |                    | FIELD RESPONDER MOBILE|
   | (Generates GeoJSON    |                    | (PWA / Android Client)|
   |  & Vector Tiles)      |                    |                       |
   +-----------+-----------+                    +-----------+-----------+
               |                                            |
               | Push Heavy Layers (Full Connectivity)      |
               |------------------------------------------->|
               |                                            |
               | Network Drops / 2G Connection             |
               | - Fallback to SMS CAP Alert Payloads       |
               | - Cache Vector Tiles in IndexedDB          |
               |<-------------------------------------------|
```

1. **Bandwidth Reduction & Tile Vectorization:**  
   Raw raster probability grids (MBs per frame) are raster-to-vector converted on the server into lightweight GeoJSON polygons compressed with Mapbox Vector Tiles (`mvt`). This reduces payload size from ~15 MB to < 100 KB per update [INFERENCE].

2. **Client-Side Progressive Web App (PWA) Caching:**  
   The frontend GIS dashboard uses Service Workers and IndexedDB to cache base CartoDEM elevation maps and administrative boundaries locally [INFERENCE]. When internet connectivity is lost, cached maps display the last known valid nowcast layer with a prominent "OFFLINE - DATA AS OF [TIMESTAMP]" warning banner [INFERENCE].

3. **Fallback SMS / Cell Broadcast Payload Structure:**  
   For remote field teams with total broadband loss, the backend automatically converts high-risk alerts into compressed CAP-compliant text payloads sent via SMS / Cell Broadcast:  
   `[SACHET-ALERT] RED WARNING: Cloudburst & Flash Flood predicted for Valley Watershed #402 (Chamoli) between 14:00-16:00 IST. Evacuate river banks. Triggers: IWV Surge 85%, CTT Drop 14C/hr.` [INFERENCE].
