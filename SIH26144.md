## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
The National Technical Research Organisation (NTRO) requires the design and development of a high-sensitivity atmospheric microbarometer infrasound sensor capable of measuring sub-audible pressure fluctuations in the 0.01 Hz to 20 Hz frequency band [PS]. Infrasound waves travel thousands of kilometers with minimal atmospheric attenuation, making them critical for detecting covert nuclear tests, distant industrial explosions, rocket launches, volcanic eruptions, and severe weather phenomena [KNOWN]. The proposed system addresses the full hardware lifecycle: a high-precision differential pressure transducer coupled to a mechanical long-period pressure equalization reservoir (backing cavity with a calibrated capillary flow leak) [RESEARCHED], a low-noise analog front-end (AFE) with active temperature compensation [RESEARCHED], a weather-resistant IP67 environmental enclosure [INFERENCE], and a porous-pipe wind-noise reduction spatial filter [RESEARCHED]. The sensor interfaces with an open-market, high-resolution (24-bit) digitizer and real-time waveform visualization software (e.g., Obspy / SeisComP / Python GUI) to demonstrate end-to-end signal detection, sensitivity estimation, and laboratory characterization [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Physical Scale of Pressure:** Ambient static pressure is $\sim 101,325\text{ Pa}$. Infrasound waves of interest are $\sim 0.001\text{ Pa}$ to $10\text{ Pa}$ ($100\text{ dB}$ to $120\text{ dB}$ lower) [KNOWN]. Without a mechanical differential reference, the sensor diaphragm will pin to the rail or destroy its dynamic range [RESEARCHED].
2. **Flicker Noise ($1/f$ Noise):** Electronics operating at $0.01\text{ Hz}$ ($T = 100\text{ s}$) are severely degraded by semiconductor flicker noise [KNOWN]. Standard operational amplifiers are unusable; chopper-stabilized or zero-drift precision amplifiers are mandatory [RESEARCHED].
3. **Thermal Drift:** A temperature change of $1^\circ\text{C}/\text{hour}$ inside an enclosed reference cavity causes gas expansion according to the ideal gas law ($\Delta P = P \cdot \frac{\Delta T}{T} \approx 350\text{ Pa}/^\circ\text{C}$) [KNOWN]. This thermal pressure shift overwhelms infrasound signals ($1\text{ mPa}$) by 6 orders of magnitude without thermal insulation and capillary leaks [RESEARCHED].
4. **Wind Turbulence:** Outdoor wind speeds above $2\text{ m/s}$ generate incoherent pressure fluctuations up to several pascals in the $0.1$–$5\text{ Hz}$ band [RESEARCHED]. Spatial acoustic filtering (WNRS) is physically mandatory for outdoor testing [RESEARCHED].
5. **Power & Field Logistics:** Field deployments require low power consumption ($<100\text{ mW}$ total sensor draw) to run continuously on small solar panels or batteries [RESEARCHED].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: High-Precision Differential Piezoresistive MEMS Transducer with Pneumatic Capillary Filter & Chopper AFE (Recommended)
* **Mechanism:** Uses a high-sensitivity micromachined piezoresistive differential pressure transducer (e.g., AllSensors DLVR / Amphenol NPC-1210 / Honeywell TruStability series) [RESEARCHED]. Port A is exposed to atmospheric pressure via a WNRS. Port B is connected to a thermally insulated $0.5\text{ L}$ reference volume fitted with a glass micro-capillary tube (establishing $f_c = 0.01\text{ Hz}$) [RESEARCHED]. The output voltage is processed by a zero-drift chopper operational amplifier and 24-bit delta-sigma ADC [RESEARCHED].
* **Complexity:** Medium (Precision acoustic mechanics + low-noise analog PCB design).
* **Cost:** Low ($\sim ₹15,000$ to $₹25,000$ for prototype).
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High (Components are commercially available; capillary sizing is physically verifiable).
* **Production Feasibility:** Very High (Scalable PCB manufacturing and 3D/CNC enclosure fabrication).

### Approach 2: Sealed Dual-Capsule LVDT / Inductive Displacement Microbarometer
* **Mechanism:** Uses an evacuated metallic aneroid capsule (similar to classical CEA MB2000/MB3 designs) whose micro-deformations ($\sim\text{nm/Pa}$) are detected using a Linear Variable Differential Transformer (LVDT) or inductive sensor [RESEARCHED]. Static pressure compensation is achieved by electro-mechanical balance coils or long-period motor equalization [RESEARCHED].
* **Complexity:** Extremely High (Requires sub-micron mechanical machining, custom LVDT coil winding, and precision magnetic shielding).
* **Cost:** High ($>₹1,500,000$ for custom precision machining).
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** Very Low (Unrealistic to machine sub-micron capsule mechanisms within hackathon timelines).
* **Production Feasibility:** Medium (High cost per unit, specialized defense manufacturing required).

### Approach 3: Fully Digital MEMS Micro-Barometer Array with Active DSP Software Equalization
* **Mechanism:** Employs multiple ultra-high-resolution digital barometers (e.g., Bosch BMP390 / ST LPS22HH) operating at high sampling rates, subtracting static DC barometric pressure in software/DSP using digital high-pass IIR filtering without a mechanical capillary [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity:** Low (Purely electronic & software).
* **Cost:** Very Low ($<₹5,000$).
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High.
* **Production Feasibility:** Rejected for Primary Infrasound. Standard digital barometers have a self-noise floor of $\sim 1\text{ Pa RMS}$ at $0.01\text{ Hz}$, which is $1,000\times$ higher than the required $<2\text{ mPa}$ infrasound threshold [RESEARCHED]. They clip under strong ambient wind or saturate 24-bit ADC dynamic range when subtracting $100,000\text{ Pa}$ static pressure [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 1 (Piezoresistive Differential MEMS with Pneumatic Capillary Filter & Chopper AFE)** is selected [INFERENCE].

### Justification & Rejections
1. *Why Approach 3 was rejected:* Purely digital surface barometers suffer from $1/f$ noise and quantization limits when measuring $1\text{ mPa}$ fluctuations on top of a $101,325\text{ Pa}$ baseline [KNOWN]. A mechanical differential reference port is physically indispensable to isolate acoustic pressure from barometric head pressure [RESEARCHED].
2. *Why Approach 2 was rejected:* Manufacturing aneroid diaphragm capsules with nm-scale displacement feedback requires specialized precision metrology facilities unavailable to student teams [INFERENCE].
3. *Why Approach 1 wins:* It matches state-of-the-art low-cost research microbarometers (such as the KNMI mini-MB and Infra-NMT) [RESEARCHED]. It delivers true differential pressure sensing down to $0.01\text{ Hz}$, uses accessible low-noise electronics, allows physical tuning of the capillary acoustic cutoff, and fits comfortably within a student team's build capabilities [INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative
1. **Acoustic Pressure Incident:** Atmospheric pressure variations ($0.01\text{–}20\text{ Hz}$) enter the star-shaped Porous-Pipe Wind-Noise Reduction System (WNRS), averaging out localized wind turbulence [RESEARCHED].
2. **Differential Mechanical Filtering:** The averaged pressure wave directly hits **Port A** (the active sensing face of the differential transducer) [RESEARCHED]. Concurrently, the pressure wave passes through the calibrated glass micro-capillary flow leak into the $500\text{ mL}$ insulated reference cavity (**Port B**) [RESEARCHED].
   - For high-frequency fluctuations ($f > 0.01\text{ Hz}$), the capillary restriction prevents rapid air flow into the cavity; Port B remains at steady static pressure, creating a true differential pressure across the diaphragm ($\Delta P = P_{\text{acoustic}}$) [RESEARCHED].
   - For slow barometric shifts ($f < 0.01\text{ Hz}$), air bleeds through the capillary, equalizing pressure across both ports ($\Delta P \approx 0$), eliminating baseline saturation [RESEARCHED].
3. **Transduction & Thermal Sensing:** The piezoresistive bridge converts diaphragm deflection into a differential microvolt signal ($\sim 20\text{–}50\text{ }\mu\text{V/Pa}$) [RESEARCHED]. An integrated PT1000 RTD continuously monitors temperature inside the cavity [RESEARCHED].
4. **Analog Front-End Processing:** An ultra-low drift voltage reference ($<5\text{ ppm/}^\circ\text{C}$) excites the bridge [RESEARCHED]. A zero-drift chopper instrumentation amplifier (ADA4522/LTC2057) boosts the signal by $100\times$ with zero $1/f$ flicker noise [RESEARCHED]. A 4th-order active Sallen-Key low-pass filter cuts off signals above $25\text{ Hz}$ to prevent alias distortion [INFERENCE].
5. **24-Bit Digitization & Timestamping:** The 24-bit Delta-Sigma ADC samples the signal at $100\text{ SPS}$ with an effective resolution $>20\text{ bits}$ [RESEARCHED]. The microcontroller tags each sample packet with precise UTC timestamps derived from an onboard GPS module or NTP clock [INFERENCE].
6. **Streaming & Visualization:** Data packets are transmitted via SeedLink over Serial/Ethernet to the open-market visualization software (ObsPy / PlotJuggler) for live waveform rendering, power spectral density calculation, and threshold-based event triggers [PS].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### AI/ML Evaluation
* **Is AI required for primary sensing?** **NO.** Primary transduction, acoustic filtering, temperature compensation, signal conditioning, and digitizing MUST remain deterministic hardware and physics-based functions [INFERENCE].
* **Is AI useful downstream?** **YES (Optional Software Layer).** AI can be applied strictly in the post-processing software layer for **automated event classification** (e.g., distinguishing industrial blasts vs volcanic eruptions vs meteors) [RESEARCHED].

### Downstream AI Module Analysis (Optional Software Feature)
1. **Purpose:** Classify detected infrasonic signals into event categories (Industrial Explosion, Thunder, Rocket Launch, Wind Noise Artifact) [INFERENCE].
2. **Model Type:** 1D-CNN or LightGBM on time-frequency spectrogram features (STFT / Continuous Wavelet Transform) [RESEARCHED].
3. **Input:** 10-second waveform segment ($1000\text{ samples}$ @ $100\text{ Hz}$) + spectrogram matrix [INFERENCE].
4. **Output:** Multi-class classification probability + confidence score [INFERENCE].
5. **Data Required:** Publicly available IRIS/CTBTO infrasound event waveform benchmarks [RESEARCHED].
6. **Why AI is Preferable for Classification:** Hand-crafted thresholds struggle with complex dispersion patterns of long-distance infrasound propagation [INFERENCE].
7. **Evaluation:** Classification Accuracy, F1-Score, and False Alarm Rate (FAR) [INFERENCE].

## 20. Risk Register

| Risk Description | Likelihood | Impact | Severity | Mitigation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Flicker ($1/f$) Noise Saturation:** Low-frequency electronic noise overwhelms $0.01\text{ Hz}$ infrasound signals. | Medium | High | High | Use zero-drift chopper operational amplifiers (ADA4522) and ultralow-drift voltage references [RESEARCHED]. |
| **Thermal Pressure Drift:** Temperature changes inside reference cavity mimic pressure waves. | High | High | High | Wrap reference cavity in thermal insulation foam and employ active differential thermistor bridge correction [RESEARCHED]. |
| **Capillary Blockage:** Dust or moisture clogs micro-capillary leak tube, destroying $0.01\text{ Hz}$ cutoff. | Medium | Medium | Medium | Install $0.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ PTFE hydrophobic air filters at capillary inlet ports [INFERENCE]. |
| **Enclosure Acoustic Leakage:** Unsealed housing seams create spurious acoustic low-pass filtering. | High | High | High | Use O-ring sealed IP67 aluminum enclosure with metal bulkhead connectors [INFERENCE]. |
| **ADC Quantization Noise:** Inadequate ADC bit depth hides sub-mPa signals. | Low | High | Medium | Use true 24-bit Delta-Sigma ADC with low-noise PGA [RESEARCHED]. |

Proceed with the development of **Approach 1 (Piezoresistive Differential MEMS with Pneumatic Capillary Filter & Chopper AFE)**.
