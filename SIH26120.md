## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
The Baghewala Field in Rajasthan, operated by Oil India Limited (OIL), extracts ultra-heavy crude oil ($14^\circ\text{--}19^\circ\text{ API}$, viscosity up to $13,000\text{ cP}$ at $50^\circ\text{C}$) from the Infracambrian Jodhpur Sandstone reservoir [PS, KNOWN]. Because of low reservoir temperature ($46\text{--}48^\circ\text{C}$) and poor primary mobility, extraction relies on Cyclic Steam Stimulation (CSS) thermal EOR combined with Sucker Rod Pump (SRP) artificial lift [PS, KNOWN]. Currently, CSS cycle parameters (steam volume, pressure, soak time, production cut-off) and SRP operations (stroke length, SPM, VFD speed) are managed as isolated silos using reactive manual practices [PS]. As the reservoir cools post-steam injection, crude viscosity increases exponentially, creating severe downstroke fluid drag that causes rod floating, impact loading, rod fatigue, pump unsetting, high Steam-Oil Ratios (SOR), and excessive energy consumption [PS, RESEARCHED]. This proposal presents an integrated, AI-enabled Well-to-Surface Digital Twin that couples a physics-informed near-wellbore thermal decay proxy with a 1D Gibbs wave equation dynamic pump card solver and real-time VFD control to continuously optimize steam utilization, eliminate rod floating, and maximize net heavy oil recovery [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Decoupled Rule-Based SCADA + Offline Reservoir Simulation
* **Mechanism**: Use standard CMG STARS offline reservoir simulations to generate lookup tables for steam injection, while executing static rule-based thresholds on SCADA PLCs (e.g., IF load $< 2000\text{ lbs}$ ON downstroke, reduce SPM by 1).
* **Evaluation**:
  * *Problem Fit*: Low. Fails to continuously integrate subsurface and surface domains [INFERENCE].
  * *Complexity*: Low.
  * *Cost*: Low software overhead, but high licensing cost for offline simulators [INFERENCE].
  * *Scalability*: Poor. Lookup tables fail when reservoir conditions deviate from pre-simulated cases [INFERENCE].
  * *Hackathon Feasibility*: High. Easy to program.
  * *Production Feasibility*: Unacceptable. Fails OIL's goal of an integrated digital twin [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Pure Data-Driven Black-Box Machine Learning (LSTM / XGBoost)
* **Mechanism**: Train deep Neural Networks (LSTM/XGBoost) end-to-end on historical steam injection, wellhead temperature, surface load, position, and production data to predict future production and control SPM.
* **Evaluation**:
  * *Problem Fit*: Moderate. Can capture non-linear trends.
  * *Complexity*: Medium.
  * *Explainability*: Extremely poor ("Black-Box"). Petroleum engineers will reject non-physical predictions [RESEARCHED].
  * *Data Requirements*: Extremely high. Needs years of continuous high-frequency field sensor logs [INFERENCE].
  * *Hackathon Feasibility*: Medium, but highly susceptible to severe hallucination/overfitting on small datasets [RESEARCHED].
  * *Production Feasibility*: High risk during unobserved operational regimes [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Hybrid Physics-Informed Digital Twin (PINN + 1D Damped Gibbs Wave Equation + Reinforcement Learning) [RECOMMENDED]
* **Mechanism**:
  1. *Subsurface Domain*: A Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) / Non-Isothermal Reduced Order Model (ROM) enforcing Fourier heat conduction and Material Balance to predict near-wellbore temperature field $T(r, z, t)$ and viscosity $\mu(z, t)$.
  2. *Mechanical Domain*: 1D Wave Equation solver incorporating dynamic fluid viscous drag $c(\mu)$ to translate surface load-position into downhole dynacards.
  3. *AI Diagnostics & Control*: ResNet CNN for real-time dynacard pattern recognition (rod floating detection) coupled with a Bayesian Optimization / RL engine to generate downstroke-decelerated VFD speed profiles and CSS cycle transition alerts.
* **Evaluation**:
  * *Problem Fit*: Perfect. Directly addresses well-to-surface coupling, rod floating, non-Newtonian crude, and CSS optimization [INFERENCE].
  * *Complexity*: High, but modularized.
  * *Explainability*: Excellent. Based on fundamental heat transfer, fluid dynamics, and wave mechanics [INFERENCE].
  * *Cost*: Open-source stack; low deployment cost [INFERENCE].
  * *Hackathon Feasibility*: High when implemented with a modular Python execution layer and interactive Streamlit/React web dashboard [INFERENCE].
  * *Production Feasibility*: Excellent; aligns with industry practices for physics-guided digital twins [RESEARCHED].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

**Selected Approach**: **Approach 3 (Hybrid Physics-Informed Digital Twin)** [INFERENCE].

### Justification & Rejection of Alternatives
1. **Why Approach 1 was rejected**: Offline lookup tables and decoupled SCADA rules cannot dynamically handle the rapid thermal decay transitions occurring post-steam injection [INFERENCE]. When wellbore oil cools, viscosity spikes unpredictably, causing sudden rod floating that static rules miss or overreact to, leading to pump shutdowns rather than optimized VFD speed adjustment [INFERENCE].
2. **Why Approach 2 was rejected**: Pure data-driven black-box neural networks lack physical constraints. If trained on sparse field data, an LSTM can predict physically impossible fluid rates or fail to obey fundamental energy conservation laws during steam soaking [RESEARCHED]. Petroleum engineers at Oil India require transparent physics-grounded models [RESEARCHED].
3. **Why Approach 3 Wins**: Approach 3 combines physical rigor with AI speed. The 1D Gibbs Wave Equation provides exact mechanical diagnostic cards at the downhole pump level, while the Physics-Informed Thermal Proxy delivers instantaneous near-wellbore temperature and viscosity profiles [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE]. This allows the digital twin to dynamically compute the exact onset of rod floating and adjust VFD downstroke speeds in real time, directly solving Oil India's challenges [PS, INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data Flow Narrative
1. **Telemetry Ingestion**: High-frequency ($10\text{--}50\text{ Hz}$) polished rod load ($lbs$) and position ($in$) data, alongside wellhead temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$), wellhead pressure ($ksc$), and VFD motor frequency ($Hz$) are captured at the wellsite edge gateway [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
2. **Edge Buffering & Transmission**: The edge gateway packages sensor telemetry into MQTT JSON payloads and publishes them to the central MQTT broker over local radio/cellular links [INFERENCE].
3. **Data Storage**: InfluxDB ingests time-series load-position points, while PostgreSQL logs metadata, CSS cycle parameters, and system alerts [INFERENCE].
4. **Subsurface Thermal Inference**: The wellhead temperature and produced fluid water-cut are fed into the non-isothermal thermal proxy model, which computes the current near-wellbore reservoir temperature field $T(r, t)$ and wellbore fluid viscosity profile $\mu(z)$ [INFERENCE].
5. **Mechanical Dynacard Generation**: The 1D Damped Gibbs Wave Equation solver uses the surface load-position cycle and calculated fluid damping factor $c(\mu)$ to solve for downhole pump load and plunger displacement, generating the Downhole Dynamometer Card [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
6. **AI Dynacard Diagnostic**: The generated dynacard array is passed to the ResNet CNN Classifier, which evaluates card geometry in $<20\text{ ms}$ to identify operational states: *Normal*, *Rod Floating*, *Fluid Pound*, or *Gas Interference* [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
7. **Closed-Loop Control Action**: If *Rod Floating* is detected, the Optimization Engine calculates an asymmetrical stroke speed profile (e.g., downstroke speed slowed from $6\text{ SPM}$ to $3\text{ SPM}$ while keeping upstroke speed at $6\text{ SPM}$) and dispatches control setpoints to the VFD via Modbus TCP [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
8. **CSS Cycle Advisory**: Simultaneously, the CSS Optimization Engine tracks cumulative oil production and steam usage to calculate marginal SOR ($SOR_{marginal}$). When thermal decay causes $SOR_{marginal}$ to exceed economic thresholds, the system flags a "CSS Cycle Cut-Off Advisory" to initiate the next steam injection phase [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: ResNet-18 Convolutional Dynacard Classifier
1. **Exact Problem**: Automatically classify surface and downhole load-position curves into diagnostic operational states (*Normal*, *Rod Floating*, *Fluid Pound*, *Gas Interference*, *Leaky Valve*) [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
2. **Input**: $2D$ normalized load-position array / image tensor ($128 \times 128 \times 1$).
3. **Output**: Diagnostic class label + confidence score (e.g., `Rod Floating`, $98.4\%$).
4. **Why Simpler Rule Fails**: Simple load threshold rules generate high false-alarm rates because load variations are non-linear and depend heavily on dynamic stroke position, well depth, and changing oil viscosity [RESEARCHED]. Pattern recognition of the closed locus loop geometry is required [RESEARCHED].
5. **Data Required**: 1,000+ labeled dynacard samples (augmented via wave equation forward simulator with introduced downhole fault modes) [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
6. **Evaluation**: F1-score, Precision, Recall, and confusion matrix across all 5 fault classes [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) for Near-Wellbore Thermal Decay
1. **Exact Problem**: Predict near-wellbore reservoir temperature field $T(r, t)$ and fluid viscosity $\mu(z, t)$ during post-steam injection production without running heavy 3D numerical grid simulations [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
2. **Input**: Initial steam volume injected ($tons$), injection temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$), soak time ($days$), elapsed production time ($hours$), produced fluid rate ($bpd$), water cut ($\%$).
3. **Output**: $1D$ radial temperature gradient $T(r)$ and wellbore fluid viscosity $\mu(z)$ profile.
4. **Why Simpler Rule Fails**: Empirical exponential cooling curves fail to capture fluid advection and non-uniform thermal diffusion in heterogeneous Jodhpur Sandstone formations [RESEARCHED].
5. **Data Required**: Physics loss function incorporating Fourier's Law of heat conduction and fluid energy balance, constrained by surface wellhead temperature measurements [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
6. **Evaluation**: Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in $^\circ\text{C}$ against analytical radial heat loss solutions and history-matched wellhead temperatures [INFERENCE].
7. **When Wrong**: Physical bounds fallback: If predicted viscosity violates physical PVT boundaries ($<10\text{ cP}$ or $>30,000\text{ cP}$), system defaults to direct PVT lookup based on measured wellhead temperature [INFERENCE].
8. **Training**: Trained off-line on physics loss function + baseline CMG STARS simulation runs [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
9. **Fine-Tuning**: Periodic auto-recalibration using daily wellhead temperature logs.
10. **RAG**: Not required.
11. **Computer Vision**: No.
12. **Speech**: No.

The 1D Damped Gibbs Wave Equation ($\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = a^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} - c \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}$) is a strict physical law governed by rod elastic modulus, cross-sectional area, and fluid damping [RESEARCHED].
```sql
CREATE TABLE wells (
    well_id VARCHAR(32) PRIMARY KEY,
    field_name VARCHAR(64) DEFAULT 'Baghewala',
    formation VARCHAR(64) DEFAULT 'Jodhpur Sandstone',
    depth_m NUMERIC(6,2) DEFAULT 1150.00,
    casing_id_in NUMERIC(4,2),
    tubing_id_in NUMERIC(4,2),
    rod_string_config JSONB, -- Segment lengths and diameters
    created_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

CREATE TABLE css_cycles (
    cycle_id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    well_id VARCHAR(32) REFERENCES wells(well_id),
    cycle_number INT NOT NULL,
    steam_injected_tons NUMERIC(8,2),
    injection_pressure_bar NUMERIC(5,2),
    steam_quality NUMERIC(3,2),
    soak_days INT,
    cycle_start_date DATE,
    production_start_date DATE,
    status VARCHAR(32) -- 'INJECTING', 'SOAKING', 'PRODUCING', 'COMPLETED'
);

CREATE TABLE dynacard_events (
    event_id BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    well_id VARCHAR(32) REFERENCES wells(well_id),
    timestamp TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE NOT NULL,
    surface_card_data JSONB NOT NULL, -- Array of [pos, load]
    downhole_card_data JSONB NOT NULL, -- Array of [disp, load]
    diagnostic_class VARCHAR(64) NOT NULL, -- e.g., 'ROD_FLOATING'
    confidence NUMERIC(5,2),
    vfd_action_taken VARCHAR(128)
);
```

### Local Storage, Caching & Synchronization Logic
1. **Edge Local Buffering**: The wellsite Raspberry Pi/Industrial PC maintains a local SQLite / DuckDB buffer storing up to $30\text{ days}$ of raw load-position $50\text{ Hz}$ telemetry [INFERENCE].
2. **Bandwidth Reduction**: Instead of streaming continuous $50\text{ Hz}$ raw telemetry over cellular links, the edge gateway computes downsampled $1\text{ Hz}$ metrics and only transmits full load-position stroke arrays when a dynacard shape anomaly (e.g., Rod Floating) or periodic audit ($1\text{ per hour}$) occurs [INFERENCE].
3. **Synchronization Protocol**: Uses MQTT Quality of Service Level 1 (QoS 1) with local disk-backed message persistence [INFERENCE]. If network connection drops in the desert, messages queue locally and auto-flush to the central cloud upon reconnection [INFERENCE].
4. **Conflict Resolution**: Master timestamping at the edge gateway using hardware RTC synchronized via NTP when online. Server treats edge-stamped events as source of truth for chronological sequencing [INFERENCE].
