## 1. Executive Snapshot

This technical dossier presents a comprehensive research and architecture proposal for **SIH26184**, issued by the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)** under the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)** [PS]. The primary objective is to transform India's cybercrime response from a reactive account-freezing mechanism into a **proactive, predictive interdiction framework** [PS, INFERENCE]. Today, the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (NCRP) processes over 8,000 complaints daily [PS]. While the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System (CFCFRMS) and Helpline 1930 freeze funds in transit, cybercriminals frequently complete cash-outs via Automated Teller Machines (ATMs), Business Correspondents (BCs), and Point-of-Sale (POS) outlets before lien markings propagate across banking channels [KNOWN, RESEARCHED]. This dossier details a production-grade yet hackathon-feasible framework—**`CyberLocate-AI`**—that combines spatio-temporal machine learning, graph-based money-mule link analysis, and GIS-enabled geospatial risk modeling to forecast cash withdrawal hotspots 15 to 90 minutes in advance [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION]. This enables targeted law enforcement dispatch and automated banking alerts to intercept cash-outs during the "golden hour" [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Geographic & Spatial Realities**:
   * India has over 2,500,000+ ATMs, Cash Recyclers, and Micro-ATMs / Business Correspondents (BCs) distributed heterogeneously across urban hubs (high density) and rural semi-urban corridors (sparse density) [KNOWN].
   * Cybercrime operations often operate cross-jurisdictionally (e.g., scam origin in Jamtara/Mewat, victim in Bengaluru, mule account in Kolkata, cash withdrawal at an ATM in Surat) [KNOWN, RESEARCHED].

2. **Temporal Constraint ("The Golden Hour")**:
   * Once a victim transfers money, fraudsters layer funds across 3 to 5 mule accounts within 10–20 minutes [RESEARCHED]. Physical ATM cash withdrawals typically occur within 30 to 120 minutes of the crime [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
   * System processing + prediction latency must be **under 15 seconds** per incoming complaint to preserve an actionable intervention window for LEAs [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

3. **Field & Connectivity Constraints**:
   * Police patrol cars and beat officers operate on variable 3G/4G/5G mobile networks with periodic dropouts in rural/semi-urban zones [KNOWN].
   * The field interface must support lightweight PWA/Mobile layouts with offline caching of last-known alert locations [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

4. **Data Availability & Privacy Constraints**:
   * Live NCRP/CFCFRMS data contains sensitive Personally Identifiable Information (PII) including Aadhaar, PAN, phone numbers, and full bank account numbers [KNOWN].
   * Strict compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 and MHA cyber-security guidelines is mandatory [KNOWN]. Data shown in prototypes must be synthetic or fully sanitized [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

### Evaluation of Approaches

1. **Approach 1: Heuristic Rule Engine + Static Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)**:
   * **Mechanism**: Flag ATMs near branches where mule accounts were registered or historical frauds occurred [INFERENCE].
   * **Rejection Reason**: Cybercriminals intentionally use ATMs far from mule account home branches to evade detection. Static rules produce excessive false positives and fail on new fraud patterns [INFERENCE].

2. **Approach 2: Deep Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GNN)**:
   * **Mechanism**: End-to-end deep learning framework modeling bank accounts as dynamic graph nodes and ATMs as spatial nodes [INFERENCE].
   * **Rejection Reason**: Training ST-GNNs requires massive, pristine historical graph datasets with exact microsecond timestamps. Black-box neural outputs are difficult to justify in court proceedings or police dispatch logs [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

3. **Approach 3 (RECOMMENDED): Hybrid Spatial-Graph Ensemble Framework**:
   * **Mechanism**: Three-stage pipeline: (1) Graph Link Analysis on incoming complaint beneficiary accounts to trace mule clusters, (2) ST-DBSCAN spatial density clustering to group candidate ATMs within travel-time radii, and (3) XGBoost/LightGBM risk scoring based on historical withdrawal velocity, ATM density, and transaction amount [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
   * **Selection Reason**: Delivers high prediction accuracy, fast execution speed (<5 seconds inference), full explainability for police officers, and can be fully demonstrated during SIH using synthetic streams [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The selected solution is **`CyberLocate-AI`: A Hybrid Graph Analytics and Spatio-Temporal Machine Learning Framework** [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

### Key Reasons for Selection

1. **Solves the Spatial Decoupling Challenge**: Pure spatial models fail because victim location $\neq$ fraudster location. By combining graph-based mule tracing with spatial travel-time probability, the system bridges digital transaction data with physical ATM geography [INFERENCE].
2. **Explainable Intelligence for Law Enforcement**: Law enforcement officers cannot deploy teams based on unexplainable "black box" scores. `CyberLocate-AI` provides transparent risk factors (e.g., *"3 complaints in last 20 mins linked to Mule Account X -> 82% probability of withdrawal within 3km ATM Cluster Y based on 14 past cases"*) [INFERENCE].
3. **High Performance under Hackathon Constraints**: The pipeline uses robust, lightweight libraries (`NetworkX`, `Scikit-Learn`, `XGBoost`, `PostGIS`, `FastAPI`, `Mapbox GL`) that can be reliably built, tuned, and demonstrated within the hackathon timeline [ASSUMPTION].

### Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative

1. **Data Ingestion & Sanitization**: An incoming complaint ticket (from 1930 or NCRP) enters via the `Ingestion Gateway`. PII (Aadhaar, phone numbers, account numbers) is immediately hashed using SHA-256 for privacy compliance, while spatial metadata (district, timestamp, amount, bank IFSC, beneficiary account hash) is stored [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
2. **Graph Traversal**: The `Mule Graph Analyzer` queries the transaction graph. If the beneficiary account is connected to known mule networks or has received multiple rapid transfers, the graph engine flags the active mule cluster and extracts connected ATM debit cards / POS identifiers [INFERENCE].
3. **Spatial Radius Mapping**: The `Geospatial Risk Engine` queries PostGIS to find all candidate ATMs within an optimal travel radius (e.g., 2km to 10km) around the inferred physical nexus of the mule account or recent local cash-outs [INFERENCE].
4. **Machine Learning Risk Scoring**: The `Spatio-Temporal Predictor` evaluates candidate ATM clusters using features such as current time-of-day, historical cash-out frequency, transaction amount magnitude, and spatial density. It assigns a unified risk score ($0.00$ to $1.00$) and estimated withdrawal window (e.g., $30 \pm 15$ minutes) [INFERENCE].
5. **Dynamic Dashboard Visualization & Alerting**: High-risk clusters ($Score > 0.75$) trigger dynamic red hotspot rings on the Mapbox GIS UI. Simultaneously, the `Multi-Channel Dispatcher` sends a WebSocket alert to the local Police Station Dashboard, dispatches an SMS to the designated beat officer, and transmits an automated Webhook payload to the bank's fraud response desk [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Module 1: Spatio-Temporal Cash Withdrawal Predictor

1. **Exact Problem Solved**: Predicts the likelihood score $P \in [0, 1]$ of a cash withdrawal occurring at a specific ATM cluster within a target time window ($t + 15\text{m}$ to $t + 90\text{m}$) [INFERENCE].
2. **Input**: Defrauded amount, time of complaint, victim location coordinates, beneficiary bank code, historical ATM cash-out density, mule graph depth score [INFERENCE].
3. **Output**: Sorted list of ATM Terminal IDs with predicted probability scores and estimated time-to-withdrawal [INFERENCE].
4. **Why Simpler Rules Fail**: Simple static rules cannot account for dynamic temporal velocity, non-linear travel times, or multi-dimensional fraud patterns [INFERENCE].
5. **Data Required**: Historical complaint logs with ground-truth cash-out locations, ATM geospatial registry, timestamped transfer logs [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
6. **Evaluation Metrics**: Precision@K (K=3 ATM clusters), Recall, Area Under ROC Curve (AUC-ROC), Mean Absolute Error (MAE) on estimated arrival time [INFERENCE].
7. **Failure Mitigation**: If model prediction confidence is $<0.50$, the system defaults to a conservative district-wide alert rather than pinpointing a specific ATM, preventing wasted police deployments [INFERENCE].
8. **Training Strategy**: Supervised tabular classification (XGBoost Classifier) trained on historical/synthetic spatio-temporal datasets [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
9. **Fine-Tuning**: Hyperparameter optimization using GridSearch/Optuna over learning rate, tree depth, and spatial decay parameters [INFERENCE].
10. **RAG Usage**: Not required [INFERENCE].
11. **Computer Vision**: Not required for core engine (Optional for post-event ATM CCTV analysis in future scope) [INFERENCE].
12. **Speech Processing**: Not required [INFERENCE].

### Offline & Low-Network Resiliency Design

1. **Local Storage & Caching**: The mobile PWA utilizes `IndexedDB` to cache the last 50 high-priority alerts and offline base map tiles (Vector Tiles for the assigned district) [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
2. **Synchronization & Retry Strategy**: When a field officer updates an alert status (e.g., *"Patrol Dispatched to ATM #402"*), the request is queued in IndexedDB. A Background Sync service worker retries transmission every 10 seconds using exponential backoff until network connection is restored [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
3. **Bandwidth Reduction**: API responses utilize Protocol Buffers or compressed JSON (Gzip/Brotli) stripping unnecessary attributes. Vector map tiles are rendered client-side rather than transferring heavy raster images [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 15. Security, Privacy & Governance

1. **PII Protection & Data Anonymization**: All citizen PII (Aadhaar number, phone number, full bank account number) is anonymized at the ingestion boundary using HMAC-SHA-256 with a rotated salt [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION]. Only authorized Law Enforcement Investigating Officers with active case assignments can view unmasked data via strict audit-logged decryption endpoints [INFERENCE].
2. **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)**:
   * `Role 1: I4C Administrator`: Full system monitoring, global heatmap visibility, cross-state analytical digests [INFERENCE].
   * `Role 2: State / District Nodal Officer`: Regional alert queue management, team dispatch authorization, PDF report downloads [INFERENCE].
   * `Role 3: Field Patrol Officer`: Read-only map views of local assigned alerts, simple binary status update toggles (*"En Route"*, *"Site Inspected"*, *"Suspect Apprehended"*) [INFERENCE].
   * `Role 4: Bank Fraud Control Desk`: Automated Webhook read access to high-risk mule account flags and connected ATM card limits [INFERENCE].
3. **Audit Trails & Evidentiary Integrity**: All user actions (searches, alert views, status changes, PDF exports) are logged into an append-only `Audit_Logs` database table. Cryptographic hashes (SHA-256) of alert dossiers ensure legal tamper-proofing under Section 63 of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) / Section 65B of Evidence Act [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
4. **False Positive & Governance Disclaimers**: The system explicitly flags outputs as *Predictive Intelligence Risk Indicators*. Physical interdiction and account holds must comply with Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) procedural requirements [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
