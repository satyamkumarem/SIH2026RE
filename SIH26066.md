# 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
**OceanEmbed** is an end-to-end, satellite embedding-based deep learning framework engineered for the **Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) - INCOIS** [PS] to reconstruct 3D subsurface ocean temperature profiles down to a depth of 1,000 meters across the North Indian Ocean [PS]. While satellite sensors deliver high-resolution daily observations of the ocean surface (SST, SSS, SSH/SLA, currents, and winds) [PS], direct subsurface thermal measurements remain spatiotemporally sparse, reliant on irregular ARGO profiling floats [PS]. OceanEmbed bridges this gap by transforming multi-modal 2D satellite surface observations into compact, physics-aware latent embeddings using a Vision Transformer / Convolutional Autoencoder backbone, and decoding these representations into continuous, depth-wise daily ocean temperature fields across 15 standard vertical levels at a 0.25° × 0.25° spatial resolution [PS]. The framework incorporates an automated data harmonization pipeline, a physics-informed stratification loss, and an independent in-situ validation engine benchmarked against gridded ARGO profiles [PS], serving operational needs in marine heatwave tracking, cyclone heat content estimation, sonar propagation modeling, and ocean state forecasting [KNOWN].

### Fundamental System Objective
To reconstruct daily three-dimensional subsurface ocean temperature fields across 15 standard depth levels (0m to 1,000m) at 0.25° horizontal resolution over the North Indian Ocean (5°N–30°N, 45°E–105°E) [PS] using latent satellite embeddings derived from multi-modal 2D surface observations, rigorously validated against independent in-situ ARGO profiling float observations [PS].

# 2. Problem Deconstruction

The problem description provided by MoES / INCOIS is deconstructed into explicit atomic requirements and technical implications:

1. **Target Spatial Domain**: North Indian Ocean bounded by **5°N to 30°N latitude** and **45°E to 105°E longitude** [PS].
   - *Technical Implication*: Latitude range = 25° (101 grid points at 0.25° spacing); Longitude range = 60° (241 grid points at 0.25° spacing) [INFERENCE]. Horizontal grid size is $101 \times 241 = 24,341$ points per horizontal slice [INFERENCE].
2. **Spatial Grid Resolution**: Standardized to **0.25° × 0.25°** (~28 km × 28 km) [PS].
3. **Temporal Resolution**: **Daily** continuous fields [PS].
4. **Input Variables (7 Surface Channels)** [PS]:
   - Sea Surface Temperature (SST) [$^{\circ}\text{C}$] [PS]
   - Sea Surface Salinity (SSS) [PSU] [PS]
   - Sea Surface Height (SSH) / Sea Level Anomaly (SLA) [m] [PS]
   - Surface Ocean Currents: Zonal velocity component ($U$) and Meridional velocity component ($V$) [m/s] [PS]
   - Surface Winds: Zonal velocity component ($U_{10}$) and Meridional velocity component ($V_{10}$) [m/s] [PS]
5. **Embedding Engine**: Deep learning representation architecture (CNN, Vision Transformer (ViT), Autoencoders, Graph Neural Networks (GNN), or Attention hybrids) to transform 7-channel 2D surface observations into compact, low-dimensional latent embeddings capturing non-linear surface-subsurface physical dynamics [PS].
6. **Target Depth Levels**: Reconstruct temperature ($^{\circ}\text{C}$) at **15 standard vertical levels (in meters)**:
   - **(0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 200, 300, 500, 700, 1000)** [PS].
   - *Technical Implication*: Output tensor size per day is $101 \times 241 \times 15 = 365,115$ vertical ocean grid points [INFERENCE].
7. **Training Target Dataset**: **GLORYS Global Ocean Reanalysis** (DOI: `10.48670/moi-00021`) [PS] providing 3D daily temperature fields.
   - *Technical Implication*: GLORYS12V1 is natively provided at $1/12^{\circ}$ (~8 km) with 50 vertical levels [RESEARCHED]. Must be spatially interpolated and vertically extracted at the specified 15 depth levels and $0.25^{\circ}$ grid [INFERENCE].
8. **In-situ Validation Dataset**: **Gridded ARGO / INCOIS Live Access Server (LAS)** independent in-situ profile observations [PS].
9. **Data Harmonization Pipeline**: Automated pipeline for spatial/temporal interpolation and regridding of heterogeneous multi-source satellite products [PS].
10. **Quantitative Evaluation & Metrics**: Skill assessment using independent ARGO observations evaluated via Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Pearson Correlation Coefficient ($r$), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Bias across depth layers [PS].
11. **Proof-of-Concept (PoC)**: Working demonstration over the **Bay of Bengal** and **Arabian Sea** regions [PS].

# 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

1. **Domain Grid Sizing**: The target region (5°N–30°N, 45°E–105°E) corresponds to a $101 \times 241$ grid [INFERENCE]. At 15 depth levels, each daily record contains $365,115$ float32 values (~1.46 MB uncompressed NetCDF tensor per day) [INFERENCE].
2. **Physical Oceanographic Constraints**:
   - Surface observations (SST, SSS, SSH) only contain indirect physical signatures of deep ocean dynamics [PS]. Altimetry (SSH) reflects integrated ocean density and thermocline displacement [KNOWN]; surface winds drive Ekman transport and vertical mixing [KNOWN]; SSS impacts upper-ocean stratification and barrier layer thickness [KNOWN].
   - Physical stratification dictates that temperature must non-linearly decrease or remain stable with depth (preventing unphysical density inversions in non-polar waters) [KNOWN].
3. **In-situ Data Sparsity**: ARGO floats provide point profiles irregularly distributed in space and time [KNOWN]. Direct interpolation of ARGO floats is insufficient for daily 0.25° grid reconstruction, necessitating reanalysis (GLORYS) for training targets [INFERENCE].
4. **Cloud Cover & Satellite Sensor Noise**: Optical SST (infrared) suffers from persistent cloud contamination during the Southwest Monsoon (June–September) over the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea [KNOWN]. Microwave SST and reanalysis interpolation must be utilized to prevent missing input patches [INFERENCE].
5. **Operational Infrastructure Realities at INCOIS**: Deployment targets Linux HPC environments running Python/PyTorch with NetCDF-4/Zarr pipelines [RESEARCHED]. Live inference must process daily surface files within minutes without requiring multi-GPU clusters for single-day evaluation [INFERENCE].

# 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: 3D U-Net Architecture (Direct Surface-to-3D Spatial Mapping)
- **Mechanism**: A 2D-to-3D U-Net encoder-decoder where 2D surface features ($7 \times 101 \times 241$) are expanded via 3D transposed convolutions into a 3D volumetric output ($15 \times 101 \times 241$).
- **Problem Fit**: Direct mapping of spatial patterns [INFERENCE].
- **Accuracy Potential**: Moderate (RMSE ~$0.60^{\circ}\text{C}$ in upper ocean) [INFERENCE].
- **Data Requirements**: High (requires long historical 3D tensor time-series) [INFERENCE].
- **Complexity**: Moderate [INFERENCE].
- **Explainability**: Low (black-box CNN filters) [INFERENCE].
- **Cost**: Low GPU memory efficiency due to large 3D feature maps [INFERENCE].
- **Scalability**: Limited to fixed spatial grids [INFERENCE].
- **Hackathon Feasibility**: High feasibility, easy to code in PyTorch [INFERENCE].
- **Production Feasibility**: Medium (high GPU memory load during batch training) [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Network (GNN) with Mesh Spatial Representation
- **Mechanism**: Models ocean grid cells as nodes in a geographic mesh graph, connecting non-adjacent spatial regions via ocean current advection vectors [INFERENCE]. Uses Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNN) to propagate surface anomalies into vertical subsurface node features [INFERENCE].
- **Problem Fit**: Strong theoretical fit for non-Euclidean fluid motion [INFERENCE].
- **Accuracy Potential**: High in dynamic eddy regions [INFERENCE].
- **Data Requirements**: High (requires graph construction and edge feature computation) [INFERENCE].
- **Complexity**: Extremely High [INFERENCE].
- **Explainability**: Medium (node/edge attention weight analysis) [INFERENCE].
- **Cost**: High computational cost for graph construction [INFERENCE].
- **Scalability**: High for irregular meshes [INFERENCE].
- **Hackathon Feasibility**: Low (complex graph construction, risk of debugging delays during 36-hour build) [INFERENCE].
- **Production Feasibility**: Medium [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Hybrid Masked Vision Transformer Encoder + Physics-Informed Volumetric Decoder (Recommended)
- **Mechanism**: Multi-modal surface inputs ($7 \times 101 \times 241$) are partitioned into spatial patches ($4 \times 4$ or $8 \times 8$) and encoded via a **Swin Transformer / Vision Transformer (ViT)** to produce a 256-dimensional compact **Satellite Latent Embedding** [INFERENCE]. A light 3D Transpose/ConvNeXt Decoder converts latent embeddings into the 15-level depth profile [INFERENCE]. Loss function combines Weighted L1 Loss, Thermocline Gradient Loss ($\nabla_z T$), and Stratification Monotonicity Loss [INFERENCE].
- **Problem Fit**: Directly fulfills the PS requirement for "compact satellite embeddings" using ViT/Attention hybrid architectures [PS].
- **Accuracy Potential**: High (Expected RMSE $< 0.45^{\circ}\text{C}$ across 0–1000m) [INFERENCE].
- **Data Requirements**: Standard 7-channel surface grids + GLORYS 3D target tensors [INFERENCE].
- **Complexity**: High, but modular (Encoder and Decoder decoupled) [INFERENCE].
- **Explainability**: High (Self-attention heatmaps reveal surface drivers of deep structures) [INFERENCE].
- **Cost**: Optimized memory footprint via patch embeddings [INFERENCE].
- **Scalability**: Excellent across varying spatial extents [INFERENCE].
- **Hackathon Feasibility**: High (Modular architecture allows parallel team development) [INFERENCE].
- **Production Feasibility**: Very High (Compact latent embeddings allow fast real-time inference) [INFERENCE].

1. **Approach 1 (3D U-Net)** was rejected because direct 2D-to-3D convolution does not extract an explicit, compact, reusable **Latent Satellite Embedding**, violating a core explicit requirement of the problem statement [PS]. ### Data-Flow Narrative
1. **Ingestion & Harmonization**: Surface satellite files (OISST, SMAP, CMEMS Altimetry, ERA5 Winds) are fetched daily [RESEARCHED]. The harmonization module crops the North Indian Ocean domain (5°N–30°N, 45°E–105°E), performs bilinear spatial regridding to $0.25^{\circ} \times 0.25^{\circ}$ ($101 \times 241$ matrix), standardizes units (e.g., Kelvin to Celsius, Pa to m/s), and stacks them into a $7 \times 101 \times 241$ input array [INFERENCE].
2. **Latent Representation**: The stacked 7-channel array is passed into the Vision Transformer / Swin Encoder [INFERENCE]. The encoder partitions the spatial domain into patches, applies self-attention across multi-modal surface variables, and compresses the spatial context into a $256$-dimensional compact latent embedding tensor $Z$ [INFERENCE].
3. **Volumetric Decoding**: The 3D Transpose ConvNeXt Decoder receives latent tensor $Z$ and reconstructs the 15 standard vertical temperature layers ($15 \times 101 \times 241$) [INFERENCE].
4. **Physics-Informed Loss Calculation**: During training, the reconstructed 3D field is compared against GLORYS12V1 ground truth [PS]. The loss function applies heavy weighting to the thermocline region (75m–200m) and penalizes vertical temperature inversions [INFERENCE].
5. **Validation Benchmarking**: Independent ARGO float profiles are queried from INCOIS LAS NetCDF stores [PS]. The system extracts model predictions at exact float coordinate locations $(x, y, t)$ and computes point-to-point skill metrics (RMSE, MAE, Pearson $r$) [PS].
6. **Visualization & Delivery**: The generated 3D daily temperature volume is written to Zarr format and served via a FastAPI backend to an interactive WebGIS frontend displaying 3D depth slices, transect views, and marine heatwave alerts [INFERENCE].

# 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: Satellite Latent Embedding Engine (Encoder)
- **Purpose**: Transform multi-modal 2D surface observation maps (7 channels) into a compact, low-dimensional latent vector capturing non-linear ocean dynamic signatures [PS].
- **Model Type**: Vision Transformer (Swin Transformer-Base) / Convolutional Autoencoder [INFERENCE].
- **Input**: 7-channel surface observation tensor $(B, 7, 101, 241)$ [PS].
- **Output**: Compact latent embedding tensor $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 256 \times 12 \times 30}$ [INFERENCE].
- **Data Required**: Historical surface fields (2010–2023) from GLORYS / satellite products [PS].
- **Why AI is Preferable**: Multi-variable non-linear relations between altimetry anomalies, wind stress, salinity, and thermocline depth cannot be modeled by linear EOF or GEM methods [RESEARCHED].
- **Evaluation**: Re-projection fidelity, embedding compression ratio, and downstream decoding skill [INFERENCE].
- **Failure Mode**: Extreme unseen surface anomalies (e.g., Super Cyclone) causing embedding out-of-distribution drift [INFERENCE].
- **Human Oversight**: Visual audit of latent activation maps by INCOIS oceanographers [INFERENCE].

### Component 2: Volumetric Subsurface Depth Reconstruction Engine (Decoder)
- **Purpose**: Decode the latent embedding $Z$ into a 3D ocean temperature volume across 15 standard depth levels [PS].
- **Model Type**: 3D Transpose ConvNeXt Decoder [INFERENCE].
- **Input**: Latent embedding tensor $Z$ [INFERENCE].
- **Output**: 3D subsurface temperature tensor $(B, 15, 101, 241)$ [$^{\circ}\text{C}$] [PS].
- **Data Required**: GLORYS 3D reanalysis temperature fields [PS].
- **Why AI is Preferable**: Subsurface temperature fields have non-linear vertical stratification that empirical lookup tables fail to generalize across seasons [RESEARCHED].
- **Evaluation**: Layer-wise RMSE, MAE, Pearson $r$, and Bias against ARGO float profiles [PS].
- **Failure Mode**: Vertical over-smoothing in sharp thermocline layers [INFERENCE].
- **Human Oversight**: Automated flagging of profiles violating hydrostatic stability ($\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial z} < 0$) [INFERENCE].

### Explicit Non-AI Components
- **Spatial Regridding & Interpolation**: Handled via standard ESMF / bilinear interpolation algorithms [PS].
- **Ocean Heat Content (OHC) & Mixed Layer Depth (MLD) Calculation**: Computed via deterministic physical equations:
  $$\text{OHC} = C_p \rho_0 \int_{0}^{D_{26}} (T(z) - 26) \, dz$$
  where $C_p = 3985\text{ J/(kg K)}$ and $\rho_0 = 1025\text{ kg/m}^3$ [KNOWN]. AI should **NOT** be used to approximate these deterministic integration formulas [INFERENCE].
