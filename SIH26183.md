## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
The **Real-Time Crypto Fraud Attribution System (RTCFAS)** is an automated, multi-chain blockchain intelligence and investigative platform designed for Indian Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs), the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), and the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) [PS]. When financial cyber fraud victims report suspect cryptocurrency wallet addresses via the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP / 1930 Helpline), bad actors rapidly layer funds through burner wallets, mixers, cross-chain bridges, and decentralized protocols to reach off-ramps [PS, KNOWN]. RTCFAS automates the complex task of multi-hop blockchain graph traversal, heuristic-based wallet clustering, and entity attribution to identify the nearest Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) or cryptocurrency exchange holding or processing the stolen funds in real-time [PS, INFERENCE]. By generating standardized legal notices, risk scores, and visual fund-flow graphs, the system enables LEAs to immediately issue freeze requests under Indian law (such as Section 91/102 CrPC / BNSS) and interface with registered VASPs via I4C’s SAHYOG portal to prevent asset siphoning [PS, KNOWN, INFERENCE].

## 2. Problem Deconstruction

### Explicit Requirements (Extracted directly from Problem Statement [PS])
1. **[REQ-01] Real-Time Wallet Ingestion**: Ingest suspect cryptocurrency wallet addresses reported through cybercrime complaint systems (NCRP / 1930) across fraud types (investment scams, task-based frauds, sextortion, ransomware, phishing, darknet transactions, and organized cyber financial crimes) [PS].
2. **[REQ-02] Automated Blockchain Tracing**: Automatically perform multi-hop blockchain tracing starting from victim-reported addresses without requiring manual expert graph analysis [PS].
3. **[REQ-03] VASP & Exchange Identification**: Identify the nearest or final destination Cryptocurrency Exchange or Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) receiving direct deposits or layered funds [PS].
4. **[REQ-04] Pattern & Typology Detection**: Detect fund movement patterns, intermediary layering wallets, burner wallets, mixers/tumblers, bridges, and privacy-enhancing mechanisms [PS].
5. **[REQ-05] Multi-Chain & Cross-Chain Support**: Support multiple major blockchain ecosystems (e.g., Bitcoin, Ethereum, TRON, BNB Chain) and trace cross-chain fund movements [PS].
6. **[REQ-06] System Integration**: Integrate via APIs with I4C platforms, specifically NCRP (National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal) and the SAHYOG portal [PS].
7. **[REQ-07] Automated Alerting & Risk Categorization**: Perform AI/ML-assisted risk detection and wallet risk categorization, generating real-time automated alerts for high-risk transfers [PS].
8. **[REQ-08] Investigation Dashboard & Visualization**: Provide interactive analytics dashboards with fund-flow visualization, automated investigative recommendations, and standardized legal report generation for LEAs [PS].
9. **[REQ-09] Scalable Architecture**: Implement scalable blockchain indexing and API integration capabilities to reduce investigation response times and improve freezing of crime proceeds [PS].

### Implicit & Derived Requirements [INFERENCE]
1. **[IMP-01] Entity Attribution Database**: Requires an extensive attribution dataset mapping known Exchange Hot/Cold Wallets, Deposit Addresses, and VASP Infrastructure (specifically FIU-IND registered VASPs) [INFERENCE].
2. **[IMP-02] Offline & Local Deployability**: Must function inside secure LEA intranet networks (e.g., State Cyber Cells) with fallback mechanisms when live external RPCs or APIs encounter rate limits [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
3. **[IMP-03] Evidentiary Integrity**: Exported investigation reports must maintain strict cryptographic chain-of-custody hash signatures suitable for submission in Indian courts under Section 63 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023 [INFERENCE, KNOWN].
4. **[IMP-04] Rate-Limit & RPC Resilience**: Multi-chain crawling must handle high transaction volumes without exceeding free/public API tiers during hackathon demonstration [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

3. **API Rate Limits & RPC Availability**: Commercial blockchain analytics APIs (Chainalysis, TRM) are closed-source and costly [RESEARCHED]. Public RPC endpoints (Infura, Alchemy, Ankr, TronGrid) enforce strict rate limits (e.g., 10-25 requests/sec) [KNOWN]. 4. **Regulatory & Legal Environment**: Under FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit - India) mandates and PMLA rules, Virtual Digital Asset Service Providers (VDASPs) catering to Indian users must register as Reporting Entities [KNOWN, RESEARCHED]. Unregistered offshore exchanges are blocked via MHA/Sahyog notices under Section 79(3)(b) IT Act [RESEARCHED]. The system must distinguish between FIU-IND registered VASPs and non-compliant offshore platforms [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Approach 1: Basic Rule-Based Traversal & Static Lookup (Rule-Based Graph Search)
* **Mechanism**: Performs Depth-First Search (DFS) on public block explorer APIs starting from the suspect address up to $N$ hops. Compares each touched address against a static CSV database of known exchange hot wallets.
* **Accuracy Potential**: Low to Moderate. Fails on unlabelled deposit addresses, multi-chain swaps, or simple layering techniques like peel chains [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Effort**: Very Low. Fast to build using standard Python scripts and Etherscan API.
* **Hackathon vs Production Feasibility**: Hackathon: High | Production: Unviable (High false-negative rate, API rate limits) [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Deep Learning & GNN-Only Transaction Pattern Classifier
* **Mechanism**: Feeds raw transaction graphs into a Graph Neural Network (GNN - PyTorch Geometric) to classify whether a wallet belongs to a scammer, mixer, or exchange based on structural graph embeddings.
* **Accuracy Potential**: High for anomaly detection, but Low for precise entity attribution (cannot reliably identify *which exact exchange* owns an unlabelled address without hard labels) [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Effort**: High ML training complexity; requires massive labelled graph datasets.
* **Hackathon vs Production Feasibility**: Hackathon: Low (Data scarcity) | Production: Moderate (as a supplementary risk score module) [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Automated Multi-Chain Graph Explorer with Hybrid Heuristics, Dynamic API Crawling, and ML Risk Scoring (RECOMMENDED HYBRID)
* **Mechanism**: Combines high-speed parallel graph traversal (Memgraph / NetworkX) with deterministic clustering heuristics (Common-Input Ownership, Change Address Detection, Deposit Address Reuse). Matches addresses against a dynamic VASP Attribution Index and uses a lightweight GNN/XGBoost model to score layering risk and typology patterns. Automatically triggers NCRP/SAHYOG alert templates [INFERENCE].
* **Accuracy Potential**: High. Combines exact deterministic labels with heuristic clustering and AI anomaly detection [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity & Effort**: Moderate to High, but well-scoped for a 4-6 student hackathon team using modular microservices.
* **Hackathon vs Production Feasibility**: Hackathon: High (using cached graph snippets + RPCs) | Production: High (scalable via Graph DB and full node RPCs) [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 3: Hybrid Automated Multi-Chain Graph Explorer with Heuristic Clustering & FIU-VASP Attribution Engine** is selected [INFERENCE].

### Justification & Rejection of Alternatives
* **Why Approach 1 was rejected**: Simple static lookup cannot unmask modern cyber fraud techniques. Scammers rarely deposit funds directly into central exchange hot wallets; they use unique one-time user deposit addresses generated by exchange APIs [PS, RESEARCHED]. Without heuristic clustering (linking deposit addresses to parent exchange wallets), Approach 1 misses >80% of targets [INFERENCE].
* **Why Approach 2 was rejected**: Pure GNN models are black-box predictors. In criminal investigations and court proceedings, LEAs require deterministic evidence (exact transaction hashes, block numbers, address ownership proofs) admissible under Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) [KNOWN, INFERENCE]. A GNN alone cannot provide legally verifiable exchange identification [INFERENCE].
* **Why Approach 3 Wins**: It provides deterministic mathematical proof when a wallet links to a VASP deposit cluster, uses heuristics to trace through intermediary burner wallets, employs lightweight ML for risk categorization [PS], and generates compliant legal documentation ready for SAHYOG platform dispatch [PS, INFERENCE].

### Step-by-Step Data Flow Narrative [INFERENCE]
1. **Ingestion**: A fraud complaint logged on NCRP triggers a JSON payload containing the victim's transaction hash, suspect wallet address (e.g., `0x71C...` or `T9z...`), token type (e.g., USDT, BTC), scammed amount, and incident timestamp [PS].
2. **Queueing & Fetching**: FastAPI pushes the job to Celery/Redis. The ingestion service queries public RPCs (Alchemy/TronGrid) or cached blockchain nodes to fetch the transaction tree [INFERENCE].
3. **Graph Traversal & Clustering**: The Graph Engine initiates a $k$-hop Breadth-First Search (BFS). If an intermediary wallet splits funds (Peel Chain), the Heuristic Module evaluates co-spending and deposit patterns to cluster sibling addresses into a single entity [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
4. **VASP Matching**: At each node, the address is checked against the **VASP Master Registry**. If an address matches a known exchange deposit pool (e.g., Binance USDT Deposit Contract or CoinDCX Deposit Wallet), the traversal halts and flags the VASP [PS, INFERENCE].
5. **AI Risk Scoring**: The transaction topology (velocity, fan-out ratio, time intervals) is passed to the ML Risk Engine to calculate a Risk Score (0-100) and tag typologies (e.g., *"Automated Layering via Mixer"* or *"Direct VASP Off-ramp"*) [PS, INFERENCE].
6. **Alerting & Report Dispatch**: The platform renders the interactive graph on the LEA dashboard, formats a Section 91/102 BNSS Freeze Notice PDF with cryptographic hashes, and prepares an API payload for the SAHYOG portal to notify the relevant exchange compliance officer [PS, INFERENCE, KNOWN].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Component 1: Wallet Risk Scoring & Fraud Typology Classifier

1. **What exact problem does AI solve?** Automatically categorizes suspect wallet behavior (e.g., Peel Chain, Mixer usage, High-velocity Layering, Mule Wallet) and assigns a composite Risk Score (0-100) to prioritize LEA investigations [PS, INFERENCE].
2. **What is the input?** Graph features: Out-degree, in-degree, average time delta between transactions, transaction value variance, ratio of round-value transfers, and cluster entropy [INFERENCE].
3. **What is the output?** Risk Score (0-100), Fraud Typology Tag (e.g., `INVESTMENT_SCAM_LAYERING`), and Confidence Level [PS, INFERENCE].
4. **Why can a simpler rule/system not solve it?** Cybercriminals continuously tweak transfer amounts and time delays to evade fixed threshold rules. An ML model detects subtle multi-variable topological patterns [INFERENCE].
5. **What data is required?** Historical labelled transaction graphs of known scammed addresses vs legitimate user wallets (synthesized/derived from public datasets like Elliptic Data Set / Ethereum Phishing Dataset) [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
6. **How will it be evaluated?** Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and ROC-AUC curve on held-out test transactions [INFERENCE].
7. **What happens when it is wrong?** False positives or false negatives do NOT block LEA action; the AI score serves purely as an investigative aid. The underlying deterministic transaction graph is always presented to the investigator [INFERENCE].
8. **Does it require training?** Yes (XGBoost / Random Forest on engineered graph features) [INFERENCE].
9. **Does it require fine-tuning?** No deep fine-tuning required for standard tree models [INFERENCE].
10. **Does it require RAG?** No [INFERENCE].
11. **Does it require computer vision?** No [INFERENCE].
12. **Does it require speech processing?** No [INFERENCE].
13. **Does it require forecasting?** No [INFERENCE].
14. **Is human oversight required?** YES. Law Enforcement Officers must review and validate the findings before issuing legal freeze orders [PS, INFERENCE].

### Areas Where AI Should NOT Be Used [INFERENCE]
* **VASP Identification**: Determining whether an address belongs to a specific exchange MUST rely on deterministic address matching, deposit contract heuristics, and verifiable on-chain logs—NOT AI prediction [INFERENCE].
* **Legal Notice Generation**: Legal freeze notices under Section 91/102 BNSS must be generated from exact database records and cryptographic proofs, not generative AI models [INFERENCE, KNOWN].

### Offline & Low-Network Resiliency Strategy [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION]
