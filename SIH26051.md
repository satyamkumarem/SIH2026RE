# SIH26051: Software-Based Model Development for Design of Area-Specific Shelter for Thermal Comfort Maintenance

---

## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
High-altitude and extreme-climate defense deployments (e.g., Ladakh, Siachen, Chang La) suffer from severe sub-zero temperatures (dipping below -35°C to -40°C) [RESEARCHED] [2.6, 2.7, 2.8]. Conventional military shelters rely heavily on fossil fuels (kerosene *bukharis* and diesel generators) for space heating [PS] [RESEARCHED] [2.6]. This reliance creates grave supply-chain logistics, fire hazards, high carbon footprints, and operational vulnerability [KNOWN] [RESEARCHED] [2.6]. While high-altitude regions like Ladakh receive abundant annual solar radiation (1900–2100 kWh/m²/year with ~7.9 daily sunshine hours and >300 cloud-free days) [PS], unoptimized shelter design results in severe nocturnal heat loss through wall envelopes and openings [PS]. This problem statement, sponsored by DRDO (Defence Research and Development Organisation) [PS], calls for a generalizable software model to simulate shelter thermal dynamics, heat loss, solar radiation gain, and composite material/geometry trade-offs under real-time ambient climatic conditions [PS]. The primary objective is to design area-specific, self-sustained passive shelters that maintain human thermal comfort while eliminating or drastically minimizing fossil fuel consumption [PS].

### Explicit Functional Requirements

1. **[PS-REQ-1] Prediction of Shelter Inside Temperature**
   * *Source Text:* "1. Prediction of shelter inside temperature based on the user defined inputs." [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* The system must calculate time-series indoor dry-bulb temperature over 24-hour cycles or custom time windows based on user-supplied climatic data, shelter geometry, thermal mass, and material layer properties [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Requires a dynamic transient thermal solver integrating conductive, convective, and radiative heat transfer equations [INFERENCE].

2. **[PS-REQ-2] Prediction of Thermal Energy Generated from Solar Radiation**
   * *Source Text:* "2. Prediction of thermal energy generated from solar radiation." [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* The software must model solar geometry, direct beam radiation, diffuse sky radiation, and reflected ground albedo onto tilted/oriented shelter surfaces, accounting for glazing transmission and absorber heat capture [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Integration of solar radiation transposition algorithms (e.g., Perez or HDKR anisotropic sky models via `pvlib` or custom solar solvers) [RESEARCHED] [2.2, 2.4, 2.11].

3. **[PS-REQ-3] Heat Flow Details Across Temperature Differentials Over Time**
   * *Source Text:* "3. Heat flow details as per the temperature difference between ambient and shelter temperature for a defined time period." [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* The engine must compute directional heat fluxes ($Q_{conductive}$, $Q_{convective}$, $Q_{radiative}$, $Q_{infiltration}$) across walls, roof, slab, windows, and doors based on $\Delta T = (T_{inside} - T_{ambient})$ over user-specified periods (e.g., hourly, daily, seasonal) [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Detailed component-level thermal balance reporting and spatial heat dissipation visualization [INFERENCE].

4. **[PS-REQ-4] User-Defined Input Interfacing**
   * *Source Text:* "The model should be user friendly and works on user defined values (real time data, material properties etc.) to simulate the cases" [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* Non-simulation experts (e.g., military engineers, shelter designers) must be able to input geographical location/weather profiles, custom dimensions, wall orientations, window-to-wall ratios (WWR), and composite material layers [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Parameterized user interface (Web GUI) with preset material databases (e.g., PUF insulation, aerogel, stone, concrete, PCM, double/triple glazing) and file uploader for weather files (EPW/CSV) [INFERENCE].

5. **[PS-REQ-5] ANSYS General Thermal Simulation Integration**
   * *Source Text:* "A general model development in ANSYS software to thermally simulate the shelter for study of heat losses and capture of real time atmospheric ambient climatic condition data will prove to be helpful. The project is conceptualised for the development of general model in ANSYS software." [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* The backend must interface with ANSYS (APDL / PyMAPDL / PyFluent) to execute finite element analysis (FEA) or computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations for high-fidelity verification [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Automated PyMAPDL script generation for geometry building, meshing, boundary condition application, solving, and parsing binary result files (`.rth`) [RESEARCHED] [3.1, 3.3, 3.11].

6. **[PS-REQ-6] Comparative Material & Design Trade-off Analysis**
   * *Source Text:* "along with comparative analysis with different materials under same ambient condition to predict the most efficient combination of materials, shape, and size etc. in terms of temperature maintenance." [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* The software must enable side-by-side parametric sweeps comparing multiple shelter configurations (e.g., Monoslope vs. Gable roof, Single PUF vs. Vacuum Insulation Panels + PCM, varying solar orientations) under identical ambient weather profiles [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Batch simulation engine and comparative reporting UI with efficiency ranking [INFERENCE].

7. **[PS-REQ-7] Minimization of External Energy and Fossil Fuels**
   * *Source Text:* "The primary objective of the work/project is to minimize the energy utilisation for thermal comfort maintenance in particular and minimization of fossil fuel application in general by designing of area specific self-sustained standalone passive shelter at defined atmospheric climatic condition." [PS]
   * *Interpretation:* The tool must report passive heating self-sufficiency percentage, auxiliary heating fuel saved (liters of kerosene/diesel), and carbon reduction metrics [INFERENCE].
   * *Implementation Implication:* Energy balance module calculating auxiliary heating demand (kWh) and equivalent fuel savings [INFERENCE].


1. **Climatic Extremes:** High Altitude Cold Regions (e.g., Ladakh at 11,000–17,600 ft altitude) feature ambient temperatures plunging to -35°C to -40°C [RESEARCHED] [2.6, 2.7], while solar irradiance peaks between 400 W/m² and 1300 W/m² during day hours [RESEARCHED] [2.6].
2. **Connectivity Constraints:** Military forward posts and remote field offices operate under limited or non-existent internet access [KNOWN]. Software must run offline locally as a desktop app or standalone offline web bundle [INFERENCE].
3. **Hardware Limitations:** Field deployment nodes may lack high-performance GPU clusters [ASSUMPTION]. High-fidelity ANSYS 3D FEA runs should be offloaded or simplified into fast 1D/3D lumped transient RC nodal solvers for real-time interactivity, with ANSYS APDL reserved for batch detailed verification [INFERENCE].
4. **License Constraints:** Full ANSYS Mechanical/Fluent licenses are expensive and restricted to institutional labs [KNOWN]. The software must provide an open-source standalone physics solver while maintaining PyMAPDL export capabilities for ANSYS license holders [INFERENCE].
5. **Language & User Digital Literacy:** Users range from high-level DRDO scientists to field army engineers [INFERENCE]. System UI must feature visual 3D previews, pre-configured material presets, and clear metric outputs (e.g., indoor °C, fuel saved in liters) [INFERENCE].

5. *What balance between 1D transient lump-parameter heat transfer models and 3D FEA meshing achieves <2% error in indoor air temperature prediction while executing in under 3 seconds on standard client hardware?*

### 1. ANSYS Mechanical / Fluent (Standalone Desktop GUI)
* **Description:** Industry-standard Finite Element Analysis (FEA) and Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) software suite [RESEARCHED] [3.3, 3.9].
* **Strengths:** Unrivaled spatial heat transfer accuracy, 3D thermal stress, and CFD convection modeling [RESEARCHED] [3.9].
* **Weaknesses:** Requires costly individual user licenses; steep learning curve; manual CAD setup for every parametric run; lacks built-in building thermal comfort (PMV/PPD) and weather file parsing workflows [INFERENCE].
* **Relevance:** Sponsoring lab explicitly requests ANSYS software model development [PS].
* **Gap:** Missing an intuitive web frontend for rapid parametric material/geometry sweeps and automated APDL generation [INFERENCE].
* **Confidence:** `[KNOWN]`

### 2. EnergyPlus / OpenStudio
* **Description:** US Department of Energy (DOE) open-source whole-building energy simulation engine [RESEARCHED] [4.1, 4.2].
* **Strengths:** Rigorously validated heat-balance engine; handles sub-hourly time steps, complex fenestration, and HVAC systems [RESEARCHED] [4.2].
* **Weaknesses:** Text-based input schema (`.idf` / `.json`) is difficult for field users [RESEARCHED] [4.2, 4.5]; setup requires significant architectural domain knowledge; no direct integration with ANSYS structural finite element models [INFERENCE].
* **Relevance:** Industry gold standard for building thermal analysis [RESEARCHED] [4.1].
* **Gap:** Complex installation and lack of automated high-altitude passive solar passive feature design optimization (Trombe walls, PCM phase transition detailed spatial FEA) [INFERENCE].
* **Confidence:** `[RESEARCHED]` [4.1, 4.2]

### 3. TRNSYS / COMSOL Multiphysics
* **Description:** Specialized transient system simulation software widely used for solar thermal systems and building heat transfer [RESEARCHED] [3.3, 3.6].
* **Strengths:** Excellent library for solar collectors, PCM storage tanks, and thermal mass walls [RESEARCHED] [3.6].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

```
===================================================================================
                        CANDIDATE ARCHITECTURAL APPROACHES
===================================================================================

[APPROACH 1: Full Native ANSYS APDL Automation]
  User Input -> Python PyMAPDL Backend -> Local ANSYS License -> FEA Solver -> 3D Plot
  * Pros: Exact FEA accuracy requested by DRDO.
  * Cons: Hardware heavy, requires local ANSYS installation & license.

[APPROACH 2: Pure Open-Source Physics Engine (EnergyPlus/Python RC)]
  User Input -> Python Lumped Capacitance + EnergyPlus API -> Plotting Dashboard
  * Pros: Fully open source, lightweight, runs on any laptop.
  * Cons: Fails explicit PS requirement for ANSYS model development.

[APPROACH 3: Dual-Core Hybrid Engine (RECOMMENDED)]
  User Input -> Lightweight Python RC Solver (Real-Time Web)
              + PyMAPDL / ANSYS Generator Module (Batch FEA Verification)
              + pythermalcomfort (PMV/PPD) + pvlib (Solar)
  * Pros: Fast interactive user experience PLUS full ANSYS script compatibility.
  * Cons: Dual engine maintenance requirement.
===================================================================================
```

### Approach 1: Full Native ANSYS PyMAPDL Pipeline
* **Mechanism:** Python frontend communicates directly via gRPC with a headless local ANSYS MAPDL instance using `ansys-mapdl-core` (`PyMAPDL`) [RESEARCHED] [3.11]. All simulations are executed strictly as 3D Finite Element models in ANSYS [INFERENCE].
* **Problem Fit:** High compliance with DRDO's request for ANSYS model development [PS].
* **Accuracy Potential:** Ultra-high (3D spatial FEA mesh) [RESEARCHED] [3.9].
* **Data Requirements:** 3D CAD step files, volumetric mesh specs, material conductivity matrices [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity:** Extremely High (mesh generation error handling, license checkout management) [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability:** High (physics-driven FEA) [KNOWN].
* **Cost:** High (requires ANSYS software licenses) [KNOWN].
* **Scalability:** Low (single-instance license lock, heavy compute per run) [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** Poor (jury laptops may not have valid ANSYS licenses installed) [INFERENCE].
* **Production Feasibility:** High for DRDO main office; Low for remote army outposts [INFERENCE].

### Approach 2: Standalone Lightweight Python Lumped-Capacitance (RC Network) Engine
* **Mechanism:** Develops a state-space 1D/3D lumped parameter thermal RC network solver in Python (using `SciPy` / `NumPy`) combined with `pvlib` for solar transposition and `pythermalcomfort` for habitability indices [RESEARCHED] [1.2, 2.3, 4.4].
* **Problem Fit:** Excellent for fast interactive shelter design and trade-off sweeps [INFERENCE].
* **Accuracy Potential:** Moderate to High ($\pm 1.5^\circ C$ dry-bulb prediction when calibrated against empirical data) [INFERENCE].
* **Data Requirements:** Tabular wall construction layer properties, EPW weather files [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity:** Moderate [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability:** High (transparent differential heat balance equations) [INFERENCE].
* **Cost:** Free & Open Source [INFERENCE].
* **Scalability:** Extremely High (runs in web browser or low-spec laptop in <1 second) [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** High [INFERENCE].
* **Production Feasibility:** Moderate (lacks full 3D stress and localized CFD fluid flow details) [INFERENCE].

### Approach 3: Dual-Core Hybrid Architecture (PyMAPDL + Fast RC Thermal Engine) -- RECOMMENDED
* **Mechanism:** Features a **Dual Engine**:
  1. *Interactive Fast Engine:* Python-based Lumped Capacitance RC solver for real-time Web UI design exploration, parametric sweeps, solar gain calculation (`pvlib`), and human comfort evaluation (`pythermalcomfort`) [RESEARCHED] [1.2, 2.3].
  2. *High-Fidelity Verification Engine:* Automated `PyMAPDL` / APDL script exporter that generates complete ANSYS simulation input files (`.cdb` / `.dat`), executes headless ANSYS runs when a license is present, and parses result files (`.rth`) [RESEARCHED] [3.1, 3.3].
* **Problem Fit:** Solves both real-time user-friendliness AND explicit ANSYS deliverable requirements [PS] [INFERENCE].
* **Accuracy Potential:** High in Fast Engine; Ultra-High in ANSYS Engine [INFERENCE].
* **Data Requirements:** Standardized material database, weather EPW files, shelter geometry parameters [INFERENCE].
* **Complexity:** Balanced & Modular [INFERENCE].
* **Explainability:** High [INFERENCE].
* **Cost:** Open-source core; zero cost during hackathon demo [INFERENCE].
* **Scalability:** Excellent [INFERENCE].
* **Hackathon Feasibility:** Very High (demonstrates live interactive UI + valid ANSYS script output) [INFERENCE].
* **Production Feasibility:** Outstanding (deployable to field laptops without ANSYS, while fully compatible with DRDO's high-end ANSYS workstations) [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The **Dual-Core Hybrid Architecture (Approach 3)** is selected as the winning approach [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Rejection of Approach 1 (Pure ANSYS):** Relying solely on ANSYS execution creates an unacceptable single-point-of-failure during the hackathon demo if ANSYS licenses or heavy GPU hardware are unavailable [INFERENCE]. Furthermore, running full 3D FEA meshes for every quick slider tweak in a web UI introduces 2–5 minute latency per simulation, destroying user experience [INFERENCE].
* **Rejection of Approach 2 (Pure Python without ANSYS):** Rejecting ANSYS entirely fails the explicit sponsor requirement: *"The project is conceptualised for the development of general model in ANSYS software."* [PS]. The jury from DRDO will evaluate whether the solution actually interfaces with or outputs valid ANSYS APDL simulation code [INFERENCE].
