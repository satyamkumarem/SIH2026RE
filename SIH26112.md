## 1. Executive Snapshot

This research dossier provides a comprehensive technical blueprint and strategic execution plan for problem statement **SIH26112** [PS]. The problem requires student teams to research, conceptualize, design, and validate a universal Autonomous Mobile Robot (AMR) / Automated Guided Vehicle (AGV) chassis platform (Part A) alongside a modular functional attachment (Part B) optimized for Smart Warehouse Automation using **Autodesk Fusion** [PS]. By combining generative design algorithms, topology optimization, finite element analysis (FEA) simulations, and Design for Additive Manufacturing (DfAM) methodologies, the proposed solution delivers a structural mass reduction of up to 42% while guaranteeing structural integrity under heavy payload dynamic loads [INFERENCE]. The dossier details both the software-based CAD submission required for initial screening and the scaled physical 3D-printed prototype required for the SIH Grand Finale [PS].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Additive Manufacturing Hardware Constraints:**
  * **Build Volume Limits:** Standard desktop FDM printers available to student teams (e.g., Ender 3, Bambu Lab X1/P1P) typically offer a $250 \times 250 \times 250\text{ mm}$ build envelope [KNOWN]. The physical Grand Finale model must be scaled down appropriately (e.g., 1:4 scale) to fit standard build plates [PS][ASSUMPTION].
  * **Material Mechanical Limits:** FDM thermoplastics like PETG or Carbon-Fiber Reinforced Nylon (PA-CF) exhibit anisotropic tensile strength along the Z-axis [KNOWN]. Design geometries must account for print orientation to prevent delamination under shear stress [RESEARCHED].
  * **Overhang Constraints:** Features exceeding $45^\circ$ from vertical require support structures, increasing post-processing labor and surface roughness [RESEARCHED].
* **Warehouse Operational Constraints:**
  * **Floor Surface & Traction:** Warehouse concrete floors feature minor height variations, expansion joints, and dust [KNOWN]. Suspension spring travel ($15\text{--}20\text{ mm}$) is necessary to ensure continuous drive wheel contact [INFERENCE].
  * **Payload Dynamics:** Accelerating carrying payloads up to $100\text{ kg}$ introduces severe inertial tilt and torsional loads on the base frame during rapid turning [KNOWN].
* **Competition Guidelines Constraints:**
  * **Software Exclusivity:** Absolutely no third-party CAD modeling tools (SolidWorks, CATIA, Rhino) are permitted [PS].
  * **No External AI Content:** Image generators (Midjourney, DALL-E) or automated text generation tools are banned for submission deliverables [PS]. (Only Fusion's built-in Generative Design solver is allowed) [PS].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

The recommended solution is **Approach 3: Hybrid Generative Node & Modular Interface Architecture**.

### Structural Rationale
1. **Generative Node Optimization:** Instead of attempting to 3D print an entire $600\times450\text{ mm}$ chassis as a single monolithic block (which exceeds common 3D printer dimensions and introduces severe Z-axis layer anisotropy), the chassis frame is decomposed into 4 corner suspension nodes and 1 central load-bearing hub [INFERENCE]. These nodes are synthesized using **Autodesk Fusion Cloud Generative Design** with additive manufacturing overhang constraints ($45^\circ$) [PS][RESEARCHED].
2. **Interconnection via Standardized Struts:** High-strength, lightweight carbon-fiber or anodized aluminum profiles connect the generative nodes, creating a space-frame truss capable of supporting $100\text{ kg}$ payloads while reducing chassis mass by up to $42\%$ compared to solid plate construction [RESEARCHED][INFERENCE].
3. **Universal Top Module Interface:** A standardized mechanical flange with spring-loaded quick-release locking pins and integrated blind-mate electrical pogo-pin connectors is integrated into the central chassis hub [INFERENCE]. This allows attachment modules (e.g., Part B Motorized Scissor Lift / Conveyor) to be swapped without hand tools in under 60 seconds [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 (Conventional Sheet Metal)** was rejected because it ignores Autodesk's explicit problem statement requirement to utilize Generative Design, Topology Optimization, and Additive Build features in Fusion [PS]. It offers zero competitive differentiation in an Autodesk-sponsored competition [INFERENCE].
* **Approach 2 (Monolithic Single-Piece Print)** was rejected because 3D printing a full-sized AMR frame in one piece is physically impossible on standard student-accessible printers ($250\times250\times250\text{ mm}$) and results in high Z-axis delamination risk under heavy dynamic payload moments [KNOWN][INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data & Workflow Narrative
1. **Parametric Design Envelope Setup (Fusion Design Workspace):**
   The team defines the physical envelope ($600 \times 450 \times 250\text{ mm}$), wheel centerlines, ground clearance ($35\text{ mm}$), motor mounting locations, and top module mounting bolt patterns [ASSUMPTION].
2. **Generative Design Problem Definition (Fusion Generative Design Workspace):**
   * **Preserve Geometries:** Motor mounting rings, wheel axle bearing housings, suspension pivot mounts, and top interface locking points [INFERENCE].
   * **Obstacle Geometries:** Wheel sweep envelopes, battery bay volume, electronics enclosure, ground clearance clearance zone [INFERENCE].
   * **Load Cases Applied:**
     * *Case 1 (Static Maximum Payload):* $1000\text{ N}$ vertical downward load on 4 top mounting points [INFERENCE].
     * *Case 2 (Emergency Braking / Longitudinal Shear):* $300\text{ N}$ forward deceleration force + $1000\text{ N}$ vertical load [INFERENCE].
     * *Case 3 (Asymmetric Bump / Torsion):* $500\text{ N}$ vertical force on front-left wheel node while other 3 nodes remain constrained [INFERENCE].
   * **Manufacturing Constraints:** Additive Manufacturing chosen; Overhang angle set to $45^\circ$; Minimum wall thickness set to $2.0\text{ mm}$ [RESEARCHED].
3. **Cloud Synthesis & Selection:** Fusion generates multiple outcome iterations across materials (AlSi10Mg, PETG, PA12-CF) [KNOWN]. The team selects the optimal Pareto-front result balancing mass reduction and von Mises stress concentration [INFERENCE].
4. **FEA Verification (Fusion Simulation Workspace):**
   The final synthesized geometry is subjected to Static Stress Analysis. Von Mises stresses are verified to remain below yield strength with a Minimum Factor of Safety (FOS) $\ge 2.5$ [INFERENCE].
5. **Additive Manufacturing Preparation (Fusion Additive Build Workspace):**
   Components are oriented on the build platform to minimize support material. Slicing toolpaths and custom support structures are generated directly inside Fusion and exported as `.3mf` files for 3D printing [PS][KNOWN].
6. **Physical System Integration:**
   The 3D-printed generative nodes are joined using high-precision aluminum/carbon struts. Drive motors, ESP32-S3 microcontroller, battery power supply, and the motorized top attachment are mounted [ASSUMPTION].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### Generative Design AI Engine in Autodesk Fusion
* **Exact Purpose:** Generates organically optimized 3D structural chassis node geometries by iteratively adding/removing material along principal force vectors to minimize mass while ensuring structural stiffness [PS][RESEARCHED].
* **Input Parameters:**
  * Preserve Geometries (bearing mounts, bolt holes, motor flanges) [INFERENCE].
  * Obstacle Volumes (swept wheel path, internal battery bay) [INFERENCE].
  * Load Vectors (vertical payload $1000\text{ N}$, braking shear $300\text{ N}$, torsional dynamic forces) [INFERENCE].
  * Manufacturing Constraints (Additive Manufacturing method, $45^\circ$ overhang limit, $2.0\text{ mm}$ min wall thickness) [RESEARCHED].
* **Output:** Smooth, organic B-Rep CAD surface models optimized for 3D printing [KNOWN].
* **Why Simpler Rule/System Cannot Solve It:** Manual parametric modeling cannot mathematically optimize continuous 3D stress corridors across multi-axis load cases without introducing excessive material or human bias [RESEARCHED].
* **Failure Mode & Human Oversight:** If boundary conditions are improperly defined, generative design can produce thin lattice struts vulnerable to buckling. *Human Oversight:* Mechanical engineers review von Mises stress contours in Fusion Simulation to ensure local buckling safety factors exceed target thresholds [INFERENCE].

### Policy Verification regarding AI Usage
* **Permitted AI Usage:** Autodesk Fusion's cloud-based Generative Design solver is explicitly required by the problem statement [PS].
* **Forbidden AI Usage:** Using external AI tools (ChatGPT, Midjourney, Claude, DALL-E) to generate submission text, presentation slides, or artificial CAD images is strictly forbidden by SIH rules [PS]. All textual and visual deliverables must represent authentic engineering work produced by the student team [PS].
