# SIH 2026 Technical Solution & Feasibility Dossier

## 1. Executive Snapshot

### Summary
Law enforcement agencies, particularly the Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB) under the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), face a major operational bottleneck due to the absence of rapid, non-invasive, point-of-testing field screening tools for illicit drug consumption [PS]. Existing biological screening techniques rely on urine or blood samples, which require invasive collection procedures, specialized personnel, sanitary facilities, and extended laboratory analysis times [PS]. This dossier proposes **NarkoBreath**, an indigenous, handheld, electrochemical breath-analysis prototype designed for rapid field screening of narcotics (including cannabinoids, opioids, and synthetic drugs) [PS, INFERENCE]. The system integrates a micro-condensation exhaled breath condensate (EBC) aerosol trap, disposable screen-printed carbon electrodes (SPCE) functionalized with electroactive receptor materials, a low-power potentiostat analog front-end (TI LMP91000), and an ESP32 micro-controller [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION]. Utilizing Differential Pulse Voltammetry (DPV) signal analysis coupled with lightweight edge pattern classification, the device delivers a deterministic "Positive / Negative / Inconclusive" determination in under 90 seconds [PS, INFERENCE]. Results are displayed on an onboard OLED screen and transmitted securely via Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) to an encrypted mobile application for tamper-evident digital chain-of-custody logging [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

* **Geographical & Climatic Constraints:**
  * Indian field conditions involve extreme temperatures ($0^\circ\text{C}$ to $48^\circ\text{C}$) and relative humidity ranging from 10% in dry deserts to 95% during monsoons [KNOWN]. Temperature and humidity drastically alter electrochemical reaction rates and breath condensation efficiency [RESEARCHED].
  * *Design Choice:* Onboard temperature/humidity sensor (SHT31) providing real-time compensation coefficients to the potentiostat peak-current calculations [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Connectivity Restrictions:**
  * Remote national highways, border checkpoints, and rural sectors frequently experience total cellular blackouts [KNOWN].
  * *Design Choice:* Offline-first device architecture; local storage on ESP32 flash memory (SPIFFS/LittleFS) holding up to 1,000 encrypted records with background syncing upon network restoration [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Power Constraints:**
  * Field operations require continuous 8–12 hour shift operation without grid power access [INFERENCE].
  * *Design Choice:* 3.7V 2600mAh LiFePO4 rechargeable battery with USB-C Power Delivery (PD) charging and hardware deep-sleep power states [ASSUMPTION].
* **Digital Literacy & Operational Field Safety:**
  * Field constables require single-button execution without complex sub-menus [INFERENCE].
  * *Design Choice:* Single physical push-button workflow ("Power On" -> "Insert Strip" -> "Blow" -> "Read Result") [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
* **Cross-Contamination & Hygiene:**
  * Law enforcement cannot reuse sampling interfaces between subjects [INFERENCE].
  * *Design Choice:* Single-use, disposable food-grade polypropylene mouthpieces integrated with an anti-backflow check valve to prevent sample contamination [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

### Selection
**Approach 3: Exhaled Breath Condensate (EBC) Micro-Condensing Collection paired with Multi-Channel Electrochemical Voltammetry.**

### Technical Justification
Illicit drugs (THC, Opioids, Amphetamines) have low vapor pressures and exist in exhaled breath primarily trapped inside microscopic fluid aerosols ($0.5\text{--}5\,\mu\text{m}$) originating from the deep lung surface lining fluid [RESEARCHED]. 
1. **Sample Capture:** A lightweight Peltier-cooled condensation chamber rapidly condenses breath humidity and traps aerosol micro-droplets into a liquid sample ($~50\text{--}100\,\mu\text{L}$) within 30 to 40 seconds [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
2. **Detection:** The liquid condensate flows over a disposable Screen-Printed Carbon Electrode (SPCE). When a Differential Pulse Voltammetry (DPV) voltage ramp ($0.0\,\text{V}$ to $+1.2\,\text{V}$) is applied by the potentiostat, target drug molecules undergo characteristic oxidation reactions at specific peak potentials ($E_{peak}$) [RESEARCHED]:
   * $\Delta^9\text{-THC}$ Phenolic oxidation: $\sim +0.50\,\text{V}$ [RESEARCHED]
   * Morphine / Opioid Isoquinoline oxidation: $\sim +0.38\,\text{V}$ [RESEARCHED]
   * Amphetamine / Synthetic Drug Amine oxidation: $\sim +0.85\,\text{V}$ [RESEARCHED]
3. **Quantification:** The magnitude of the resulting oxidation current peak ($I_{peak}$) is directly proportional to drug concentration, enabling robust threshold-based qualitative classification [RESEARCHED].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
* **Approach 1 (MOS Gas Sensors):** Metal-oxide sensors measure volatile organic compounds (VOCs) via bulk electrical resistance change. They are incapable of capturing heavy non-volatile drug molecules like Morphine ($MW = 285.34\,\text{g/mol}$) or THC ($MW = 314.47\,\text{g/mol}$) and suffer catastrophic false positives from ambient alcohol, acetone, or breath humidity [RESEARCHED].
* **Approach 2 (Raman Spectroscopy):** While scientifically precise, integrating 785nm excitation lasers, high-resolution diffraction gratings, and cooled CCD array detectors into a portable handheld device under hackathon constraints is technically infeasible and financially prohibitive (> ₹2,00,000) [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].

### Detailed Data-Flow Narrative
1. **Sample Capture Phase:** The suspect expires air through the single-use hygienic mouthpiece. The MPX5010DP differential pressure sensor continuously monitors breath flow rate and duration, ensuring a valid sample volume ($\ge 3.0\,\text{L}$ total breath over $10\text{--}15\,\text{seconds}$) [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
2. **Condensation & Sample Delivery:** The exhaled aerosol passes through a micro-channel Peltier cooling plate maintained at $10^\circ\text{C}$ below ambient temperature, forcing liquid droplet condensation. The resulting liquid micro-sample ($~60\,\mu\text{L}$) drops into the reaction zone of the disposable SPCE strip [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
3. **Voltammetric Sweep Activation:** The ESP32 triggers the external MCP4725 12-bit DAC to generate a Differential Pulse Voltammetry (DPV) staircase waveform ($0.0\,\text{V}$ to $+1.2\,\text{V}$, pulse amplitude $50\,\text{mV}$, pulse width $50\,\text{ms}$, step potential $5\,\text{mV}$) [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION]. The TI LMP91000 potentiostat drives the working electrode potential relative to the Ag/AgCl reference electrode and routes the resulting redox current to its internal Transimpedance Amplifier (TIA) [RESEARCHED].
4. **Digitization & Signal Conditioning:** The output voltage from the TIA is sampled by a 16-bit external ADC (ADS1115) at 860 samples/sec [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION]. The ESP32 applies a 9-point Savitzky-Golay smoothing filter to remove high-frequency thermal noise and performs non-linear polynomial baseline subtraction to isolate pure Faraday oxidation peaks [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
5. **Peak Extraction & Classification:** The edge classification engine searches for local current maxima within defined potential windows ($E_{peak} \pm 50\,\text{mV}$). If $I_{peak}$ exceeds the pre-calibrated baseline decision threshold ($I_{th}$) with a Signal-to-Noise Ratio ($\text{SNR} > 3.0$), the targeted drug class is flagged [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
6. **Result Rendering & Chain-of-Custody Logging:** The classification outcome (POSITIVE / NEGATIVE / INCONCLUSIVE) appears on the OLED display within 5 seconds post-sweep. Simultaneously, an encrypted BLE packet containing the raw voltammogram curve, peak values, system diagnostics, timestamp, and hardware ID is pushed to the Flutter Mobile App [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION]. The app appends GPS coordinates, officer metadata, and computes a SHA-256 hash before storing the entry locally or syncing to the NCB cloud portal [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

```
   RAW DPV SIGNAL          DSP SMOOTHING             FEATURE EXTRACTION            CLASSIFICATION
+------------------+    +------------------+    +-------------------------+    +------------------+
| Voltammogram     | -> | Savitzky-Golay   | -> | Peak Potential (E_peak) | -> | Decision Tree /  | -> POSITIVE / NEGATIVE /
| Array (240 pts)  |    | Baseline Removal |    | Peak Current (I_peak)   |    | 1D-CNN Model     |    INCONCLUSIVE
+------------------+    +------------------+    +-------------------------+    +------------------+
```

### AI/ML Component Analysis

* **1. Voltammetric Peak & Pattern Classifier**
  * *Purpose:* To accurately classify overlapping oxidation peaks, handle background capacitive baseline shifts, and detect illicit drug presence from noisy DPV curves [RESEARCHED, INFERENCE].
  * *Model Type:* Lightweight 1D Convolutional Neural Network (1D-CNN) or Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost / Random Forest quantized via TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers) [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
  * *Input:* 240-element float array representing baseline-corrected current measurements ($I_{Faradaic}$) sampled across $0.0\,\text{V}\text{--}+1.2\,\text{V}$ [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
  * *Output:* Multi-class probability vector: $[\text{Cannabis}, \text{Opioids}, \text{Synthetic Drugs}, \text{Negative}]$ plus a confidence metric [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
  * *Data Required:* Minimum 300 calibrated DPV sweeps across varying drug concentrations ($0.1\,\mu\text{M}\text{--}100\,\mu\text{M}$) and common interferents (nicotine, alcohol, caffeine) [RESEARCHED, ASSUMPTION].
  * *Why AI is Preferable:* Simple fixed thresholding fails when multiple substances are consumed simultaneously or when breath humidity shifts the oxidation peak potential by $\pm 30\text{--}50\,\text{mV}$ [RESEARCHED]. A 1D-CNN learns non-linear peak shape characteristics regardless of minor horizontal shifts [RESEARCHED].
  * *Evaluation:* Evaluated via Confusion Matrix, F1-Score, Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC-AUC), and False Positive Rate (FPR) on an independent validation set [RESEARCHED].
  * *Failure Mode:* If model confidence is $< 80\%$, the device automatically defaults to **INCONCLUSIVE** and prompts a retry with a fresh SPCE strip [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
  * *Human Oversight:* All field positives flagged by the device must be verified by a certified FSL laboratory GC-MS/LC-MS test before statutory prosecution [PS, INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
* **Breath Volume Validation:** AI should NOT be used to judge whether a subject blew sufficiently into the device. Simple deterministic rule-based integration of the pressure transducer signal ($V_{total} = \int Q_{flow} \, dt \ge 3.0\,\text{L}$) is 100% verifiable, transparent, and non-probabilistic [INFERENCE].
* **Battery & Hardware Self-Diagnostics:** Battery voltage levels, sensor strip insertion detection, and temperature limits must rely purely on deterministic hardware comparator logic [INFERENCE].

### Offline Synchronization & Storage Mechanism
1. **Local Edge Storage:** The ESP32 buffers up to 100 test runs on SPIFFS flash memory in cyclic queue format. If BLE connection to the phone is severed, test data remains preserved [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
2. **Mobile SQLite Buffer:** The Flutter mobile app writes all received BLE records immediately into a local encrypted SQLite database (SQLCipher) [ASSUMPTION].
3. **Background Synchronization Queue:** An asynchronous background worker monitors network availability. Upon detecting an active 4G/5G/Wi-Fi connection, the queue pushes records in batches of 10 using HTTP/2 POST requests with TLS 1.3 encryption [ASSUMPTION].
4. **Conflict Handling:** Records are immutable and append-only, identified by UUID v4 generated at the device level. Server-side conflict resolution is eliminated because test records cannot be modified after creation [INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION].
