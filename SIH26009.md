## 1. Executive Snapshot

MOIL Limited, a Miniratna CPSE under the Ministry of Steel, is India’s largest producer of manganese ore, supplying approximately 45–50% of domestic demand [RESEARCHED][1.2]. Current manganese reserve estimation and mine production planning rely heavily on traditional manual surveys, core drilling logs, and historical production records [PS]. These manual processes are time-intensive, spatially discrete, and vulnerable to production mismatches caused by unforeseen equipment breakdowns, weather disruptions, and blasting operational bottlenecks [PS]. This dossier presents an end-to-end, AI/ML and Space Technology driven system designed to dynamically predict subsurface manganese ore reserve potential using multispectral satellite observations and borehole geological data, while simultaneously forecasting daily mine production shortfalls and recommending optimal operational realignments to maintain supply continuity [INFERENCE].

*   **Atomic Requirement 1 (Reserve Estimation Baseline & Bottleneck):**
    *   *Source Text:* "At present, reserve estimation and production planning are mainly based on manual surveys, drilling results, and production records. These methods are time-consuming and sometimes lead to a mismatch between expected and actual ore production." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation:* Existing static estimation models fail to account for spatial continuous variability between boreholes and live operational friction, leading to inaccurate ore yield forecasts [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* The solution must ingest discrete core drilling data (depth, grade %, lithology) alongside continuous spatial data layers to interpolate mineral continuity [INFERENCE].
    *   *Tag:* [PS]

*   **Atomic Requirement 2 (Multi-Source Reserve Identification & Mapping):**
    *   *Source Text:* "Identify and map manganese reserves more accurately using surface and sub-surface indicators." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation:* The system must blend surface satellite observations (spectral absorption, soil/vegetation anomalies) with subsurface geological indicators (drill cores, fault lineaments, overburden depth) [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Develop a Mineral Prospectivity Mapping (MPM) model combining satellite Band Ratios (SWIR/VNIR) and spatial machine learning algorithms [INFERENCE].
    *   *Tag:* [PS]

*   **Atomic Requirement 3 (Space Technology Feature Integration):**
    *   *Source Text:* "...uses geological data, historical production, equipment performance, and satellite/space technology inputs (such as rainfall, soil moisture, vegetation index, and land temperature)..." [PS]
    *   *Interpretation:* Space inputs must explicitly feed both reserve identification (e.g., geobotanical stress anomalies, Land Surface Temperature, mineral absorption) and shortfall prediction (e.g., heavy rainfall, soil moisture affecting pit haul roads) [INFERENCE].
    *   *Implementation Implication:* Automated pipeline to pull freely available satellite data (Sentinel-2 MSI, Landsat-8/9 TIRS, GPM/IMD rainfall) and compute NDVI, NDWI/NDMI, LST, and SWIR Manganese Band Ratios [INFERENCE].

## 5. Constraints & Environment Realities

*   **Geographic & Operating Footprint:**
    *   MOIL operates 11 major mines spread across the Saoner-Nagpur-Bhandara belt in Maharashtra and the Balaghat district in Madhya Pradesh [RESEARCHED][1.3].
    *   Mines comprise both underground operations (e.g., Balaghat, Asia's deepest underground manganese mine reaching >383m depth) and opencast pits (e.g., Dongri Buzurg, Tirodi) [RESEARCHED][1.1, 1.3].
    *   *Constraint Implication:* Satellite remote sensing inputs (optical/thermal) primarily detect surface indicators, alteration halos, vegetation stress, and pit topography for opencast mines and prospective greenfield areas [INFERENCE]. For underground mines, satellite data tracks surface subsidence, environmental impact, and surface haulage, while subsurface reserve mapping must rely heavily on 3D borehole/structural geology interpolation [INFERENCE].
*   **Network & Connectivity Constraints:**
    *   Deep opencast pit floors and underground shafts suffer from zero or intermittent cellular coverage (2G/3G patchy) [KNOWN].
    *   *Constraint Implication:* The edge data collection system for equipment telemetry and daily blast logs must support offline caching (PWA / SQLite local store) with auto-sync when field tablets reconnect to mine office Wi-Fi [INFERENCE].
*   **Hardware & Compute Infrastructure:**
    *   Mine site office computers often run mid-tier hardware without dedicated high-end GPUs [KNOWN].
    *   *Constraint Implication:* Heavy AI/ML training and satellite spectral processing must occur in the cloud or server-side API; the frontend client must remain lightweight and render geospatial layers via vector tiles [INFERENCE].
*   **Monsoonal Weather & Field Conditions:**
    *   Central India experiences heavy monsoon rainfall (June–September), frequently causing open-pit flooding, haul road degradation, and complete blasting holds [RESEARCHED][3.6].
    *   *Constraint Implication:* Shortfall algorithms must dynamic-weight monsoonal precipitation and soil saturation levels using satellite microwave/optical soil moisture proxies [INFERENCE].

## 9. Candidate Solution Approaches

### Candidate 1: Rule-Based & Statistical Overlay System (Non-AI Baseline)
*   **Mechanism:** Uses empirical thresholding on satellite band ratios ($B11/B12 > 1.4$) [RESEARCHED][1.10] combined with linear interpolation (IDW) of borehole data for reserves. Shortfalls are calculated via simple deterministic threshold rules (e.g., IF rainfall $> 50\text{ mm}$ THEN halt pit operations; IF machine hours $> 500\text{ hrs}$ THEN flag breakdown) [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros:** Low computational complexity, 100% explainable, rapid hackathon build [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons:** High false-positive rate in reserve mapping due to surface noise; rigid shortfall alerts that cannot model complex interactions (e.g., combined effect of mild rain + partial shovel breakdown) [INFERENCE].

### Candidate 2: Hybrid Machine Learning & Geospatial Multi-Layer System (Recommended)
*   **Mechanism:**
    1.  *Reserve Identification:* Uses ensemble Supervised ML (Random Forest / XGBoost) trained on resampled Sentinel-2 spectral indices (Mn Oxide Index, Iron Index, NDVI, LST) fused with spatial borehole Kriging layers and structural lineaments to output a continuous Mineral Prospectivity Map (MPM) [RESEARCHED][1.2, 2.1].
    2.  *Shortfall Prediction:* Uses Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (LightGBM/XGBoost) trained on historical shift logs, equipment IIoT telemetry (vibration, temp, load), and satellite/GPM weather feeds to forecast daily production shortfalls [RESEARCHED][3.3, 3.6].
    3.  *Prescriptive Actions:* Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) or heuristic rule-matrix to generate optimized fleet re-allocation and bench schedule shifts [INFERENCE].
*   **Evaluation:**
    *   *Problem Fit:* Excellent. Solves both reserve mapping and operational shortfall prediction with high precision [INFERENCE].
    *   *Accuracy Potential:* High ($>85\%$ ROC-AUC for prospectivity, $>90\%$ accuracy for downtime prediction based on benchmarks) [RESEARCHED][1.3, 3.6].
    *   *Complexity:* Moderate. Completely achievable by a 4–6 student team in a hackathon [INFERENCE].
    *   *Explainability:* High using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values for ML decisions [INFERENCE].

### Candidate 3: Deep End-to-End Neural Network (3D CNN + Graph Neural Network + LSTM)
*   **Mechanism:** A 3D Spatial-Convolutional Neural Network (3D-CNN) processes voxelized subsurface drill data and satellite rasters for reserve identification, while a Temporal Graph Neural Network (GNN-LSTM) models the mine logistics network (shovels, dumpers, crushers) for shortfall prediction [INFERENCE].
*   **Pros:** Handles non-linear spatial-temporal relationships natively [INFERENCE].
*   **Cons:** Severe data hungry requirements (requires tens of thousands of labeled drill holes and sensor streams), extremely high compute cost, black-box nature, unfeasible for a hackathon build without massive proprietary datasets [INFERENCE].

## 10. Recommended Approach & Why

**Selected Strategy:** **Candidate 2 (Hybrid Machine Learning & Geospatial Multi-Layer System)**.

### Why Candidate 2 Wins
1.  **Strict Requirement Alignment:** Candidate 2 directly maps to every explicit requirement in SIH26009 [PS]. It effectively combines satellite inputs (NDVI, soil moisture, LST, spectral band ratios) with subsurface borehole data for reserve mapping, and uses equipment/weather metrics for shortfall forecasting [INFERENCE].
2.  **Feasibility for Student Hackathon Team:** Random Forest and XGBoost algorithms can be trained efficiently on standard CPU/GPU resources using Python (`scikit-learn`, `xgboost`, `lightgbm`, `geopandas`) without requiring massive supercomputing clusters [INFERENCE].
3.  **Explainability for MOIL Engineers:** Mining domain experts require clear justifications before re-deploying multi-crore heavy machinery or changing blasting plans [KNOWN]. Candidate 2 supports SHAP analysis, allowing the system to explicitly state: *"Production shortfall predicted due to 70% probability of Shovel-02 hydraulic failure AND 35mm expected monsoonal rainfall in Pit-B"* [INFERENCE].

### Why Alternatives Were Rejected
*   *Candidate 1 (Rule-Based)* was rejected because linear rules cannot handle complex non-linear mineralizations or multi-variable operational bottlenecks, leading to high false alarm rates [INFERENCE].
*   *Candidate 3 (Deep 3D CNN / GNN)* was rejected due to extreme data dependency, excessive computational requirements, poor model explainability, and unrealistic implementation timeline for a hackathon MVP [INFERENCE].

### Data Flow Narrative
1.  **Data Ingestion:** Satellite imagery tiles are fetched via Google Earth Engine API or Copernicus Open Access. Simultaneously, local mine managers upload borehole drilling CSV files and daily equipment operational logs [INFERENCE].
2.  **Geospatial Preprocessing:** The backend raster engine computes critical spectral indices for Manganese oxides ($B11/B12$), iron oxides ($B4/B2$), vegetation stress ($NDVI$), and Land Surface Temperature ($LST$) [RESEARCHED][1.10, 2.1]. The vector engine performs 3D Kriging interpolation on borehole assay points to estimate sub-surface manganese grade distribution [INFERENCE].
3.  **ML Inference:**
    *   The *Reserve Prospectivity Model* evaluates raster pixel stacks against ground-truth mine boundaries to generate a probability map of unmined manganese reserves [INFERENCE].
    *   The *Shortfall Predictor* processes daily equipment telemetry, scheduled blasting windows, and GPM satellite rain forecasts to compute predicted daily tonnage and shortfall probability [INFERENCE].
4.  **Prescriptive Solving:** If a shortfall risk exceeds threshold ($>15\%$), the MILP solver evaluates operational constraints (dumper speeds, bench capacities) and outputs recommended corrective adjustments [INFERENCE].
5.  **Visualization & Action:** The FastAPI server delivers vector tiles and JSON payloads to the React dashboard, rendering reserve maps, shortfall risk meters, and prescriptive action cards [INFERENCE].

## 13. AI/ML Justification

### 1. Manganese Reserve Prospectivity Model
*   **Purpose:** Predict spatial probability ($0.0 - 1.0$) of subsurface manganese ore occurrence [INFERENCE].
*   **Model Type:** Random Forest Classifier / XGBoost [RESEARCHED][1.2, 2.1].
*   **Input:** Sentinel-2 Band Ratios ($B11/B12$, $B4/B2$), NDVI anomaly, Land Surface Temperature, DEM Slope, Structural Lineament Density, Interpolated Borehole Grade (Mn%) [RESEARCHED][1.10, 2.1].
*   **Output:** GeoTIFF / Raster grid of reserve prospective scores [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required:** Labeled spatial coordinates of known MOIL ore bodies (positive samples) and non-mineralized barren zones (negative samples) + satellite rasters + borehole logs [INFERENCE].
*   **Why AI Over Simple Rules:** Spectral signatures of manganese oxides overlap with other iron/clay minerals; supervised ML learns non-linear decision boundaries across multi-spectral and topographical dimensions that fixed rules miss [INFERENCE].
*   **Evaluation Metric:** ROC-AUC, Precision-Recall AUC, Spatial Cross-Validation ($k$-fold based on spatial blocks) [RESEARCHED][1.2].
*   **Failure Mode & Mitigation:** False positives (predicting ore where none exists). Mitigated by requiring a minimum probability confidence threshold ($>0.75$) before flagging target zones for exploratory drilling [INFERENCE].
*   **Human Oversight:** Mining Geologists must validate high-probability target zones with field sampling before capital allocation [INFERENCE].

### 2. Mine Production Shortfall Predictor
*   **Purpose:** Forecast daily ore extraction shortfall (tonnage deficit) 1–7 days in advance [INFERENCE].
*   **Model Type:** LightGBM / XGBoost Regressor [RESEARCHED][3.6, 3.10].
*   **Input:** Target tonnage, equipment operational hours, MTBF/MTTR metrics, vibration/temperature anomaly indicators, forecasted rainfall (mm), soil moisture index (NDMI), blasting hold duration [RESEARCHED][3.3, 3.6].
*   **Output:** Predicted Tonnage Produced, Shortfall Tonnage, Shortfall Risk Score (Low/Medium/High) [INFERENCE].
*   **Data Required:** Historical shift production logs, equipment maintenance logs, IMD/GPM historical weather [INFERENCE].
*   **Why AI Over Simple Rules:** Operational breakdowns result from compound events (e.g., moderate rain + 2 haul trucks undergoing routine maintenance = bottleneck at crusher). ML captures these feature interactions automatically [INFERENCE].
*   **Evaluation Metric:** Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE $<10\%$) [INFERENCE].
*   **Failure Mode & Mitigation:** False negatives (failing to predict a breakdown). Mitigated by incorporating safety buffers and alerting maintenance teams on any abnormal telemetry spikes [INFERENCE].
*   **Human Oversight:** Mine Dispatch Manager reviews predicted shortfalls before executing suggested fleet adjustments [INFERENCE].

### Where AI Should NOT Be Used
*   *Deterministic Inventory & Tonnage Accounting:* Raw production tallying, weightbridge readings, and grade blending arithmetic must use exact deterministic math, not probabilistic AI predictions [INFERENCE].
*   *Spatial Coordinate Transformations:* Map projections (UTM to WGS84) must use standard spatial library formulas (`pyproj` / GDAL), never ML approximations [INFERENCE].

```sql
-- Borehole Geological Data
CREATE TABLE boreholes (

    latitude DOUBLE PRECISION,

-- Daily Production & Shortfall Tracking
CREATE TABLE daily_production (
    log_id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
    mine_id VARCHAR(50),
    log_date DATE,
    target_tonnage DOUBLE PRECISION,
    actual_tonnage DOUBLE PRECISION,
    shortfall_tonnage DOUBLE PRECISION,
    rainfall_mm DOUBLE PRECISION,
    active_excavators INT,
    active_dumpers INT,
    blasting_delays_hrs DOUBLE PRECISION,
    shortfall_risk_score DOUBLE PRECISION
);

-- Equipment Telemetry & Health
CREATE TABLE equipment_status (
    equipment_id VARCHAR(50) PRIMARY KEY,
    mine_id VARCHAR(50),
    equipment_type VARCHAR(50), -- Shovel, Dumper, Crusher
    operating_hours DOUBLE PRECISION,
    vibration_index DOUBLE PRECISION,
    engine_temp_c DOUBLE PRECISION,
    hydraulic_pressure_psi DOUBLE PRECISION,
    failure_probability DOUBLE PRECISION,
    last_serviced DATE
);
```

### Offline & Low-Network Synchronization
*   **Local Caching (PWA / IndexedDB):** Field engineers entering pit floors or underground shafts use a Progressive Web App (PWA) with client-side IndexedDB caching [INFERENCE].
*   **Store-and-Forward Sync:** Shift logs, equipment status checks, and blasting delays recorded offline are tagged with a UTC timestamp and queued locally [INFERENCE]. Upon returning to mine site office Wi-Fi, the PWA automatically pushes queued JSON payloads to the FastAPI backend [INFERENCE].
*   **Conflict Resolution:** Last-Write-Wins (LWW) policy based on verified server timestamps, with manual override prompts for critical safety data conflicts [INFERENCE].
*   **Bandwidth Reduction:** GeoJSON layers are vector-tiled (`.pbf` format) and raster imagery is served as Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs (COGs), reducing map data transmission overhead by up to 80% [INFERENCE].
